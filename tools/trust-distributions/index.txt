# Is this trust distribution inside the section 100A green zone?

PCG 2022/2 sorts section 100A arrangements into white, green, and red zones. There is no blue zone: that was proposed in the draft, PCG 2022/D1, and did not survive into the final guideline. This tool classifies the facts you supply against the green and red zones, works out Division 6 shares, and calculates a section 99B amount on a foreign trust distribution.

Reference release solomons-sword/v0.1.3, tagged source on GitHub.

Published 24 August 2026. Last reviewed 11 September 2026.

Section 100A can treat a trust distribution as a reimbursement agreement and tax it at the top rate instead of the beneficiary's own rate. The ATO's finalised guideline PCG 2022/2 says where the risk sits.

The white zone, for income years ending before 1 July 2014, is out of scope because the tool does not take an income year. Division 6 shares follow the High Court's decision in Bamford.

Where the tool does not implement a rule, such as streamed capital gains and franked dividends under the Division 6E carve-out, it refuses the input rather than returning a number it cannot stand behind.

## What this tool does

- Classifies a trust distribution against the ATO's section 100A green and red risk zones under PCG 2022/2, from facts you supply.
- Works out each beneficiary's proportionate share of trust income under Division 6, following the Bamford case.
- Calculates the assessable amount on a foreign trust distribution under section 99B, after corpus and previously taxed income deductions.

## Get it

Needs Python 3.10 or later. Install the reference release in your Python environment.

```
python -m pip install "solomons-sword==0.1.3"
```

## Fabricated worked example

This section 99B example assumes a beneficiary who was resident during the income year, a gross receipt of $150,000, and $50,000 of qualifying corpus. It assumes no corpus attributable to notionally assessable income and no other exclusions. The name and amounts are fabricated.

```
solomons-sword s99b-check --beneficiary "Fabricated Beneficiary" --gross 150000 --corpus 50000
```

Selected output from release 0.1.3, verified on 11 September 2026. The command exits 0 and also prints its statutory basis and review boundaries.

```
Gross Receipt:           $150,000.00
Corpus Exemption:        $50,000.00
Assessable under s99B:   $100,000.00
```

### Try a refusal

Change --corpus 50000 to --corpus 160000. The exemptions now exceed the gross receipt. The command exits 2 and returns no assessment:

```
error: exemptions ($160,000.00) exceed the gross receipt ($150,000.00); check the inputs rather than assuming a nil assessable amount
```

This verifies the published command's behaviour on fabricated inputs. Confirm residence, the character of the corpus, and each exclusion before using the tool with real facts.

## Common questions

### Is there a blue zone in PCG 2022/2?

No. The blue zone appeared only in the draft, PCG 2022/D1. The finalised PCG 2022/2 has white, green, and red zones only.

### What is the white zone?

It covers arrangements in income years ending before 1 July 2014. This tool does not take an income year, so the white zone is out of scope here.

### How is each beneficiary's share of trust income worked out?

Proportionately, following the High Court's decision in Bamford: a beneficiary's share of trust income sets the proportion of the trust's taxable income assessed to them.

### What will this tool refuse to calculate?

Anything it does not implement, including streamed capital gains and franked dividends under the Division 6E carve-out. It refuses the input rather than returning a number it cannot stand behind.
