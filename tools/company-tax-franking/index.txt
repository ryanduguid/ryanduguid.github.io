# Company tax and franking worksheets

A company gets the lower tax rate only if it is a base rate entity: aggregated turnover under the year's threshold, and no more than 80% of assessable income being base rate entity passive income. Get that test wrong and the company pays the wrong income tax for the year. The rate used to frank a dividend is a separate imputation question that turns on prior-year facts, not this year's test; this tool keeps the two apart and the worked example below does not determine a maximum franking rate. It runs the test, tracks the franking account and checks the Division 203 benchmark rule.

Software demonstration only. Ryan Duguid is not a registered tax agent or BAS agent. The examples assume supplied facts. Have a registered tax agent verify those facts and review their tax treatment before relying on a result. A successful calculation is not confirmation of entitlement, liability or compliance.

Reference release the-exchequer-tally/v0.1.3, tagged source on GitHub. The worked example on this page is pinned to that release so its figures stay reproducible; the current release is the-exchequer-tally/v0.1.6. pip install the-exchequer-tally fetches it.

Published 24 August 2026. Last reviewed 24 September 2026.

This tool runs the base rate entity test and tracks a franking account ledger covering PAYG instalments, company tax paid, dividends paid and received, and refunds. It works out franking deficit tax and checks the 30% offset reduction for supported debit types. The debit types and applicable exceptions determine whether a reduction applies, as explained under Common questions. It also checks distributions in a franking period against the Division 203 benchmark rule for over-franking tax and franking debit shortfalls.

It builds the statement fields a dividend distribution statement needs, too. That is a review helper for the statement, not a lodgement or a compliance certificate.

## What this tool does

- Tests whether a company is a base rate entity for a given financial year, against the aggregated turnover threshold and the 80% passive income test.
- Tracks a franking account ledger and checks franking deficit tax and any offset reduction for supported debit types, subject to the conditions under Common questions.
- Checks distributions in a franking period against the Division 203 benchmark rule, for over-franking tax and franking debit shortfalls.

## Limits

Nothing this engine produces is tax, legal or financial advice, an assessment or a determination. Outputs are review aids: confirm every rate, threshold and consequence against the current law and the entity's facts before acting, and leave lodgement decisions with a registered practitioner.

## Fictional worked example

With uv installed, run this pinned package directly. No clone or input file is needed.

For the income year ending in 2025, use fictional aggregated turnover of $4,500,000, assessable income of $800,000 and base rate entity passive income of $120,000. The caller must establish those classifications before running the test.

```
uvx --from the-exchequer-tally==0.1.3 the-exchequer-tally bre-test --fy 2025 --turnover 4500000 --assessable 800000 --passive 120000
```

```

Base Rate Entity:        True

```

Change --fy 2025 to --fy 2017. Release 0.1.3 has no bundled rate table for that year. It exits 2 and returns no rate.

## Common questions

### What makes a company a base rate entity?

Two tests, both of which must hold for the income year: aggregated turnover below that year's threshold, and no more than 80% of assessable income being base rate entity passive income.

### What is franking deficit tax?

Tax payable where a franking account ends the year in deficit. Where the deficit exceeds 10% of the credits arising in the year, the offset may be reduced by 30%, but only for the part of the liability that relates to franking debits arising under the provisions listed in section 205-70(8). Check the debit types and any exceptions before applying the reduction.

### What is the benchmark rule?

Under Division 203, a company must frank all distributions in a franking period to the same extent. Departing from the benchmark franking percentage produces over-franking tax or a franking debit shortfall.
