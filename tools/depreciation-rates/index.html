Depreciation rates and effective lives

Search the 7,430 effective lives in the Commissioner of Taxation’s 2025 determination, registered on 15 September 2025 and unamended on 13 November 2026. Each row shows the life, the prime cost and diminishing value rates it gives and the date it was last set.

General information, not tax advice. The search and the calculator run in your browser and send nothing you type to a server.

Published 13 November 2026. Last reviewed 13 November 2026.

Find an asset

Type an asset to search both tables of the determination at once, or choose your industry to see its rows first. Table A lists assets by industry and Table B lists assets used across industries.

Under section 6 of the determination, an asset used in an industry named in Table A takes that industry’s life where a row describes the asset or its function. Table B applies where Table A does not.

For example laptop, forklift, carpet or solar. Every word you type must appear in the row, the headings above it or its industry.

Browse the tables

Open an industry in Table A or a letter in Table B to read its rows in the determination’s order. A row with no life is a heading for the rows indented under it, or points to another row.

Work out a year’s deduction

Enter a cost, an effective life and the day the asset was first used or installed ready for use to see its decline in value for each income year. Choosing ‘Use this life’ on a row above fills in the life.

Decline in value inputs

Whole years, a decimal or a fraction as the determination prints it, such as 10, 7.5 or 6 2/3.

Enter the inputs and work out the deduction.

Income years end on 30 June. The working assumes you hold the asset for all of each later year and use it only for a taxable purpose, with no second element of cost. Each year’s amount rounds to the nearest cent, with exact half cents rounded up.

An asset you started to hold before 10 May 2006 is not covered, because its diminishing value rate is 150% and not 200%.

Which determination applies to your asset

The Commissioner’s life for an asset is the one in force when you entered a contract to acquire it, otherwise acquired it or started to construct it, where its start time falls within 5 years of then. Otherwise the life in force at the start time applies (subsection 40-95(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997). Plant acquired before 11.45 am on 21 September 1999, by legal time in the Australian Capital Territory, has its own rule in that subsection.

The 2025 determination commenced on 16 September 2025 and repealed the 2015 determination. Each row’s ‘last updated’ date is the date the Commissioner last reviewed and determined that life. For an asset acquired earlier, check the determination in force at that time: the ATO lists them on its page Effective life determinations, rulings and law.

You may instead work out an effective life yourself under section 40-105. You make the choice for the income year in which the asset’s start time occurs (subsection 40-95(3)).

How a life becomes a rate

Prime cost spreads the cost evenly over the life: cost × (days held ÷ 365) × (100% ÷ effective life), and never more than the value left (section 40-75). Diminishing value works on the value left: base value × (days held ÷ 365) × (200% ÷ effective life), for an asset you started to hold on or after 10 May 2006 (section 40-72). An earlier asset uses 150% (section 40-70).

Days held can be 366 in a leap year and the divisor stays 365, as the ATO’s Guide to depreciating assets 2026 says. You choose one method for each asset and cannot change it (section 40-65).

The rates shown beside each life are 100% and 200% divided by the life, rounded to 2 decimal places. The calculator works from the exact life, so a life of 6⅔ years is 30% and not 29.99%.

Capped lives

A row marked ‘capped life available’ can take a shorter life set by section 40-102, where you use the Commissioner’s life and the cap that applies at the relevant time is shorter. The Act’s tables give no start date for any item, and the dates each cap began were not read for this page.

Pools, write-offs and the car limit

Four rules can replace or limit a deduction worked out from an effective life. Each is stated from the law in force on 1 October 2026 and the ATO page named.

Low-value pool

A low-cost asset costs less than $1,000 at the end of the income year you start to use it, and can go into a low-value pool (section 40-425). The pool declines by 18¾% of the cost of low-cost assets added that year and 37½% of the closing balance of the year before (section 40-440).

Once you pool one low-cost asset, each later one goes in too, and an asset stays in the pool (section 40-430). An asset deducted under the small business rules cannot be pooled this way.

Small business pool and instant asset write-off

A small business entity, with aggregated turnover under $10 million, that chooses simplified depreciation deducts in full an asset costing less than $20,000 (sections 328-110 and 328-180). The $20,000 threshold is permanent for assets first used or installed ready for use on or after 1 July 2026, under the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 2) Act 2026.

A temporary rule gives the same $20,000 for assets first used or installed from 1 July 2023 to 30 June 2026 (section 328-180 of the Income Tax (Transitional Provisions) Act 1997). Other assets go to the general small business pool, deducted at 15% in their first year and 30% of the opening balance after that (section 328-190). A pool balance under $20,000 is written off (section 328-210).

Assets costing $300 or less

An asset costing $300 or less is deducted in full where you use it mainly to earn income that is not from carrying on a business (subsection 40-80(2)). The asset must not be part of a set, or one of several identical items, costing more than $300 in total that year.

Car limit

The cost of a car designed mainly for carrying passengers is reduced to the car limit for the financial year you started to hold it: $69,883 for 2026-27 and $69,674 for 2025-26 (section 40-230 and the ATO’s figures). Car limit for depreciation gives each year and its source.

Capital works

Construction costs of capital works are deducted under Division 43 and not by effective life: 2.5% a year for works begun after 26 February 1992, or 4% for parts used in the ways Table 43-145 lists (section 43-25). Those uses include short-term traveller accommodation and industrial activities and, for works begun after 7:30 pm by legal time in the Australian Capital Territory on 9 May 2023, build to rent.

Works begun before 27 February 1992 take 4% if begun after 21 August 1984 and before 16 September 1987, and 2.5% in any other case. No deduction is available for a period before construction is complete (section 43-30).

Source, licence and checks

Every row comes from Schedule 2 of the Income Tax Assessment (Effective Life of Depreciating Assets) Determination 2025 (F2025L01097), made on 2 September 2025 and registered on 15 September 2025. The data file holds all 9,311 rows, the source’s details and a count of every table row read.

A program builds the file from the Register’s EPUB and stops at any row it cannot read. On 13 November 2026 every row was compared with the Register’s Word version and with the text of its authorised PDF, with no difference in a description, life, mark or date.

Limits

The page does not decide whether something is a depreciating asset, who holds it, what it cost or how far it is used for a taxable purpose. Check a life against the registered determination before relying on it.

The calculator leaves out balancing adjustments, second elements of cost, private use, pooling, the car limit, capped lives you have not entered and any rule that replaces these methods for a period. A life the Commissioner has not listed can be worked out under section 40-105 or requested with the ATO’s effective life review application form.
