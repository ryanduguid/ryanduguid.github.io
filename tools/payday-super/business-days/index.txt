# Payday Super business days

Payday Super contributions must generally reach the fund within 7 business days after payday. The weekdays below, from 1 July 2026 to 31 August 2027, are not business days for any employer: each is a public holiday for the whole of a state or territory.

## What counts as a business day

Section 6(1) of the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 defines a business day as a day other than a Saturday, a Sunday or ‘a day which is a public holiday for the whole of’ any State, the ACT or the NT.

One national calendar applies to every employer. The ATO says a state or territory-wide public holiday is not a business day ‘even if you are not in that state or territory’, so Western Australia Day on 7 June 2027 stops the clock for an employer in Sydney.

A public holiday for only part of a state or territory does not count; the ATO’s example is Royal Hobart Show Day. For the same reason, these days are business days and are not in the table:

- part-day holidays: Christmas Eve evening in SA, QLD and the NT, and New Year’s Eve evening in SA and the NT
- regional show days and other local holidays, such as Royal Hobart Regatta in southern Tasmania (8 February 2027) and the Royal Queensland Show day for the Brisbane area (12 August 2026 and 11 August 2027)
- Melbourne Cup Day (3 November 2026), which a non-metropolitan Victorian council can replace with a local holiday
- King’s Birthday in WA (28 September 2026), which Karratha and Port Hedland observed on 3 August 2026 instead
- Recreation Day in Tasmania (2 November 2026), observed only where Royal Hobart Regatta is not
- Easter Tuesday in Tasmania (30 March 2027), which section 6 of the Statutory Holidays Act 2000 gives only to certain employees

The bank holiday on the first Monday in August in NSW and the ACT does not count either: NSW says it is not a declared public holiday. In 2026 and 2027 that Monday is NT Picnic Day, so it is in the table anyway.

## Counting a due date

A contribution is on time if the fund receives it, with the information needed to allocate it, within 7 business days after payday. Payday itself is not counted.

The ATO’s own example: for earnings paid on Thursday 30 July 2026, the fund must receive the contribution by Tuesday 11 August 2026. Without a holiday, the seventh business day after 30 July would be Monday 10 August; NT Picnic Day on 3 August is not a business day, so the due date is a day later.

The ATO allows longer in 4 situations: the first contribution for a new employee or to a new fund (20 business days after payday), out-of-cycle payments, exceptional circumstances affecting multiple employers and its bunching rule for overlapping due dates. The Payday Super checker uses this calendar for its due dates.

## What the calendar covers

The calendar covers 1 July 2026, when Payday Super began, to 31 August 2027, for all 6 states, the ACT and the NT in one national list. It holds the weekday public holidays for the whole of a state or territory that each official page below listed on 14 November 2026.

It does not cover:

- paydays before 1 July 2026, which follow the quarterly super guarantee rules
- dates after 31 August 2027: Business Victoria still lists the 2027 Friday before the AFL Grand Final as ‘Subject to AFL schedule’, so the calendar stops before that holiday can fall
- a public holiday declared or moved after 14 November 2026, such as a one-off holiday, which would change the due dates around it; check the jurisdiction’s page before relying on a date
- an earlier deadline in a fund’s rules, an award or an enterprise agreement, or the time a clearing house or fund takes to process a payment
