## What the inputs need

A small business entity has aggregated turnover under $10 million and has chosen simplified depreciation. A medium sized business could write off an asset only from 12 March 2020 to 30 June 2021. From 7.30 pm AEDT on 6 October 2020 to 30 June 2023, temporary full expensing applied instead; for a small business entity the calculator writes the whole asset off then if the asset was acquired from that time, and for any other entity it refuses such an asset.

The limits turn on the dates: the asset is written off in the income year it is first used or installed ready for use, and some limits apply only to an asset first acquired after a budget’s 7.30 pm announcement. Where the limit turns on acquisition after a budget, an asset acquired on that budget day itself (12 May 2015, 2 April 2019 or 6 October 2020) is refused, as the calculator takes dates, not times.

The cost is the asset’s whole cost; it must be less than the limit. The deduction is the cost times the share of use for a taxable purpose. An asset at or above the limit goes into the small business pool, or for other entities is depreciated under Division 40.

## How the instant asset write-off calculator works

The instant asset write-off calculator decides whether one depreciating asset is written off in full in the income year it is first used, or installed ready for use, for a taxable purpose, from 2020-21 to 2026-27. It finds the cost limit that applies from the business, the date the asset was first acquired and the date it was first used, and writes the asset off if its cost is less than that limit. The deduction is the cost times the taxable use, rounded to the cent.

Its results match the instant-asset-write-off command of Ryan Duguid’s australian-accounting calculators on 366 test cases, refusals included.

## What the instant asset write-off calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a business is a small business entity or medium sized business, whether it chose simplified depreciation, or the taxable use: you state those. It does not work out the small business pool, the car limit, second element costs added later, a balancing adjustment on disposal, backing business investment, or temporary full expensing for entities other than small business entities using simplified depreciation, which it refuses. It refuses an asset acquired on a budget day itself where the limit turns on acquisition after that budget. Results are planning estimates: check them against your records and the ATO’s guidance.
