## How the FBT return calculator works

The calculator lays out one employer’s FBT return for the FBT year ended 31 March 2026 or 2027 from the benefits you enter. It adds the taxable values of type 1 benefits, those whose provider could claim GST credits, and of type 2 benefits, excluded benefits included. Type 1 is grossed up by (0.47 + 0.10) / ((1 - 0.47) x 1.10 x 0.47), about 2.0802, and type 2 by 1 / (1 - 0.47), about 1.8868. Each is rounded to the dollar at items 14A and 14B, as the ATO’s worked example does, and item 16 is 47% of their sum at item 15. Item 23 adds each category’s gross taxable value, employee contributions, reductions and taxable value. An employee whose benefits that are not excluded total more than $2,000 has a reportable fringe benefits amount of that total times 1 / (1 - 0.47), with the cents dropped as the ATO’s examples show. A rebatable employer’s item 17 is 47% of the amounts over the $30,000 cap for each employee, and its rebate at item 18 is 47% of item 16 less item 17, for its rebatable days. A public benevolent institution, health promotion charity, hospital or ambulance service pays FBT only on the grossed-up amount over its $30,000 or $17,000 cap for each employee, at item 14C. Up to $5,000 of salary packaged entertainment comes off the amount over a cap. Given the instalments reported, items 20 to 22 show the payment or credit.

## What the FBT return calculator does not cover

The calculator does not value a benefit: you enter each one’s amounts as worked out for it. It does not lodge a return, decide whether a benefit is type 1, excluded, exempt or salary packaged, or count the cars, loans and units of accommodation at items 23A, 23B, 23C and 23F, or the employees at item 10. It does not cover a government body under section 57A(2), an employer partly a public benevolent institution and partly rebatable, live-in residential care workers under section 58, amortised or reducible benefits, section 135G, one employee’s benefits from several employers, or an exempt electric car for a section 57A employer. Each employee you name is taken as one person, as the caps apply to each individual. It does not work out payroll tax, which each state and territory imposes under its own Act. For instalments, it does not decide whether they are payable or work out the ATO’s F1 amount from the notional tax amount; you enter it.
