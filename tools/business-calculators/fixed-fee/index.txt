## How the fixed fee pricing calculator works

The fixed fee pricing calculator turns an estimate of hours into a fee you can quote before the work starts. Enter your hourly rate with a 0% margin, or your cost per hour with the margin you want the fee to carry: fee = hours × rate / (1 − margin). A margin is a share of the fee, so a 40% margin on a $95 cost is $158.33 an hour, not the $133 a 40% markup would give. The fee then rounds up to the cent or to the next $10, $50 or $100, so rounding never takes it below your rate or margin.

GST at 10% is added to the fee when you tick the box, and the fee including GST is split into monthly instalments. Each instalment is the total divided evenly and rounded down to the cent; the first instalment also carries the cents left over, at most one cent for each instalment after it. The instalments add up to the fee to the cent, every instalment after the first is the same amount for a recurring payment, and if the engagement ends early the client has paid at least an even share of the fee for each month paid. The effective hourly rate is the fee before GST over the hours, so you can see what rounding and the margin earn an hour.

## What the fixed fee pricing calculator leaves out

The fixed fee pricing calculator prices one piece of work. For several services or entities, price each and add the fees. It does not suggest a market rate, allow for work beyond the hours you estimated, or decide whether your supply is taxable: add GST only if you are registered for GST and the supply is taxable.
