## How the company tax and franking calculator works

The calculator applies the base rate entity test of section 23AA of the Income Tax Rates Act 1986 to this year’s figures: aggregated turnover under $50 million, and base rate entity passive income of no more than 80% of assessable income. A base rate entity pays 25% of its taxable income, and any other company 30% (subsection 23(2)).

The rate for franking a dividend comes from a second test. Section 995-1 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 runs the base rate entity test on the previous year’s turnover, passive income and assessable income, so a company can pay tax at 30% and frank at 25%, or the reverse; a company that did not exist in the previous year franks at 25%. The maximum franking credit is the dividend times 1 over the gross-up rate, which is 100% less that rate, over that rate (section 202-60): one third of the dividend at 25% and three sevenths at 30%. A credit above the maximum is taken to be the maximum (section 202-65).

The shareholder includes the dividend and its franking credit in assessable income and gets a tax offset equal to the credit (section 207-20). Tax at the marginal rate you enter, less the offset, is the extra tax the dividend brings; where the offset is larger, an individual gets the excess refunded (section 67-25).

The calculator reproduces the outcome of each ATO example: Happy Feet (3.8% passive income, 25%), Coffee and Cake (28.6%, 25%), Best Equity (100%, 30%), SoftDev (85%, 30%) and Hats 4 Cats ($170 million of aggregated turnover, 30%). For Dillmore Manufacture’s $100,000 dividend franked at 30% it gives $42,857.14, the exact credit; the ATO rounds the gross-up rate to 2.3333 and shows $42,857.75, which is more than the section 202-60 maximum.

## How the calculation statement and loss carry back work

The optional calculation statement works the company tax return from assessable income to the amount due. Prior-year losses are deducted only where you state that the continuity of ownership test or the same or similar business test was passed (section 165-10), oldest first; net exempt income absorbs them first, and without a chosen amount the most allowed is deducted (section 36-17). Gross tax is taxable income at the year’s rate, rounded half up to the cent. Offsets come off in the return’s order: non-refundable non-carry forward offsets (C), carry forward offsets (D), refundable offsets (E) and the franking deficit tax offset (F); then the interest charge (G) is added and credits (H), refundable offsets in excess of tax (I) and PAYG instalments (K) taken off, giving S, the amount due or refundable. The statement’s taxable income must equal the taxable income entered above.

A loss carry back tax offset is worked only for the claim years the law gives one: 2020-21 to 2022-23 under Division 160 as it stood (this page offers 2021-22 and 2022-23), and 2026-27 under the Division 160 that Act No. 71 of 2026 substituted for income years from 1 July 2026, for a company that is not a significant global entity. There is none for 2023-24 to 2025-26. The page carries the year’s own tax loss back to one or both of the 2 income years before it: each amount after that year’s net exempt income, at the rate of the loss year, capped at the earlier year’s unused income tax liability, and the total capped at the franking account balance. The offset is refundable, at label E.

## What the company tax and franking calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out aggregated turnover, assessable income or base rate entity passive income: enter figures you have established, with the turnover of connected entities and affiliates included, as the ATO’s Hats 4 Cats example shows. Without the statement, taxable income gives the tax before offsets, PAYG instalments and other credits. The statement does not judge the loss tests, works no part-year loss (section 165-20) or Subdivision 165-B year, does not cover consolidated and MEC groups, foreign residents or companies taxed at other rates, and refuses franking tax offsets with a chosen loss deduction (section 36-17(5)) or in excess of tax (section 36-55), and carry forward offsets with net exempt income. On this page the carry back takes the year’s own loss to the 2 years before it; earlier loss years carried back under the former Division 160 are left to the WebMCP tool.

Not covered: the rates of RSA providers and pooled development funds (subsections 23(3) to (5)), life insurance companies (section 23A), not-for-profit companies and credit unions; corporate unit trusts and public trading trusts; and income years before 2021-22, when the base rate was 27.5% or 26%. A shareholder that is a company, trust, super fund or non-resident is outside its scope; a company shareholder cannot have excess franking credits refunded (section 67-25). Franking account balances, franking deficit tax, the benchmark rule and the holding period rule are not tested: the company tax and franking worksheets track the franking account and the benchmark rule. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.
