## How the FBT loan calculator works

The FBT loan calculator values one loan fringe benefit for the FBT year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. The notional interest is the interest the loan would have earned at the statutory interest rate, 8.27%, on its balance at the end of each day: the total of the daily balances × 8.27% ÷ 365. The gross taxable value is the amount by which that exceeds the interest that accrued on the loan for the year, never below nil.

Where the employee, not an associate, would have been able to deduct interest on the loan, the otherwise deductible rule reduces the value. The gross deduction is the notional interest × the share of the loan used to produce the employee’s assessable income. The notional deduction is the gross deduction less the deduction the employee could claim for the interest that accrued. The taxable value is the gross taxable value less the notional deduction, never below nil.

Section 8.8 of the ATO’s guide for employers works a $50,000 loan at 4% for the whole year, 60% used for interest-bearing investments, at the 5.65% rate of 2016-17: $825 less a $495 reduction, a taxable value of $330. This page opens on the same loan at 8.27%: notional interest of $4,135.00 less $2,000.00 accrued is $2,135.00; the gross deduction of $2,481.00 less the $1,200.00 deduction for the interest accrued leaves $1,281.00; the taxable value is $854.00. Run at 5.65%, the same steps give the guide’s $330, and its section 8.5 example’s $325; the tests hold both.

## What the FBT loan calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a loan fringe benefit arises or whether an exemption in section 17 applies, such as a loan at an arm’s length rate by a lender to the public or an advance for work expenses accounted for within 6 months. It does not treat deferred or unpaid interest, which can be a further loan or a debt waiver fringe benefit, a loan made before 1 July 1986 or a housing loan made before 3 April 1986, which can take a lower rate, a car loan, whose otherwise deductible amount follows the car substantiation rules in subsection 19(1), or a loan provided jointly to an employee and an associate. It does not work out the employee’s deduction, and it does not check the declaration the rule requires. A loan fringe benefit carries no GST credit, so it is grossed up at the Type 2 rate, 1.8868; this calculator stops at the taxable value and does not gross it up or work out FBT. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s guide or a registered tax agent.
