## How the motor vehicle duty calculator works

The calculator works out the duty on registering, or transferring the registration of, one passenger vehicle with a duty date in 2026-27, on the dutiable value you enter in whole dollars. Each state and territory charges a rate for every $100, or part of $100 ($200 in Victoria), of the value or of the part over a threshold: New South Wales $3, and $1,350 plus $5 over $45,000; Victoria $8.40 to the luxury car tax threshold, then $10.40, $14 and $18, or $8.40 throughout for a green car or a primary producer’s car; Queensland by the engine row, $2 more above $100,000; South Australia’s banded scale with a $5 minimum; Tasmania’s scale or its fleet discount rate; the ACT’s rate for the emissions category under the determination in force on the duty date, used vehicles other than Category AAA paying Category C’s; and the Northern Territory’s $3, with the first $50,000 of an electric vehicle exempt. Western Australia charges 2.75% to $25,000, a rate rising to 6.5% at $50,000 and 6.5% above, rounded down to 5 cents. It is a port of the motor-vehicle-duty command in the australian-accounting repository, checked against that command case by case.

## What the motor vehicle duty calculator does not cover

The calculator covers passenger vehicles only: it refuses motorcycles, goods and commercial vehicles, buses, heavy vehicles, trailers and special or conditionally registered vehicles. It does not work out the dutiable value, decide whether an exemption or concession applies beyond the ones it asks about, or add registration, CTP insurance or other fees. It refuses a duty date outside 2026-27 and a value with cents. Results are planning estimates: check them against the registration office’s quote.
