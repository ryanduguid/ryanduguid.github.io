## How the Division 7A loan schedule calculator works

The Division 7A loan schedule calculator sets out a complying loan from a private company year by year, from the first income year after the year it was made to the end of its term. Each year it works the minimum yearly repayment under subsection 109E(6) at that year’s benchmark rate, the interest at that rate, how much of the repayment reduces the loan and the balance left at 30 June. Leave the repayments blank to pay the minimum each year; enter more in any year to see the later minimums fall and the loan end sooner.

In the last year the minimum is the balance and a full year’s interest. A repayment on 30 June carries that day on the reduced balance, so repaying the loan then needs a day’s interest less than the minimum; a blank entry repays the loan and no more, and a repayment that repays the loan is no shortfall.

If a year’s repayment is less than its minimum, subsection 109E(1) takes the company to pay a dividend of the shortfall at the end of that year, limited by section 109Y to its distributable surplus. The schedule stops at that year. The heading of subsection 109E(1) speaks of the first year in which a payment falls short, and the sources applied do not say how later years are worked.

The minimum for a single year matches the Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator. The ATO’s worked example on Loans by private companies takes $55,000 at 5.95% over 7 years for $9,835 and then $50,430 at 5.45% over 6 years for $10,079, to the nearest dollar; those years are before the published rates this page holds.

## What the Division 7A loan schedule calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a payment is a loan, whether the agreement is in writing before the lodgment day or charges at least the benchmark rate, or whether a mortgage meets the 110% test: it takes the term you choose. It works only a loan whose written term is the maximum, 7 or 25 years: subsection 109E(6) counts the remaining term from the loan’s own term, so a loan agreed for fewer years has higher minimums than this page shows. It refuses a loan made before 4 December 1997, which Division 7A reaches only once its term or amount is varied (subsection 109D(5)); a loan made before 1 July 2020, whose first year is before the ATO rates it holds; a loan made through an interposed entity (Subdivision E); a loan that refinances another or was extended under a mortgage, whose term subsections 109N(3A) to (3D) and 109E(3A) and (3B) reduce or restart; and a repayment made between 30 June and the lodgment day for the year the loan was made, which the ATO takes off the loan and also counts in the next year.

It assumes each repayment is made on 30 June and that the company’s income year ends on 30 June. A repayment made earlier in a year carries less interest. It does not apply section 109Q, which lets the Commissioner disregard a shortfall that would cause undue hardship, section 109R, which disregards a repayment funded by a new loan from the company, section 109Y’s limit or the regulations that subsection 109E(5) allows. It does not combine loans with different terms: each is a separate amalgamated loan with its own schedule.

The funding dividend is arithmetic on the marginal rate you give. It does not check that the company has the profits or franking credits to pay it, and it is not advice on whether to repay by dividend, salary or cash. Future benchmark rates are not known: the figures from 2027-28 rest on your assumption. Confirm the figures with the ATO Division 7A calculator and decision tool or a registered tax agent.
