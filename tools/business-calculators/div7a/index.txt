## How the Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator works

The Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator works out the least a borrower must repay on one amalgamated loan in one income year after the year the loan was made. It applies the formula in subsection 109E(6) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936: the loan not repaid at the end of the previous income year, times the year’s benchmark interest rate, divided by 1 minus (1 ÷ (1 + the rate)) raised to the remaining term in years.

The interest for the year is the benchmark rate on the whole balance, and the principal is the rest of the repayment. That split assumes the whole repayment is made on the last day of the income year. The ATO works interest out from the daily balance, so a repayment made earlier in the year carries less interest and more of it reduces the principal.

The ATO’s worked example on Loans by private companies takes $55,000 at 5.95% with 7 years remaining and gives $9,835 to the nearest dollar; this calculator gives $9,834.92 for those figures.

## What the Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator does not cover

It does not decide whether a payment or loan is a Division 7A loan, whether the loan agreement complies, whether a shortfall is a deemed dividend or how much of it, or how a distributable surplus limits one. It works one income year only, with no repayment schedule, and none for the year in which the loan was made. It takes the balance as you enter it: it does not work it out from the loans made, repayments made before the lodgment day or the previous year’s interest, and it does not combine several loans. It does not handle an income year that ends on a day other than 30 June. Subsection 109E(5) lets regulations set another amount, and this calculator applies the formula only. Confirm the figures with the ATO Division 7A calculator and decision tool or a registered tax agent.
