## How the trust loss tests checker works

A trust can deduct a tax loss from an earlier year only if it passes the tests in Schedule 2F to the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936. The checker ports the trust-loss-tests command of australian-accounting. A fixed trust must pass the 50% stake test or meet the section 266-45 conditions. A non-fixed trust must pass the pattern of distributions test where it distributed income or capital in the income year and in an earlier year, must not have failed that test for the loss before, must keep any more than 50% stake individuals had, and no group may begin to control it. Every trust except a complying super fund, approved deposit fund or pooled superannuation trust, a deceased estate or an exempt entity unit trust must also pass the income injection test (section 270-10(1)(d)).

An excepted trust, such as a family trust, is outside the stake, pattern and control tests. A fact left unknown stays unknown, so a test can be not established rather than passed or failed, and the loss is deductible only when every test that applies is passed.

## What the trust loss tests checker does not cover

The checker does not decide the facts: whether a family trust election is in force, who held the stakes, or which distributions are test year distributions. Its WebMCP tool also works the pattern of distributions test from stated test year distribution percentages; the page takes the result. It refuses widely held trusts, bad debts and current-year losses, and it does not cover deducting part of a loss year, family trust distribution tax (Division 271) or sections 269-75 to 269-85. It covers 2024-25 to 2026-27. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.
