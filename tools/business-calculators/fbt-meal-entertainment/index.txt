## How the meal entertainment calculator works

The meal entertainment calculator values an employer’s meal entertainment fringe benefits for the FBT year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027, where the employer has elected to value them under Division 9A. Both methods start from the year’s total expenses for providing meal entertainment to everyone, employees, associates and clients alike, less contributions from employees and their associates that the employer did not reimburse.

Under the 50/50 split method the taxable value is half those expenses. Under the 12-week register method it is those expenses × the register percentage: the value of the meal entertainment fringe benefits the register records for employees and their associates, over the value of all the meal entertainment it records. A register kept for 12 weeks in the year ending 31 March 2027 is valid for that year and the 4 after it. The calculator refuses a register first valid before the year ending 31 March 2023, or one that ceased to be valid because a year’s expenses rose more than 20% above those of its first valid year.

The ATO’s guide for employers describes both methods but works no example in figures. This page opens on example 18 of the ATO’s FBT return 2026 instructions, $4,000 of meal entertainment under the 50/50 split: $2,000. For the register method, the tests work a register of $1,000 of fringe benefits out of $3,000 of meal entertainment from the Act: the register percentage shows as 33.33% but applies as a third exactly, so $900 of expenses gives $300.00.

When the register method is chosen, the result also shows the 50/50 split figure for the same expenses. Which method to elect, if either, is the employer’s choice; the calculator does not recommend one.

## What the meal entertainment calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide what is meal entertainment, whether the election is available or made, or whether a register meets the record and representative period rules in sections 37CC and 37CE. It does not value meal entertainment provided under a salary packaging arrangement, which cannot be elected, or entertainment valued by its actual cost as an expense payment, property or residual benefit. It does not apply exemptions; under the 50/50 split the property benefits and minor benefits exemptions do not apply. It does not take a register whose first valid year had nil expenses or check whether a later register replaced this one, split the value between Type 1 and Type 2 benefits, gross it up or work out FBT. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s guide or a registered tax agent.
