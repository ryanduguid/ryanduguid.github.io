## How the R&D tax offset calculator works

The calculator works one company’s research and development tax offset under section 355-100 for an income year from 2021-22 to 2026-27. The offset is worked on the notional deductions, or, where they total less than $20,000, on the part paid to research service providers that are not associates and as CRC contributions. A company with aggregated turnover under $20 million that exempt entities do not control gets its corporate tax rate plus 18.5 percentage points, and the offset is refundable. Any other company gets its corporate tax rate plus a premium of 8.5% on the part up to 2% of its total expenses and 16.5% on the part above, and the offset is non-refundable. The corporate tax rate is 25% for a base rate entity that is not an RSA provider, PDF or other company taxed at a special rate, and 30% otherwise. Notional deductions above $150 million get the corporate tax rate only.

A clawback amount is the recoupment amount you enter plus the lesser of the feedstock revenue and the feedstock expenditure. Section 355-450 works the offset again on the notional deductions less the clawback amount, and takes the clawback amount at the corporate tax rate as the deduction amount. The offset less the adjusted offset and the deduction amount, divided by the corporate tax rate for the present year, is included in assessable income in that year. Each amount is worked exactly and rounded to the cent once.

## What the R&D tax offset calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether activities are core or supporting R&D activities, whether they are registered with AusIndustry, whether the company is an R&D entity, or which expenditure it can deduct. It leaves out associate expenditure and its payment (section 355-480), overseas findings and activities overseas, R&D partnerships and offsets of earlier years. You enter the notional deductions, total expenses, turnover, control and clawback amounts as established. It does not work out the recoupment amount itself, including the grant cap and repayments, balancing adjustment clawbacks or catch up deductions. It assumes no earlier clawback or catch up amount for the offset year, and covers only companies and income years starting on or after 1 July 2021. It refuses a clawback amount when the notional deductions, or the notional deductions less the clawback, are under $20,000, because the Act and the ATO’s example do not settle how the threshold applies then. It does not carry a non-refundable offset forward. Results are planning estimates: check them against your records and the ATO’s guidance.
