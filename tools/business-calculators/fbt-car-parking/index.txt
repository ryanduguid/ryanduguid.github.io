## How the FBT car parking calculator works

The FBT car parking calculator values the car parking fringe benefits an employer provides at one work car park in the FBT year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. It first applies the threshold test: a benefit arises only if a commercial parking station within 1 km charged a lowest all-day fee of more than $11.48 on Wednesday 1 April 2026, the first business day of the year. Enter the highest of the nearby stations’ lowest fees that day. At $11.48 or less, no car parking benefit arises and the taxable value is nil.

Where a benefit arises, the calculator values it by the method you choose. The commercial parking station method values each benefit at the lowest all-day fee charged nearby on its day; the market value method at a qualified valuer’s arm’s length amount; the average cost method at the average of the lowest fees on the first and last days a benefit was provided. Each takes off employee contributions, never below nil. The 12-week register method grosses a register’s 12 weeks up to a year: the register value × 52 ÷ 12 × the days in the availability period ÷ 366. The spaces method values each space at its daily rate amount × its days ÷ 366 × 228, scales the total down where fewer employees than spaces are covered, and takes off contributions.

This page opens on example 13 in section 16.2.4.4 of the ATO’s guide for employers: Rent-a-spot’s $272 monthly fee over 22 business days is $12.36 a day, above the $11.48 threshold. With Rent-a-spot the only station within 1 km, an employee who parks on each of those 22 days receives 22 benefits worth $271.92. The guide works the daily rate, not a total. The tests also hold the guide’s example 15, $18.50 a benefit by the average cost method, example 17, $5,416.67 by the register method, and example 18, $1,061.31 by the spaces method, each at its own year’s threshold.

The method is the employer’s choice, made by election for every method but the commercial parking station method; the calculator values the method chosen and does not recommend one.

## What the FBT car parking calculator does not cover

Apart from the threshold test, the calculator does not decide whether a car parking benefit arises. It does not check that a car was parked for more than 4 hours between 7 am and 7 pm at premises the employer controls near the employee’s primary place of employment, that the car was used to travel between home and work that day, and that a facility is a commercial parking station within 1 km by the shortest practicable route. It does not check that a fee is representative (sections 39AA and 39AB) or set at arm’s length (subsection 39E(2)), or that an election, valuer’s report or register is valid; it checks only a register’s age, not the 10% increase rule in subsection 39GF(3). Nor does it check that the numbers of employees and spaces behind the spaces method’s reduction are representative (subsection 39FB(6)). Under the station, market value and average cost methods it takes contributions off in total, which is right only where no benefit’s contribution is more than that benefit’s value: an excess on one benefit does not reduce another. It does not apply exemptions, such as the small business exemption in section 58GA, the exempt employers or the disabled persons’ parking exclusion. It values one work car park: under the commercial parking station method it takes one value for every benefit, and under the spaces method one group of spaces sharing a daily rate amount and availability period. It does not split the value between Type 1 and Type 2 benefits, gross it up or work out FBT. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s guide or a registered tax agent.
