## ASIC fees from 1 July 2026

ASIC’s standard fees for 2026-27 apply to fees paid from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, as ASIC’s fee data and INFO 30 gave them on 22 November 2026. GST does not apply to ASIC fees.

The regulations state base amounts, and ASIC raises some of them each 1 July in line with the Consumer Price Index; the table gives ASIC’s indexed amounts. A special purpose company is defined in regulation 3 of the review fees regulations; it includes a company whose constitution prohibits distributions to its members and whose sole purpose is to act as trustee of a regulated superannuation fund. A company in liquidation and an entity registered with the Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission pay no review fee (Schedule 1 Part 1 items 10 and 11).

## How the ASIC fees calculator works

For a standard fee, the calculator returns ASIC’s 2026-27 amount. For a late fee, it compares the day you paid or lodged with the due date you enter. Paying or lodging on or before the due date is not late. A review fee paid after its due date but within one month after it costs $102, and later $428 (Schedule 1 Part 3 of the review fees regulations); a document lodged late attracts the same 2 amounts (Schedule 2 item 14 of the fees regulations). A late fee is on top of the fee itself, and ASIC can charge more than one late fee when several obligations are missed.

The one month begins the day after the due date, which is not counted (item 6 of subsection 36(1) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901). Under subsection 2G(1), a month runs to just before the same day of the next calendar month, or to the end of that month when it has no such day. A fee due on Tuesday 15 September 2026 is within one month if paid by Thursday 15 October; one due on Monday 30 November 2026 is within one month if paid by Thursday 31 December, because that month starts on 1 December.

## What the ASIC fees calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out a due date. An annual review fee is due 2 months after the review date (subsection 1351(3) of the Corporations Act 2001), a review date can change, and a document’s lodgement period depends on the document, so you enter the due date shown by ASIC or the form’s rules. When a deadline falls on a weekend or public holiday, subsection 105(3) of the Corporations Act or subsection 36(2) of the Acts Interpretation Act may allow the next business day. The calculator does not apply that: if the due date, or the last day of the first month, is a Saturday or Sunday and you paid or lodged after it but by the following Wednesday, it declines to give a tier. It does not check public holidays.

Not covered: payments and lodgements before 1 July 2026 or after 30 June 2027; upfront review fees; deregistration and the review fee exceptions in subsection 1351(4); registered schemes, notified foreign passport funds, small transferring financial institutions and CCIVs; registered Australian bodies and foreign companies; credit licensee late fees; fee waivers and reviews of a late fee; and the industry funding levy. Check the amount against ASIC’s invoice before you pay.
