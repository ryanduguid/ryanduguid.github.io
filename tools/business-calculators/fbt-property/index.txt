## How the FBT property calculator works

The FBT property calculator values one property fringe benefit, goods or other property you give an employee in the FBT year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. An in-house benefit is goods identical or similar to those you, or an associate, sell in the ordinary course of business; any other property is an external benefit. Choose the rule that applies to the property. Under a salary packaging arrangement an in-house benefit takes the notional value. Goods you make take the lowest arm’s length price where you sell identical goods to the trade, 75% of the lowest public price where you sell them to the public, and otherwise 75% of the notional value. Goods you buy for your business take the lesser of the arm’s length purchase price and the notional value. The calculator takes the employee’s contribution off, never below nil, and shows any excess, which does not reduce another benefit.

Where the employee would have had a once-only income tax deduction had they bought the property themselves, the otherwise deductible rule reduces the value. The gross deduction is the value before the contribution × the share of use that would be deductible; the notional deduction is that less the deduction for the contribution. For an in-house benefit outside salary packaging, the calculator then takes off the part of the employee’s $1,000 in-house reduction for the year that their other in-house benefits have not used.

This page opens on the desk example in section 17.3 of the ATO’s guide for employers: a desk you make and sell to the public for at least $900, which an employee buys for $500, has a taxable value of 75% × $900 − $500 = $175. The tests also hold the guide’s other section 17.3 and 17.5 examples.

## What the FBT property calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a property fringe benefit arises or whether property is in-house. Nor does it apply an exemption, such as property consumed on your premises on a working day (section 41), a minor benefit (section 58P) or an eligible work-related item such as a portable electronic device (section 58X). It does not value airline transport (paragraph 42(1)(ab)), a car property benefit, whose otherwise deductible amount follows the car substantiation rules, a benefit provided jointly to an employee and an associate, whose reduction is limited to the employee’s interest, or an expense payment, which has its own calculator. It does not check the declaration the otherwise deductible rule needs (paragraph 44(1)(c)) or work out the employee’s deduction. It values one benefit: the $1,000 in-house reduction is for all of an employee’s in-house fringe benefits in the year, so enter what the others have not used. It stops at the taxable value and does not gross it up or work out FBT. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s guide or a registered tax agent.
