## How the SMSF tax calculator works

The calculator works the income tax calculation statement at item 13 of a complying self managed superannuation fund’s annual return for an income year from 2020-21 to 2026-27. Gross income is the assessable contributions, net non-arm’s length income and the fund’s other income. Exempt current pension income is the income from segregated current pension assets plus the actuary’s proportion of the other income, rounded down to the dollar. Taxable income is the assessable income less deductions; a loss gives nil. The non-arm’s length component is the lesser of the non-arm’s length income and the taxable income less the assessable contributions and plus the deductions attributable to them (section 295-545(2A)), taxed at 45%; the rest of the taxable income is the low tax component, taxed at 15%. No-TFN contributions income is taxed at a further 32%. Non-refundable, carry forward and refundable offsets come off in that order, and the $259 supervisory levy, the interest charge, credits and PAYG instalments give the amount due or refundable at label S. A newly registered fund also pays the levy for its first year (label N); an existing fund wound up in the year pays none for the next (label M). Each amount is worked exactly and rounded to the cent once.

## What the SMSF tax calculator does not cover

The calculator takes a complying Australian superannuation fund only: a non-complying fund, taxed at 45% on all its income, and a fund that is not an Australian superannuation fund are refused. It does not decide which income is non-arm’s length, segregated or exempt, what is deductible, the actuary’s proportion, or the amount of any offset or credit. It does not work out capital gains, losses carried forward from earlier years, the transfer balance cap, Division 296 tax, the tax on contributions above the caps or the fund’s instalment rate. It refuses income from segregated current pension assets when the fund has disregarded small fund assets, as that income goes in with the actuary’s proportion. Results are planning estimates: check them against the fund’s records and the ATO’s instructions.
