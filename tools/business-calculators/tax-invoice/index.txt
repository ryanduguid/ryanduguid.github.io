## How the tax invoice generator works

The generator makes one tax invoice from the supplier, recipient and lines you enter, in this browser: nothing is stored or sent. It checks the invoice against the 7 details section 29-70(1) requires: that it is intended to be a tax invoice, the supplier’s identity and ABN, what is supplied with its quantity and price, the extent each supply is taxable, the date issued and the GST payable. When the supplies total $1,000 or more, including GST, it also requires the recipient’s name or ABN. It refuses an invoice that lacks a required detail and names each one missing.

GST on a taxable line is one-eleventh of a price that includes GST, or 10% of one that excludes it. With 2 or more taxable lines, the total invoice rule adds the exact GST and rounds the total once to the nearest cent, half a cent up; the taxable supply rule rounds each line’s GST to the nearest cent, half a cent up, and adds them. When each supply is taxable and prices include GST, the invoice says ‘Total price includes GST’ and shows the GST. When taxable and GST-free or input taxed supplies are together, each taxable line is marked * with a statement that its price includes GST, or that GST is added, and each other line is labelled. With no taxable supply, the document is headed ‘Invoice’ and says it includes no GST, as no tax invoice is needed.

The supplier’s ABN, and the recipient’s if given, must pass ABN Lookup’s check digit test: subtract 1 from the first digit, weight the 11 digits 10, 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17 and 19, and the sum must divide by 89. An RCTI is headed ‘Recipient created tax invoice’, needs both parties’ ABNs and the recipient’s name, a taxable line and your confirmation that the Determination allows it, and states that the GST shown is payable by the supplier. ## What the tax invoice generator does not cover

The generator does not look up an ABN on the Australian Business Register, so it cannot show that either party is registered for GST or that the ABN is active. Only a supplier registered for GST makes a taxable supply and can issue a tax invoice. It does not decide whether a supply is taxable, GST-free or input taxed, or apportion a mixed supply: you enter each part as its own line. It does not cover adjustment notes, credit notes, discounts or negative lines, the margin scheme, wine equalisation tax or luxury car tax, GST groups and joint ventures, agents, co-owners or more than one recipient, non-monetary or foreign currency consideration, or eInvoicing through Peppol. For an RCTI it does not write, hold or check the written agreement, including one embedded in the RCTI (section 9 of the Determination), or check that the recipient may issue RCTIs. Results are review aids: check the invoice against the ATO’s guidance before you issue it.
