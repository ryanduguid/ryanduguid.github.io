## How the work expenses calculator works

For working from home, the calculator shows each method open to you beside the actual cost and names the higher. From 2022-23 the fixed rate is your recorded hours times 67c (2022-23 and 2023-24) or 70c (from 1 July 2024). It covers energy, phone, internet, stationery and computer consumables, so only cleaning, decline in value and repairs are added beside it (PCG 2023/1). For 2020-21 and 2021-22, the 52c rate needed a dedicated home office and covered energy, cleaning and the decline in value of home office furniture; phone, internet, stationery, other assets’ decline in value and repairs went beside it. The 80c shortcut covered every working from home expense. Every hourly rate needs at least one additional running expense incurred from working from home. The actual cost is the total of the work-related amounts you enter.

For a car you owned or leased, the cents per kilometre method counts only the first 5,000 business kilometres, at 72c for 2020-21 and 2021-22, 78c for 2022-23, 85c for 2023-24, 88c for 2024-25 and 2025-26, and 91c for 2026-27 (section 28-25). The log book method takes the car expenses times the business use percentage, business kilometres over total kilometres (section 28-90). A log book serves the year it was kept and the next 4 years (section 28-115) and must cover at least 12 continuous weeks (section 28-120).

For self-education, expenses count only when, at the time you incurred them, you were employed and the study kept up or improved the skills or knowledge for your current work, or was likely to increase your income from it. For 2021-22 and earlier, a prescribed course of education reduces category A by $250, less any category C, D and E expenses (section 82A). From 2022-23 there is no reduction. Each deduction is shown to the cent and again with the cents disregarded, as the ATO’s examples show the figure for the return.

## What the work expenses calculator does not cover

The calculator refuses an expense reimbursed or paid by an employer or anyone else, or covered by an allowance. It also refuses a car you did not own or lease, a log book kept more than 4 years before the claim year, one covering under 12 weeks and one that no longer represents the car’s use. It also refuses cents per kilometre expenses in category D beside log book car expenses in category A. It does not cover occupancy expenses such as rent, mortgage interest, rates and house insurance, or decide whether your home is a place of business. It also leaves out the substantiation rules and their exceptions, occupation-specific guidance, cars taken on hire or jointly owned, a new log book after an ATO notice or an added car, and the electric vehicle home charging rate. It does not work out decline in value, apportion an expense or work out the tax saved. Years before 2020-21 and after 2026-27 are not covered: no cents per kilometre rate had been determined for 2027-28 when the calculator was checked.
