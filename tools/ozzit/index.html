What Ozzit is

An Excel workbook, not a program. Open it, copy a function into your own model, and the formula travels with the file. Every definition is a native LAMBDA stored as a defined name, so the arithmetic stays inside the workbook where a reviewer can read it.

The v3.8.0 workbook carries 170 named formulas: 165 LAMBDA functions and 5 help tables that document them inside the workbook. Each module also ships an editable demonstration worksheet, and the formula source is published as plain text in the repository’s src/ folder and indexed in functions.csv.

The Australian helpers cover GST addition and extraction with a 10% default rate, 1 July financial year labels, 5 ATO small business benchmark ratio helpers, modelling depreciation schedules and pools, AASB 16 lessee schedules and journals, PAYG withholding, hire purchase, CGT parcels, general income tax rates, Division 7A minimum repayments, margin scheme GST and FBT gross-up. The rest of the library is general financial modelling.

Release v3.8.0 adds 14 functions (oz.LeaseJournalλ, oz.HPScheduleλ, oz.HPJournalλ, oz.FinanceCompareλ, oz.CGTParcelsλ, oz.NetCapitalGainλ, oz.IncomeTaxλ, oz.Div7AMinRepaymentλ, oz.MarginSchemeGSTλ, oz.FBTGrossUpλ, oz.LowValuePoolλ, oz.SmallBusinessPoolλ, oz.IndexFactorλ and oz.BulletLoanλ), moves Ryan Duguid’s original work to the Apache License 2.0 and adds native workbook_source and audit CLI commands.

Who it is for and what it needs

Ozzit suits an accountant or analyst who builds models in Excel and wants the working visible rather than hidden in an add-in or a macro. It is not a bookkeeping tool, and it never touches a ledger.

One worked example

Take $1,100 that is assumed to be wholly taxable and GST-inclusive at 10%. Enter this formula in a cell of the Ozzit workbook:

=oz.GSTExtractλ(1100)

The result is 100.00. Check: 1100 / 11 = 100.00.

That is the whole of the function’s contribution: 10% arithmetic on an amount you supply.

Whether the supply is wholly taxable, whether a tax invoice supports it and whether the credit is claimable in that period are decisions for the reviewer. The function cannot see the evidence and does not classify the supply.

The 15 November 2026 native Excel record evaluates this formula against the exact v3.6.0 release workbook. It returned 99.999999999999986 before display rounding. The file hash was unchanged after the check. The repository also retains its historical v3.2.0 screenshot record.

Download and use it

For an older workbook, check the licence at its tag. The v3.8.0 README describes releases through v3.4.2 as MIT. The v3.1.0 grant covers the tooling and documentation and excludes the workbook and formula sources. The v3.2.0 grant explicitly includes them.

Needs Microsoft 365 or Excel 2024 or later. Nothing is installed for the workbook alone, or install the workbook and CLI via Homebrew.

  • Download the v3.8.0 workbook under the Apache License 2.0. The release page also publishes SHA256SUMS, a provenance record and a Sigstore attestation bundle.
  • Or install via the Homebrew tap: brew tap ryanduguid/tap && brew install ozzit (installs the ozzit CLI and places ozzit.xlsx, oz.txt and functions.csv under $(brew --prefix)/share/ozzit).
  • Check the file before you trust it: the v3.8.0 asset is 553,054 bytes with SHA-256 6395c321ec12a1c77ae5a62c79cb9784f3bf99a408b8c1f97df278d15b13499a.
  • Read the source, guides and changelog, or start with the guide to opening the workbook and copying functions.

To use a function in your own file, copy the cell that calls it, or copy the definition from the workbook’s name manager into your model. The copied definition travels with your workbook under the licence for the Ozzit release you copied. Cite the library with the metadata in the v3.8.0 CITATION.cff, which names the release this page documents. The default branch may already carry a later version.

Limits

The functions calculate; a person decides the accounting treatment and signs off.

  • The GST helpers apply 10% unless you supply another rate. They do not classify a supply, test registration or prepare a BAS.
  • The ATO benchmark ratio helpers restate the ATO’s arithmetic for amounts you supply. They ship no benchmark ranges and do not read a tax return.
  • oz.PrimeCostλ and oz.DiminishingValueλ are modelling schedules. They are not ATO Division 40 methods and they do not implement immediate write-off or low-value pools.
  • The AASB 16 functions are lessee arithmetic. They do not decide the lease term, what counts as a lease payment or whether an arrangement contains a lease, and they do not cover lessor accounting or the short-term and low-value exemptions.
  • The library leaves out the Medicare levy, tax offsets, Division 7A interest and part-year resident, working holiday maker or special income tax schedules beyond full-year resident and foreign-resident general rates.
  • The 13-week cash flow, practice calculator and retention reconciliation workbooks in the repository’s templates folder are separate templates, not functions in this library.
  • Known defects are listed in the repository’s changelog and issues. Check a schedule against an independent calculation before relying on it.

Common questions

What is Ozzit?

An Excel workbook containing 165 native LAMBDA functions and 5 help tables for financial modelling under Australian conventions, published free under the Apache License 2.0. It has no add-in, macro or external dependency.

Which Excel versions can run it?

Microsoft 365 or Excel 2024 or later, which support LAMBDA and dynamic arrays. LibreOffice and earlier Excel versions cannot evaluate the functions.

Does Ozzit calculate Australian tax?

Ozzit applies 10% GST by default, labels Australian financial years, calculates PAYG withholding from supplied coefficients, and from v3.8.0 covers general-rate income tax (2024-25 to 2026-27), Division 7A minimum repayments, CGT parcels, margin scheme GST, FBT gross-up and depreciation pools. It leaves out the Medicare levy, tax offsets and tax advice.

Where do I get the current release?

Download Ozzit v3.8.0 from the GitHub release page or the Homebrew tap (published 24 November 2026). The release includes ozzit.xlsx, SHA256SUMS, a provenance record and a Sigstore attestation bundle. Compare the downloaded workbook’s SHA-256 with the published value before use.
