# Ozzit Financial functions

The 46 Financial formulas cover amortisation, depreciation, corkscrews, IRR, rolling sums, GST and AASB 16 leases. Each entry gives what the workbook’s own help supplies: the signature, parameters, notes and worked examples.

Release v3.5.0. Read from the released Financial formula source, which the workbook compiles.

Published 20 October 2026. Last reviewed 20 October 2026.

## Using these functions

Each named formula is a native Excel LAMBDA in the free Ozzit workbook, or one of its 5 help tables. It needs Microsoft 365 or Excel 2024 or later. Enter a call in a cell of the workbook, or copy the definition from its name manager into your own model. Leave room for any spilled result.

The text below is the help each function carries in the v3.5.0 workbook. The workbook remains the authority, and a calculation does not decide an accounting treatment. Download the workbook and check its hash.

Creates a corkscrew amortisation schedule

Creates an amortisation schedule array for one or more loans. Payments are assumed monthly. Each month lands in the period holding its start. ### oz.Depreciateλ

oz.Depreciateλ(InitialValues, InServiceDates, LifeInYears, Timeline, [SalvageValues], [DisposalDates], [DisposalCosts], [Methods], [Factors])

Create a block of CAPEX, Opening Balance, Depreciation Values, and Book Value for each asset

Creates an array of CAPEX, depreciation, and book values for a model's timeline

Note: The methods used here differ from Excel's functions in several ways. Please see webpage for details.

Parameters:

InitialValues (required)

The purchase values of each asset to depreciate

InServiceDates (required)

The dates from which to start depreciating each asset

LifeInYears (required)

The whole number of years over which to depreciate each asset. More than 0 and no more than 100. A date here is not a life: the arguments read InitialValues, InServiceDates, LifeInYears, Timeline.

Timeline (required)

The model's timeline or a row of period start dates. Timeline can be any interval: days, weeks, months, quarters or years

SalvageValues (optional, Default = 0)

The book values of each asset after fully depreciated.

DisposalDates (optional, Default = End of life)

The dates on which the assets are removed from the books. A disposal before the end of life writes the remaining book value off in that month.

DisposalCosts (optional, Default = 0)

The costs of removing each asset from the books, charged in the disposal period.

Methods (optional, Default = SLN)

The desired depreciation method. Must be one of these method codes:

- SLN=Straight Line Depreciation
- SYD=Sum of Year's Digits
- DB =Declining Balance
- DDB=Double Declining Balance
- VDB=Variable Declining Balance
- DV =Diminishing balance at 200% of straight line. Salvage value ignored
- PC =Straight line, whole years. Salvage value ignored

Factors (optional, Default = 2)

Only applicable to DDB and VDB methods.

### oz.LeaseLiabilityλ

oz.LeaseLiabilityλ(Payments, Rate, [InAdvance])

Present value of the lease payments that are not paid at the commencement date

Present value of the lease payments that are not paid at the commencement date.

Note: AASB 16 paragraph 26 measures the lease liability at the present value of the lease payments not paid at the commencement date, discounted at the interest rate implicit in the lease where that rate can be readily determined and at the lessee's incremental borrowing rate where it cannot. When InAdvance is TRUE, the first supplied payment is paid at the measurement date and excluded from the liability. Add a payment made at or before commencement to the right-of-use asset cost instead. Rate is per period. Convert an effective annual rate with PeriodRateλ(AnnualRate, PeriodsPerYear). Divide a nominal annual rate by PeriodsPerYear only when its compounding periods match the payments. Include an amount expected to be payable under a residual value guarantee, or the exercise price of a purchase option, in the final period where paragraph 27 brings it into the lease payments. IRRλ() over the same cash flows gives an implicit rate per year; convert it to the rate per period, (1 + annual) ^ (1 / 12) - 1 for months, before passing it here. Arithmetic only: this does not determine the lease term, decide what is a lease payment, or decide whether the arrangement contains a lease. AASB 16 compilation checked 24 August 2026.

Parameters:

Payments (required)

One row or one column of lease payments, one per period, entered positive. Include the measurement-date payment first when InAdvance is TRUE.

Rate (required)

Discount rate per period, not per year.

InAdvance (optional, Default = FALSE)

TRUE when the first supplied payment is paid at the measurement date.

```
=oz.LeaseLiabilityλ({100,100,100}, 0.05)
```

### oz.LeaseScheduleλ

oz.LeaseScheduleλ(Payments, Rate, [InAdvance])

Unwinds an AASB 16 lease liability: opening, payment, interest and closing for each period

Unwinds an AASB 16 lease liability over its term.

Note: Returns four rows: opening liability, payment, interest, closing liability. When InAdvance is TRUE, the first supplied payment is paid at the measurement date and is excluded from both the opening liability and the payment row. Each remaining payment follows one period of interest, so closing is opening plus interest less payment and the last closing balance is nil. The result has one fewer column for payments in advance; a sole measurement-date payment returns one zero column. The opening balance is LeaseLiabilityλ() on the same arguments. Discount rate per period, not per year.

TRUE when the first supplied payment is paid at the measurement date.

### oz.LeaseRemeasureλ

oz.LeaseRemeasureλ(RevisedPayments, Rate, CarryingLiability, CarryingROU, [InAdvance])

Remeasures an AASB 16 lease liability for revised payments and adjusts the right-of-use asset

Remeasures a lease liability for revised payments and adjusts the asset.

Note: Returns four rows: revised lease liability, adjustment, revised right-of-use asset, amount recognised in profit or loss. AASB 16 paragraph 42(b) remeasures the liability where future payments change because an index or a rate used to determine them has changed, and only when the cash flows change. Paragraph 43 keeps the discount rate unchanged for that revision, and uses a revised rate only where the change arises from a floating interest rate, so pass the original rate unless it is the rate itself that moved. When InAdvance is TRUE, the first revised payment is paid at the remeasurement date and excluded from the revised liability. Paragraph 39 takes the remeasurement to the right-of-use asset, and where that asset is reduced to nil any further reduction goes to profit or loss: row four carries that remainder, negative, because it is a credit. Arithmetic only. AASB 16 compilation checked 24 August 2026.

Parameters:

RevisedPayments (required)

Revised lease payments for the remaining term, one per period, positive. Include a payment made at the remeasurement date first when InAdvance is TRUE.

Rate (required)

Discount rate per period. Unchanged, unless a floating rate moved.

CarryingLiability (required)

Lease liability before remeasurement and before any payment due that day. Do not deduct the first revised payment.

CarryingROU (required)

Right-of-use asset carried immediately before the remeasurement.

InAdvance (optional, Default = FALSE)

TRUE when the first revised payment is paid at the remeasurement date.

### oz.PeriodRateλ

### oz.DayCountRateλ

oz.DayCountRateλ(Timeline, APR, [Convention], [EndDates])

Interest rate for each period of a timeline under a day count convention: 30/360, Actual/360, Actual/365 or Actual/Actual

Interest rate for each period of a timeline under a day count convention.

Note: Returns a row with one rate per timeline period, whichever way the timeline runs: APR multiplied by the days in the period and divided by the days in the year, as the convention counts them. Pass the result to DebtSculptVariableλ() or DebtSculptVariableLRVλ() as PeriodRates in place of a flat twelfth of the APR. With start dates each period runs from its date to the day before the next, and the last period takes its length from the one before it; with end dates each runs from the day after the previous date to its own, and the first period is the one inferred. A gap of 28 days or more is read as whole calendar months, a shorter one as days. 30/360 counts every month as 30 days on the European rule. Under Actual/Actual each calendar year uses its own length as the denominator (the ISDA rule), including periods spanning several years.

Parameters:

Timeline (required)

A row or column of period dates, at least two, evenly spaced.

APR (required)

Annual percentage rate, one value or one per period.

Convention (optional, Default = 3)

1 = 30/360, 2 = Actual/360, 3 = Actual/365, 4 = Actual/Actual. Any other number is #VALUE!.

EndDates (optional, Default = FALSE)

TRUE, or a number other than 0, when the timeline shows period end dates rather than start dates.

```
=oz.DayCountRateλ(EDATE(DATE(2026,7,1), {0,1,2}), 0.073)
```

The workbook help shows 0.0062, 0.0062, 0.0060.
