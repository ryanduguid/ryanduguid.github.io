# Depreciation rates and effective lives

Search the 7,430 effective lives in the Commissioner of Taxation’s 2025 determination, registered on 15 September 2025 and unamended on 13 November 2026. Each row shows the life, the prime cost and diminishing value rates it gives and the date it was last set.

General information, not tax advice. The search and the calculator run in your browser. A cost, life or date you enter stays there; a search is kept in the page address so that it can be linked, and reaches this site’s host when that address is opened.

Income years end on 30 June. The working assumes you hold the asset for all of each later year and use it only for a taxable purpose, with no second element of cost. Each year’s amount rounds to the nearest cent, with exact half cents rounded up.

An asset you started to hold before 10 May 2006 is not covered, because its diminishing value rate is 150% and not 200%. The calculator checks only the day of first use, so do not use it for an asset you held before 10 May 2006 and first used later.

## Which determination applies to your asset

The Commissioner’s life for an asset is the one in force when you entered a contract to acquire it, otherwise acquired it or started to construct it, where its start time falls within 5 years of then. Otherwise the life in force at the start time applies (subsection 40-95(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997). Plant acquired before 11.45 am on 21 September 1999, by legal time in the Australian Capital Territory, has its own rule in that subsection.

The 2025 determination commenced on 16 September 2025 and repealed the 2015 determination. Each row’s ‘last updated’ date is the date the Commissioner last reviewed and determined that life. Where a row’s ‘last updated’ date is later than the time that rule fixes for your asset, check the life in force at that time. Earlier lives were published in tables attached to taxation rulings, a practice that ended when TR 2022/1 was withdrawn, as the ATO says on its page Effective life determinations, rulings and law.

You may instead work out an effective life yourself under section 40-105. You make the choice for the income year in which the asset’s start time occurs (subsection 40-95(3)).

## How a life becomes a rate

Prime cost spreads the cost evenly over the life: cost × (days held ÷ 365) × (100% ÷ effective life), and never more than the value left (section 40-75). Diminishing value works on the value left: base value × (days held ÷ 365) × (200% ÷ effective life), and never more than the base value, for an asset you started to hold on or after 10 May 2006 (section 40-72). An earlier asset uses 150% (section 40-70).

Days held can be 366 in a leap year and the divisor stays 365, as the ATO’s Guide to depreciating assets 2026 says. You choose one method for each asset (section 40-65) and cannot change it (section 40-130).

The rates shown beside each life are 100% and 200% divided by the life, rounded to 2 decimal places. The calculator works from the exact life, so the diminishing value rate for a life of 6 2/3 years is 30% and not 29.99%.

## Capped lives

A row marked ‘capped life available’ can take a shorter life set by section 40-102, where you use the Commissioner’s life and the cap that applies at the relevant time is shorter. A cap applies to an asset whose start time is on or after the date shown. The vessel cap applies to a vessel held on or after 1 July 2012, and one held before then is treated as held from that day.

The dates are set by the Acts that added each cap: the Taxation Laws Amendment Act (No. 4) 2002 (Schedule 4, item 15), the Tax Laws Amendment (2005 Measures No. 1) Act 2005 (Schedule 2, item 3), the Tax Laws Amendment (2006 Measures No. 7) Act 2007 (Schedule 5, item 2) and the Tax Laws Amendment (Shipping Reform) Act 2012 (Schedule 2, item 9).

## Pools, write-offs and the car limit

Four rules can replace or limit a deduction worked out from an effective life. Each is stated from the law in force on 1 October 2026, with the ATO’s figure where one is named.

### Low-value pool

A low-cost asset costs less than $1,000 at the end of the income year you start to use it, and can go into a low-value pool (section 40-425). Each year the pool declines by 18.75% of the taxable use percentage of the cost of low-cost assets added that year, and by 37.5% of the pool’s closing balance for the year before together with the taxable use percentage of the opening adjustable value of low-value assets added that year (section 40-440). A low-value asset is one already depreciated by diminishing value whose opening adjustable value is under $1,000, and that is not a low-cost asset.

Once you pool one low-cost asset, each later one goes in too, and an asset stays in the pool (section 40-430). An asset deducted under the small business rules cannot be pooled this way. Nor, in assessments for 2026-27 and later income years, can an asset that, when you first used it or had it installed ready for use, you reasonably expected to use mainly to earn your assessable labour income (subsection 40-425(9)). An asset allocated to a pool for 2025-26 or an earlier income year is unaffected (item 17 of Schedule 4 to the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026).

### Small business pool and instant asset write-off

A small business entity, with aggregated turnover under $10 million, that chooses simplified depreciation deducts the taxable purpose proportion of an asset costing less than $20,000 in the year it is first used or installed ready for use (sections 328-110 and 328-180). It must be a small business entity, and have chosen simplified depreciation, both for that year and for the year it started to hold the asset. The $20,000 threshold is permanent for assets first used or installed ready for use on or after 1 July 2026, under the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 2) Act 2026.

A temporary rule gives the same $20,000 for assets first acquired at or after the 2015 budget time and first used or installed ready for use from 1 July 2023 to 30 June 2026 (section 328-180 of the Income Tax (Transitional Provisions) Act 1997). Other assets go to the general small business pool, deducted at 15% in their first year and 30% of the opening balance after that (section 328-190). Where the pool’s balance before that year’s deduction is under $20,000, the whole balance is deducted (section 328-210).

### Assets costing $300 or less

The decline in value of an asset costing $300 or less is its cost where you use it mainly to earn income that is not from carrying on a business (subsection 40-80(2)). The asset must not be part of a set, or one of several identical or substantially identical items, costing more than $300 in total that year. Use for a purpose other than a taxable purpose reduces the deduction (subsection 40-25(2)).

### Car limit

The cost of a car designed mainly for carrying passengers is reduced to the car limit for the financial year you started to hold it: $69,883 for 2026-27 and $69,674 for 2025-26 (section 40-230 and the ATO’s figures). The limit does not apply to a car fitted out for transporting disabled people in wheelchairs for profit, or to one that is over the limit only because of modifications that let a person with a disability use it for a taxable purpose (subsection 40-230(2)). Car limit for depreciation gives each year and its source.

## Capital works

Construction costs of capital works are deducted under Division 43 and not by effective life: 2.5% a year for works begun after 26 February 1992, or 4% for parts used in the ways Table 43-145 lists (section 43-25). Those uses include short-term traveller accommodation and industrial activities and, for works begun after 7:30 pm by legal time in the Australian Capital Territory on 9 May 2023, build to rent.

Works begun before 27 February 1992 and used as Table 43-140 describes take 4% if begun after 21 August 1984 and before 16 September 1987, and 2.5% in any other case (subsection 43-25(2)). The ATO’s Rental properties 2026 guide shows no deduction for construction started before 22 August 1979. No deduction is available for a period before construction is complete (section 43-30).

## Limits

The page does not decide whether something is a depreciating asset, who holds it, what it cost or how far it is used for a taxable purpose. Check a life against the registered determination before relying on it.

The calculator leaves out balancing adjustments, second elements of cost, private use, pooling, the car limit, capped lives you have not entered and any rule that replaces these methods for a period. Diminishing value is not available for some assets, such as in-house software, intellectual property other than copyright in a film, and spectrum licences (subsection 40-72(2)). Section 40-27 can take away the deduction for a previously used asset in residential rental premises where the letting is not a business. A life the Commissioner has not listed can be worked out under section 40-105 or requested with the ATO’s effective life review application form.

### What will my income tax bill be?

For 2026-27, a resident pays no tax on the first $18,200, 15% from $18,201 to $45,000, 30% to $135,000, 37% to $190,000 and 45% above that, plus the 2% Medicare levy. Start with taxable income for the correct income year, then account for levies, offsets, study loans and tax already withheld. A basic tax bracket result alone is incomplete.

- Gather income statements, investment statements, deductible expenses and PAYG credits.
- Use the ATO return estimate and reconcile each adjustment before treating it as a budget.

The linked experimental income tax worksheet excludes levies and offsets; check its supported years.

2.

### Which expenses reduce my taxable income?

To claim a work-related expense, you must have paid it yourself without reimbursement, it must directly relate to earning your income, and you need a record to prove it, usually a receipt. From 2026-27, eligible taxpayers can claim a standard deduction of up to $1,000 for work-related expenses without records; it is reduced by any work-related expenses they claim that it covers. An expense needs a connection to assessable income and the evidence required for that type of claim. Private spending and reimbursed amounts need separate treatment.

- List each expense, its purpose, date, amount and reimbursement.
- Record your work use calculation and check the ATO category before claiming.

A receipt proves spending, but does not by itself establish deductibility.

### What steps are involved in completing a return?

Most pre-fill data from employers, banks, health funds and government agencies is finalised by the end of July; check that it includes all your income before you lodge. If you lodge your own return, it is due by 31 October; to use a registered tax agent’s later dates, engage them before then. Collect the year’s records, reconcile them with pre-fill information, calculate supported claims, then review and lodge through an authorised channel.

- Check income statements are ready and account for income missing from pre-fill.
- Keep a copy of the reviewed return, declaration and lodgement receipt.

The checklist prepares your records; lodgement happens with the ATO or your registered agent.

### Where can I obtain or recover my TFN?

Applying for a TFN is free, online or on paper depending on your residency and circumstances. If you have a TFN but can’t remember it, find it in ATO online services through myGov, or phone the ATO. Use the ATO’s TFN application or lost-TFN route. The right application process depends on residency and circumstances.

- If you already have a TFN, use the ATO recovery instructions before applying again.
- Complete any identity checks directly with the official service.

### What dates govern the annual tax cycle?

The income year runs from 1 July to 30 June. A return you lodge yourself is due by 31 October, quarterly BAS is due on 28 October, 28 February, 28 April and 28 July, and STP finalisation is due by 14 July. Build a calendar from the dates that apply to your return, activity statements, payroll and entity obligations.

- Record the period, lodgement date, payment date and responsible person for each obligation.
- Confirm the dates in your ATO account or with your registered agent; record any approved extension.

An agent’s program or a particular account can have different dates from a general calendar.

### Which methods can I use to settle my tax bill?

You can pay the ATO by BPAY, direct deposit, debit card, Government EasyPay, in person at Australia Post or through an international money remitter. Credit card payments are accepted until 30 June 2027. Use the ATO payment instructions for the relevant account and payment reference.

- Check the amount owing and available payment methods in the official service.
- Allow for processing time and keep the receipt; reconcile the payment when it appears.

### Which earnings are taxable?

You must declare employment income, government payments and allowances, investment income such as interest, dividends, rent and capital gains, business, partnership and trust income, super pensions and annuities, and foreign income. Some compensation and insurance payments, scholarships, prizes and awards are also taxable. Check each source of income against the ATO’s categories. Amounts missing from pre-fill still need review.

- Reconcile employment, business, investment, government and foreign income records.
- List unusual receipts separately and record why they are included or excluded.

A bank deposit is not automatically taxable income, and taxable income is not limited to deposits.

### How long will the tax return process take?

The ATO says most returns lodged online are processed within 10 business days, though its How to lodge your tax return page still says 12 for myTax. For a paper return, most refunds are issued within 50 business days. Separate your preparation time from the ATO’s processing time. Missing records, amendments and reviews can extend the overall process.

- Complete the record checklist before setting a preparation deadline.
- After lodgement, use the official progress service and respond to any information request.

### Where can I submit a completed return?

You can lodge online with myTax, through a registered tax agent or on paper by mail. Registered tax agents are the only people who can charge a fee to prepare and lodge your return. Individuals can use the lodgement options on the ATO’s tax return page, including myTax where eligible, a registered agent or paper.

- Choose the route for your circumstances and confirm the income year.
- Review the declaration and save the lodgement receipt after submission.

### Which tax rules changed from 1 July 2026?

From 1 July 2026, the 16% personal tax rate fell to 15% and the cents per kilometre rate rose to 91 cents. From the same date, Payday Super generally requires contributions to reach the fund within 7 business days of payday, and the $20,000 instant asset write-off became permanent for small businesses. Most changes start on 1 July, so compare 2025-26 with 2026-27 rather than calendar years.

- Use the ATO 'Before you prepare your tax return' guidance for each year.
- Record each changed rate or rule, effective date, source and affected calculation in a change log.

### What records do I need to support my working-from-home claim?

For the fixed rate method, keep a record of the actual hours you work from home for the whole year, such as a timesheet, roster or diary, because estimates are not accepted. ### Should I use the cents-per-kilometre or logbook method for car expenses?

### How do I keep a valid vehicle logbook?

A logbook must cover at least 12 continuous weeks and be broadly representative of your travel. It stays valid for 5 years, but you need a new 12-week logbook if your circumstances change. A useful logbook records each business trip and lets another person check the business use percentage.

### Can I deduct a laptop or tools immediately, or must I depreciate them?

An employee can deduct the work-related share of an item costing $300 or less straight away, unless it is part of a set costing more than $300; a dearer item is depreciated over its effective life. ### Which work clothing, uniforms and laundry expenses can I claim?

You can claim clothing that is protective, a compulsory uniform, a registered non-compulsory uniform, or occupation-specific and not conventional. ### What can I claim if I have lost the receipt?

For 2025-26 and earlier years, if your total work-related expense claim is $300 or less, you can claim it without written evidence, but you must be able to show how you worked it out. From 2026-27 that exception no longer applies. Eligible taxpayers get a standard deduction of up to $1,000, or their labour income if less, without records. Work-related expenses claimed in the return generally need written evidence, although the travel and overtime meal allowance exceptions remain, and most of them reduce the standard deduction; union and professional association fees do not. Keep any written evidence for 5 years from your return's due date, or from the date you lodge it if that is later, and longer while a dispute with the ATO about the claim is unresolved. Try to replace missing evidence before deciding whether a claim can be supported.

- Request duplicate invoices or download supplier records and match them to payment evidence.
- Write down the expense, work purpose, missing record and the ATO evidence rule you relied on.

A bank statement or a general receipt exception does not automatically support every claim.

### Is my activity a hobby or a business for tax purposes?

Generally, a business involves continuous and repeated activities done to make a profit. The ATO asks whether you intend to and have a prospect of making a profit, whether the scale is enough to make one, and whether the activities are repeated and continuous; a hobby you don’t seek to profit from is not a business. ### Should I operate as a sole trader or a company?

A sole trader reports business income as their own and is personally liable for the business’s debts. A company is a separate legal entity with higher set-up and running costs and an annual ASIC review; it pays tax at 25% or 30%, and it pays directors wages or fees rather than drawings. ### How much should I set aside for income tax as a sole trader?

### How do I add GST to a price or extract GST from an inclusive amount?

GST is 10% of the GST-exclusive price. To find the GST included in a GST-inclusive price, divide it by 11: $110 includes $10 of GST. The calculation applies to a wholly taxable sale; classify GST-free, input-taxed and mixed sales first.

### When must my business register for GST?

Register within 21 days once your GST turnover reaches $75,000 ($150,000 for a non-profit). Taxi and ride-sourcing drivers must register whatever their turnover. Check current and projected GST turnover and any compulsory-registration rule for your activity.

- Build a monthly turnover schedule and review what counts towards GST turnover.

33.

### What is the difference between GST-free and input-taxed sales?

Neither includes GST in the price. You can claim GST credits for purchases you use to make GST-free sales, but not for purchases that relate to input-taxed sales, such as financial supplies and selling or renting out residential premises. Classify each sale before coding it, because the category decides whether GST credits on related purchases can be claimed.

- Classify each income stream using the ATO guidance and retain the basis for the classification.
- Review purchase credits and any apportionment separately for input-taxed activities.

Do not give both categories the same purchase-credit treatment just because no GST appears on the sale.

### Do I charge GST when selling to overseas customers?

Exported goods are GST-free if they are exported within 60 days of the earlier of receiving any payment or issuing an invoice for them. Some other supplies for consumption outside Australia, such as certain services, can also be GST-free. An overseas customer does not automatically make a sale GST-free.

- Record what you sold, where it is supplied or used, customer circumstances and any export evidence.
- Check the relevant goods or services rule before choosing the GST code.

Export conditions and evidence requirements differ between goods and services.

### What information must appear on a valid tax invoice?

If a customer asks, you must issue a tax invoice within 28 days for any sale over $82.50 including GST. It must show that it is a tax invoice, your identity and ABN, the date, what was sold with quantity and price, the GST amount and how much of each sale is taxable. ### When is my BAS due, and when must I pay it?

Quarterly BAS is due on 28 October, 28 February, 28 April and 28 July. Monthly BAS is due on the 21st day of the following month. Payment is due by the same date. Use the period and due date shown for your BAS account.

- Put the cash payment in the forecast and assign someone to retain the receipt.

General quarterly dates can differ from the dates that apply to your account.

### Should I account for GST on a cash or accrual basis?

A business with aggregated turnover under $10 million can choose either the cash or the accrual basis for GST. Compare the timing of receipts and payments under the methods your business is allowed to use.

### Why does my BAS GST figure differ from the GST balance in Xero?

On the cash basis, you account for GST on the BAS covering the period in which you receive or make payment for your sales and purchases. Bridge the BAS calculation to the GST control account at the same date and on the same basis.

### How do I correct a mistake in a BAS I have already lodged?

You can generally correct a GST error on a later BAS lodged within the period of review for the period of the error, but not to claim GST credits after the 4-year time limit, and not more than once. Revising the earlier BAS instead can attract penalties or general interest charge. Work out whether the error can be corrected on a later BAS or requires a revision.

- Record the original period, error type, amount, discovery date and supporting transactions.
- Apply the ATO’s correction conditions and retain the approved amendment or later-period adjustment.

Do not choose a later-period correction without checking the period of review and the 4-year credit time limit.

43.

### What should I do if a super payment is late, rejected or missing?

Since 1 July 2026, super that is unpaid, late, short or paid to the wrong fund makes you liable to the super guarantee charge, which the ATO assesses. The charge includes notional earnings and may include an administrative uplift of up to 60%; paying the fund what you owe as soon as possible, even in part, reduces it. Investigate the payment trail and apply the current late-payment process.

- Reconcile payroll, clearing house status, rejections and fund receipt for each affected employee.
- Resolve the cause, record any replacement payment and review the ATO’s reporting and charge requirements.

Sending a replacement does not automatically remove a late-payment consequence.

### Do I have to pay super for a contractor who invoices through an ABN?

If you pay a contractor under a contract that is mainly for their labour, meaning more than half its dollar value, they are an employee for super guarantee purposes and you may need to pay super for them. An ABN makes no difference. Review the actual contract and working arrangement against the ATO’s super eligibility guidance.

- Record who performs the work, how they are paid and whether the contract is for their labour.
- Retain the eligibility conclusion separately from the supplier’s ABN and invoice.

An ABN is not proof that super is unnecessary.

### How do I calculate an employee’s final pay, including unused leave?

Final pay is the wages owing, including penalty rates and allowances, plus any unused annual leave with loading if it would have been paid during employment; it can also include accrued or pro rata long service leave. Sick and carer’s leave isn’t paid out, and most awards require final pay within 7 days after the last day of employment. Build final pay from the employee’s entitlements and the circumstances of departure.

- Gather the award or agreement, termination date, unpaid hours, leave balances and notice details.
- Check leave loading, redundancy and long service leave where applicable, then apply the relevant tax treatment.

### When does my business become liable for payroll tax in NSW?

For 2026-27, NSW payroll tax applies to taxable wages above $1,200,000 a year, at a rate of 5.45%. Review Australian taxable wages, NSW connection, grouping and the applicable threshold.

### Do contractor payments count towards NSW payroll tax?

Payments to a contractor who provides the services of a worker under a relevant contract are liable for NSW payroll tax unless an exemption applies. Exemptions include some services provided for 90 days or less in a financial year and services the contractor provides to the public. Contractor payments need a separate payroll tax review under the NSW relevant-contract rules.

- Keep the contract, services, days worked and evidence for any exclusion claimed.
- Reconcile reviewed contractor amounts to the payroll tax workpaper.

An invoice, ABN or GST registration alone does not establish an exclusion.

### What does an employee cost after super, leave, insurance and other on-costs?

Start with wages plus the 12% super guarantee, then add leave, workers compensation, any payroll tax and other on-costs. Add annual wages, super and the other costs you expect to pay, then consider productive capacity separately.
