### How do I calculate my working-from-home deduction?

Under the fixed rate method, you claim 70 cents for each hour you work from home in 2024-25 and 2025-26. The actual cost method is the alternative. Compare the eligible working-from-home methods for the income year using the required evidence for each.

- Record actual hours worked at home and the costs or rate that the chosen method covers.
- Keep separately claimed costs out of any category already included in the chosen method.

Eligibility, rates and record requirements must be checked for the year; this site does not select the method for you.

### What records do I need to support my working-from-home claim?

For the fixed rate method, keep a record of the actual hours you work from home for the whole year, such as a timesheet, roster or diary, because estimates are not accepted. Also keep at least one record, such as a bill, for each running expense the rate covers. Keep evidence of hours and costs that supports the particular working-from-home method you use.

- Record dates and hours throughout the required period and retain bills or receipts.
- Keep a note of the method, year, work use basis and costs excluded to prevent double claims.

A reconstructed estimate may not meet the ATO’s requirements for your method.

### Should I use the cents-per-kilometre or logbook method for car expenses?

The cents per kilometre rate is 91 cents for 2026-27 (88 cents for 2024-25 and 2025-26), capped at 5,000 business kilometres per car. The logbook method has no kilometre cap but needs a valid logbook. Compare the methods you are eligible to use, using the same car and income year.

- For cents per kilometre, document eligible kilometres and check the year’s rate and cap.
- For the logbook method, gather a valid logbook, odometer readings and actual running costs.

Vehicle type, ownership and entity structure can change which methods are available.

### How do I keep a valid vehicle logbook?

A logbook must cover at least 12 continuous weeks and be broadly representative of your travel. It stays valid for 5 years, but you need a new 12-week logbook if your circumstances change. A useful logbook records each business trip and lets another person check the business use percentage.

- Record the required trip dates, odometer readings, distance and business purpose.
- Keep opening and closing odometer readings and review whether changed use requires a new logbook.

Check the ATO’s required recording period and validity conditions before relying on the logbook.

### How do I calculate the work-related share of phone and internet expenses?

Work out your work use percentage on a reasonable basis over a continuous 4-week period, using an itemised bill or your own record of work and private calls and internet use, then apply it to the whole income year. Apply a supported work use percentage to eligible phone and internet costs.

- Gather bills and a representative usage record that distinguishes private from work use.
- Use the business use calculator and retain the usage calculation with the result.

Do not separately claim costs already covered by your chosen working-from-home method.

16.

### Can I deduct a laptop or tools immediately, or must I depreciate them?

An employee can deduct the work-related share of an item costing $300 or less straight away, unless it is part of a set costing more than $300; a dearer item is depreciated over its effective life. A small business with aggregated turnover under $10 million that uses the simplified depreciation rules can write off each eligible asset costing less than $20,000 that is first used or installed ready for use on or after 1 July 2023. Separate employee deductions from business asset rules, then identify whether immediate deduction or decline in value applies.

- Record cost, acquisition date, first use, work use share and whether the item forms part of a set.
- Check the applicable year’s asset rules and retain the calculation in an asset register.

The depreciation worksheet covers a bounded ordinary first-year method; it does not decide immediate-deduction eligibility.

17.

### Which work clothing, uniforms and laundry expenses can I claim?

You can claim clothing that is protective, a compulsory uniform, a registered non-compulsory uniform, or occupation-specific and not conventional. Laundry can be worked out at $1 a load of work clothing only, or 50 cents a load if mixed, and a laundry claim of $150 or less needs no written evidence. Work clothing claims depend on the type of clothing and its connection to your work. Wearing ordinary clothes at work does not establish a claim.

- Separate protective clothing, qualifying uniforms, occupation-specific clothing and ordinary items.
- Retain purchases and a supportable laundry calculation, with any reimbursement recorded.

Check the ATO category and evidence requirements before adding an amount to the return.

18.

### Can I claim course fees, professional memberships and self-education expenses?

Self-education is deductible when it maintains or improves the specific skills or knowledge you need for your current employment, or is likely to increase your income from it. Check how the course or membership relates to your current income-earning activities.

- Keep invoices, course details, the work connection and any employer reimbursement.
- Split private components and costs of gaining a new occupation from costs relevant to current work for review.

A professional label or employer recommendation alone does not settle the tax treatment.

### What can I claim if I have lost the receipt?

If your total work-related expense claim is $300 or less, you can claim it without written evidence, but you must be able to show how you worked it out. Keep any written evidence for 5 years from the date you lodge your return. Try to replace missing evidence before deciding whether a claim can be supported.

- Request duplicate invoices or download supplier records and match them to payment evidence.
- Write down the expense, work purpose, missing record and the ATO evidence rule you relied on.

A bank statement or a general receipt exception does not automatically support every claim.

### Does my business qualify for the instant asset write-off this financial year?

Yes, if your aggregated turnover is under $10 million and you use the simplified depreciation rules. You can deduct the full cost of each eligible asset costing less than $20,000 that is first used or installed ready for use in the year. The $20,000 limit applied in 2025-26 and is permanent from 1 July 2026. Test eligibility against the rules enacted for the asset’s relevant year and circumstances.

- Record aggregated turnover, asset cost, acquisition and first use dates, and business use share.
- Check the current ATO small business depreciation rules, including exclusions and any applicable election.

The ordinary depreciation worksheet does not determine instant asset write-off eligibility.

### Do I need an ABN for freelance or contracting work?

You’re entitled to an ABN if you’re carrying on or starting an enterprise in Australia. You’re not entitled to one for work you do as an employee, even if you or your employer calls it contracting. ABN entitlement depends on carrying on or starting an enterprise. An invoice or a client’s request alone does not establish it.

- Record the work arrangement, independence, activity and evidence that the enterprise has started.
- Read the ABR entitlement guidance before applying.

An ABN does not decide whether a worker is an employee for tax, super or workplace law.

### How do I apply for an ABN, and what information do I need?

Apply online through the ABR. Details such as your TFN help the ABR identify you and process the application faster; if it needs to check details, it aims to review the application within 20 business days. Apply through the official ABR route after checking entitlement.

- Gather the entity structure, business activity, start date and contact information required by the ABR.
- Enter identity information directly into the official application and save its confirmation.

23.

### Is my activity a hobby or a business for tax purposes?

Generally, a business involves continuous and repeated activities done to make a profit. The ATO asks whether you intend to and have a prospect of making a profit, whether the scale is enough to make one, and whether the activities are repeated and continuous; a hobby you don’t seek to profit from is not a business. Assess the pattern of activity, commercial purpose and how the activity is organised.

- Document your intention, pricing, repetition, scale, records and plan to make a profit.
- Keep the conclusion and supporting facts, and reassess when the activity changes.

There is no single revenue figure in this guide that classifies every activity.

24.

### Should I operate as a sole trader or a company?

A sole trader reports business income as their own and is personally liable for the business’s debts. A company is a separate legal entity with higher set-up and running costs and an annual ASIC review; it pays tax at 25% or 30%, and it pays directors wages or fees rather than drawings. Compare liability, ownership, administration, tax and how profits will reach the owners.

- Prepare the same forecast under each structure, including compliance costs and money taken out.
- Discuss asset protection, existing contracts and restructuring costs with the relevant advisers before choosing.

A lower company tax rate alone does not establish a lower total cost or a suitable structure.

### How much should I set aside for income tax as a sole trader?

The ATO automatically enters an individual, including a sole trader, into PAYG instalments when their latest return shows instalment income of $4,000 or more, tax payable of $1,000 or more and estimated tax of $500 or more. You can also enter voluntarily. Reserve cash against an estimate of your annual personal tax position and instalments already paid.

- Forecast business profit alongside salary and other personal income, deductions and credits.
- Place expected instalments and the remaining tax payment into the cash forecast on their due dates.

A fixed percentage of sales can miss deductions, other income and tax already paid.

26.

### Do I need a separate business bank account?

A sole trader doesn’t have to have a business bank account, although it’s a good idea. A partnership, company or trust must have a separate bank account for tax purposes. A separate account makes business transactions easier to trace. The requirements also depend on the entity.

- Record which accounts hold business money and reconcile each to its statement.
- For a company or trust, keep entity funds and private funds clearly identified and correctly recorded.

Check the account requirements for your structure before opening or changing banking arrangements.

27.

### How do I pay myself and record drawings as a sole trader?

What you draw doesn’t change what a sole trader reports: all your business income goes in your individual tax return, whatever amounts you transfer to your personal account. Sole-trader drawings transfer the owner’s money out of the business; they are recorded separately from operating expenses.

- Record the transfer against drawings or the owner’s equity account in your agreed chart.
- Keep drawings visible in the cash forecast so the business can meet upcoming obligations.

A sole trader’s drawings do not set the amount of taxable business profit.

28.

### Do the personal services income rules apply to my contracting income?

For income that is mainly a reward for your personal effort or skill, the PSI rules apply when 80% or more of it comes from one client and its associates, unless you pass the results test or hold a personal services business determination. Review whether the income is mainly a reward for personal effort or skill, then work through the applicable PSI tests.

- Keep contracts, invoices, working arrangements and evidence for any test relied on.
- Record the income streams separately and review personal-services-business conditions with your adviser.

An ABN or company structure does not by itself switch off the PSI rules.

29.

### How do I report income from a side hustle, delivery work or online sales?

Income from providing services or assets for a fee through a sharing economy platform, such as ride-sourcing, renting out a room or sharing assets, is taxable, and GST can apply. Platform operators report these transactions to the ATO. Reconcile gross platform activity to payouts before deciding how to report it.

- Export sales, fees, refunds and activity records for the income year.
- Document whether the activity is business, employment or another income category and check GST separately.

Net bank deposits can omit fees and gross receipts that need separate reporting.

30.

### Can I offset a business loss against my salary or other income?

Only if you meet the non-commercial loss rules: your income for the income requirement must be under $250,000 and you must pass one of 4 tests, such as earning at least $20,000 of assessable income from the business. Otherwise the loss is deferred. A business loss needs a separate review before it can reduce other income.

- Keep the loss calculation, activity history and records relevant to the non-commercial loss tests.
- Record whether an exception, test or discretion applies, or whether the loss must be deferred.

A loss in the accounts does not automatically offset salary in the tax return.

31.

### How do I add GST to a price or extract GST from an inclusive amount?

GST is 10% of the GST-exclusive price. To find the GST included in a GST-inclusive price, divide it by 11: $110 includes $10 of GST. The calculation applies to a wholly taxable sale; classify GST-free, input-taxed and mixed sales first.

- Choose whether your amount already includes GST, then calculate the components.
- Keep the source invoice and confirm that the whole amount is subject to GST.

The calculator does not classify GST-free, input-taxed or mixed supplies.

32.

### When must my business register for GST?

Register within 21 days once your GST turnover reaches $75,000 ($150,000 for a non-profit). Taxi and ride-sourcing drivers must register whatever their turnover. Check current and projected GST turnover and any compulsory-registration rule for your activity.

- Build a monthly turnover schedule and review what counts towards GST turnover.
- Use the ATO registration guidance to determine the trigger and registration date.

### What is the difference between GST-free and input-taxed sales?

Neither includes GST in the price. You can claim GST credits for purchases you use to make GST-free sales, but not for purchases that relate to input-taxed sales, such as financial supplies and selling or renting out residential premises. Classify each sale before coding it, because the category decides whether GST credits on related purchases can be claimed.

- Classify each income stream using the ATO guidance and retain the basis for the classification.
- Review purchase credits and any apportionment separately for input-taxed activities.

Do not give both categories the same purchase-credit treatment just because no GST appears on the sale.

### Do I charge GST when selling to overseas customers?

Exported goods are GST-free if they are exported within 60 days of the earlier of receiving any payment or issuing an invoice for them. ### What should I do if a super payment is late, rejected or missing?

Since 1 July 2026, super that is unpaid, late, short or paid to the wrong fund makes you liable to the super guarantee charge, which the ATO assesses. 61.

### How do I build a 13-week cash flow forecast?

Forecast cash by the week it is expected to move through the bank.

- Start with the reconciled bank balance and schedule receipts, suppliers, wages, tax, debt and drawings across 13 weeks.
- Use the browser forecast for a short scenario, or the existing workbook for a detailed worked example.

Fictional worked example

Start a fictional forecast on 28 September 2026 with $200 opening cash and a $100 buffer. Enter $1,000 receipts and $300 payments in week 1, and zero in every later week. Closing cash is $900. Delay the full $1,000 receipt by two weeks: cash closes at minus $100 in weeks 1 and 2, then returns to $900 in week 3. The lowest balance is minus $100, so the funding gap to the buffer is $200. The eventual closing balance is unchanged.

Weekly closing balances can hide a shortage within the week; review daily timing where cash is tight.

### Why is my business profitable but short of cash?

Profit and cash diverge because income and expenses can be recognised at different times from receipts and payments.

- Bridge profit to cash using receivables, payables, inventory, tax, capital spending, debt and drawings.

### How much must I sell each month to break even?

Break-even sales cover fixed costs after variable costs. For one product, divide fixed costs by contribution per unit.

- Use the same period for fixed costs and the expected selling price and variable cost per unit.
- Round required units up and compare them with practical sales capacity.

### What is the difference between gross margin and markup?

Gross margin divides gross profit by revenue. Markup divides gross profit by cost.

- Use sales and the directly related cost for the same goods or period.
- Compare both percentages with the calculator and keep GST treatment consistent.

A zero denominator has no meaningful percentage; the calculator leaves that percentage undefined.

Divide annual costs plus target profit by realistic billable hours.

The calculated rate is a cost recovery target before GST; customers’ willingness to pay needs separate evidence.
