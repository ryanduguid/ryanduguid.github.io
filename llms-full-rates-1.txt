Use these tables in Excel

In Excel for Windows with Power Query, choose Data, Get Data, From Other Sources, Blank Query. Open Advanced Editor, replace its contents with this query and choose Done, then Close & Load. If prompted, choose Anonymous access for this public source. Microsoft’s Text/CSV guide explains the connector.

```
let
    Source = Csv.Document(
        Web.Contents("https://duguid.com.au/rates/super-guarantee/super-guarantee-rates.csv"),
        [Delimiter = ",", Encoding = 65001, QuoteStyle = QuoteStyle.Csv]

    Headers = Table.PromoteHeaders(Source, [PromoteAllScalars = true]),
    Rates = Table.TransformColumnTypes(Headers, {
        {"period_start", type date},
        {"period_end", type date},
        {"general_sg_rate_percent", type number},
        {"notes", type text}
    }, "en-AU")
in
    Rates
```

### Negative gearing: application and acquisition cut-off

Schedule 2 item 5 of the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 applies the amendments from the 2027-28 income year. Schedule 2 commenced on 27 June 2026, the day after Royal Assent. The inserted section 26-155(2)(a) excludes amounts relating to an ownership interest in a residential dwelling last acquired before 7:30 pm ACT legal time on 12 May 2026. For this exception, section 26-155(3) uses the contract date for a dwelling acquired under contract, rather than settlement.

Other exceptions cover new residential dwellings and prescribed activities, widely held unit trusts, complying superannuation entities and amounts relating to fringe benefits: sections 26-155(2), (4), (5) and 26-160. New-dwelling and prescribed-activity conditions depend on legislative instruments; this page does not determine whether a particular property qualifies. A 2026-27 purchase can therefore affect later deductions even though the deduction restriction applies from 2027-28.

### CGT: separate application rules

The negative gearing acquisition cut-off is not a general CGT grandfathering rule. Schedule 1 has separate rules: items 82 to 84 address income year and CGT-event application, and item 60 inserts transitional section 119-1 for the minimum tax on gains from CGT events on or after 1 July 2027. Check the asset, event and applicable transition provisions separately before calculating a gain.

## Common questions

### Is the $1,000 instant tax deduction law?

Yes. Schedule 4 Part 1 item 17 of the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 applies it from the 2026-27 income year. Section 25-130 covers individuals who are Australian residents at some time in the year and derive assessable labour income. The deduction starts at the lesser of $1,000 and that income, then is reduced, to no less than zero, by specified work-related deductions. It is not an extra $1,000 on top of those same expenses. Income protection, personal sickness and accident insurance premiums, and membership of a trade, business or professional association are excluded from the section 8-1 reduction.

### Is the negative gearing and CGT discount reform law?

Yes. The negative gearing amendments apply from 2027-28, but an earlier acquisition can affect later treatment. Section 26-155 has a pre-7:30 pm ACT legal time, 12 May 2026 ownership interest exception and a contract date rule. CGT has separate application and transition provisions; do not apply that acquisition cut-off to every CGT asset.

### What does TBD mean on the ATO’s list?

TBD is the development status reported in the ATO summary. That entry alone does not establish whether a consultation draft, bill or instrument exists. Check Treasury, Parliament and the Federal Register for the measure. Various means the package needs component-level checking.

### How often is this page checked?

At the events in the rates register’s review cadence: the 1 July rollover, the May Budget, the December MYEFO and each sitting of Parliament, plus any reader report.

# Super guarantee rate history

The general super guarantee rate is 12%. It reached 12% on 1 July 2025 and no further increases are scheduled. Payday super, from 1 July 2026, changed when contributions must be paid, generally within 7 business days of each payday, and left the rate alone.

Verified 30 August 2026 against the ATO’s super guarantee rate table, the Treasury Laws Amendment (Payday Superannuation) Act 2025 and the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992. The general SG rate table was rechecked against the ATO on 14 September 2026.

## Current rate

12% since 1 July 2025, with no later step scheduled. From 1 July 2026, Payday Super changed the timing: contributions are generally due within 7 business days of each payday rather than quarterly. The rate stayed at 12%, but timing was not the only change: contributions are now worked out on qualifying earnings rather than ordinary time earnings, and the maximum contributions base is an annual limit rather than a quarterly one.

## Common questions

### What is the super guarantee rate for 2025-26?

12%. The rate reached its final step of 12% on 1 July 2025.

### What is the super guarantee rate for 2026-27?

12%. There are no further scheduled increases.

### Did the super guarantee rate change on 1 July 2026?

No. Payday Super changed the timing from that date: pay within 7 business days of each payday instead of quarterly. The rate itself has been 12% since 1 July 2025.

### When did the super guarantee rate stop increasing?

The last increase was to 12% on 1 July 2025. Earlier steps were annual each 1 July: 11.5% (2024), 11% (2023), 10.5% (2022), 10% (2021).

# Division 7A benchmark interest rate

The benchmark interest rate for the 2026-27 income year is 8.77% for companies with a 30 June year end. Section 109N(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 does not name a figure: it points at the Reserve Bank’s indicator lending rate for bank variable housing loans, the last one published before the income year starts. ## How to work it out yourself

Take the Reserve Bank’s F5 Indicator Lending Rates table and read the bank variable housing loans series for owner-occupiers. The rate for an income year is the last figure published before that year began. The Reserve Bank publishes F5 in the first week after the month it covers, so the last figure published before 1 July is the one for May. For the year that started on 1 July 2026, that is May 2026, which was 8.77%. If the company uses a substituted accounting period, apply the same rule to that period’s start date instead of 1 July.

Reading the June figure instead is the usual mistake, and it changes the answer in a normal year. June 2025 sat at 8.27% while May 2025 sat at 8.37%, and 8.37 is the benchmark rate for 2025-26. Section 109N(2) also leaves room for regulations to set the rate a different way, and none currently do.

The data is at the Reserve Bank’s statistical tables, table F5.

## What the rate is for

A loan from a private company to a shareholder or an associate that is still unpaid at the company’s lodgment day for the year the loan was made is treated as a dividend unless an exclusion applies. For a loan not otherwise excluded, two relevant paths avoid that result: repaying the loan in full before the lodgment day, or a written agreement made before that day that satisfies section 109N. Section 109N(1) asks for 3 things: a written agreement, a rate of interest for years after the year the loan was made that equals or exceeds that year’s benchmark rate, and a term inside the maximum. The minimum yearly repayment is then calculated using the current year’s benchmark rate under section 109E. The maximum term is 7 years, or 25 years where the loan is fully secured by a registered mortgage over real property and the property’s value, net of prior liabilities, is at least 110% of the loan.

A higher benchmark rate increases the minimum yearly repayment when the unpaid balance and remaining term are unchanged.

## Common questions

### What is the Division 7A benchmark interest rate for 2026-27?

8.77% for companies with a 30 June year end. It is the Reserve Bank’s indicator lending rate for bank variable housing loans last published before 1 July 2026, which was the May 2026 figure.

### What was the benchmark rate for 2025-26?

8.37% for companies with a 30 June year end, using the May 2025 figure. The June 2025 figure of 8.27% was not published until after the income year had started, so it does not apply.

### Where does the rate come from?

Section 109N(2) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 points at the Reserve Bank’s indicator lending rate for bank variable housing loans, the last one published before the start of the income year, unless regulations provide another method. None currently do.

### Does a change in the rate affect loans already on foot?

Yes. The minimum yearly repayment uses that year’s benchmark rate, the unpaid balance and the remaining term. A higher rate increases the repayment when the other 2 inputs are unchanged.

### What is the maximum term for a complying Division 7A loan?

Seven years, or 25 years where the loan is fully secured by a registered mortgage over real property whose value, less prior liabilities, is at least 110% of the loan when it is first made.

# Cents per kilometre rate for car expenses

The rate is 91 cents per kilometre for the income year commencing 1 July 2026, set for that year alone, and was 88 cents for the 2 years before it. The method covers the first 5,000 business kilometres per car per year and no further. One rate applies to every car, whatever its engine size, and no receipts have to be kept for the car expenses themselves. This is the rate for the cents per kilometre method, one of the car expense methods: it is a rate table, not a determination that a particular taxpayer, car or business kilometre is eligible, and the choice between methods is not made here.

Each rate below is taken from the determination that sets it, read on 14 September 2026: the 2026 determination (F2026L00785) and the 2024 determination (F2024L00697), both made under section 28-25 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997.

2025-26 | 88 | Determination 2024 (F2024L00697), section 6
2024-25 | 88 | Determination 2024 (F2024L00697), section 6

Earlier years were set the same way, each determination repealing the one before it. The 2024 determination repealed the 2023 determination, and the 2026 determination repealed the 2024 one. ## Why 2025-26 did not move, and why 2026-27 is different

A determination is not always written one year at a time, and the 2 most recent ones are drafted differently. The 2024 determination set the rate 'for income years commencing on or after 1 July 2024', and its own note says it applies to the year commencing 1 July 2024 and to any subsequent income year until it is repealed or varied. Nothing replaced it for 2025-26, so 88 cents carried through unchanged.

The 2026 determination repealed it and is written for one year only. Section 6 sets the rate 'for the income year commencing on 1 July 2026', and it carries no note extending that to subsequent years. So 91 cents does not carry forward the way 88 cents did. A further determination has to be registered to set the rate for 2027-28, and until one is registered, there is no rate for that year to rely on.

## How the method works

For business claims, the ATO limits this method to sole traders and partnerships with at least one individual partner, claiming for a car. Private travel does not qualify. This eligibility guidance was checked on 22 September 2026; the historical rate table review remains dated 14 September 2026.

Section 28-25 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 sets the calculation: business kilometres travelled by the car in the income year, multiplied by the rate determined for that year. Four features of it catch people out.

- 5,000 kilometres is a ceiling, not a threshold. If the car travelled 5,085 business kilometres, you claim 5,000 and lose the extra 85. The Act uses that exact example.
- One rate for every car. Engine size stopped mattering when the old engine capacity bands were replaced by a single determined rate.
- No substantiation of the car expenses. Section 28-35 removes that requirement for this method, which is not the same as needing no records: the business kilometres still have to be a reasonable estimate you can explain.
- The rate is meant to cover everything. In setting it, the Commissioner must have regard to average operating costs, which the Act’s own note describes as including registration, insurance and depreciation as well as fuel and maintenance. Claiming those separately on top of the rate is double counting.

## Common questions

### What is the cents per kilometre rate for 2026-27?

91 cents per kilometre, set by section 6 of the Income Tax Assessment (Cents per Kilometre Deduction Rate for Car Expenses) Determination 2026.

### What was the rate for 2025-26?

88 cents per kilometre. The 2024 determination set that rate for income years commencing on or after 1 July 2024, and nothing replaced it before the 2025-26 year, so it carried through.

### What rate applies for 2027-28?

Not yet set. Section 6 of the 2026 determination sets 91 cents for the income year commencing on 1 July 2026 only. It has no note extending that rate to later years, so a further determination must be registered before there is a rate for 2027-28.

### How many kilometres can I claim under this method?

The first 5,000 business kilometres per car per income year. Kilometres above 5,000 are discarded rather than paid at a lower rate, so a car that travelled 5,085 business kilometres claims 5,000.

### Does the rate depend on the size of the car’s engine?

No. A single rate applies to every car for the income year. The engine capacity bands belong to an older version of the method.

### Can I claim fuel, registration and insurance as well as the rate?

No. The Commissioner sets the rate by having regard to average operating costs, and the Act names registration, insurance, depreciation, fuel and maintenance as examples of those costs. They are already inside the rate.
