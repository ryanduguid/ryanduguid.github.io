### Does my company qualify for the lower company tax rate?

The 25% rate applies to a base rate entity: a company with aggregated turnover under $50 million and no more than 80% of its assessable income from base rate entity passive income. Other companies pay 30%. Test the company’s income and aggregated turnover conditions for the relevant year.

- Reconcile taxable income, passive income categories and connected entity information.
- Use the existing company tax example only after the inputs and applicable conditions have been established.

The example does not determine eligibility merely from accounting profit.

### How should I record money paid to a director as wages, dividends or a loan?

Wages and director’s fees are assessable income to you, and the company must withhold PAYG from them. A loan to a shareholder or their associate can be treated as a dividend under Division 7A unless it is repaid, or put under a complying written agreement, before the company’s lodgment day. Record a director payment according to its legal basis and supporting documents.

- Gather payroll records, dividend documents or loan terms and trace each payment.
- Review withholding, super, franking and Division 7A consequences before posting or reclassifying it.

Changing the ledger label does not change the underlying transaction.

### Does taking money out of my company create a Division 7A problem?

It can. A payment, loan or forgiven debt from a private company to a shareholder or their associate can be treated as a dividend under Division 7A, generally unfranked. A loan avoids this if it is fully repaid, or put under a complying written agreement, before the company’s lodgment day. Review company payments, loans and debt forgiveness involving shareholders or associates under Division 7A.

- Reconcile the person’s account and gather agreements, dates and repayment evidence.
- Use the existing review case to organise the evidence and identify unresolved legal questions.

The repayment worksheet does not decide whether a loan is complying or whether a deemed dividend arises.

### How do I calculate the minimum yearly repayment on a complying Division 7A loan?

The minimum yearly repayment uses the benchmark interest rate (8.77% for 2026-27 and 8.37% for 2025-26 for a 30 June year end), the unpaid balance and the remaining term. The maximum term is 7 years, or 25 years for a loan fully secured by a registered mortgage over real property. Use the minimum yearly repayment calculation for an established complying loan and the correct year.

- Gather the relevant opening balance, benchmark rate, remaining term and qualifying repayments.
- Reproduce the existing Division 7A case, then retain the inputs and compare required with actual qualifying repayments.

Eligibility, amalgamation and repayment classification must already have been reviewed.

### Which payments count as repayments of a Division 7A loan?

A repayment may not count if you reborrow a similar or larger amount from the company afterwards, or use money borrowed from the company to make it. A dividend can be set off against a minimum yearly repayment only if both exist and the offset is agreed by the end of the income year, usually 30 June. Review the source, timing and surrounding arrangements for each proposed repayment.

- Trace the payment through bank and loan records and identify any reborrowing or circular movement.
- Document the section 109R review and qualifying amount before using the minimum repayment worksheet.

A credit to a director loan account is not automatically a qualifying Division 7A repayment.

### What must a family trust document before distributing income at year-end?

A resolution making beneficiaries presently entitled to trust income is only effective for tax if it is made by 30 June, or earlier if the deed requires. If it isn’t effective, the trustee or other beneficiaries may be assessed instead, and a trustee may be taxed at the top rate. Build the distribution file from the deed, beneficiary position, income calculation and trustee decision.

- Check the deed and timing requirements, prepare the relevant income and distribution schedules, and retain resolutions.
- Use the existing trust example to separate arithmetic from missing documents and unresolved entitlement.

### How do dividends and franking credits affect my tax return?

A franking credit is tax the company has already paid on the dividend. Include the dividend and franking credit in your return; the credit reduces your tax, and an eligible individual gets any excess refunded. If your franking credits for the year total $5,000 or more, you must have held the shares at risk for at least 45 days (90 for preference shares). Reconcile the dividend statement to cash received, the franked or unfranked amount and franking credits.

- Record any foreign, trust or other adjustments and the relevant holding period evidence.
- Use the ATO investment and imputation guidance to determine what appears in the return.

A stated franking credit does not automatically establish entitlement to use the offset.

### Does providing a company car or paying an employee’s private expenses create FBT?

It can. FBT applies if a company car is used or available for an employee’s private use, and reimbursing or paying for an expense an employee incurs can be an expense payment fringe benefit. FBT is 47% of the grossed-up taxable value for FBT years ending 31 March. Identify the benefit, recipient, private use and FBT year before valuing anything.

- Gather vehicle records or expense invoices, employee contributions and any claimed exemption evidence.
- Apply the appropriate valuation method and reconcile the resulting taxable value to the FBT workpaper.

The existing worksheet grosses up supplied taxable values; it does not decide whether a benefit or exemption exists.

### What information do I need to prepare an ATO payment plan proposal?

A payment plan must clear the debt in the shortest possible fixed period, with weekly, fortnightly or monthly instalments. General interest charge keeps accruing daily, you must pay new tax debts on time, and income tax and activity statement debts need separate plans. Prepare a proposal that shows how the debt and ongoing obligations could be paid.

- Reconcile the ATO debt, outstanding lodgements and available cash, and prepare a realistic payment forecast.
- Use the ATO support route to discuss terms and retain any accepted arrangement.

A forecast does not establish acceptance, interest relief or removal of other obligations.

### Could my related businesses be grouped together for NSW payroll tax?

Businesses can be grouped for NSW payroll tax through related corporations, common employees, common control, tracing of interests, phoenix operators or subsuming smaller groups. Only one group member claims the tax-free threshold, against the group’s combined wages. Assess the relationships between businesses against the NSW grouping rules.

- Prepare an ownership and control diagram, shared-employee details and financial relationships.
- Document each grouping test and any exclusion application, then reconcile group wages.

Separate company names or accounts do not by themselves prevent payroll tax grouping.

Revenue NSW payroll tax · What is payroll tax grouping?, Revenue NSW

### Which expenses can I claim for a rental property?

Loan interest, council rates and qualifying repairs and maintenance may be deductible for income-producing use. A depreciating asset costing $300 or less may qualify for an immediate deduction only if used predominantly to produce non-business assessable income and the set and identical or substantially identical asset cost tests are met. Check the second-hand residential asset restrictions before claiming. Other depreciating assets, eligible capital works and borrowing expenses have separate deduction rules. Apportion private use. Separate rental income, private use, borrowing costs, repairs and capital items before claiming expenses.

- Gather agent statements, invoices, loan interest, ownership details and rental availability records.
- Apportion costs where required and reconcile the deduction schedule to the supporting documents.

The treatment depends on the expense and property use; a rental label alone does not establish deductibility.

### Is work on my rental property a repair, an improvement or a depreciating asset?

Qualifying repairs and maintenance for income-producing use may be immediately deductible. Initial repairs for defects that existed when you acquired the property, improvements and replacements of an entire unit of property are capital. Assess any deduction under the depreciating asset or capital works rules separately. Eligible expenditure may form part of the CGT cost base. For property acquired after 7.30 pm (ACT time) on 13 May 1997, and for expenditure after 30 June 1999 on property acquired earlier, the cost base is reduced by capital works deductions claimed or claimable. Capital expenditure does not automatically produce a deduction over several years. Review what the work did to the property and when the relevant damage or condition arose.

- Keep invoices, scope of work, purchase condition and dates; split distinct components where supported.
- Assess repair, capital works and depreciating asset treatment separately and update the relevant schedules.

Cost alone does not distinguish a repair from an improvement or initial repair.

93.

### How do I work out the capital gain when I sell an investment property?

The capital gain is the capital proceeds less the cost base. An Australian resident individual who owned the property for at least 12 months can usually reduce the gain by the 50% CGT discount. For a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027, the first enacted stage of reform treats gains that accrue from that date differently: cost base indexation replaces the discount for assets held at least 12 months, and a minimum 30% tax can apply to certain net capital gains of individuals. Gains accrued before then keep their existing treatment, such as the discount, but the pre-CGT exemption does not cover gains accruing from that date. An owner can choose to keep the current rules for an eligible new residential dwelling or affordable housing. Build the capital gains calculation from the relevant event, proceeds and adjusted cost base.

- Gather purchase and sale contracts, costs, ownership and occupancy history, and capital works records, and for a property acquired before 1 July 2027 and sold on or after that date, its market value immediately before that date, where required.
- Review any exemptions, adjustments, residency and discount conditions before calculating the net result.

A sale price minus purchase price calculation can omit material tax adjustments.

### How do capital losses and the CGT discount affect tax on shares I sell?

Apply capital losses first. An Australian resident individual who owned the shares for at least 12 months can then reduce the remaining gain by the 50% CGT discount. For a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027, the first enacted stage of reform treats gains that accrue from that date differently: cost base indexation replaces the discount for assets held at least 12 months, and a minimum 30% tax can apply to certain net capital gains of individuals. Gains accrued before then keep their existing treatment, such as the discount, but the pre-CGT exemption does not cover gains accruing from that date. Calculate each gain or loss, apply eligible capital losses, then consider any available discount.

- Retain trade records, cost base adjustments, holding periods and carried-forward loss balances, and for shares acquired before 1 July 2027 and sold on or after that date, each holding’s market value immediately before that date, where required.
- Reconcile the final calculation to the year’s CGT schedule and keep the remaining loss record.

The bounded worksheet assumes the gains, losses and discount eligibility have already been established.

### Do I owe tax when I sell, swap or spend cryptocurrency?

Often, yes. Selling crypto or swapping one crypto asset for another is a CGT event, so a gain can be taxable. Review each crypto disposal and income event, including swaps and spending, rather than only withdrawals to cash.

- Export exchange and wallet activity, fees and Australian dollar values with dates.
- Reconcile transfers between your own wallets separately from transactions that require tax review.

The tax treatment depends on the activity and event; this site does not import wallets or classify transactions.

### How do I report ETF distributions and their tax adjustments?

Declare ETF distributions, including amounts reinvested through a distribution reinvestment plan. Your ETF tax statement, such as an AMMA statement, shows the amounts to report and where, and non-assessable amounts mostly adjust the cost base of your units. Use the fund’s annual tax statement and any AMMA statement to reconcile the distribution components.

- Match cash, reinvestments, capital gains, foreign income and credits to the statement.
- Record cost base increases or decreases and carry them into the investment register.

Cash received alone can differ from the amount and components that belong in the tax return.

### What records does my accountant need for an SMSF annual return and audit?

Trustees must appoint an approved SMSF auditor each year, at least 45 days before the SMSF annual return is due. The auditor needs a statement of financial position and an operating statement before starting, and any requested information within 14 days; the audit must be finished before the return is lodged. Prepare the financial records and trustee evidence needed for both the return and the independent audit.

- Gather bank and investment records, valuations, contributions, pensions, minutes, member balances and supporting agreements.
- Reconcile opening and closing balances and resolve the auditor’s record requests before finalising the return.

This checklist does not perform an SMSF audit or certify compliance.

### When does an investment property become liable for land tax in NSW?

For owners entitled to the general threshold, NSW land tax for the 2026 land tax year applies when the combined taxable value of their non-exempt NSW land exceeds $1,075,000. Some trusts and related companies do not qualify for that threshold, and foreign-owner surcharge land tax has no tax-free threshold. Check ownership, land values, exemptions and any surcharge conditions for the relevant NSW land tax year.

- Reconcile all relevant NSW landholdings, ownership interests and valuation notices.
- Use Revenue NSW’s current assessment guidance and retain the calculation and exemption evidence.

Purchase price and one property’s value alone do not establish the full land tax position.

### Does a Hunter Valley coal mining employer pay the Coal LSL levy?

Yes, for eligible employees in the black coal mining industry. The employer reports and pays the levy monthly, currently 2.7% of each eligible employee’s eligible wages. The levy depends on whether each employee is an eligible employee in the black coal mining industry, then on that employee’s eligible wages for the month.

- List each employee’s role and check it against the Coal LSL eligible employee guidance.
- Work out each eligible employee’s eligible wages for the month using the section 3B formula that applies.

The calculator does not decide whether an employee is eligible; it calculates the levy on the wages you supply.
