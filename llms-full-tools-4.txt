51.

### Why does my Xero bank balance differ from my bank statement?

Reconcile the bank statement and Xero at the same date before posting a correction.

- Compare statement lines with ledger transactions, outstanding payments and deposits.
- Investigate missing feeds, duplicates, opening balances and items dated outside the period.

Do not post a balancing journal while the cause remains unknown.

52.

### How do I identify and fix duplicate bank transactions in Xero?

Identify whether the duplicate is a statement line, an accounting transaction or both.

- Match date, amount, bank reference and source document; distinguish repeated genuine payments.
- Use the appropriate Xero correction after review, then rerun the bank reconciliation.

Removing the wrong item can remove a real transaction; retain the correction trail.

### How do I correct a customer payment matched to the wrong invoice?

Trace the payment and both customer invoices before changing the match.

- Save the original allocation details and check whether the invoice or period is locked.
- Correct the allocation through Xero’s supported workflow and recheck both customer balances and the bank reconciliation.

A different bank receipt should not be created merely to fix an allocation.

54.

### How do I record a personal purchase paid from the business account?

Code the private payment according to the entity and the person’s relationship to it.

- Keep the receipt, purpose and repayment evidence, and separate any business portion.
- For a sole trader, review drawings; for a company, review the director or shareholder account and tax consequences.

A company payment cannot automatically be treated as sole-trader drawings.

55.

### Which business records should I keep, and for how long?

Keep most business records for 5 years. Keep records for depreciating assets and CGT assets until 5 years after you dispose of the asset. Keep records that let someone trace transactions, calculations and decisions for the required retention period.

- Create a register covering tax, payroll, assets, loans, contracts and entity records.
- Assign retention dates using the relevant ATO and other legal requirements, including longer periods for continuing assets or disputes.

Do not apply one blanket retention date to every document.

### How should I organise my chart of accounts?

Use a chart of accounts that supports reporting and reconciliations at a manageable level of detail.

- Separate assets, liabilities, equity, revenue and expenses; keep control accounts identifiable.
- Use consistent account mappings and tracking categories for the dimensions you need to report.

Do not create a new account for every customer or supplier when the subledger already tracks them.

57.

### How do I split a loan repayment between principal and interest?

Split each repayment using the loan statement and the agreement. Interest is a finance cost; principal reduces the loan balance.

- Reconcile opening principal, advances, principal repayments and closing principal.
- Use the loan calculator to sense-check a fixed-rate monthly schedule, then compare with the lender’s statement.

Fees, daily interest, variable rates and balloons need the actual lender schedule.

58.

### Should a purchase be recorded as an asset or an expense?

Apply your accounting policy to the nature, use and expected benefit of the purchase, then assess tax separately.

- Record what was acquired, cost, useful life and when it became available for use.
- If capitalised, update the asset register and depreciation; document any tax difference.

An accounting asset policy and an immediate tax deduction rule can give different treatments.

### Why do my aged receivables or payables differ from the general ledger?

Compare the aged report and ledger on the same date with the same filters.

- Trace journals to control accounts, prepayments, credits, foreign-currency adjustments and excluded contacts.
- Prepare a bridge to the ledger and resolve unexplained items before closing.

A matching total alone does not establish that individual balances are collectible or payable.

60.

### What should I reconcile and check before closing the month or financial year?

Close from reconciled balances and documented review decisions.

- Reconcile bank, receivables, payables, GST, payroll, super, loans, fixed assets and key accruals.
- Review cut-off, unusual journals and variances, then retain the pack and approval before locking the period.

Fictional worked example

At 30 September 2026, a fictional bank statement shows $12,000 and the ledger bank account shows $11,700. A $300 customer receipt is on the statement but missing from the ledger. After confirming the invoice and posting the missing receipt, both bank balances are $12,000. The aged receivables report still shows $9,500 against a $10,000 control account. Record that separate $500 exception, its owner and the evidence needed to resolve it before completing the close.

The existing close pack supports review; it does not automatically approve journals or finalise accounts.

61.

### How do I build a 13-week cash flow forecast?

Forecast cash by the week it is expected to move through the bank.

- Start with the reconciled bank balance and schedule receipts, suppliers, wages, tax, debt and drawings across 13 weeks.
- Use the browser forecast for a short scenario, or the existing workbook for a detailed worked example.

Fictional worked example

Start a fictional forecast on 28 September 2026 with $200 opening cash and a $100 buffer. Enter $1,000 receipts and $300 payments in week 1, and zero in every later week. Closing cash is $900. Delay the full $1,000 receipt by two weeks: cash closes at minus $100 in weeks 1 and 2, then returns to $900 in week 3. The lowest balance is minus $100, so the funding gap to the buffer is $200. The eventual closing balance is unchanged.

Weekly closing balances can hide a shortage within the week; review daily timing where cash is tight.

### Why is my business profitable but short of cash?

Profit and cash diverge because income and expenses can be recognised at different times from receipts and payments.

- Bridge profit to cash using receivables, payables, inventory, tax, capital spending, debt and drawings.
- Use the fictional Newcastle example to inspect a profitable business with a temporary cash shortage.

Loan proceeds and principal repayments affect cash without being ordinary trading revenue and expense.

### Can my business afford another employee?

Compare the extra cash costs of hiring with conservative additional receipts and available cash.

- Calculate annual staff cost, then phase wages, super, setup costs and training into the forecast.
- Test slower sales, a delayed start and a minimum cash buffer before committing.

Annual profitability does not establish that each payroll payment will be affordable.

### What would buying equipment outright versus financing it do to my cash flow?

Compare the timing and total payments of outright purchase and finance.

- Put the purchase price into the relevant cash week for the outright option.
- For finance, schedule deposit, fees, repayments and any balloon; compare the cash minimum and total cost.

### How much working capital or overdraft capacity will I need next quarter?

Find the lowest forecast balance and compare it with the cash buffer you need.

- Include current bank cash, committed payments, realistic receipts and stressed collection dates.
- Use the forecast funding gap result as an initial amount to investigate, then review timing and facility terms.

A calculated gap is not a credit approval or proof that borrowing is suitable.

### How can I reduce overdue customer balances and get paid sooner?

Prioritise overdue balances by amount, age, dispute and likelihood of collection.

- Reconcile the aged ledger, assign an owner and record the next action for each overdue account.
- Review invoice accuracy, payment terms and billing timing; update receipt dates using what customers confirm.

Changing a forecast date does not improve collection evidence.

### How do I plan for seasonal cash shortages?

Plan from the seasonal payment and receipt pattern, including the costs incurred before sales arrive.

- Compare prior periods with current orders, staffing and supplier commitments.
- Stress-test the low season and put any agreed funding or cost changes into the forecast.

A straight average month can conceal the quarter’s lowest cash balance.

### How do I update my forecast each month using actual results?

Replace completed periods with reconciled actuals and explain the difference before rolling the forecast forward.

- Save the previous forecast, match actual receipts and payments, and classify timing versus permanent changes.
- Refresh remaining assumptions and extend the horizon with a dated version and owner.

Do not overwrite the old forecast if you still need it to explain accuracy and decisions.

### How much must I sell each month to break even?

Break-even sales cover fixed costs after variable costs. For one product, divide fixed costs by contribution per unit.

- Use the same period for fixed costs and the expected selling price and variable cost per unit.
- Round required units up and compare them with practical sales capacity.

The calculator assumes one product or a stable representative mix; it excludes tax and financing cash timing.

72.

### What is the difference between gross margin and markup?

Gross margin divides gross profit by revenue. Markup divides gross profit by cost.

- Use sales and the directly related cost for the same goods or period.
- Compare both percentages with the calculator and keep GST treatment consistent.

A zero denominator has no meaningful percentage; the calculator leaves that percentage undefined.

### What hourly rate should I charge to cover costs and earn a target profit?

Divide annual costs plus target profit by realistic billable hours.

- Include the owner’s pay target in costs if it must be covered before the stated profit.
- Allow for leave, administration, marketing and unpaid time when estimating billable hours.

The calculated rate is a cost recovery target before GST; customers’ willingness to pay needs separate evidence.

### Which customers, jobs or service lines are making money?

Build contribution by customer, job or service line from consistent revenue and direct cost assignments.

- Allocate labour using recorded hours and identify material costs, discounts, rework and write-offs.
- Keep shared overhead allocation visible and compare results before and after that allocation.

An allocation method can change apparent profitability without changing total business profit.

### Does my company qualify for the lower company tax rate?

The 25% rate applies to a base rate entity: a company with aggregated turnover under $50 million and no more than 80% of its assessable income from base rate entity passive income. Other companies pay 30%. Test the company’s income and aggregated turnover conditions for the relevant year.

- Reconcile taxable income, passive income categories and connected entity information.
- Use the existing company tax example only after the inputs and applicable conditions have been established.

The example does not determine eligibility merely from accounting profit.

The minimum yearly repayment uses the benchmark interest rate (8.77% for 2026-27 and 8.37% for 2025-26 for a 30 June year end), the unpaid balance and the remaining term. ### Which payments count as repayments of a Division 7A loan?

A repayment may not count if you reborrow a similar or larger amount from the company afterwards, or use money borrowed from the company to make it. A dividend can be set off against a minimum yearly repayment only if both exist and the offset is agreed by the end of the income year, usually 30 June. ### How do dividends and franking credits affect my tax return?

A franking credit is tax the company has already paid on the dividend. Include the dividend and franking credit in your return; the credit reduces your tax, and an eligible individual gets any excess refunded. If your franking credits for the year total $5,000 or more, you must have held the shares at risk for at least 45 days (90 for preference shares). Reconcile the dividend statement to cash received, the franked or unfranked amount and franking credits.

### What information do I need to prepare an ATO payment plan proposal?

A payment plan must clear the debt in the shortest possible fixed period, with weekly, fortnightly or monthly instalments. ### Could my related businesses be grouped together for NSW payroll tax?

Businesses can be grouped for NSW payroll tax through related corporations, common employees, common control, tracing of interests, phoenix operators or subsuming smaller groups. ### Which expenses can I claim for a rental property?

Loan interest, council rates and qualifying repairs and maintenance may be deductible for income-producing use. A depreciating asset costing $300 or less may qualify for an immediate deduction only if used predominantly to produce non-business assessable income and the set and identical or substantially identical asset cost tests are met. Check the second-hand residential asset restrictions before claiming. Other depreciating assets, eligible capital works and borrowing expenses have separate deduction rules. Apportion private use. Separate rental income, private use, borrowing costs, repairs and capital items before claiming expenses.

- Gather agent statements, invoices, loan interest, ownership details and rental availability records.
- Apportion costs where required and reconcile the deduction schedule to the supporting documents.

The treatment depends on the expense and property use; a rental label alone does not establish deductibility.

### Is work on my rental property a repair, an improvement or a depreciating asset?

Qualifying repairs and maintenance for income-producing use may be immediately deductible. Initial repairs for defects that existed when you acquired the property, improvements and replacements of an entire unit of property are capital. Assess any deduction under the depreciating asset or capital works rules separately. Eligible expenditure may form part of the CGT cost base. For property acquired after 7.30 pm (ACT time) on 13 May 1997, and for expenditure after 30 June 1999 on property acquired earlier, the cost base is reduced by capital works deductions claimed or claimable. Capital expenditure does not automatically produce a deduction over several years. Review what the work did to the property and when the relevant damage or condition arose.

- Keep invoices, scope of work, purchase condition and dates; split distinct components where supported.
- Assess repair, capital works and depreciating asset treatment separately and update the relevant schedules.

Cost alone does not distinguish a repair from an improvement or initial repair.

### How do I work out the capital gain when I sell an investment property?

The capital gain is the capital proceeds less the cost base. An Australian resident individual who owned the property for at least 12 months can usually reduce the gain by the 50% CGT discount. For a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027, the first enacted stage of reform treats gains that accrue from that date differently: cost base indexation replaces the discount for assets held at least 12 months, and a minimum 30% tax can apply to certain net capital gains of individuals. Gains accrued before then keep their existing treatment, such as the discount, but the pre-CGT exemption does not cover gains accruing from that date. An owner can choose to keep the current rules for an eligible new residential dwelling or affordable housing. Build the capital gains calculation from the relevant event, proceeds and adjusted cost base.

- Gather purchase and sale contracts, costs, ownership and occupancy history, and capital works records, and for a property acquired before 1 July 2027 and sold on or after that date, its market value immediately before that date, where required.
- Review any exemptions, adjustments, residency and discount conditions before calculating the net result.

A sale price minus purchase price calculation can omit material tax adjustments.

### How do capital losses and the CGT discount affect tax on shares I sell?

Apply capital losses first. An Australian resident individual who owned the shares for at least 12 months can then reduce the remaining gain by the 50% CGT discount. For a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027, the first enacted stage of reform treats gains that accrue from that date differently: cost base indexation replaces the discount for assets held at least 12 months, and a minimum 30% tax can apply to certain net capital gains of individuals. Gains accrued before then keep their existing treatment, such as the discount, but the pre-CGT exemption does not cover gains accruing from that date. Calculate each gain or loss, apply eligible capital losses, then consider any available discount.

- Retain trade records, cost base adjustments, holding periods and carried-forward loss balances, and for shares acquired before 1 July 2027 and sold on or after that date, each holding’s market value immediately before that date, where required.
- Reconcile the final calculation to the year’s CGT schedule and keep the remaining loss record.

The bounded worksheet assumes the gains, losses and discount eligibility have already been established.

### What records does my accountant need for an SMSF annual return and audit?

### When does an investment property become liable for land tax in NSW?

For owners entitled to the general threshold, NSW land tax for the 2026 land tax year applies when the combined taxable value of their non-exempt NSW land exceeds $1,075,000. Some trusts and related companies do not qualify for that threshold, and foreign-owner surcharge land tax has no tax-free threshold. Check ownership, land values, exemptions and any surcharge conditions for the relevant NSW land tax year.

- Reconcile all relevant NSW landholdings, ownership interests and valuation notices.
- Use Revenue NSW’s current assessment guidance and retain the calculation and exemption evidence.

### Does a Hunter Valley coal mining employer pay the Coal LSL levy?

## How the Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator works

The Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator works out the least a borrower must repay on one amalgamated loan in one income year after the year the loan was made. It applies the formula in subsection 109E(6) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936: the loan not repaid at the end of the previous income year, times the year’s benchmark interest rate, divided by 1 minus (1 ÷ (1 + the rate)) raised to the remaining term in years.

The interest for the year is the benchmark rate on the whole balance, and the principal is the rest of the repayment. That split assumes the whole repayment is made on the last day of the income year. The ATO works interest out from the daily balance, so a repayment made earlier in the year carries less interest and more of it reduces the principal.

The ATO’s worked example on Loans by private companies takes $55,000 at 5.95% with 7 years remaining and gives $9,835 to the nearest dollar; this calculator gives $9,834.92 for those figures.

## What the Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a payment or loan is a Division 7A loan, whether the loan agreement complies, whether a shortfall is a deemed dividend or how much of it, or how a distributable surplus limits one. It works one income year only, with no repayment schedule, and none for the year in which the loan was made. It takes the balance as you enter it: it does not work it out from the loans made, repayments made before the lodgment day or the previous year’s interest, and it does not combine several loans. It does not handle an income year that ends on a day other than 30 June. Subsection 109E(5) lets regulations set another amount, and this calculator applies the formula only. Confirm the figures with the ATO Division 7A calculator and decision tool or a registered tax agent.
