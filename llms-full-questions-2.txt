### How much super must I pay, and which earnings count towards it?

The super guarantee rate is 12%, as it has been since 1 July 2025. From 1 July 2026 it is worked out on qualifying earnings, up to a maximum contribution base of $270,830 for 2026-27. Identify the applicable super regime and eligible earnings for the pay date before calculating a contribution.

- From July 2026, use the ATO Payday Super guidance and map each pay component to the relevant earnings definition.
- Check worker eligibility, limits and payroll settings; retain the calculation and contribution evidence.

The old quarterly SG worksheet does not establish current Payday Super entitlement.

### By what date must a super contribution reach the employee’s fund?

Since 1 July 2026, the fund must receive the contribution within 7 business days after payday, or within 20 business days for the first contribution for a new employee or to a new fund. Test receipt at the employee’s fund against the applicable deadline.

- Record payday, pathway, amount, remittance date and confirmed fund receipt date.
- Use the existing timing example to distinguish timely remittance from evidence of timely receipt.

The timing tool needs the contribution amount supplied and the correct pathway selected.

43.

### What should I do if a super payment is late, rejected or missing?

Since 1 July 2026, super that is unpaid, late, short or paid to the wrong fund makes you liable to the super guarantee charge, which the ATO assesses. The charge includes notional earnings and may include an administrative uplift of up to 60%; paying the fund what you owe as soon as possible, even in part, reduces it. Investigate the payment trail and apply the current late-payment process.

- Reconcile payroll, clearing house status, rejections and fund receipt for each affected employee.
- Resolve the cause, record any replacement payment and review the ATO’s reporting and charge requirements.

Sending a replacement does not automatically remove a late-payment consequence.

### Do I have to pay super for a contractor who invoices through an ABN?

If you pay a contractor under a contract that is mainly for their labour, meaning more than half its dollar value, they are an employee for super guarantee purposes and you may need to pay super for them. An ABN makes no difference. Review the actual contract and working arrangement against the ATO’s super eligibility guidance.

- Record who performs the work, how they are paid and whether the contract is for their labour.
- Retain the eligibility conclusion separately from the supplier’s ABN and invoice.

An ABN is not proof that super is unnecessary.

Use the ATO tax withheld calculator or tax tables for the pay period, whether weekly, fortnightly or monthly. The employee’s TFN declaration or withholding declaration tells you whether to withhold extra for a study or training support loan. A resident annual income tax worksheet is not a payroll withholding calculator.

### How do I calculate an employee’s final pay, including unused leave?

Final pay is the wages owing, including penalty rates and allowances, plus any unused annual leave with loading if it would have been paid during employment; it can also include accrued or pro rata long service leave. Sick and carer’s leave isn’t paid out, and most awards require final pay within 7 days after the last day of employment. Build final pay from the employee’s entitlements and the circumstances of departure.

- Gather the award or agreement, termination date, unpaid hours, leave balances and notice details.
- Check leave loading, redundancy and long service leave where applicable, then apply the relevant tax treatment.

The generic staff cost calculator cannot determine final pay entitlements.

### When does my business become liable for payroll tax in NSW?

For 2026-27, NSW payroll tax applies to taxable wages above $1,200,000 a year, at a rate of 5.45%. Review Australian taxable wages, NSW connection, grouping and the applicable threshold.

- Prepare a wages reconciliation that includes the categories Revenue NSW requires.
- Check the monthly registration trigger and annual reconciliation requirements for the relevant year.

Book payroll expense alone can omit taxable benefits, super or grouped wages.

### Do contractor payments count towards NSW payroll tax?

Payments to a contractor who provides the services of a worker under a relevant contract are liable for NSW payroll tax unless an exemption applies. Exemptions include some services provided for 90 days or less in a financial year and services the contractor provides to the public. Contractor payments need a separate payroll tax review under the NSW relevant-contract rules.

- Keep the contract, services, days worked and evidence for any exclusion claimed.
- Reconcile reviewed contractor amounts to the payroll tax workpaper.

An invoice, ABN or GST registration alone does not establish an exclusion.

### What does an employee cost after super, leave, insurance and other on-costs?

Start with wages plus the 12% super guarantee, then add leave, workers compensation, any payroll tax and other on-costs. Add annual wages, super and the other costs you expect to pay, then consider productive capacity separately.

- Enter the annual super amount and other costs such as insurance, payroll tax, recruitment and equipment.
- Allow for paid leave when estimating productive hours, and add relief staff costs only where expected.

Do not add ordinary paid leave again if it is already included in the annual salary.

### Why does my Xero bank balance differ from my bank statement?

Reconcile the bank statement and Xero at the same date before posting a correction.

- Compare statement lines with ledger transactions, outstanding payments and deposits.
- Investigate missing feeds, duplicates, opening balances and items dated outside the period.

Do not post a balancing journal while the cause remains unknown.

### How do I identify and fix duplicate bank transactions in Xero?

Identify whether the duplicate is a statement line, an accounting transaction or both.

- Match date, amount, bank reference and source document; distinguish repeated genuine payments.
- Use the appropriate Xero correction after review, then rerun the bank reconciliation.

Removing the wrong item can remove a real transaction; retain the correction trail.

Code the private payment according to the entity and the person’s relationship to it.

- Keep the receipt, purpose and repayment evidence, and separate any business portion.

### Which business records should I keep, and for how long?

Keep most business records for 5 years. Keep records for depreciating assets and CGT assets until 5 years after you dispose of the asset. Keep records that let someone trace transactions, calculations and decisions for the required retention period.

- Create a register covering tax, payroll, assets, loans, contracts and entity records.
- Assign retention dates using the relevant ATO and other legal requirements, including longer periods for continuing assets or disputes.

Do not apply one blanket retention date to every document.

Close from reconciled balances and documented review decisions.

- Reconcile bank, receivables, payables, GST, payroll, super, loans, fixed assets and key accruals.

### How do I build a 13-week cash flow forecast?

Forecast cash by the week it is expected to move through the bank.

- Start with the reconciled bank balance and schedule receipts, suppliers, wages, tax, debt and drawings across 13 weeks.
- Use the browser forecast for a short scenario, or the existing workbook for a detailed worked example.

Fictional worked example

Start a fictional forecast on 28 September 2026 with $200 opening cash and a $100 buffer. Enter $1,000 receipts and $300 payments in week 1, and zero in every later week. Closing cash is $900. Delay the full $1,000 receipt by two weeks: cash closes at minus $100 in weeks 1 and 2, then returns to $900 in week 3. The lowest balance is minus $100, so the funding gap to the buffer is $200. The eventual closing balance is unchanged.

Weekly closing balances can hide a shortage within the week; review daily timing where cash is tight.

### Why is my business profitable but short of cash?

Profit and cash diverge because income and expenses can be recognised at different times from receipts and payments.

- Bridge profit to cash using receivables, payables, inventory, tax, capital spending, debt and drawings.

- Keep invoices, scope of work, purchase condition and dates; split distinct components where supported.
- Assess repair, capital works and depreciating asset treatment separately and update the relevant schedules.

Cost alone does not distinguish a repair from an improvement or initial repair.

### How do I work out the capital gain when I sell an investment property?

The capital gain is the capital proceeds less the cost base. An Australian resident individual who owned the property for at least 12 months can usually reduce the gain by the 50% CGT discount. For a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027, the first enacted stage of reform treats gains that accrue from that date differently: cost base indexation replaces the discount for assets held at least 12 months, and a minimum 30% tax can apply to certain net capital gains of individuals. Gains accrued before then keep their existing treatment, such as the discount, but the pre-CGT exemption does not cover gains accruing from that date. An owner can choose to keep the current rules for an eligible new residential dwelling or affordable housing. Build the capital gains calculation from the relevant event, proceeds and adjusted cost base.

- Gather purchase and sale contracts, costs, ownership and occupancy history, and capital works records, and for a property acquired before 1 July 2027 and sold on or after that date, its market value immediately before that date, where required.
- Review any exemptions, adjustments, residency and discount conditions before calculating the net result.

A sale price minus purchase price calculation can omit material tax adjustments.

### How do capital losses and the CGT discount affect tax on shares I sell?

Apply capital losses first. An Australian resident individual who owned the shares for at least 12 months can then reduce the remaining gain by the 50% CGT discount. For a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027, the first enacted stage of reform treats gains that accrue from that date differently: cost base indexation replaces the discount for assets held at least 12 months, and a minimum 30% tax can apply to certain net capital gains of individuals. Gains accrued before then keep their existing treatment, such as the discount, but the pre-CGT exemption does not cover gains accruing from that date. Calculate each gain or loss, apply eligible capital losses, then consider any available discount.

- Retain trade records, cost base adjustments, holding periods and carried-forward loss balances, and for shares acquired before 1 July 2027 and sold on or after that date, each holding’s market value immediately before that date, where required.
- Reconcile the final calculation to the year’s CGT schedule and keep the remaining loss record.

The bounded worksheet assumes the gains, losses and discount eligibility have already been established.

### What records does my accountant need for an SMSF annual return and audit?

Trustees must appoint an approved SMSF auditor each year, at least 45 days before the SMSF annual return is due. The auditor needs a statement of financial position and an operating statement before starting, and any requested information within 14 days; the audit must be finished before the return is lodged. Prepare the financial records and trustee evidence needed for both the return and the independent audit.

- Gather bank and investment records, valuations, contributions, pensions, minutes, member balances and supporting agreements.
- Reconcile opening and closing balances and resolve the auditor’s record requests before finalising the return.

### When does an investment property become liable for land tax in NSW?

For owners entitled to the general threshold, NSW land tax for the 2026 land tax year applies when the combined taxable value of their non-exempt NSW land exceeds $1,075,000. Some trusts and related companies do not qualify for that threshold, and foreign-owner surcharge land tax has no tax-free threshold. Check ownership, land values, exemptions and any surcharge conditions for the relevant NSW land tax year.

- Reconcile all relevant NSW landholdings, ownership interests and valuation notices.
- Use Revenue NSW’s current assessment guidance and retain the calculation and exemption evidence.

Purchase price and one property’s value alone do not establish the full land tax position.

### Does a Hunter Valley coal mining employer pay the Coal LSL levy?

Yes, for eligible employees in the black coal mining industry. The employer reports and pays the levy monthly, currently 2.7% of each eligible employee’s eligible wages. The levy depends on whether each employee is an eligible employee in the black coal mining industry, then on that employee’s eligible wages for the month.

- List each employee’s role and check it against the Coal LSL eligible employee guidance.
- Work out each eligible employee’s eligible wages for the month using the section 3B formula that applies.
