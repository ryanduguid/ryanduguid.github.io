Evidence for the work

Evidence is material that another person can inspect: a primary source, a public release or citation, a reproducible test, or a clearly bounded example. It supports a limited claim about the work. It does not turn a tool output into advice, a compliance decision or a lodgement.

What counts as evidence

EvidenceWhat can be checked
Primary-source hierarchy Rules are traced to Commonwealth legislation first, then regulator guidance and accounting standards where they apply. Secondary summaries are not the authority for a calculation.
Synthetic public examples Public fixtures and examples use synthetic data, so a reader can inspect the behaviour without receiving client information.
Versioned releases and citations Public package pages, registry records, release history and citations identify a version or published contribution that can be revisited later.
Local-data boundary Client-sensitive data is kept local to the intended workflow. Public repositories are not a channel for client records or outcomes.
Automated tests and human review Automated tests check specified software behaviour. A human reviewer retains professional judgement, approval and every lodgement decision.

What this proves and what it does not

These public records can show that named software, package records, credentials and upstream contributions exist at the linked locations. They can also show the stated source, version and review boundaries for a particular public artefact.

They do not prove an employer's endorsement, Chartered Accountants ANZ endorsement, regulatory approval, a client outcome or accounting compliance. Passing automated tests does not establish that a result is correct for a real set of facts. Nothing here is tax, legal or financial advice, and it creates no professional relationship or engagement.

A qualified person must review the relevant records and current primary sources before relying on a result. Professional judgement, approval and lodgement remain human-only decisions.
