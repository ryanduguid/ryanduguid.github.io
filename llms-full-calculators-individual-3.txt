## How the novated lease calculator works

The novated lease calculator compares a year of a novated car lease with paying the same car costs from your after-tax pay. Under the lease your employer pays the lease and running costs from your salary, claims back the GST and passes it on, so less of your salary is taxed.

The FBT exemption for electric cars decides how the costs are split. If you are a current employee and the car is a battery electric or hydrogen fuel cell car, was first held and used on or after 1 July 2022 and no luxury car tax had become payable on a supply or importation before any of the benefits counted, the whole cost comes from your pre-tax salary with no FBT. Its value under the statutory formula, 20% of the cost price for a full year, is still reported on your income statement, grossed up. That reportable amount is not taxed but counts for the Medicare levy surcharge and study loan repayments. A plug-in hybrid kept the exemption only under a lease in place and in use before 1 April 2025.

Without the exemption, the usual approach is the employee contribution method: you pay the car’s taxable value from your after-tax pay, which leaves no FBT, and the rest of the cost from your pre-tax salary. The calculator works both ways for an exempt car, so you can see what the exemption is worth, and says which condition fails when it does not apply.

## What the novated lease calculator does not compute

The calculator covers the FBT years ending 31 March 2024 to 2027, for one car and one employer, with the salary as your only income. It does not compute:

- the lease payments themselves, the residual value, interest or fees, which come from your lease quote;
- the operating cost method, FBT left over when the costs are less than the taxable value, or an employer that does not claim GST credits;
- a vehicle designed to carry a tonne or more or 9 or more passengers, which is not a car for FBT and so not covered by the exemption;
- a car benefit for a former or future employee, which the exemption does not cover (section 8A(1)(a));
- whether luxury car tax became payable on another supply or an importation, which you answer. If it first became payable between benefits, section 8A(1)(c) treats the benefits before and after differently. The calculator needs one treatment for the year;
- a car with a value at first sale different from its cost price, or a home charger;
- the end of the lease, when you pay the residual or refinance;
- salary sacrificed to super, other income, deductions, or a spouse and dependants for the Medicare levy surcharge.

Confirm the figures with your employer, your lease provider or a registered tax agent.
