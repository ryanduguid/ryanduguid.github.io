## How the first home owner grant calculator works

The first home owner grant calculator gives the grant for a first home buyer who buys or builds a new home under a contract made from 1 July 2025 to 22 November 2026, beside the transfer duty the same buyer pays. It takes the amount and the value cap in force on the contract date, and pays nothing above the cap or for an established home.

- New South Wales: $10,000; the price must not exceed $600,000 for a home bought, and the land and building contract $750,000 for a home built.
- Victoria: $10,000 for a home worth up to $750,000.
- Queensland: $30,000, or the consideration if less, for a home worth under $750,000.
- Western Australia: $10,000. The cap is $800,000 south of the 26th parallel for contracts from 7 May 2026, $750,000 before, and $1,000,000 north of it.
- South Australia: $15,000, or the consideration if less, with no value cap.
- Tasmania: $30,000 for contracts from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026, then $20,000 to 30 June 2027, or the consideration if less, with no value cap. The transaction must be completed within 24 months, or a period the Commissioner extends, and not replace a transaction for the same property, between or benefiting substantially the same parties, entered before 1 July 2025 for a 2025-26 contract or 1 July 2026 for a 2026-27 contract.
- Northern Territory: the HomeGrown Territory Grant of $50,000, with no value cap. The contract must not replace one signed before 1 October 2024.
- Australian Capital Territory: no grant since 1 July 2019. The territory’s Home Buyer Concession Scheme is a duty concession, and the duty result applies it.

## Eligibility facts and transfer duty

Each grant needs 4 facts, which you state and the calculator does not test. Every applicant is a person aged 18 or over. At least one applicant is an Australian citizen or permanent resident. No applicant or spouse is excluded by the prior ownership rules below. The applicants meet the residence requirement: 12 continuous months starting within 12 months in New South Wales; 12 months for at least one applicant in Victoria; 6 continuous months starting within 12 months in Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia and Tasmania; and 12 months after taking possession or completion in the Northern Territory. A home built also needs its building contract price, without the land, to be at least the grant, since no grant exceeds that consideration. A Northern Territory contract must not replace one signed before 1 October 2024. A Tasmanian transaction must be completed within 24 months, or a period the Commissioner extends, and not replace a transaction for the same property, between or benefiting substantially the same parties, entered before the 1 July starting its grant period. The calculator computes nothing until the facts a transaction needs are stated, and in Western Australia until you choose where the home is.

For a home bought, the result adds the transfer duty calculator’s duty for an eligible first home buyer and its general duty on the same value, for contracts its rates cover: from 1 July 2025 in Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and the Northern Territory, from 7 May 2026 in Western Australia, and from 1 July 2026 in New South Wales, Tasmania and the Australian Capital Territory. Earlier contracts there get the grant without the duty.

## Prior ownership rules

The sources read state these rules. New South Wales: no applicant or spouse owned a home or other residential property in Australia before 1 July 2000. Victoria: none owned one before 1 July 2000, or lived for a continuous 6 months or more in a home they owned on or after 1 July 2000. Western Australia: none owned residential property on or after 1 July 2000 and lived in it for a continuous 6 months or more beginning on or after 1 July 2004. Tasmania: none owned a residential property before 1 July 2000, or owned and lived in one for more than 6 months after 1 July 2000. For Queensland, South Australia and the Northern Territory the sources read do not set the rule out, so state the fact against the revenue office’s own eligibility rules.

## What the first home owner grant calculator does not compute

The calculator refuses a contract dated before 1 July 2025 or after 22 November 2026, and a value below the grant amount outside Queensland, South Australia and Tasmania, where the sources read did not confirm the rule. For a home built it needs the building contract price to be at least the grant, so it does not compute a smaller grant on a cheaper contract. It does not cover owner-builders, substantially renovated homes, the ADF and family violence exemptions, the Commissioner’s discretion over the residence requirement, or schemes that are not first home owner grants, such as regional building grants, shared equity schemes and Commonwealth deposit schemes. For a home built, the duty falls on the land, and the first home buyer concessions on vacant land are not computed. Confirm the grant with the revenue office or an approved agent before relying on it.

## How the concessional catch-up calculator works

The concessional catch-up calculator works out each earlier year’s unused concessional contributions cap, the shortfall of that year’s contributions below its general cap, from 2018-19. Where an earlier year’s contributions went over its cap and the balance just before it was under $500,000, it applies the unused cap of the 5 years before that one, earliest first, as subsections 291-20(4) and (5) required for that year. What is left of the previous 5 years’ unused cap is carried into the year you choose.

With a total super balance under $500,000 on 30 June before the year, the cap available is the general cap plus that carried amount. The calculator shows how much of it this year’s contributions use, any excess, and what is left of each year’s amount with the year after which it lapses. A proposed contribution is added to the contributions so far, so you can see how far it goes before it becomes excess.

The ATO’s concessional contributions cap page works Sam’s example: unused cap of $14,000, $13,000, $14,500, $13,500 and $12,500 from 2019-20 to 2023-24 gives $67,500, so his 2024-25 cap is $97,500, and contributions of $97,500 use it all with no excess. The calculator opens on his figures; the ATO says only that his balance was below $500,000, so the $450,000 is fictional.

## What the concessional catch-up calculator does not cover

The calculator works the cap only. It does not decide what counts as a concessional contribution, in which year a contribution counts, or whether a deduction notice is valid, and it does not work out the tax on excess contributions or Division 293 tax. It does not cover a defined benefit interest, or non-concessional contributions and their bring-forward. It covers 2024-25, 2025-26 and 2026-27.

Your carry-forward amounts are on ATO online services (Super, then Information, then Carry forward concessional contributions), which use the contributions your funds have reported. Confirm the figures there or with a registered tax agent before you contribute.

## How the income tax calculator works

The income tax calculator works out one individual’s tax for a full income year from 2020-21 to 2026-27: the tax on taxable income at the year’s rates, the offsets, the Medicare levy and the Medicare levy surcharge. The offsets are the low income tax offset and, for 2020-21 and 2021-22, the low and middle income tax offset. It then takes that total, and any study or training loan repayment, from the tax withheld you enter, to estimate a refund or an amount owing.

It can also work out the beneficiary tax offset, the invalid and invalid carer tax offset, the zone or overseas forces tax offset and the private health insurance rebate. The first 3 come off the tax but not below nil. The rebate is worked from your private health insurance statement: what you were entitled to at your tier, less the premium reduction you received, comes off the total and can make it negative; a premium reduction above your entitlement is added to the total.

An Australian resident pays nothing on the first $18,200 and then each part of taxable income at its rate. The low income tax offset of up to $700 comes off a resident’s tax, but not below nil. The Medicare levy is 2% of a resident’s taxable income; up to the low-income threshold there is none, and from there to the phase-in limit it is 10% of the income over the threshold, if that is less. The ATO’s example on Medicare levy reduction for low-income earners, read 17 November 2026, gives Angie, single with $29,000 of taxable income in 2025-26, a levy of $98.90, as this calculator does.

The Medicare levy surcharge applies to a resident without private patient hospital cover whose income for surcharge purposes is above the year’s threshold. For each day without cover it charges 1%, 1.25% or 1.5% of taxable income and reportable fringe benefits, by the tier the income falls in.

For 2026-27 the Medicare levy thresholds are those the Act held on 17 November 2026, the same as 2025-26. If Parliament raises them for 2026-27, a person just above $28,011 will pay less than shown here.

## What the income tax calculator does not compute

The calculator gives the tax of one person who was a resident, a foreign resident or a working holiday maker for the whole year. It does not compute:

- the Medicare levy reduction for families, which depends on family taxable income and dependants, or a Medicare levy exemption;
- the seniors and pensioners tax offset and its higher Medicare levy thresholds, the net medical expenses tax offset, which ended after 2018-19, or any other offset, such as for super contributions or foreign income tax;
- for the rebate, a lifetime health cover loading, a policy for dependants only, a claim of a spouse’s share, or dependent children for a person with no spouse who maintained no dependant; for the invalid carer offset, shared maintenance, Family Tax Benefit Part B shared care or successive spouses; for the zone offset, shared maintenance or more than one area;
- a change of family or cover status during the year beyond the days without cover, a spouse’s trust income that the surcharge test adds, the net amount on which family trust distribution tax has been paid, which counts in income for surcharge purposes and in the amount the surcharge is levied on, or exempt foreign employment income, which the ATO’s surcharge page adds to income for surcharge purposes when taxable income is $1 or more;
- a first home super saver released amount, which is taxed with a 30% offset but left out of repayment income and income for surcharge purposes, or, for 2023-24 and earlier, the taxed element of a super lump sum up to the low rate cap, which a person from preservation age to under 60 could leave out of income for surcharge purposes;
- a study loan debt smaller than the repayment: the repayment cannot exceed what is still owed (section 154-20(1)(c) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003), which the calculator does not ask for;
- a part-year resident’s reduced tax-free threshold, a working holiday maker who is an Australian resident, or one with other income besides working holiday taxable income;
- tax on superannuation or employment termination remainders, income averaging, or a minor’s unearned income;
- Division 293 tax, excess contributions, PAYG instalments or other credits besides the tax withheld you enter.

## How the take-home pay calculator works

The take-home pay calculator takes one salary for a year, month, fortnight or week and works out the year’s income tax, offsets, Medicare levy, any Medicare levy surcharge and any study or training loan repayment on it. Salary sacrificed to super comes off first. From 2026-27 it also takes off the standard deduction of up to $1,000 for work-related expenses. It then gives what is left for the year, a month, a fortnight and a week, and an estimate of employer super guarantee. Supply dated earnings to calculate super using the applicable quarters or paydays. Employer super goes to a fund and is separate from take-home pay.

Those take-home amounts are the year’s tax spread evenly: the year’s net amount divided by 12, 26 and 52. They are not the amounts an employer withholds each pay. For a weekly, fortnightly or monthly pay, the calculator also works the PAYG amount withheld from one pay under the year’s Schedule 1, and Schedule 8 with a study loan, and the year’s withholding less the year’s tax, which is the refund or amount owing when the tax return is assessed. Withholding does not allow for the low and middle income tax offset, so in 2021-22 the offset comes back as part of the refund: $3,000 a fortnight has $670 withheld each pay and $1,543 back. For working holiday makers, who are withheld from under another schedule, use the ATO’s tax withheld calculator.

The example on arrival is 2025-26, before the standard deduction: on $75,000 the Medicare levy is $1,500, as the ATO’s example of Matthew on What is the Medicare levy gives. In 2026-27 the same salary is $74,000 of taxable income.

When the salary includes super, the calculator splits it: at 12%, a package of $112,000 is $100,000 of salary and $12,000 of super. For the package estimate, the annual earnings limit caps the estimate of employer super.

## What the take-home pay calculator does not compute

The tax and PAYG estimates assume one employer, a salary paid evenly over the full year and no other income, deductions or offsets. Dated super can use irregular payments that add up to the year’s gross salary.

- separate tax or withholding treatment for overtime, bonuses, allowances or leave loading. PAYG estimates use 52 weekly or 26 fortnightly pays and omit withholding under scales 1, 5 and 6, withholding for working holiday makers, offsets claimed in a withholding declaration and Medicare levy variations;
- 2025-26 withholding with a study loan that mixes the 2 Schedule 8s: the year’s withholding and refund take the one from 24 September 2025 for every pay, and the component before that date is shown beside it;
- work-related deductions you claim, which reduce the standard deduction dollar for dollar except those section 25-130(3) disregards, such as professional association fees;
- salary sacrifice of anything but super, reportable fringe benefits, excess concessional contributions or Division 293 tax;
- everything the income tax calculator leaves out, such as the Medicare levy reduction for families and the seniors and pensioners tax offset;
- a family’s surcharge: this calculator works out the surcharge for a single person with no dependants, and the income tax calculator works out a family’s;
- a study loan debt smaller than the repayment, which section 154-20(1)(c) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003 caps at what is still owed;
- whether an employee is entitled to super guarantee, the lower transitional rate for employees on Norfolk Island (11% for 2026-27), or a second employer’s separate maximum contribution base;
- actual PAYG withholding from the optional dated earnings. Those entries calculate super only. Without dates, super is estimated on the year’s earnings and rounded once. For example, $260,000 from 26 fortnightly $10,000 pays beginning 8 July 2025 gives $29,400 using the quarterly caps, compared with the $30,000 annual estimate.

## How the lump sum in arrears calculator works

Salary, wages or a Centrelink or compensation payment that you receive as a lump sum, for earlier years, is taxed in the year you receive it, often at a higher rate than it would have been in the years it was for. The tax offset gives back the difference. The calculator first tests whether the arrears are at least 10% of the rest of your normal taxable income for the year. If they are, it works out the tax the arrears add this year and the tax they would have added in the years they accrued in, and the offset is the amount by which the first exceeds the second.

For the 2 latest accrual years it adds the arrears to that year’s taxable income at that year’s resident rates. Arrears from any earlier years are taxed at the average of those 2 years’ rates, so the oldest years’ arrears can share one row. Where a recent year was itself a year you got this offset for, or arrears of such a year accrued in it, its income is adjusted as the Act directs.

The ATO’s page on lump sum payments in arrears works Yan’s breakdown of arrears by year, received in 2025-26. The calculator opens on it; the ATO does not give Yan’s taxable incomes, so the $100,000, $30,000 and $17,000 are fictional. Anthony’s, Sudeep’s and Heather’s examples are among the boundary cases.

## What the lump sum in arrears calculator does not cover

The calculator works the offset for a person resident in Australia for the whole of the year of receipt and of each recent accrual year, entitled to the full tax-free threshold, for a year of receipt of 2024-25, 2025-26 or 2026-27. It does not decide what is a lump sum in arrears: it takes the arrears you enter as eligible income that accrued more than 12 months before you received it. It refuses an employment termination payment, unused leave paid on termination, a super lump sum or income stream, above-average special professional income or primary producer averaging in the year of receipt or a recent accrual year, and does not cover a minor’s income or a trustee.

It does not work out the Medicare levy or surcharge on the arrears, which have their own rules, or apply the offset against your tax. The ATO works the offset when you lodge with the amounts for each year; confirm the figures with the ATO or a registered tax agent.

## How the sole trader tax set-aside calculator works

The calculator takes a year’s business income and expenses, any wages from a job and any tax agent fees, gifts or personal super contributions you claim. ## How the Division 293 tax calculator works

The Division 293 tax calculator works out the extra 15% tax on concessional super contributions for one income year when income and contributions together exceed $250,000. It adds up Division 293 income, the income measure used for the Medicare levy surcharge without reportable super contributions, then takes the contributions less any excess over the year’s cap, raised by unused cap carried forward when your total super balance allows it. Those are the low tax contributions. The taxable contributions are the lesser of the low tax contributions and the amount by which they and Division 293 income exceed $250,000, so only the part above the threshold is taxed, and never more than the contributions. Each step is shown, with the room left under the threshold when you are below it.

Excess concessional contributions are not taxed by Division 293: they are added to your assessable income and taxed at your marginal rate less a 15% offset instead. A one-off event, such as a capital gain, an employment termination payment or back pay, can take you over the threshold for a single year. The ATO assesses Division 293 tax on its own notice once it has your return and your fund’s contribution reports. It is payable 21 days after the notice, and you can elect within 60 days to have it released from super.

## What the Division 293 tax calculator does not compute

The calculator covers 2021-22 to 2026-27, for contributions to accumulation interests. It does not compute:

- defined benefit interests, whose defined benefit contributions the regulations work out and whose tax is deferred to a debt account (Subdivision 293-D), or the rules for State higher level office holders with constitutionally protected funds and for Commonwealth justices and judges (Subdivisions 293-E and 293-F);
- contributions the Commissioner has disregarded or reallocated to another year, which stay in low tax contributions, or which contributions count: enter them as your fund reports them;
- contributions that count differently for Division 293 than for the cap: a super guarantee amnesty amount the Commissioner paid to your fund, which is not a low tax contribution (section 293-30(4)(c)), and a contribution refunded under section 99G(6) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993, which is not a concessional contribution (section 291-25(2)(e));
- each part of Division 293 income, the unused cap or your total super balance, which you enter;
- 2027-28 and later years, whose cap is not yet known, or years before 2021-22;
- the refund for a former temporary resident who receives a departing Australia superannuation payment, interest, or the release from super itself.
