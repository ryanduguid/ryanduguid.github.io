## How the Division 293 tax calculator works

The Division 293 tax calculator works out the extra 15% tax on concessional super contributions for one income year when income and contributions together exceed $250,000. It adds up Division 293 income, the income measure used for the Medicare levy surcharge without reportable super contributions, then takes the contributions less any excess over the year’s cap, raised by unused cap carried forward when your total super balance allows it. Those are the low tax contributions. The taxable contributions are the lesser of the low tax contributions and the amount by which they and Division 293 income exceed $250,000, so only the part above the threshold is taxed, and never more than the contributions. Each step is shown, with the room left under the threshold when you are below it.

Excess concessional contributions are not taxed by Division 293: they are added to your assessable income and taxed at your marginal rate less a 15% offset instead. A one-off event, such as a capital gain, an employment termination payment or back pay, can take you over the threshold for a single year. The ATO assesses Division 293 tax on its own notice once it has your return and your fund’s contribution reports. It is payable 21 days after the notice, and you can elect within 60 days to have it released from super.

## What the Division 293 tax calculator does not compute

The calculator covers 2021-22 to 2026-27, for contributions to accumulation interests. It does not compute:

- defined benefit interests, whose defined benefit contributions the regulations work out and whose tax is deferred to a debt account (Subdivision 293-D), or the rules for State higher level office holders with constitutionally protected funds and for Commonwealth justices and judges (Subdivisions 293-E and 293-F);
- contributions the Commissioner has disregarded or reallocated to another year, which stay in low tax contributions, or which contributions count: enter them as your fund reports them;
- contributions that count differently for Division 293 than for the cap: a super guarantee amnesty amount the Commissioner paid to your fund, which is not a low tax contribution (section 293-30(4)(c)), and a contribution refunded under section 99G(6) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993, which is not a concessional contribution (section 291-25(2)(e));
- each part of Division 293 income, the unused cap or your total super balance, which you enter;
- 2027-28 and later years, whose cap is not yet known, or years before 2021-22;
- the refund for a former temporary resident who receives a departing Australia superannuation payment, interest, or the release from super itself.

Confirm the figures with your Division 293 notice from the ATO or a registered tax agent.

## How the income tests calculator works

The income tests calculator adds up the 6 income measures that the income tests section of an individual tax return feeds, from the same amounts. Each measure starts from taxable income without any assessable first home super saver released amount, then adds the parts its own definition names.

- Income for surcharge purposes, for the Medicare levy surcharge and the private health insurance rebate, adds family trust distribution amounts, fringe benefits, reportable super contributions and investment losses.
- Division 293 income, for the extra 15% tax when it and low tax contributions together exceed $250,000, is the same less reportable super contributions.
- Rebate income, for the seniors and pensioners tax offset, adds super contributions, investment losses and the adjusted fringe benefits total.
- Repayment income, for study and training loans, adds investment losses, fringe benefits, exempt foreign employment income and super contributions.
- Adjusted taxable income, for the dependant (invalid and invalid carer) tax offset, adds adjusted fringe benefits, target foreign income, investment losses, tax-free pensions and super contributions, less child support paid.
- Co-contribution total income starts from assessable income instead, adds fringe benefits and employer contributions and takes off business deductions.

The ATO’s page When you must repay your loan, last updated 13 October 2026 and read 19 November 2026, works Christina’s repayment income: taxable income of $50,420, a $1,250 net investment loss, $4,560 of reportable fringe benefits, $2,580 of exempt foreign employment income and $15,000 of reportable super contributions give $73,810. The calculator opens on her figures, with her loss taken as a financial investment loss, the benefits from an employer that pays FBT, the contributions from her employer, her target foreign income as the exempt income alone and assessable income equal to taxable income; those splits are fictional. Tom’s income for surcharge purposes of $117,000, from the ATO’s surcharge page, is among the boundary cases.

## What the income tests calculator does not cover

The calculator works the measures only. It does not decide whether an offset, the surcharge, a study loan repayment, Division 293 tax or a co-contribution applies, or work out their amounts; the income tax calculator works the surcharge and a study loan repayment. It covers a person resident in Australia for the whole of 2024-25, 2025-26 or 2026-27: a foreign resident pays no Medicare levy or surcharge and repays a study loan on worldwide income, and a part-year resident’s measures were not checked.

It does not add a spouse’s income, work out family tax benefit or child care subsidy income, which also count a partner’s, or the Commonwealth Seniors Health Card income test, which Services Australia applies. It takes each amount as you enter it: it does not decide what is a reportable fringe benefit, target foreign income or a tax-free pension, or work out excess concessional contributions. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s income tests calculator or a registered tax agent.

## How the super projection calculator works

The super projection calculator projects one accumulation super account from 2026-27 to the age you retire, a year at a time, and gives the balance then in future dollars and at its present value, in the dollars of today’s wages. Each year your employer contributes its rate of your salary, up to the maximum contribution base, and your own contributions are added. The fund takes 15% contributions tax from the employer and salary sacrifice contributions, takes out the dollar fee, the percentage fee and the premiums, and claims them as a deduction, which saves 15% of them. The return and the percentage fee are worked on the balance at the start of the year and on half the year’s contributions less the dollar fee and premiums, as they come in through the year. After the first year, your salary, contributions, fees and premiums grow at the wage growth rate.

The present value divides the balance at retirement once by 1 plus the present value rate (3.7% unless you change it) to the power of the years projected, rounded to the cent, so that it reads against today’s wages. The calculator is general information about super, not advice: it shows the effect of the figures you enter and does not recommend a fund, an investment option or a contribution.

## The assumptions and why they are reasonable

- Retirement at 67: the default ASIC’s Instrument 2022/603 sets for anyone younger. Super is preserved until 60, so an earlier age is projected to 60, as the Instrument takes the greater of the age you enter and your preservation age.
- Today’s dollars at 3.7% a year: the rate the Instrument sets, the long-term growth in wages the Treasury’s 2023 Intergenerational Report expects, so the result compares with what wages buy now.
- Wage growth of 3.7% a year for your salary, contributions, fees and premiums: the same long-term forecast, as ASIC’s guide expects contributions to rise with wages.
- A return of 6.1% a year after investment fees and tax on earnings, steady every year: ASIC’s assumption for a balanced investment option. Real returns vary and can be negative in some years; your fund’s option may have a different mix.
- Administration fees of $59 a year and 0.11% of the balance, and insurance premiums of $599 a year: ASIC’s assumptions from industry statistics. Your statement shows your own.
- Employer contributions at the 12% super guarantee on salary up to the $270,830 maximum contribution base for 2026-27, which is assumed to grow with wages.
- 15% contributions tax and the fund’s deduction for fees and premiums, as the law now stands; tax and super law are assumed not to change.
- Contributions within the 2026-27 caps, with the caps assumed to rise with wages after that.

## What the super projection calculator does not compute

The projection covers one accumulation account from 2026-27, with the rules of that year assumed to continue. It does not compute:

- defined benefit interests, or an account already paying a pension or a transition to retirement income stream;
- income in retirement, the age pension or how long the balance would last;
- the low income super tax offset, the government co-contribution or spouse contributions, which would add to the balance of someone on a lower income;
- Division 293 tax for income over $250,000, excess contributions, the nil non-concessional cap for a total super balance at the transfer balance cap, or tax on a withdrawal;
- years of good and bad returns, career breaks, or contribution, activity, switching and adviser fees, any of which can lower the balance.

Use your fund’s own retirement estimate or a financial adviser for a forecast you would act on.

## How the government super amounts calculator works

The calculator works 3 amounts from the same answers. Total income for the co-contribution is your assessable income, less any FHSS released amount, plus reportable fringe benefits and reportable employer super contributions (less any excess concessional contributions), less business deductions. Adjusted taxable income, which decides LISTO, is taxable income without any FHSS released amount, plus adjusted fringe benefits, target foreign income, investment losses, tax-free pensions and reportable super contributions, less child support paid, as the income tests calculator works it.

The super co-contribution is half your personal contributions, at most $500. Above the year’s lower income threshold the $500 falls by 3.333 cents for each dollar; at the higher threshold, $15,000 above it, none is payable. An amount under $20 is raised to $20. It needs total income below the higher threshold, at least 10% of total income before business deductions from employment or a business, a lodged return, non-concessional contributions within your cap, a total super balance below the general transfer balance cap, and an age under 71.

The year’s thresholds are $39,837 and $54,837 for 2020-21, $41,112 and $56,112 for 2021-22, $42,016 and $57,016 for 2022-23, $43,445 and $58,445 for 2023-24, $45,400 and $60,400 for 2024-25, $47,488 and $62,488 for 2025-26, and $49,293 and $64,293 for 2026-27.

LISTO is 15% of your concessional contributions, to the nearest cent, at most $500 and at least $10, where adjusted taxable income is $37,000 or less and the same 10% test is met. The ATO pays both amounts to your fund, rounded up to a multiple of 5 cents.

The spouse contribution tax offset is 18% of your contributions for your spouse, counting at most $3,000, which falls by a dollar for each dollar of the spouse’s income above $37,000. It is nil when the spouse’s income is $40,000 or more, the spouse’s non-concessional contributions exceed the spouse’s cap, or the spouse’s total super balance has reached the general transfer balance cap. The most it can be is $540.

## What the government super amounts calculator does not cover

The calculator refuses a person who held a temporary visa at any time in the year: the exceptions for New Zealand citizens and some prescribed visas are not worked out. It also refuses a person not resident in Australia for the whole year, a cap or balance answer of not known where that answer decides an amount, and nil total income where a contribution leaves the 10% test to decide.

It leaves out contributions your fund had not received by 30 June, LISTO the ATO estimates without a return, concessional contributions to constitutionally protected funds and other amounts LISTO disregards, a deceased person’s income, interest on late payments, and 2019-20 and earlier. It does not cover 2027-28, when the law changes LISTO and the co-contribution thresholds are not yet published. It takes each amount and answer as you enter them; confirm them with your fund, the ATO or a registered tax agent.

## Estimate the maximum from contributions

The calculator counts up to $15,000 of eligible contributions a year and $50,000 in total ($30,000 for determinations requested before 1 July 2022). It counts contributions earliest first, using non-concessional contributions first in a year that contains both kinds of personal contribution. It treats contributions on the same day as made at the same time and counts the non-concessional ones first. It adds 100% of the non-concessional part and 85% of the concessional part. It compounds daily at the ATO’s published shortfall interest charge rates, from the first day of the contribution month (1 July 2017 for 2017-18 contributions) through the determination date. Earnings round down to whole dollars per original contribution.

The verified rates support contribution ledger estimates for determinations through 31 December 2026.

## How the release is taxed

The assessable amount is the gross released less the non-concessional component, floored at zero. It counts in the income year you requested the release. The 30% tax offset is non-refundable. The marginal rate option here assumes the ordinary 2% Medicare levy and rounds withholding down to whole dollars. Any Commonwealth debt offset comes out after withholding.

If you do not know the withholding, calculate the assessable amount and offset first. The net release and minimum recontribution need the withholding amount.

The 20% FHSS tax and minimum recontribution figures apply conditionally under the First Home Super Saver Tax Act 2017 and Division 313. Check the contract, notification and recontribution deadlines with the ATO. For determinations made before 15 September 2024, see Treasury Laws Amendment (2023 Measures No. 3) Act 2023, Schedule 4, items 28 and 30. ## How the work expenses calculator works

For self-education, expenses count only when, at the time you incurred them, you were employed and the study kept up or improved the skills or knowledge for your current work, or was likely to increase your income from it. For 2021-22 and earlier, a prescribed course of education reduces category A by $250, less any category C, D and E expenses (section 82A). From 2022-23 there is no reduction. ## What the work expenses calculator does not cover

The calculator refuses an expense reimbursed or paid by an employer or anyone else, or covered by an allowance. It also refuses cents per kilometre expenses in category D beside log book car expenses in category A. It does not cover occupancy expenses such as rent, mortgage interest, rates and house insurance, or decide whether your home is a place of business. It also leaves out the substantiation rules and their exceptions, occupation-specific guidance, cars taken on hire or jointly owned, a new log book after an ATO notice or an added car, and the electric vehicle home charging rate. It does not work out decline in value, apportion an expense or work out the tax saved. Years before 2020-21 and after 2026-27 are not covered: no cents per kilometre rate had been determined for 2027-28 when the calculator was checked.

## How the bonus and back payment withholding calculator works

The bonus and back payment withholding calculator works the PAYG amount an employer withholds from a 2026-27 pay that includes a bonus, commission, back payment or similar payment, by the methods of the ATO’s Schedule 5, and shows each step by its number. Either method is acceptable, and Method B usually comes closer to the tax the payee will owe.

Method A spreads the payment over the year: it works out what the tax table withholds from this pay’s earnings, then from those earnings plus a 52nd, 26th or 12th of the payment, and withholds the difference times 52, 26 or 12, but no more than 47% of the payment. A commission or bonus for a defined period shorter than a year can be spread over the pays it relates to instead.

Method B(i) is for a back payment of earlier pays in the same financial year: each of those pays is worked again with its share of the back payment, and what was withheld from it then comes off. Method B(ii) is for back pay of an earlier financial year and for a bonus or other payment that does not relate to one pay: it works like Method A, but on the average of this year’s pays to date, and takes into account earlier payments this year worked the same way.

With a study or training loan, each tax table amount includes the Schedule 8 component, and the 47% limit applies to the two together. Under Method A the calculator also gives the tax and study loan repayment the payment adds to a year of this pay, so a payee can see whether the withholding will cover it.

## What the bonus and back payment withholding calculator does not compute

The calculator applies the 2026-27 schedules only, to a resident claiming the tax-free threshold or a foreign resident. It does not compute:

- payments made before 1 July 2026, or scales 1, 5 and 6: a resident not claiming the tax-free threshold, or one with a full or half Medicare levy exemption;
- a payee who has not quoted a tax file number, who has 47% withheld, or 45% for a foreign resident, or a working holiday maker, whose payments all fall under Schedule 15;
- a withholding variation, a withholding declaration’s offsets, or a Medicare levy variation;
- payments for a single pay period, which are added to that pay’s earnings, and unused leave or employment termination payments on termination, which are withheld under other schedules;
- back payments of earlier pays with different earnings or withholding each: Method B(i) here takes the same amounts for each, so work pays that differ one group at a time;
- the split of a back payment between this financial year and earlier ones, which you enter; lump sum E reporting; or the tax offset for a lump sum in arrears, which the ATO works out on assessment.

## How the salary packaging calculator works

The salary packaging calculator shows what packaging is worth to an employee of a public benevolent institution, health promotion charity, public or not-for-profit hospital or public ambulance service for one year. Your employer pays chosen expenses from your salary before tax. Those benefits are exempt from FBT up to the employer’s cap, measured on their grossed-up value: the taxable value times 1.8868 when the employer gets no GST credit, or 2.0802 when it does. So about $15,900 of Type 2 benefits fills a $30,000 cap and about $9,010 a $17,000 cap. Salary packaged meal entertainment and venue hire can take up to $5,000 more of grossed-up value before FBT applies.

The calculator works your tax with and without the packaging and shows the difference. It also gives the reportable fringe benefits amount your income statement will show. That amount is not taxed, but it is added back for the Medicare levy surcharge, study loan repayments and other income tests, so packaging can raise a surcharge or a repayment while it lowers your income tax. If the benefits pass the cap, the employer owes FBT on the excess and usually recovers it from your package; the calculator takes that off the saving.

## What the salary packaging calculator does not compute

The calculator covers the FBT years ending 31 March 2023 to 2027, for one employer and the salary as your only income. It does not compute:

- rebatable employers, such as other registered charities, whose FBT is reduced by a rebate of 47% up to a $30,000 cap, or ordinary employers, who pay full FBT on packaged benefits;
- benefits outside the cap, such as car parking and meal entertainment that is not salary packaged, exempt benefits such as portable electronic devices, or a novated car lease, which the novated lease calculator works;
- an ambulance service employee not predominantly involved in providing or supporting its ambulance services, whose benefits are not exempt (section 57A(3)(b));
- package fees, the cost of the benefits themselves, or whether a benefit is Type 1 or Type 2, which your employer decides;
- excluded fringe benefits, such as remote area housing, which count towards the cap but are not reportable: entered here, they would raise the reportable amount;
- salary sacrificed to super, other income, deductions, or a spouse and dependants for the Medicare levy surcharge;
- how the FBT year from 1 April to 31 March lines up with the income year from 1 July: the benefits are taken as packaged evenly in the income year.

## How the novated lease calculator works

The novated lease calculator compares a year of a novated car lease with paying the same car costs from your after-tax pay. Under the lease your employer pays the lease and running costs from your salary, claims back the GST and passes it on, so less of your salary is taxed.

The FBT exemption for electric cars decides how the costs are split. If you are a current employee and the car is a battery electric or hydrogen fuel cell car, was first held and used on or after 1 July 2022 and no luxury car tax had become payable on a supply or importation before any of the benefits counted, the whole cost comes from your pre-tax salary with no FBT. Its value under the statutory formula, 20% of the cost price for a full year, is still reported on your income statement, grossed up. That reportable amount is not taxed but counts for the Medicare levy surcharge and study loan repayments. A plug-in hybrid kept the exemption only under a lease in place and in use before 1 April 2025.

Without the exemption, the usual approach is the employee contribution method: you pay the car’s taxable value from your after-tax pay, which leaves no FBT, and the rest of the cost from your pre-tax salary. The calculator works both ways for an exempt car, so you can see what the exemption is worth, and says which condition fails when it does not apply.

## How the payroll tax calculator works

The payroll tax calculator estimates one employer’s payroll tax for the whole of 2026-27 in one state or territory. Each jurisdiction allows a deduction or tax-free threshold, which an employer that also pays wages elsewhere in Australia shares in proportion to its wages there. Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia and the Northern Territory reduce the deduction as Australian wages grow, until it reaches nil. The result shows the deduction, the amount taxed, the tax at each rate and, in Victoria and Queensland, each surcharge or levy on its own line, with the provision applied.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the tests hold them. Revenue NSW: $1,500,000 of NSW wages are taxed on $300,000, and $900,000 of $3,000,000 has a threshold of $360,000. The Victorian State Revenue Office: $80 million in Victoria and $70 million in New South Wales give surcharge thresholds of $5,333,333.33 and $53,333,333.33. The Queensland Revenue Office: $1,356,789 of $1,789,776 has a deduction of $932,460 to the dollar. RevenueWA: annual wages of $1,104,000 give $6,600; its other 2 examples round the deduction to the dollar and come out 1 and 3 cents lower than this calculator. RevenueSA: $800,000 of $1,600,000 has a deduction of $300,000 and $500,000 of $2,000,000 one of $150,000. The Tasmanian State Revenue Office: $2,400,000 gives $54,400, and $2,400,000 of $3,000,000 gives $72,800. The ACT Revenue Office and the Territory Revenue Office publish no worked figures that could be read, so the tests work examples from the Acts by hand: $84,375 on $3,000,000 in the Australian Capital Territory and $41,250 on $3,000,000 in the Northern Territory. This calculator opens on Revenue NSW’s first example.

## What the payroll tax calculator does not compute

The payroll tax calculator gives one annual estimate and no advice. It does not decide which payments are taxable wages or where they are taxable, and does not cover:

- an employer that is a member of a group, whose threshold and rate depend on the group’s wages and which members share the deduction;
- an employer that paid wages for only part of 2026-27, whose threshold every jurisdiction reduces by the days it employed;
- monthly or other periodic returns, and the annual reconciliation of amounts already paid;
- exemptions, rebates and discounts, such as Queensland’s apprentice and trainee rebate and its 1% discount for regional employers, claimed in the return, and the ACT’s 6.85% cap for an eligible university;
- any year other than 2026-27.

Confirm the figure with the revenue office or a registered tax agent before relying on it.
