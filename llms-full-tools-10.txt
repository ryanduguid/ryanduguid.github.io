## How the bonus and back payment withholding calculator works

The bonus and back payment withholding calculator works the PAYG amount an employer withholds from a 2026-27 pay that includes a bonus, commission, back payment or similar payment, by the methods of the ATO’s Schedule 5, and shows each step by its number. Either method is acceptable, and Method B usually comes closer to the tax the payee will owe.

Method A spreads the payment over the year: it works out what the tax table withholds from this pay’s earnings, then from those earnings plus a 52nd, 26th or 12th of the payment, and withholds the difference times 52, 26 or 12, but no more than 47% of the payment. A commission or bonus for a defined period shorter than a year can be spread over the pays it relates to instead.

Method B(i) is for a back payment of earlier pays in the same financial year: each of those pays is worked again with its share of the back payment, and what was withheld from it then comes off. Method B(ii) is for back pay of an earlier financial year and for a bonus or other payment that does not relate to one pay: it works like Method A, but on the average of this year’s pays to date, and takes into account earlier payments this year worked the same way.

With a study or training loan, each tax table amount includes the Schedule 8 component, and the 47% limit applies to the two together. Under Method A the calculator also gives the tax and study loan repayment the payment adds to a year of this pay, so a payee can see whether the withholding will cover it.

## What the bonus and back payment withholding calculator does not compute

The calculator applies the 2026-27 schedules only, to a resident claiming the tax-free threshold or a foreign resident. It does not compute:

- payments made before 1 July 2026, or scales 1, 5 and 6: a resident not claiming the tax-free threshold, or one with a full or half Medicare levy exemption;
- a payee who has not quoted a tax file number, who has 47% withheld, or 45% for a foreign resident, or a working holiday maker, whose payments all fall under Schedule 15;
- a withholding variation, a withholding declaration’s offsets, or a Medicare levy variation;
- payments for a single pay period, which are added to that pay’s earnings, and unused leave or employment termination payments on termination, which are withheld under other schedules;
- back payments of earlier pays with different earnings or withholding each: Method B(i) here takes the same amounts for each, so work pays that differ one group at a time;
- the split of a back payment between this financial year and earlier ones, which you enter; lump sum E reporting; or the tax offset for a lump sum in arrears, which the ATO works out on assessment.

## How the salary packaging calculator works

The salary packaging calculator shows what packaging is worth to an employee of a public benevolent institution, health promotion charity, public or not-for-profit hospital or public ambulance service for one year. Your employer pays chosen expenses from your salary before tax. Those benefits are exempt from FBT up to the employer’s cap, measured on their grossed-up value: the taxable value times 1.8868 when the employer gets no GST credit, or 2.0802 when it does. So about $15,900 of Type 2 benefits fills a $30,000 cap and about $9,010 a $17,000 cap. Salary packaged meal entertainment and venue hire can take up to $5,000 more of grossed-up value before FBT applies.

The calculator works your tax with and without the packaging and shows the difference. It also gives the reportable fringe benefits amount your income statement will show. That amount is not taxed, but it is added back for the Medicare levy surcharge, study loan repayments and other income tests, so packaging can raise a surcharge or a repayment while it lowers your income tax. If the benefits pass the cap, the employer owes FBT on the excess and usually recovers it from your package; the calculator takes that off the saving.

## What the salary packaging calculator does not compute

The calculator covers the FBT years ending 31 March 2023 to 2027, for one employer and the salary as your only income. It does not compute:

- rebatable employers, such as other registered charities, whose FBT is reduced by a rebate of 47% up to a $30,000 cap, or ordinary employers, who pay full FBT on packaged benefits;
- benefits outside the cap, such as car parking and meal entertainment that is not salary packaged, exempt benefits such as portable electronic devices, or a novated car lease, which the novated lease calculator works;
- an ambulance service employee not predominantly involved in providing or supporting its ambulance services, whose benefits are not exempt (section 57A(3)(b));
- package fees, the cost of the benefits themselves, or whether a benefit is Type 1 or Type 2, which your employer decides;
- excluded fringe benefits, such as remote area housing, which count towards the cap but are not reportable: entered here, they would raise the reportable amount;
- salary sacrificed to super, other income, deductions, or a spouse and dependants for the Medicare levy surcharge;
- how the FBT year from 1 April to 31 March lines up with the income year from 1 July: the benefits are taken as packaged evenly in the income year.

Confirm the figures with your employer’s salary packaging provider or a registered tax agent.

## How the novated lease calculator works

The novated lease calculator compares a year of a novated car lease with paying the same car costs from your after-tax pay. Under the lease your employer pays the lease and running costs from your salary, claims back the GST and passes it on, so less of your salary is taxed.

The FBT exemption for electric cars decides how the costs are split. If you are a current employee and the car is a battery electric or hydrogen fuel cell car, was first held and used on or after 1 July 2022 and had no luxury car tax payable at any sale or importation, the whole cost comes from your pre-tax salary with no FBT. Its value under the statutory formula, 20% of the cost price for a full year, is still reported on your income statement, grossed up. That reportable amount is not taxed but counts for the Medicare levy surcharge and study loan repayments. A plug-in hybrid kept the exemption only under a lease in place and in use before 1 April 2025.

Without the exemption, the usual approach is the employee contribution method: you pay the car’s taxable value from your after-tax pay, which leaves no FBT, and the rest of the cost from your pre-tax salary. The calculator works both ways for an exempt car, so you can see what the exemption is worth, and says which condition fails when it does not apply.

## What the novated lease calculator does not compute

The calculator covers the FBT years ending 31 March 2024 to 2027, for one car and one employer, with the salary as your only income. It does not compute:

- the lease payments themselves, the residual value, interest or fees, which come from your lease quote;
- the operating cost method, FBT left over when the costs are less than the taxable value, or an employer that does not claim GST credits;
- a vehicle designed to carry a tonne or more or 9 or more passengers, which is not a car for FBT and so not covered by the exemption;
- a car benefit for a former or future employee, which the exemption does not cover (section 8A(1)(a));
- whether luxury car tax became payable on another supply or an importation, which you answer (section 8A(1)(c)), or luxury car tax first payable part-way through the year, which leaves the benefits before it exempt;
- a car with a value at first sale different from its cost price, or a home charger;
- the end of the lease, when you pay the residual or refinance;
- salary sacrificed to super, other income, deductions, or a spouse and dependants for the Medicare levy surcharge.

Confirm the figures with your employer, your lease provider or a registered tax agent.

## How the payroll tax calculator works

The payroll tax calculator estimates one employer’s payroll tax for the whole of 2026-27 in one state or territory. Each jurisdiction allows a deduction or tax-free threshold, which an employer that also pays wages elsewhere in Australia shares in proportion to its wages there. Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia and the Northern Territory reduce the deduction as Australian wages grow, until it reaches nil. The result shows the deduction, the amount taxed, the tax at each rate and, in Victoria and Queensland, each surcharge or levy on its own line, with the provision applied.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the tests hold them. Revenue NSW: $1,500,000 of NSW wages are taxed on $300,000, and $900,000 of $3,000,000 has a threshold of $360,000. ## What the payroll tax calculator does not compute

- an employer that paid wages for only part of 2026-27, whose threshold every jurisdiction reduces by the days it employed;
- monthly or other periodic returns, and the annual reconciliation of amounts already paid;
- exemptions, rebates and discounts, such as Queensland’s apprentice and trainee rebate and its 1% discount for regional employers, claimed in the return, and the ACT’s 6.85% cap for an eligible university;

## How the SGC and Payday Super calculator works

From 1 July 2026, under Payday Super, an employer owes super guarantee for each payday, and the employee’s fund must receive it within 7 business days, or a longer period for a first contribution, out-of-cycle pay or an ATO determination. The calculator finds that due date from the site’s business days calendar, then works out the super guarantee charge for one employee if the fund received less than 12% of qualifying earnings by then: the final shortfall still unpaid at the assessment, notional earnings at the GIC rate on the amount that was late, and the administrative uplift, reduced if the ATO had not itself assessed or estimated the employer’s SG charge from 1 July 2026 to the payday, and for a voluntary disclosure. A contribution counts from the day the fund received it with what it needs to allocate it to the employee. For a quarter that ended by 30 June 2026 it applies the old law instead: the shortfall at that year’s charge percentage, 10% nominal interest and the $20 administration component.

## What the SGC and Payday Super calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employee and one payday or quarter, and no advice. It does not cover:

- the choice loading for not meeting choice of fund obligations;
- the late payment offset under the old section 23A for quarterly contributions made late;
- bunched contributions under section 18C(2) item 4;
- a contribution made from 1 to 28 July 2026 that item 186 of the transition applies first to a shortfall for the quarter ending 30 June 2026: count only what is left of it;
- a disclosed contribution with no receipt day, whose notional earnings section 36(3) works out as if the fund received it 7 business days after it was paid;
- exemption certificates, and amounts paid that have not reached the fund;
- GIC and penalties once the charge is payable;
- Norfolk Island salary or wages, of which item 2(2) of Schedule 2 to the Tax and Superannuation Laws Amendment (Norfolk Island Reforms) Act 2015 (Compilation No. 1, 1 July 2026) for quarters to 30 June 2026, and item 189 of Schedule 1 to the Payday Superannuation Act for paydays in 2026-27, count only part, for a transitional rate of 7% to 10% for 2022-23 to 2025-26 and 11% for 2026-27 (Table 21 of the ATO’s key superannuation rates), both Acts read 19 November 2026: the calculator refuses them rather than apply the general rate;
- paydays after 30 June 2027, whose maximum contributions base is not yet known, and quarters before July 2022.

## How the long service leave calculator works

Long service leave comes from each state and territory’s own Act, not the National Employment Standards. The calculator measures the service from the first day to the last, less the days you exclude, and applies the Act you choose. While employment continues, it gives the leave the employee can take now and when the next amount becomes available. When employment ends, it decides from the length of service and the reason whether the Act pays anything, works out the weeks it pays, deducts leave already taken or paid, and values the rest at the weekly ordinary pay. Each step names its provision. Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory give full leave after 7 years and the other states and territories after 10; New South Wales and the Australian Capital Territory can pay a pro rata amount from 5 years, the others from 7, and Victoria not before 7 years at all.

## What the long service leave calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate under one Act for one employee, and no legal advice. It does not cover:

- which Act applies to employment in more than one state or territory, and whether an award, enterprise agreement or contract gives a better entitlement, including the long service leave terms of a pre-2010 award or agreement that section 113 of the Fair Work Act 2009 preserves;
- portable schemes, such as building and construction, contract cleaning, community services and security, and the black coal mining industry, Commonwealth and state public servants, Queensland seasonal employees in the sugar industry and meat works and other seasonal employees, and Tasmanian mining employees, who have their own rules;
- whether a break keeps service continuous, which absences count, and whether a reason for leaving qualifies, such as a pressing necessity or serious misconduct: you decide those;
- averaging pay for varied hours, casual or part-time work, commission or piece rates;
- leave taken in advance of the entitlement, cashing out, and service before 23 June 1990, which some Acts count under earlier rules;
- tax withheld from the payment.

The figures follow each Act as read on 19 November 2026; a future date assumes it is unchanged. Confirm the result with the state or territory’s long service leave regulator or a lawyer before relying on it.

## How the fuel tax credit calculator works

The fuel tax credit calculator works out a business’s fuel tax credits for fuel acquired in one income year from 2022-23 to 2026-27. Each line takes the rate the ATO published for the day the fuel was acquired, for its fuel and its use: a heavy vehicle travelling on a public road (fuel for a bus’s or coach’s passenger air-conditioning included) takes the rate net of the road user charge, every other business use takes the full rate, and a light vehicle on a public road gets nothing. The page adds the lines and gives the whole dollar amount for label 7D on the business activity statement, with the rate, its period and its ATO page for each line.

A small claimant can also enter a line by its cost and the average pump price instead of its litres, split one heavy vehicle’s diesel between public roads and other use by the basic method, and take every line at the rate on the last day of a tax period that spans only an indexation change. Rates for every period since 1 July 2022 are held here, so an earlier period’s credits can be worked out for an amendment.

## What the fuel tax credit calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether the business is entitled to a credit: you state the facts that settle it. Nor does it decide who acquired the fuel in a hire chain, whether a road is a public road or travel on it is incidental, whether a vehicle meets the environmental criteria of section 41-25, or how fuel is apportioned between uses: you state those. It does not hold the auxiliary equipment percentages of PCG 2016/11, the ATO’s list of heavy vehicles used mainly off public roads, the consumption rates for articulated combinations, or rates for aviation fuel, packaging and supply, domestic heating or electricity generation. It does not work Division 44 adjustments, the attribution of credits to tax periods or the conversion of LPG kilograms to litres. It refuses fuel acquired before 1 July 2022, after 31 January 2027, or outside the income year chosen.

Fuel acquired after 19 November 2026, when the rates were read, carries a warning to check the ATO’s rates page for a change since. Confirm the result with the ATO’s fuel tax credit tools or a registered tax or BAS agent before relying on it.
