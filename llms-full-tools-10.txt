## How the fuel tax credit calculator works

The fuel tax credit calculator works out a business’s fuel tax credits for fuel acquired in one income year from 2022-23 to 2026-27. Each line takes the rate the ATO published for the day the fuel was acquired, for its fuel and its use: a heavy vehicle travelling on a public road (fuel for a bus’s or coach’s passenger air-conditioning included) takes the rate net of the road user charge, every other business use takes the full rate, and a light vehicle on a public road gets nothing. The page adds the lines and gives the whole dollar amount for label 7D on the business activity statement, with the rate, its period and its ATO page for each line.

A small claimant can also enter a line by its cost and the average pump price instead of its litres, split one heavy vehicle’s diesel between public roads and other use by the basic method, and take every line at the rate on the last day of a tax period that spans only an indexation change. Rates for every period since 1 July 2022 are held here, so an earlier period’s credits can be worked out for an amendment.

## What the fuel tax credit calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether the business is entitled to a credit: you state the facts that settle it. Nor does it decide who acquired the fuel in a hire chain, whether a road is a public road or travel on it is incidental, whether a vehicle meets the environmental criteria of section 41-25, or how fuel is apportioned between uses: you state those. It does not hold the auxiliary equipment percentages of PCG 2016/11, the ATO’s list of heavy vehicles used mainly off public roads, the consumption rates for articulated combinations, or rates for aviation fuel, packaging and supply, domestic heating or electricity generation. It does not work Division 44 adjustments, the attribution of credits to tax periods or the conversion of LPG kilograms to litres. It refuses fuel acquired before 1 July 2022, after 31 January 2027, or outside the income year chosen.

Fuel acquired after 19 November 2026, when the rates were read, carries a warning to check the ATO’s rates page for a change since. Confirm the result with the ATO’s fuel tax credit tools or a registered tax or BAS agent before relying on it.

## How the unused leave calculator works

When employment ends, unused annual leave, leave loading and long service leave are taxed by when the leave accrued and why the employment ended. Leave for employment before 18 August 1993, and all of it from 16 August 1978 on a genuine redundancy, early retirement scheme or invalidity, is capped at 30% and withheld at 32%; it is lump sum A. Only 5% of long service leave for the period before 16 August 1978 is assessable; the whole amount is lump sum B. The rest goes through Schedule 7’s marginal rate calculation with that 5%, spread over a year of pays and withheld at the employee’s Schedule 1 rate, and is reported as unused leave on termination.

## What the unused leave calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employee’s unused leave paid on termination from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, and no advice. It does not cover:

- splitting a payment between the periods by days of service under sections 83-95 to 83-115: enter each part;
- whether a termination is a genuine redundancy, an early retirement scheme or invalidity;
- unused leave paid after an employee’s death;
- a working holiday maker, whose payments Schedule 15 covers;
- an employee who has not claimed the tax-free threshold, or claims a Medicare levy variation or the seniors and pensioners offset (scales 1, 5 and 6), or has a withholding variation;
- a post-17 August 1993 payment under $300 with long service leave from before 16 August 1978, where Schedule 7 does not say how its limit applies;
- employment termination payments, which the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator covers.

Confirm the figures with Schedule 7 or a registered tax agent before relying on them.

## How the workers compensation premium calculator works

Every state and territory scheme bills a premium on the wages an employer estimates for the policy period and adjusts it to the wages actually paid. The calculator takes the wages, the rate your insurer gives your industry classification and the facts your scheme asks for, and applies the scheme’s published method as far as it goes for an employer whose premium does not yet turn on its own claims: icare’s small employer formula in New South Wales, the Premiums Order for a new or small employer in Victoria, WorkCover Queensland’s notice for a policy that starts in 2026-27, ReturnToWorkSA’s formula with its discount bands and claims cost cap in South Australia, and the insurer’s rate times wages in the other 4. It adds GST, and Queensland’s insurance duty, and, given the actual wages, works the premium again and shows the adjustment.

## What the workers compensation premium calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employer and one policy, and no advice. It does not cover:

## How the company tax and franking calculator works

The calculator applies the base rate entity test of section 23AA of the Income Tax Rates Act 1986 to this year’s figures: aggregated turnover under $50 million, and base rate entity passive income of no more than 80% of assessable income. A base rate entity pays 25% of its taxable income, and any other company 30% (subsection 23(2)).

The rate for franking a dividend comes from a second test. Section 995-1 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 runs the base rate entity test on the previous year’s turnover, passive income and assessable income, so a company can pay tax at 30% and frank at 25%, or the reverse; a company that did not exist in the previous year franks at 25%. The maximum franking credit is the dividend times 1 over the gross-up rate, which is 100% less that rate, over that rate (section 202-60): one third of the dividend at 25% and three sevenths at 30%. A credit above the maximum is taken to be the maximum (section 202-65).

The shareholder includes the dividend and its franking credit in assessable income and gets a tax offset equal to the credit (section 207-20). Tax at the marginal rate you enter, less the offset, is the extra tax the dividend brings; where the offset is larger, an individual gets the excess refunded (section 67-25).

The calculator reproduces the outcome of each ATO example: Happy Feet (3.8% passive income, 25%), Coffee and Cake (28.6%, 25%), Best Equity (100%, 30%), SoftDev (85%, 30%) and Hats 4 Cats ($170 million of aggregated turnover, 30%). For Dillmore Manufacture’s $100,000 dividend franked at 30% it gives $42,857.14, the exact credit; the ATO rounds the gross-up rate to 2.3333 and shows $42,857.75, which is more than the section 202-60 maximum.

## What the company tax and franking calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out aggregated turnover, assessable income or base rate entity passive income: enter figures you have established, with the turnover of connected entities and affiliates included, as the ATO’s Hats 4 Cats example shows. Taxable income gives the tax before offsets, PAYG instalments and other credits.

Not covered: the rates of RSA providers and pooled development funds (subsections 23(3) to (5)), life insurance companies (section 23A), not-for-profit companies and credit unions; corporate unit trusts and public trading trusts; and income years before 2021-22, when the base rate was 27.5% or 26%. A shareholder that is a company, trust, super fund or non-resident is outside its scope; a company shareholder cannot have excess franking credits refunded (section 67-25). Franking account balances, franking deficit tax, the benchmark rule and the holding period rule are not tested: the company tax and franking worksheets track the franking account and the benchmark rule. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.

## How the foreign buyer surcharge calculator works

The calculator applies the chosen state or territory’s own test of who is foreign to your answers, once for its duty surcharge and once for its land tax surcharge, as each revenue office states them. The duty surcharge is its rate times the dutiable value of the buyer’s share: 9% in New South Wales, 8% in Victoria, Queensland and Tasmania (1.5% on Tasmanian primary production land), and 7% in Western Australia and South Australia. The ACT and the Northern Territory charge none.

The land tax surcharge is for a full land tax year on the land value you enter: 5% in New South Wales, 4% in Victoria from $50,000 of taxable land ($25,000 for a trust), 3% of the value over $350,000 in Queensland, 2% in Tasmania and 0.75% in the ACT. Western Australia and South Australia charge none, and the Northern Territory has no land tax. Victoria charges a fixed trust’s surcharge on the land held for absentee beneficiaries only. The general transfer duty is worked on the whole property, for all buyers together, at the general rates.

The calculator reproduces the revenue offices’ examples: Revenue NSW’s $90,000 of surcharge purchaser duty and $39,187 of transfer duty on $1 million, and $60,000 of surcharge land tax on $1.2 million; Victoria’s $80,000 for Andrew, $16,000 for Emily’s $200,000 share and $20,000 for Clara’s half of a fixed trust’s $1 million; RevenueSA’s $42,000 and $21,000 for Peter; Western Australia’s $14,000 for Simon; Queensland’s $1,500 on $400,000; and Tasmania’s $8,000 on $400,000.

## Who is foreign in each state and territory

An individual who is an Australian citizen, holds a permanent visa or is a New Zealand citizen on a special category visa is not foreign for Queensland, Western Australian, South Australian and Tasmanian duty. New South Wales also requires a permanent resident or New Zealand citizen to have been in Australia 200 days of the previous 12 months (Revenue Ruling G009); Victoria gives a New Zealand citizen no exception unless they ordinarily reside in Australia for 6 consecutive months around settlement. Victorian and Queensland land tax charge absentees: non-citizens without a permanent visa who do not ordinarily reside in Australia and were away on the day or for over 6 months of the year.

A company is foreign when it is incorporated outside Australia, or foreign persons with associates hold 50% or more (more than 50% in Victoria) of its shares or votes, or in New South Wales, Queensland, Tasmania and Western Australia of its potential voting power. New South Wales instead asks whether one foreign person with associates holds 20% or more, or foreign persons together 40% or more, wherever the company is incorporated. Trusts and super funds follow the same shares of capital or members’ balances. Victorian land tax counts absentee interests instead, which you enter apart: a company pays absentee owner surcharge when absentees hold more than 50%, a fixed trust on the land held for absentee beneficiaries, and a complying super fund none. A foreign person who ordinarily lives in Australia is no absentee.

A discretionary trust is foreign in New South Wales if a foreign person could benefit, now or by amending the deed (section 104JA); in Victoria and Tasmania if foreign persons could be given more than 50%, or 50% or more, of the capital; in Queensland if foreign takers in default hold 50% or more; in Western Australia if a foreign person controls it, as a foreign trustee or appointor does, or foreign takers in default hold 50% or more; in South Australia if a trustee, appointor, named object or taker in default is foreign; and in the ACT only if a named foreign beneficiary could receive 50% or more. Victorian land tax looks only at named (specified) beneficiaries who are absentees.

## What the foreign buyer surcharge calculator leaves to you

The calculator does not decide exemptions, refunds or ex gratia relief, which each Commissioner grants on application. Among them are Australian-based developers and build to rent in New South Wales and Victoria, exempt permanent residents who live in the home in New South Wales, significant contributors and large developers in Queensland, and refunds when a buyer stops being foreign within 12 months in South Australia. When one is sought, it refuses. It also refuses a partner (provisional) or retirement visa holder, whom the revenue offices treat differently, and does not trace interests through other companies and trusts or decide who is an associate.

Not covered: the Commonwealth’s foreign investment application fees and its restrictions on foreign purchases of established homes from 1 April 2025, which RevenueSA notes; landholder and corporate trustee acquisitions; concessions on general duty; Queensland’s absentee land tax rates, which replace the general rates; Victorian trust surcharge rates; control of a Western Australian discretionary trust by a foreign person other than its trustee or appointor, such as one whose consent a distribution needs; control of a Victorian company through its board or through influence the Commissioner finds, rather than through shares or votes; a Victorian beneficiary nomination for trust land bought before 2006, under which the surcharge starts at $50,000 rather than $25,000; joint land tax assessments; property converted to residential use later; and a surcharge charged again when a buyer becomes foreign within 3 years. The transfer duty calculator and the land tax calculator work the general duty with first home concessions and the general land tax. ## How the business days calculator works

The calculator skips Saturdays, Sundays and the public holidays of the state or territory you choose, using each government’s published dates for 2026 and 2027. Between 2 dates, it counts the business days after the start date up to and including the end date, so a Friday to the next Monday is 1. Forward or back, it finds the date that is that many business days after or before the start, and lists each weekday holiday it skipped.

‘Any state or territory’ skips a day that is a public holiday throughout any one of them, as the site’s Payday Super business days table counts them, and leaves out Melbourne Cup Day and the WA King’s Birthday because neither applies throughout its state.

## What the business days calculator does not cover

Part-day holidays, such as the evenings of Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve in South Australia and the Northern Territory, count as business days. So do regional holidays, among them the Royal Queensland Show in Brisbane, Tasmania’s Royal Hobart Regatta and Recreation Day, and show days, and days off for one group only, such as Tasmania’s Easter Tuesday for the public service. A council or region’s local substitute for Melbourne Cup Day or the WA King’s Birthday is not applied: the state date is still skipped.

The calculator does not decide whether a law counts business days, clear days or calendar days, or which state’s holidays apply. Dates after 31 December 2027 are refused until the governments confirm the following year, and Victorian dates after 31 August 2027 until the AFL schedules its Grand Final. The calculator does not work out a superannuation guarantee or Payday Super due date.

## How the GST margin scheme calculator works

The GST margin scheme calculator first checks the conditions of section 75-5 of the GST Act that it can decide: what is sold, a written agreement with the buyer, and how you acquired the property. The sale must be a taxable sale (you are registered for GST, you sell in the course of your enterprise, and the sale is not GST-free or input taxed) of a freehold interest in land, a stratum unit or a lease of at least 50 years, and you must not have acquired it with GST on the whole price. The written agreement is made on or before the sale, or within a further period the Commissioner allows.

Under the margin scheme the GST is 1/11 of the margin, the GST-inclusive price less the consideration you paid for the land (subsection 75-10(2)), or less an approved valuation for property held since before 1 July 2000 (subsection 75-10(3)), as at 1 July 2000 or, if you registered after then, the date of effect of your registration or the earlier day you applied. A margin that is not more than nil gives no GST. Building and development costs are left out of the consideration (section 75-14); you may claim input tax credits for them where each purchase is a creditable acquisition (section 11-5). For one lot of a subdivision, only the lot’s share of the consideration counts (section 75-15), by any fair and reasonable measure such as area, number of lots or anticipated selling price (GSTR 2006/8, paragraphs 58 to 61).

From a GST-exclusive price the calculator adds GST of one tenth of the price less the consideration, which is 1/11 of the margin on the resulting price (GSTR 2006/8, paragraph 145). Beside the margin scheme it gives the GST without it, 1/11 of the GST-inclusive price, and the difference. Each GST rounds to the nearest cent, half a cent up (section 9-90). The buyer cannot claim an input tax credit on a margin scheme sale (section 75-20), so the scheme suits a buyer who could not claim one anyway.

The calculator reproduces GSTR 2006/8’s worked examples: $20,000 on Bob’s house and land (Example 3), $4,000 on Josine’s 800 square metre lot (Example 4), $13,636.36 on Adam’s Lot 1 (Example 6) and a $100,000 GST-inclusive price from Example 14’s $96,364, here $100,000.40 to the cent.

## What the GST margin scheme calculator does not cover

The GST margin scheme calculator refuses a sale to your associate, because section 75-13 takes the GST-inclusive market value as the price. It also refuses property inherited, acquired within a GST group or joint venture, as a GST-free going concern, farm land or government land, or from an associate: paragraphs 75-5(3)(b) to (g), item 2A of subsection 75-10(3) and section 75-11 set their own tests and margins. It refuses property acquired partly in a sale with GST on the whole price, which has an increasing adjustment (section 75-22).

Not covered: adjustments for bad debts and for consideration paid later (sections 75-25 and 75-27), the valuation day for government land under item 4 of subsection 75-10(3), and whether the sale is taxable in the first place, such as whether residential premises are new. The calculator takes your valuation as given and does not check that it meets the Commissioner’s requirements for an approved valuation (section 75-35). Have a registered tax agent confirm eligibility and the result before you settle.
