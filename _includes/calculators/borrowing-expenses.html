Sources applied

  • The deduction: section 25-25 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 at the ATO’s legal database, read 19 November 2026. Borrowing expenses are deductible to the extent the money is used to produce assessable income (subsections (1) to (3)). Each year’s maximum is the expenditure left over the days left in the period of the loan, times those days in the year (subsection (4)). The period is the shortest of the contract, the time to repayment and 5 years from the first borrowing (subsection (5)). At $100 or less the expenditure is deducted in the year it is incurred (subsection (6)). Every day is counted, both ends included, under the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 sections 2G(2) and 36(1): 5 years from 29 February end on 28 February.
  • The examples: the ATO’s Borrowing expenses (last updated 22 May 2026), read 19 November 2026: Peter’s $1,600 from 3 July gives $318.07 in the first year and $1.75 in the sixth, and Fiona and Max’s $1,670 at 170,000/209,000 rental use gives $259.62, $271.53, $271.53, $272.27, $271.53 and $11.90. This page opens on both and gives those figures, each maximum rounded to the cent and carried forward as the ATO’s examples are.
  • From 2027-28: section 26-155, read 19 November 2026, inserted by Act No 49 of 2026 for the 2027-28 income year and later years, quarantines residential rental deductions that exceed residential rental income after the other provisions have worked them out. This page works the year and flags it. A holiday home or other leisure facility is refused unless subsection 26-50(3) applies all year (section 26-50).
  • The calculation ports the borrowing-expenses-schedule command of the australian-accounting calculators, and its tests run the same cases through both.
  • The accounting column: AASB 9 Financial Instruments (compilation 6, 31 December 2025) and AASB 123 Borrowing Costs (compilation 2, 30 June 2021), read in the copies saved from the AASB’s Digital Standards Portal on 19 November 2026, which returned HTTP 503 when read again that evening and on 20 November 2026. The borrowing expenses reduce the liability at initial recognition (AASB 9 paragraph 5.1.1) and are amortised by the effective interest method over the expected life (Appendix A, paragraphs B5.4.1 to B5.4.4). A loan repaid early is extinguished and what is left is written off (paragraphs 3.3.1 to 3.3.3). Refinancing with the same lender is tested at 10% under paragraph B3.3.6, counting only the fees paid to that lender; at 10% or more the fees are part of the gain or loss on extinguishment (paragraph B3.3.6A). AASB 123 paragraphs 5, 6(a) and 8 capitalise borrowing costs on a qualifying asset. The page cites the paragraphs and quotes none of them.

How the borrowing expenses calculator works

The borrowing expenses calculator works out the deduction for costs of borrowing money, such as loan establishment fees, mortgage registration and lender’s mortgage insurance, under section 25-25 for up to 3 loans and every income year of each. Each year’s maximum is what is left of the expenditure over the days left in the period of the loan, times those days in the year, rounded to the cent. The deduction is that maximum times the year’s income use, which can change from year to year.

A loan repaid early ends its period that day, so that year deducts what is left. A loan that refinances an earlier one spreads its own expenses from its own first day. When everything incurred for borrowing in a year comes to $100 or less of income use, it is deducted that year instead.

The optional accounting column sets the same expenses against the loan under AASB 9: the effective interest rate that discounts the loan’s monthly payments to the amount borrowed less the expenses, the expenses recognised each year by that rate, the write-off when a loan is extinguished, the 10% test for a refinancing with the same lender, and the amortised cost at 30 June. Beside each year’s tax deduction it shows the difference, and what tax and accounting each still carry at 30 June.

What the borrowing expenses calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide which costs are borrowing expenses rather than interest or a capital cost of the property, or how much of the money is used to produce income: you state those. It refuses a loan first drawn before 1 July 2017, an income year after 2027-28, a holiday home or other leisure facility unless subsection 26-50(3) applies all year, and a refinancing whose dates do not meet. It works years after 2027-28 under section 25-25 as read and marks them projected. It does not apply section 26-155, interest deductions, redraws, Division 7A loans or debt forgiveness.

The accounting column takes every expense as integral to the effective interest rate, fixed monthly payments in arrears and the expected life you give. It does not work a modification under 10%, a variable rate repricing, fair value through profit or loss, AASB 112 deferred tax or the AASB 123 capitalisation period. Confirm the result with the ATO’s Borrowing expenses page or a registered tax agent before relying on it.
