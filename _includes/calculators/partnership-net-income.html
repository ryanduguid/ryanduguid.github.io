Sources applied

  • Net income and each partner’s share: sections 90, 91 and 92 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, read 27 November 2026, with sections 92(2), (2A), (2AA) and 92A, 94 and Division 5A for the cases refused.
  • Supplied by you: the partnership’s income and deductions, the deductions under section 290-150 or Division 36 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997, and each partner’s interest and residency.

How the partnership net income calculator works

A partnership lodges a return but pays no tax on its income (section 91 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936). The calculator ports the partnership-net-income command of australian-accounting. Its net income is its assessable income less its deductions, worked out as if it were a resident taxpayer, leaving out deductions under section 290-150 (personal super contributions) or Division 36 (tax losses) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997; where those deductions are larger, the excess is a partnership loss (section 90).

Each partner includes their individual interest in the net income in their own assessable income, or deducts their interest in the partnership loss, and takes their interest in exempt and non-assessable non-exempt income (section 92). Each share is rounded half up to the cent, and a cent left over goes to the largest share so the shares add up to the partnership’s figure.

What the partnership net income calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out the partnership’s assessable income or deductions, or the partners’ interests: you enter them as established. It refuses a partner resident for part of the year, a foreign resident partner unless all the partnership’s income is from Australian sources, salary or interest allocated to partners, a corporate limited partnership, venture capital and foreign hybrid partnerships, a partner without real and effective control of their share (section 94) and a segregated exempt or pension asset interest. It covers 2024-25 to 2026-27. The page takes 4 partners; its WebMCP tool takes up to 50. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.
