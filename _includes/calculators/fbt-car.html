Sources applied

  • Statutory formula: taxable value = base value × 20% × days used or available for private use ÷ days in the FBT year, less employee contributions, from section 9 of the Fringe Benefits Tax Assessment Act 1986 (Compilation No. 97, 1 July 2026), as the 5 steps in section 7.8 of ATO: Fringe benefits tax - a guide for employers, chapter 7 state them, both read 15 November 2026. Paragraph 9(2)(a) makes the cost price two-thirds once the FBT year starts later than the fourth anniversary of the day the car was first held; accessories fitted later are not reduced.
  • Operating cost method: taxable value = operating costs × (100% − business use percentage), less employee contributions, from subsection 10(2) of the Act. Under subsection 10(5), when the statutory formula gives a lower value, that value applies instead.
  • Rates: the Type 1 or Type 2 gross-up rate and the 47% FBT rate from this site’s FBT rate table. Each value rounds half up to the cent before contributions come off, never below nil, and the grossed-up value and the FBT each round half up from the figure before them; the Act prescribes no rounding.
  • Electric cars exemption: a car benefit is exempt if it is provided to a current employee, the car is a battery electric or hydrogen fuel cell electric vehicle when the benefit is provided, and no luxury car tax has become payable on a supply or importation of the car before the benefit is provided, from section 8A of the Act; the car must also have been first both held and used on or after 1 July 2022, from Schedule 1 item 7(2) of the Treasury Laws Amendment (Electric Car Discount) Act 2022, both read 19 November 2026. From 1 April 2025 a plug-in hybrid is exempt only under Schedule 2 item 5(2) of that Act: a commitment made before 1 April 2025 for a period that began before then and includes the benefit, no new commitment since for a period that includes the benefit, and an exempt car benefit with the car before that day. The ATO’s FBT on plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (last updated 14 March 2025), read 19 November 2026, lists what makes a new commitment.
  • Luxury car tax at the first retail sale: payable when the car’s luxury car tax value, its price with GST but without luxury car tax, is above the threshold for fuel-efficient vehicles for the financial year of the sale, from the ATO’s Luxury car tax rate and thresholds (last updated 1 June 2026) for each year from 2012-13 to 2026-27, and its Electric cars exemption page (last updated 1 April 2026), both read 19 November 2026. A car benefit needs the car held and used or available for use, so a first retail sale on or before the day the car was first held and used, or before 1 April 2026, comes before every benefit in the FBT year; one after 31 March 2027 comes after every benefit in it, so its luxury car tax cannot fail section 8A and no threshold applies.
  • An exempt car’s taxable value, grossed-up value and FBT are nil. Its value before the exemption still counts in the employee’s individual fringe benefits amount, from subsection 135P(3) of the Act, unless it is an excluded fringe benefit: the ATO’s Electric vehicles and fringe benefits tax fact sheet (published 24 February 2023), read 19 November 2026, excludes a pooled or shared car; and car expense payment, property and residual benefits for the car in the same period, such as its fuel, electricity, registration and insurance, are exempt under subsection 53(1).

How the FBT car calculator works

The FBT car calculator values one car’s fringe benefits for the FBT year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027, which has 365 days. Under the statutory formula, the taxable value is the car’s base value times 20%, times the days it was used or available for an employee’s private use, divided by 365, less what the employee contributed. The base value is the cost price plus non-business accessories fitted later; if the employer or an associate first held the car before 1 April 2022, only two-thirds of the cost price counts.

Under the operating cost method, the taxable value is the year’s operating costs times the private use share, 100% less the business use percentage, less contributions. The method applies only if the employer elected it and kept log book and odometer records; without them the business use percentage is nil. If the statutory formula gives a lower value, the calculator uses that value, as subsection 10(5) of the Act requires.

The taxable value is then grossed up: at the Type 1 rate, 2.0802, if the employer, or another provider, is entitled to a GST credit for providing the car, or the Type 2 rate, 1.8868, if not, as section 2.3 of the guide’s chapter 2 sets out. FBT is 47% of the grossed-up value. The FBT return for the year ending 31 March 2026, read 15 November 2026, asks for whole dollars at each taxable value and grossed-up amount and works the FBT from the total of all benefits, so a return’s figures can differ from these by the cents.

Example 28 in section 7.8 of the ATO’s guide for employers takes a $60,000 car available for 182 of 365 days, with $1,000 of fuel paid by the employee. It shows $5,983 at step 4, leaving out the cents of $5,983.56, and a taxable value of $4,983; this calculator gives $4,983.56. Example 31 in section 7.9 takes $19,580 of operating costs, 75% business use and a $2,000 contribution, and gives $2,895; this calculator gives $2,895.00 when the statutory formula value is higher.

How the FBT car calculator checks the electric cars exemption

For a battery electric, hydrogen fuel cell electric or plug-in hybrid car, the calculator checks the electric cars exemption in section 8A, one condition to a row. The car must be provided to a current employee, first both held and used by anyone on or after 1 July 2022, and have had no luxury car tax payable on its first retail sale or on any other sale or import before the benefit. A first retail sale after 31 March 2027, such as a dealer’s demonstrator sold later, comes after every benefit in the year, so its luxury car tax does not count. A first retail sale within the year after the car was first held and used may come between the year’s benefits, so the calculator refuses one on which luxury car tax was payable: that tax does not affect a benefit provided before the sale. For the first retail sale, the calculator compares the luxury car tax value, the price with GST and any accessories sold with the car but without luxury car tax, with the fuel-efficient threshold for the financial year of the sale: $91,661 for 2026-27, and $91,387 for 2024-25 and 2025-26. A plug-in hybrid also needs a commitment made before 1 April 2025 for a period that began before then and includes the benefit, no new commitment since for a period that includes the benefit, such as an optional extension, a break in the novation, changed lease payments or residual value, or a new employer, and an exempt car benefit before that day. When every condition is met, the taxable value and FBT are nil, and the result shows the value before the exemption, which still counts towards the employee’s reportable fringe benefits amount unless the car is a pooled or shared car.

The ATO’s Electric cars exemption page gives 3 examples, each a boundary case below: Shelly’s car, first used on 1 April 2022, is not exempt; John’s, held from 15 June 2022 but first used on 5 July 2022, is exempt; GHI Co’s $85,000 car was above 2022-23’s $84,916 threshold when first sold, so it is not. Its plug-in hybrid page’s Simon, whose lease runs from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2027, stays exempt; Tony’s break in his novation from 1 May 2025 is a new commitment, so his car is not.

What the FBT car calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a vehicle is a car, whether a benefit arises, or whether the work vehicle exemption or a reduction applies. For the electric cars exemption it does not decide the kind of car, its dates, its luxury car tax value or a plug-in hybrid’s commitments, which you state. It takes the fuel-efficient threshold, so it does not cover a car that was not fuel-efficient under the Luxury Car Tax Act when first sold, or a car never sold at retail in Australia, such as one its user imported, and it reads a value above the threshold as luxury car tax payable, so a supply exempt from that tax is not covered either. The changes announced on 5 May 2026 for electric cars from 1 April 2027 were not law when read on 19 November 2026; they are listed under announced, not yet law. It does not work out operating costs: add deemed depreciation, at 25% of the car’s depreciated value for a car acquired on or after 10 May 2006, and deemed interest at the statutory interest rate on the ATO’s FBT rates and thresholds page, both apportioned to the days held. It values one car for one FBT year. It does not work out a leased car value, apply the older statutory percentages kept for commitments made before 10 May 2011, or work out the reportable fringe benefits amount or FBT for other benefits. On 15 November 2026 chapter 7 of the ATO’s guide said it was being reviewed after the Full Federal Court’s decision of 27 March 2026 in SEPL Pty Ltd as trustee of the SFT Trust v Commissioner of Taxation [2026] FCAFC 36, on whether the directors of a discretionary trust’s corporate trustee were employees and whether their cars were provided in respect of employment, a question this calculator leaves to you.
