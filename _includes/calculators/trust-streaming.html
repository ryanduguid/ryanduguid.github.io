How the trust streaming calculator works

The calculator shares a resident discretionary trust’s net income for one income year between up to 6 beneficiaries and the trustee. A beneficiary specifically entitled to part of a capital gain or franked distribution is treated as entitled to the gain or distribution times the benefit they receive over the net benefit referable to it. The part nobody is specifically entitled to is shared by adjusted Division 6 percentage: present entitlement less the amounts specifically entitled to, over trust income less all such amounts, as the ATO’s examples work it. A share of the capital gain over the gain, times the gain remaining after losses and concessions, is the attributable gain. The beneficiary has an extra capital gain of twice that for a gain the trust discounted or reduced by the small business 50% reduction, or 4 times for both, and then applies their own discount. A share of the franked distributions gives the same share of the distributions less directly relevant deductions, and of the franking credits. Where net income without franking credits is less than the net capital gain and net franked distributions together, both are reduced rateably. Division 6E takes the capital gain, franked distributions and franking credits out of income and net income, and each beneficiary is assessed on the rest of the net income in proportion to their present entitlement to the rest of the income. The trustee is assessed under section 99A at 45% on the part no beneficiary is presently entitled to, with its share of a discounted gain grossed up, and gets its share of the franking credits as a tax offset. Each amount is worked exactly and rounded to the cent once.

What the trust streaming calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a beneficiary is presently or specifically entitled, whether the deed allows streaming, or whether a resolution was made in time: you enter the entitlements as established. It takes one capital gain and treats the franked distributions as one class (section 207-59); a trust with several gains needs each worked separately. It assumes every beneficiary is a resident not under a legal disability and assessed under section 97, so it leaves out section 98 assessments for minors, beneficiaries under a legal disability and non-residents, Division 6AA, and the trustee’s choice to be assessed on a capital gain (section 115-230). It does not apply section 99 to deceased estates or other section 99A(2) trusts, and does not cover non-resident trusts, managed investment trusts, AMITs, public trading trusts or trust losses. It does not work out the capital gain, losses, discount, trust income or net income, the 45-day holding rule or other franking integrity rules, the Medicare levy, or any beneficiary’s own tax. It gives no advice on section 100A reimbursement agreements, family trust elections or family trust distribution tax, or distributions to a bucket company and Division 7A: those are out of scope. Results are planning estimates: check them against the trust deed, the resolutions and the ATO’s guidance.
