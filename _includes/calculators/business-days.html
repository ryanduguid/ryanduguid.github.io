How the business days calculator works

The calculator skips Saturdays, Sundays and the public holidays of the state or territory you choose, using the dates confirmed for 2026 and 2027. Between 2 dates, it counts the business days after the start date up to and including the end date, so a Friday to the next Monday is 1. Forward or back, it finds the date that is that many business days after or before the start, and lists each weekday holiday it skipped.

‘Any state or territory’ skips full-day public holidays that apply throughout at least one state or territory. It counts Melbourne Cup Day and the WA King’s Birthday as business days because regions can observe them on different dates.

What the business days calculator does not cover

Part-day holidays, such as the evenings of Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve in South Australia and the Northern Territory, count as business days. So do regional holidays, among them the Royal Queensland Show in Brisbane, Tasmania’s Royal Hobart Regatta and Recreation Day, and show days, and days off for one group only, such as Tasmania’s Easter Tuesday for the public service. A council or region’s local substitute for Melbourne Cup Day or the WA King’s Birthday is not applied: the state date is still skipped.

The calculator covers dates through 31 December 2027. Check the applicable law for which days and which place count towards a deadline. The separate SGC and Payday Super calculator works out those due dates within its own coverage period.
