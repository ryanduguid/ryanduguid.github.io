How the income tax calculator works

The income tax calculator works out one individual’s tax for a full income year: the tax on taxable income at the year’s rates, the low income tax offset, and the Medicare levy. An Australian resident pays nothing on the first $18,200 and then each part of taxable income at its rate. A foreign resident pays from the first dollar at 30%, 37% and 45%. A working holiday maker pays 15% up to $45,000 and then the same rates as a resident.

The low income tax offset of up to $700 comes off a resident’s tax, but not below nil. The Medicare levy is 2% of a resident’s taxable income; below the low-income threshold there is none, and from there to the phase-in limit it is 10% of the income over the threshold, if that is less. The ATO’s example on Medicare levy reduction for low-income earners, read 17 November 2026, gives Angie, single with $29,000 of taxable income in 2025-26, a levy of $98.90, as this calculator does; its example on What is the Medicare levy gives Matthew $1,500 on $75,000.

For 2026-27 the Medicare levy thresholds are those the Act holds today, the same as 2025-26. If Parliament raises them for 2026-27, a person just above $28,011 will pay less than shown here.

What the income tax calculator does not compute

The calculator gives the tax of one person who was a resident, a foreign resident or a working holiday maker for the whole year. It does not compute:

  • the Medicare levy reduction for families, which depends on family taxable income and dependants, or a Medicare levy exemption;
  • the seniors and pensioners tax offset and its higher Medicare levy thresholds, or any other offset, such as for private health insurance, dependants or super contributions;
  • the Medicare levy surcharge or the private health insurance rebate;
  • a part-year resident’s reduced tax-free threshold, a working holiday maker who is an Australian resident, or one with other income besides working holiday taxable income;
  • tax on superannuation or employment termination remainders, income averaging, or a minor’s unearned income;
  • study loan repayments, which the study and training loan repayment calculator works out, and the tax already withheld or a refund.

Confirm the figures with the ATO’s Simple tax calculator or a registered tax agent.
