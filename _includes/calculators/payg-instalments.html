How the PAYG instalments calculator works

The PAYG instalments calculator works out one quarterly pay as you go instalment by the method on the activity statement. Under option 1 the instalment is the amount the ATO notifies at T7. Varying it, the first instalment of the income year is 25% of the estimated tax for the year, the second 50%, the third 75% and the fourth 100%, each less the earlier instalments. The third and fourth add back any credits you were entitled to claim for earlier instalments, claimed or not; no credit can arise before the second, so the calculator refuses one entered for the first or second. A negative result is a credit to claim at 5B.

Under option 2 the instalment is the quarter’s instalment income at T1 times the instalment rate at T2. A varied rate is the estimated tax for the year divided by the estimated instalment income, times 100. If the varied rate is lower than the rate used for the previous quarter, the earlier instalments less any credits claimed, less the earlier instalment income times the varied rate, is a credit for 5B. That comparison is with last quarter’s rate, not a new rate the ATO gives at T2. No step is rounded: only the instalment and the credit drop their cents, so 25% of an estimate of $399.99 is $99.9975, an instalment of $99.

For 2026-27, the instalment amount the ATO notifies already includes its GDP adjustment of 5%, which raises the base year’s income to allow for growth; the adjustment does not change an instalment rate. The ATO’s own example: instalment income of $20,100 at a rate of 1.7% gives $341.70, entered as 341 at T11 and 5A.

What the PAYG instalments calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out instalment income, the tax on it or whether you can choose between the 2 options. It does not cover monthly instalments, the 2 instalments a year some payers make, annual instalments or consolidated groups, and it does not estimate general interest charge on a variation that proves too low. It does not check whether you may claim a credit or when an instalment is due. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s PAYG instalments calculator or a registered tax agent.
