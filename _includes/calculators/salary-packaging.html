How the salary packaging calculator works

The salary packaging calculator shows what packaging is worth to an employee of a public benevolent institution, health promotion charity, public or not-for-profit hospital or public ambulance service for one year. Your employer pays chosen expenses from your salary before tax. Those benefits are exempt from FBT up to the employer’s cap, measured on their grossed-up value: the taxable value times 1.8868 when the employer gets no GST credit, or 2.0802 when it does. So about $15,900 of Type 2 benefits fills a $30,000 cap and about $9,010 a $17,000 cap. Salary packaged meal entertainment and venue hire can take up to $5,000 more of grossed-up value before FBT applies.

The calculator works your tax with and without the packaging and shows the difference. It also gives the reportable fringe benefits amount your income statement will show. That amount is not taxed, but it is added back for the Medicare levy surcharge, study loan repayments and other income tests, so packaging can raise a surcharge or a repayment while it lowers your income tax. If the benefits pass the cap, the employer owes FBT on the excess and usually recovers it from your package; the calculator takes that off the saving.

What the salary packaging calculator does not compute

The calculator covers the FBT years ending 31 March 2023 to 2027, for one employer and the salary as your only income. It does not compute:

  • rebatable employers, such as other registered charities, whose FBT is reduced by a rebate of 47% up to a $30,000 cap, or ordinary employers, who pay full FBT on packaged benefits;
  • benefits outside the cap, such as car parking and meal entertainment that is not salary packaged, exempt benefits such as portable electronic devices, or a novated car lease, which the novated lease calculator works;
  • an ambulance service employee not predominantly involved in providing or supporting its ambulance services, whose benefits are not exempt (section 57A(3)(b));
  • package fees, the cost of the benefits themselves, or whether a benefit is Type 1 or Type 2, which your employer decides;
  • excluded fringe benefits, such as remote area housing, which count towards the cap but are not reportable: entered here, they would raise the reportable amount;
  • salary sacrificed to super, other income, deductions, or a spouse and dependants for the Medicare levy surcharge;
  • how the FBT year from 1 April to 31 March lines up with the income year from 1 July: the benefits are taken as packaged evenly in the income year.

Confirm the figures with your employer’s salary packaging provider or a registered tax agent.
