How the endorsement review works

An entity the ATO has endorsed as a deductible gift recipient (DGR), or as an income tax exempt charity, must tell the ATO in writing when it stops being entitled. The ATO publishes 3 worksheets for the yearly self-review it recommends, and for a review after a change to the entity’s rules, structure or activities. Choose the worksheet that matches the ATO notice of endorsement, answer each question and the page gives the worksheet’s outcome.

Every ‘Yes’ means the entity continues to be entitled on that point. A ‘No’ to a condition of entitlement means the entity is not entitled: the page says what notice the law requires, under section 426-45 of Schedule 1 to the Taxation Administration Act 1953, and from when. A cancelled ABN ends the endorsement without notice from the entity. A ‘No’ on receipts does not end the endorsement but needs action at once, as the Commissioner may revoke it for a contravention. A missing winding up clause is a condition of entitlement under section 30-125(1)(c), so it gives ‘not entitled’ as well as the amendment the worksheet asks for.

Keep the completed review with the entity’s records once its board, committee or trustee approves it: the ATO asks that it not be sent in.

DGR status for a donor

This page is the entity’s own review. A donor checking whether a recipient is shown as a DGR should search the ABN Lookup deductible gift recipient listing, which shows the DGR item and the date the status started. A DGR named in the income tax law need not be endorsed, and the listing’s absence does not by itself decide whether a gift is deductible.

What the endorsement review does not cover

The review asks only what the worksheets ask. It does not test the special conditions of the DGR category’s table item, the trust fund or community charity guidelines, or for a charity that has not yet started its activities, the reasonable grounds test of section 50-110(5)(b): the result lists these as not asked. It does not decide whether the entity is a charity, which category it falls in or whether a gift to it is deductible. It does not look up the entity on any register, does not apply for, change or revoke an endorsement and does not lodge anything with the ATO or the ACNC. It does not cover a DGR named in the law rather than endorsed, an entity endorsed for other income tax exemptions, or fringe benefits tax concessions.
