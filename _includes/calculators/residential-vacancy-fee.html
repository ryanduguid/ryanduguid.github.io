One foreign owner’s dwelling for one vacancy year

A vacancy year runs 12 months from the day the owner could first occupy the dwelling, or its anniversary. The dwelling and its notice date are not used for a fee waived in full in a vacancy year starting before 9 April 2024.

How the residential vacancy fee calculator works

Under Part 6A of the Foreign Acquisitions and Takeovers Act 1975, a foreign owner of a residential dwelling lodges a vacancy fee return each vacancy year. The owner pays an annual vacancy fee if the dwelling was residentially occupied, or genuinely available to rent, for fewer than 183 days. The calculator ports the residential-vacancy-fee command of australian-accounting. The fee equals the foreign investment application fee for the dwelling, worked from the fee schedule in force on the notice date or entered as paid, and is doubled for a vacancy year starting on or after 9 April 2024. A late return makes the owner liable whatever the days, and an exemption under section 43D of the Foreign Acquisitions and Takeovers Regulation 2015 removes the fee. It also gives the return’s due date and how long to keep records.

What the residential vacancy fee calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether you are a foreign person, when the vacancy year starts, which days count as occupied or whether an exemption applies: you enter those. It does not cover build-to-rent developments, partial fee waivers, a waiver or remission under section 115H, the fee schedule before 2021 (enter the application fee paid instead), a fee above the statutory cap floor, or state and territory vacancy taxes and surcharges. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.
