Sources applied

  • The ABN test: ABN Lookup’s Format of the ABN (version 9.9.7), read 27 November 2026: subtract 1 from the first digit, weight the 11 digits 10, 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17 and 19, and the number passes when the sum divides by 89. A first digit of 0 cannot have 1 taken from it, so it fails.
  • The ACN test: Appendix D, Australian Company Number (ACN) Check Digit, of ASIC’s EDGE Document Messages Specification (version 5.10, 1 December 2024), read 27 November 2026: weight digits 1 to 8 by 8 down to 1; the check digit is 10 less the remainder of the sum divided by 10, or 0 when that is 10.
  • Supplied by you: the number and which identifier it is. Only ASCII digits and spaces are accepted; anything else is reported as not in the format rather than removed.

How the ABN and ACN check works

An Australian Business Number has 11 digits: a 9 digit identifier with 2 leading check digits worked out by a modulus 89 calculation. An Australian Company Number has 9 digits, the last a check digit from a modified modulus 10 calculation. The page applies the test each register publishes to the digits you enter and reports one of 3 results: the digits pass, they fail, or they are not an ABN or ACN in form (the wrong number of digits, or a character other than a digit or a space).

A failed check digit means the number is not valid as written, usually a typing or transcription error: check it against the invoice, letter or record it came from. Spaces are ignored; other separators such as hyphens are not, so that a malformed number cannot become a different, valid one.

What a valid check digit does not show

The check is arithmetic only. It does not show that a number was ever issued, that it is active, that it belongs to the business named on an invoice, or that the business is registered for GST. Many numbers that were never issued still pass. For an ABN’s status, the entity’s name and its GST registration, search ABN Lookup; for a company, search ASIC’s registers.

What the ABN and ACN check does not cover

The page does not call ABN Lookup, ASIC or any other register, and sends nothing off the page. It does not check a tax file number, an ARBN, an ARSN or a withholding payer number, does not work out the ABN a company would have from its ACN, and does not decide whether to withhold from a payment to a supplier who quotes no ABN.
