Periods in Australia

Enter each stay in Australia from the day you arrived to the day you left; both days count. Leave the arrival blank if you were already here on 1 July, the departure blank if you were still here on 30 June, and both blank to skip a row. Overlapping periods count each day once.

The test is not met, however many days, only when both are true: your usual place of abode is outside Australia and you do not intend to take up residence here. These are your answers; the calculator does not test them.

Sources applied

  • The test: Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 s 6(1), ‘resident or resident of Australia’ paragraph (a)(ii), compilation 193 (1 October 2026), read 26 November 2026, the source the australian-accounting residency-tests command cites.
  • The count: TR 2023/1 paragraphs 83 to 87 and footnote 51, read 26 November 2026: days in Australia are added together whether continuous or not, part of a day counts as a whole day, the year runs from 1 July to 30 June, and 184 days are needed in a year with 29 February.
  • The calculator agrees with the command on 667 test cases, refusals included.
  • Supplied by you: the year, the days you arrived and left, and your answers on your usual place of abode and intention.

How the 183-day residency day count works

Under paragraph (a)(ii) of the definition of ‘resident’ in s 6(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, you are a resident if you have been in Australia, continuously or intermittently, for more than one-half of the income year. The calculator adds up the distinct days you were in Australia between 1 July and 30 June, counting the day you arrived and the day you left as whole days. More than one-half is 183 days, or 184 in a year that includes 29 February.

The test has one exception, which needs both of 2 facts: your usual place of abode is outside Australia, and you do not intend to take up residence in Australia. If both are true, you are not a resident under this test however many days you spent here. TR 2023/1 says a person on an extended holiday is the usual case.

What the 183-day residency day count does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether you are an Australian resident. The 183-day test is one of 4. You are also a resident if you reside in Australia according to ordinary concepts, if your domicile is in Australia and your permanent place of abode is not outside Australia, or if you are a member of a Commonwealth superannuation scheme the definition names (the PSS or CSS), or the spouse or child under 16 of one. Those tests turn on facts and judgements this page does not ask for; read TR 2023/1 for each. Nor does it test your usual place of abode or intention, apply a tax treaty’s tie-breaker rules for a person resident in 2 countries, or work out part-year residency or the tax that follows. Confirm your residency with the ATO or a registered tax agent.
