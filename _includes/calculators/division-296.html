Sources applied

  • Liability, thresholds and shares: sections 296-15 to 296-50 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (compilation 268, 1 October 2026), read 17 November 2026. The large superannuation balance threshold is $3,000,000 and the very large one $10,000,000 for 2026-27 (sections 296-30 and 296-35). Each share is the balance less the threshold, over the balance, times 100, rounded to 2 decimal places with a third decimal place of 5 or more rounding up (subsections 296-40(2) and (3) and 296-45(2) and (3)). Taxable superannuation earnings are the earnings times the first share, when the balance is greater than $3,000,000 and the earnings greater than nil (subsection 296-40(1)); the very large component is the earnings times the second share (subsection 296-45(1)).
  • 2026-27 only: section 296-1 of the Income Tax (Transitional Provisions) Act 1997 (compilation 104, 1 October 2026), read 17 November 2026, uses only the balance at the end of the year, not the balance just before its start, and excuses a person who dies on or before 30 June 2027. Section 296-50 takes the balance to be nil after death, and section 296-260 leaves LRBA amounts out of the balance.
  • Rates: 15% of taxable superannuation earnings plus 10% of the very large component, from section 5 of the Superannuation (Building a Stronger and Fairer Super System) Imposition Act 2026, read 17 November 2026. The same thresholds, rates and exceptions are on ATO: Division 296 tax on large super balances (last updated 3 November 2026), read 17 November 2026. Each amount stays exact until it rounds half up to the cent; the Act sets no rounding for the amounts.

How the Division 296 tax calculator works

The Division 296 tax calculator works out one person’s Division 296 tax for 2026-27, the first year it applies. Tax arises when the total superannuation balance at 30 June 2027 is greater than $3,000,000 and the year’s total superannuation earnings are greater than nil. The share of the balance above $3,000,000 times the earnings gives taxable superannuation earnings, taxed at 15%. Above $10,000,000, the share above that threshold times the earnings gives the very large superannuation balance earnings component, taxed at a further 10%.

The Explanatory Memorandum to the Treasury Laws Amendment (Building a Stronger and Fairer Super System) Bill 2026 works 2 examples. Jordan has $4,000,000 at the end of 2026-27 and $100,000 of earnings: 25% of the balance is above $3,000,000, so taxable superannuation earnings are $25,000 and the tax $3,750. Kelly has $12,000,000 and $500,000 of earnings: 75% and 16.67%, so $375,000 taxed at 15% ($56,250) and $83,350 at 10% ($8,335), $64,585 in all. This calculator opens on Jordan’s figures, and both are among its boundary cases.

What the Division 296 tax calculator does not cover

The calculator works out one year from the balance and earnings you supply. It does not project a balance, compare a withdrawal or set tax inside super against tax outside it: those turn on future earnings, contributions and withdrawals that no source states, and choosing between them is financial advice. It covers 2026-27 only. From 2027-28 the thresholds are indexed (paragraphs 296-30(b) and 296-35(b)) and the balance just before the start of the year counts too, and those figures are not yet published.

It does not work out the total superannuation balance, a fund’s Division 296 fund earnings or how they are attributed to each interest (sections 296-55 to 296-75), or the earnings of defined benefit and other interests under section 296-70. Only tax attributable to a defined benefit interest can be deferred to a debt account. It does not cover temporary residents who depart Australia (sections 296-195 to 296-205), the notice of assessment, payment or release authorities. The ATO assesses the tax from what funds report, and the notice of assessment sets the amount. Confirm the figures with the ATO or a registered tax agent.
