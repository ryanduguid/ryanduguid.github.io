What the inputs need

The most tax is what the lump sum can bear in the person’s return, before the Medicare levy. The amount withheld is what the fund takes out when it pays, under Schedule 12 of the withholding schedules; it is a credit against that tax. A departing Australia superannuation payment, to a former temporary resident who has left Australia, bears its own final tax.

For one lump sum from a complying fund paid on a condition of release: not an income stream commutation or a payment in breach of the payment rules. A roll-over and a payment to the trustee of a deceased estate are worked only for withholding. The fund states the interest’s value and tax free component just before the payment, and the untaxed element.

The lump sum’s tax free component is its share of the interest’s, rounded to the cent; the rest is taxable, taxed in the fund except the untaxed element. Leave that at 0 for a fund that pays tax on its income, as most do.

Needed only with an untaxed element. An untaxed element from service before 1 July 1983 is refused: part of it then becomes tax free under section 307-150.

You decide whether the person is a death benefits dependant: a spouse or former spouse, a child under 18, someone interdependent with or dependent on the member, or anyone if the member died in the line of duty (section 302-195).

Two registered medical practitioners, one a specialist, certify death is likely within 24 months. A disability superannuation benefit is refused: section 307-145 enlarges its tax free component.

The low rate cap applies only at preservation age and under 60, which from 1 July 2024 no one is. Enter the amounts counted for earlier lump sums. Other lump sums at that age in the same year share the cap, so they are refused while any is left.

Withholding is worked for payments from 13 October 2020. With a TFN, a resident’s rate for each part is its ceiling rate plus the 2% Medicare levy, and the total rounds to the nearest dollar. Without one, the fund withholds 47% (45% for a foreign resident) of the taxable component, or of the untaxed element at 60 or over, ignoring cents; no rate is stated for a non-dependant’s death benefit or a member benefit under $200, so those are refused. The calculator assumes the payment is taxable in Australia and applies no tax treaty.

Take the components from the fund’s or the ATO’s statement. The working holiday maker rate applies when the payment includes amounts from contributions made whilst the person was a working holiday maker. An unclaimed money roll-over with an untaxed element is refused: section 5(2) taxes any excess untaxed roll-over amount in it at nil.

Sources applied

  • Components: section 307-125 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 at the ATO’s legal database, read 22 November 2026: each component bears the same proportion to the lump sum as the interest’s component bears to the interest’s value just before the payment. The Act states no rounding; the tax free component is rounded half up to the cent and the taxable component is the rest. Section 307-275 makes the taxable component an element taxed in the fund except as the fund states an element untaxed in the fund.
  • Members: sections 301-10 to 301-35 and 301-95 to 301-115, read 22 November 2026. At 60 or over only the untaxed element is assessable, at no more than 15% within the untaxed plan cap. From preservation age to under 60 the taxed element is taxed at no more than 0% within the low rate cap and 15% above it; the untaxed element at 15% within the low rate cap the taxed element leaves (section 301-105(6)) and 30% above it, within the untaxed plan cap. Under preservation age the taxed element is taxed at no more than 20% and the untaxed element at 30% within the untaxed plan cap. Above that cap the untaxed element has no offset: the top marginal rate, 45% in each year from 2020-21 to 2026-27 (the ATO’s Tax rates, Australian residents, read 22 November 2026). A terminal medical condition (section 303-10) or a lump sum under $200 that closes the interest (section 301-225) makes the whole lump sum tax free.
  • Death benefits: to a death benefits dependant, tax free (section 302-60); to anyone else, the taxed element at no more than 15% and the untaxed element at no more than 30% (section 302-145), read 22 November 2026.
  • Caps: the low rate cap amount (section 307-345) and the untaxed plan cap amount (section 307-350) for each year are Tables 9 and 10 of the ATO’s Payments from super (last updated 16 September 2026), read 22 November 2026: the low rate cap $215,000 to $235,000 from 2020-21 to 2023-24, and the untaxed plan cap $1,565,000 in 2020-21 to $1,935,000 in 2026-27. Preservation age is 55 for those born before 1 July 1960, rising a year for each later year of birth to 60 for those born after 30 June 1964.
  • Examples: the ATO’s Tax on super benefits (last updated 2 August 2023), read 22 November 2026, gives Tony’s, Suzi’s, Frankie’s, Peta’s, Mei’s, Howard’s and Anh’s lump sums, and Schedule 12 of the withholding schedules gives Heather’s, Dean’s and Chloe’s, each tested here before the 2% Medicare levy the ATO adds.
  • Withholding: Schedule 12 of the withholding schedules, the ATO’s Schedule 12 from 13 October 2020 to 30 June 2024 and Schedule 12 of the Taxation Administration (Withholding Schedules) Instruments 2024, 2025, (No. 2) 2025 and 2026, read for the command on 22 November 2026; the 2020 schedule and the 2026 instrument were read again on 23 November 2026. Table A’s rate for each part is the ceiling rate above plus the 2% Medicare levy, nil where the ceiling is nil, and a foreign resident’s rate leaves the levy out. Nothing is withheld from a terminal medical condition payment, a member benefit under $200 that closes the interest, a death benefit to a dependant or a death benefit to the trustee of a deceased estate. A roll-over has 47% withheld from the untaxed element above the untaxed plan cap left: Tom’s $9,400 in 2020-21 and $15,040 in 2026-27. Without a TFN, 47% or 45% applies with no offsets. The total rounds to the nearest dollar; without a TFN, cents are ignored.
  • Departing Australia superannuation payments: section 5 of the Superannuation (Departing Australia Superannuation Payments Tax) Act 2007, read 23 November 2026: nil on the tax free component, 35% on the element taxed in the fund and 45% on the element untaxed in the fund, or 65% on each element where the payment includes amounts from contributions made whilst a working holiday maker (section 5(3)). The Act states no rounding; each element’s tax is rounded half up to the cent. Section 301-175 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 makes the payment not assessable income and not exempt income, taxed under that Act instead. Table 14 of the ATO’s Payments from super (last updated 16 September 2026), read 23 November 2026, gives the same rates.
  • Supplied by you: the year, the payment date, the recipient, the lump sum, the interest’s value and tax free component, the untaxed element, for a member the date of birth, a terminal medical condition, a disability superannuation benefit and the caps used, for withholding the TFN and residency, and for a departing Australia superannuation payment its components and whether it includes working holiday maker contributions.

How the super lump sum tax calculator works

The super lump sum tax calculator splits one lump sum from a complying fund, paid from 1 July 2020 to 30 June 2027, into its components. It gives the most income tax each part can bear before the Medicare levy. A member’s treatment turns on age on the payment day and preservation age; a death benefit’s turns on whether the person receiving it is a death benefits dependant. Each rate is a ceiling: the offsets stop the tax on a part going above it, so a person on lower marginal rates pays less, and the Medicare levy is added on top.

It also works the amount the fund withholds from the same lump sum under Schedule 12, for a payment from 13 October 2020, including from a roll-over and a death benefit paid to a deceased estate, and the final tax on a departing Australia superannuation payment in 2025-26 to 2027-28.

Its results match the super-benefit-tax command of Ryan Duguid’s australian-accounting calculators on the ATO’s examples and 532 test cases, and its super-withholding and dasp-tax commands on 613 more, refusals included.

What the super lump sum tax calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work the tax at the person’s own marginal rates, the Medicare levy, withholding from income streams (Schedule 13), the tax on a roll-over or a deceased estate, a tax treaty, or payments from a non-complying or foreign fund. It does not decide dependency, a terminal medical condition, the components or the elements: you state those. It refuses a disability superannuation benefit, an untaxed element from service before 1 July 1983, and other lump sums in the same year that share the low rate cap.

Confirm the result with the fund, the ATO’s guidance on super lump sums or a registered tax agent before relying on it. The redundancy and ETP withholding calculator covers employment termination payments.
