One super lump sum from a complying fund
The member

What the inputs need

For one superannuation lump sum from a complying fund, paid on a condition of release: not a roll-over, not a commutation of an income stream and not a payment in breach of the payment rules. The fund gives you the interest’s value and tax free component just before the payment, and the element untaxed in the fund.

The lump sum’s tax free component is its share of the interest’s tax free component, rounded to the cent; the rest is the taxable component. Of that, the fund states the element untaxed in the fund, and the element taxed in the fund is the remainder. Leave the untaxed element at 0 for a fund that pays tax on its income, as most do.

Needed only with an untaxed element. An untaxed element from service before 1 July 1983 is refused: part of it then becomes tax free under section 307-150.

You decide whether the person is a death benefits dependant: a spouse or former spouse, a child under 18, someone in an interdependency relationship or otherwise dependent on the member, or anyone when the member died in the line of duty (section 302-195).

Two registered medical practitioners, one a specialist, certify that the illness or injury is likely to result in death within 24 months. A disability superannuation benefit is refused: its tax free component grows under section 307-145.

The low rate cap applies only at preservation age and under 60, which from 1 July 2024 no one is. Enter the amounts counted for earlier lump sums, as the fund or earlier results show them. Other lump sums at that age in the same income year are refused while any of the cap is left, because they share it.

Sources applied

  • Components: section 307-125 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 at the ATO’s legal database, read 22 November 2026: each component bears the same proportion to the lump sum as the interest’s component bears to the interest’s value just before the payment. The Act states no rounding; the tax free component is rounded half up to the cent and the taxable component is the rest. Section 307-275 makes the taxable component an element taxed in the fund except as the fund states an element untaxed in the fund.
  • Members: sections 301-10 to 301-35 and 301-95 to 301-115, read 22 November 2026. At 60 or over only the untaxed element is assessable, at no more than 15% within the untaxed plan cap. From preservation age to under 60 the taxed element is taxed at no more than 0% within the low rate cap and 15% above it; the untaxed element at 15% within the low rate cap the taxed element leaves (section 301-105(6)) and 30% above it, within the untaxed plan cap. Under preservation age the taxed element is taxed at no more than 20% and the untaxed element at 30% within the untaxed plan cap. Above that cap the untaxed element has no offset: the top marginal rate, 45% in each year from 2020-21 to 2026-27 (the ATO’s Tax rates – Australian resident, read 22 November 2026). A terminal medical condition (section 303-10) or a lump sum under $200 that closes the interest (section 301-225) makes the whole lump sum tax free.
  • Death benefits: to a death benefits dependant, tax free (section 302-60); to anyone else, the taxed element at no more than 15% and the untaxed element at no more than 30% (section 302-145), read 22 November 2026.
  • Caps: the low rate cap amount (section 307-345) and the untaxed plan cap amount (section 307-350) for each year are Tables 9 and 10 of the ATO’s Payments from super (last updated 16 September 2026), read 22 November 2026: the low rate cap $215,000 to $235,000 from 2020-21 to 2023-24, and the untaxed plan cap $1,565,000 in 2020-21 to $1,935,000 in 2026-27. Preservation age is 55 for those born before 1 July 1960, rising a year for each later year of birth to 60 for those born after 30 June 1964.
  • Examples: the ATO’s Tax on super benefits (last updated 2 August 2023), read 22 November 2026, gives Tony’s, Suzi’s, Frankie’s, Peta’s, Mei’s, Howard’s and Anh’s lump sums, and Schedule 12 of the withholding schedules gives Heather’s, Dean’s and Chloe’s, each tested here before the 2% Medicare levy the ATO adds.
  • Supplied by you: the year, the payment date, the recipient, the lump sum, the interest’s value and tax free component, the untaxed element, and for a member the date of birth, a terminal medical condition, a disability superannuation benefit and the caps used.

How the super lump sum tax calculator works

The super lump sum tax calculator splits one lump sum from a complying fund into its tax free component and its elements taxed and untaxed in the fund, and gives the most income tax each part can bear before the Medicare levy, for a payment from 1 July 2020 to 30 June 2027. A member’s treatment turns on age on the payment day and preservation age; a death benefit’s turns on whether the person receiving it is a death benefits dependant. Each rate is a ceiling: the offsets stop the tax on a part going above it, so a person on lower marginal rates pays less, and the Medicare levy is added on top.

Its results match the super-benefit-tax command of Ryan Duguid’s australian-accounting calculators on the ATO’s examples and 532 test cases, refusals included.

What the super lump sum tax calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work the tax at the person’s own marginal rates, the Medicare levy, the amount the fund withholds, income streams, roll-overs, departing Australia superannuation payments, death benefits paid to a deceased estate, or payments from a non-complying or foreign fund. It does not decide dependency, a terminal medical condition, the components or the elements: you state those. It refuses a disability superannuation benefit, an untaxed element from service before 1 July 1983, and other lump sums in the same year that share the low rate cap.

Confirm the result with the fund, the ATO’s guidance on super lump sums or a registered tax agent before relying on it. The redundancy and ETP withholding calculator covers employment termination payments.
