Luxury car tax for one tax period

For a business registered or required to be registered for GST. You establish each line: that the vehicle is a car and not one the Act excludes (an emergency vehicle, a car fitted out for wheelchairs, a commercial vehicle not designed mainly to carry passengers, a motor home or campervan), that the supply or importation is taxable (no quote, not GST-free or input taxed), its luxury car tax value and its combined fuel consumption rating.

Lines

Each line is one sale, importation, adjustment or refund in the tax period. A sale’s and an importation’s threshold is the tax period’s year; an adjustment takes the year of the sale it adjusts.

Sources applied

  • The tax: section 5-15 (sales) and section 7-15 (importations) of the A New Tax System (Luxury Car Tax) Act 1999 at the ATO’s legal database, read 22 November 2026: 33% × 10/11 × (luxury car tax value − threshold), rounded half up to the cent; nil at or below the threshold, the car then not being a luxury car (subsection 25-1(1)). A sale’s tax is reduced by the tax already payable on an earlier supply or importation of the car, not below nil (subsection 5-15(2)). The ATO’s Working out the LCT on a sale and on an import (both last updated 1 June 2026), read 22 November 2026, give the examples tested here: Matty Bee Motors’ $88,000 car, $2,157.30, and Jimmy’s Mustang, $6,207.30 paid to the Department of Home Affairs.
  • Rate and thresholds: the ATO’s Luxury car tax rate and thresholds (last updated 1 June 2026), read 22 November 2026: 33%, and each financial year’s threshold and fuel-efficient car limit from 2012-13 to 2026-27, typed here and checked against that page. 2027-28 is not yet published and is refused.
  • Fuel-efficient cars: subsection 25-1(4) gives the fuel-efficient car limit to a car with a combined rating of at most 3.5 litres per 100 km for supplies and importations from 1 July 2025, and 7 litres before (Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Incentives and Integrity) Act 2025, Schedule 1 items 2 and 4(1)). The 7 litre rating continues for a car that, before 1 July 2025, was supplied or imported and used in Australia other than for a quotable purpose (item 4(2)), as the ATO’s Definitions page also says, read 22 November 2026.
  • Adjustments: an increasing or decreasing adjustment when the corrected tax on a sale differs from the tax previously attributed (sections 15-20 and 15-25); a decreasing adjustment of the tax payable on the supply for a registered buyer who did not quote and has used the car only for a quotable purpose, and an increasing one when a car bought under quote, or after that decreasing adjustment, is used for another purpose (section 15-30). 1E takes the tax on sales and the increasing adjustments, 1F the decreasing adjustments, each in whole dollars, as the ATO’s BAS page Luxury car tax (LCT) (last updated 1 May 2025), read 22 November 2026, sets out.
  • Refunds: a registered primary producer (section 18-5), for one car a financial year, or a tourism operator (section 18-10), for each car, who bore the tax on a refund-eligible car is refunded the lesser of that tax and $10,000, for cars supplied or imported from 1 July 2019 (Treasury Laws Amendment (2019 Measures No. 2) Act 2019, Schedule 2). The refund is claimed on the ATO’s form within 4 years, not on the BAS (Luxury car tax credits and refunds, last updated 16 July 2025, read 22 November 2026).
  • Supplied by you: the tax period’s year, and for each line what it is, the supply’s year, the luxury car tax value, the fuel consumption rating, the earlier, attributed, payable or borne tax, the claimant and whether the car is refund-eligible.

How the luxury car tax calculator works

The luxury car tax calculator works out the luxury car tax on a tax period’s sales and importations of luxury cars, in a financial year from 2019-20 to 2026-27, with the price and change of use adjustments and the amounts for labels 1E and 1F on the business activity statement. Each car takes the threshold for the financial year of its supply or importation, or the fuel-efficient car limit for a car rated at most 3.5 litres per 100 km from 1 July 2025 and at most 7 litres before. The tax is 33% of the GST-exclusive value over that threshold. Tax on an importation is shown apart, because it is paid to the Department of Home Affairs with any customs duty. A primary producer’s or tourism operator’s refund of the tax borne on a refund-eligible car, up to $10,000, is shown apart too, because it is claimed on the ATO’s form.

Its results match the luxury-car-tax command of Ryan Duguid’s australian-accounting calculators on the ATO’s examples and 306 test cases, refusals included.

What the luxury car tax calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a vehicle is a car or a luxury car, whether you could quote or a supply is taxable, the value of a car sold with other goods, or whether a car is refund-eligible: you state those. It does not work bad debt adjustments, the adjustments in subsections 15-30(1A) and (3A), a public institution’s adjustment, the credit for an entity that is not registered, the refund for cars supplied before 1 July 2019, or which tax period a line belongs to. It refuses a tax period outside 2019-20 to 2026-27, a supply year outside 2012-13 to the tax period’s, and a second primary producer refund in the period. A primary producer’s refund in an earlier period of the same year also rules out another, which the page cannot see.

Confirm the result with the ATO’s luxury car tax guidance or a registered tax or BAS agent before relying on it. The vehicle classifier shows whether a vehicle is a car for luxury car tax.
