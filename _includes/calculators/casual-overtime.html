How the casual and overtime pay calculator works

The calculator works one employee’s gross pay for one pay period from the rates you enter, then the super guarantee on the part that is ordinary time earnings. Ordinary hours are paid at the base hourly rate, plus the casual loading as a percentage of it for a casual. Each penalty or overtime line is its hours at the percentage of the base rate you enter, such as 150% for time and a half. Annual leave is paid at the base rate with any leave loading. Each amount is worked exactly and rounded to the cent once.

Ordinary time earnings are the pay for ordinary hours, the casual loading, penalty rates and shift loadings on ordinary hours, paid leave and a leave loading, unless the loading is paid for overtime the employee would have worked. Pay for overtime hours is not ordinary time earnings. For a payday from 1 July 2026, the super guarantee is 12% of qualifying earnings, which here are the ordinary time earnings in the pay; for a payday from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026, it is 12% of the ordinary time earnings under the quarterly rules. The calculator refuses a pay whose ordinary time earnings alone exceed the maximum contribution base.

What the casual and overtime pay calculator does not cover

The calculator applies no award, enterprise agreement or minimum rate, and does not check that the rates you enter meet one: the Fair Work Ombudsman’s pay guides and Pay and Conditions Tool give award rates. It does not decide which hours are ordinary hours or overtime, whether a casual loading compounds with a penalty rate, or how an award rounds its rates; enter those as your award or agreement states them. It leaves out allowances, commission, bonuses, salary sacrifice, other qualifying earnings, PAYG withholding, payroll tax and workers compensation, and it does not track the maximum contribution base across a quarter or year: it assumes earlier pays have not reached it. Paydays before 1 July 2025 or after 30 June 2027 are refused. The ATO’s draft qualifying earnings ruling may change when it is finalised. Results are planning estimates: check them against the award or agreement and the ATO’s guidance.
