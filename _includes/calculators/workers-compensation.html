Sources applied

  • New South Wales: clauses 6.7 and 6.10 of SIRA’s Workers Compensation Market Practice and Premiums Guidelines 2026, for policies issued or renewed from 4 pm on 30 June 2026, read 19 November 2026. A small employer’s basic tariff premium, wages times the WIC rate, does not exceed $30,000 for a 12-month policy; above that the employer is experience rated. SIRA’s premium calculation page says ‘less than $30,000’; the Guidelines, which bind insurers, include exactly $30,000, as icare does. The apprentice incentive is at least apprentice wages times the rate. icare’s premium calculation for small employers, read the same day: P = APP + D + M − SER − PD − A + Q + CCC, where the Safe Employer Reward is APP times its percentage and its example, Rob’s Restaurant, has $1,200,000 of wages at 2.250%, an APP of $27,000 and a reward of $2,025, which this calculator matches. icare’s premium updates for 2026-27 (updated 9 November 2026): 2025-26 WIC rates kept for 2026-27 and a minimum premium of $240. icare’s 2025-26 rate table gives each WIC’s dust diseases rate including GST. The calculator refuses a premium below $240, because icare does not say whether the minimum includes GST.
  • Victoria: clauses 6 to 12 of the WorkCover Premiums Order (No. 34) 2026/2027 (Victoria Government Gazette P1, 29 April 2026) and the values WorkSafe set in Gazette S 284 of 2 June 2026, both read 19 November 2026. A new or small employer’s premium rate has an employer performance rating of 1 (clause 12(4)); a small employer has annualised remuneration for 2025-26 of $200,000 or less (clause 12(5)). The preliminary premium is remuneration, to the dollar, times the premium rate (clause 9(2)), less a deductible benefit of the premium rate times the deductible amount of 15,500 times the days registered over 365, rounded to the cent (clauses 11 and 6(4)); the WorkCover premium is the greater of that and the $400 minimum, rounded down to the cent (clauses 6 and 9(1)); an excess buy-out adds 0.1 of it (clause 10); GST is added (clause 8). Enter the industry rate for a new employer and the premium rate on WorkSafe’s notice for a small one, which includes any capping. The exemption limit for 2026-27 is $7,500 (clause 7).
  • Queensland: sections 3.2, 4.1, 5.2, 8.3 and 10.1 of WorkCover Queensland Notice (No. 1) of 2026 (Queensland Government Gazette No. 43, 26 June 2026), read 19 November 2026. A policy that commences in 2026-27 pays wages over $100 times the industry rate per $100, which excludes GST and duty, less the apprentice discount on apprentice wages, with no prior-year adjustment; the minimum, including GST and duty, is $200, $150, $100 or $50 by the quarter it commences. WorkCover Queensland’s premium page (last updated 5 June 2026) applies the industry rate for the first 18 months; the notice applies it to a policy that commences in 2026-27, and this calculator follows the notice. Insurance duty is 5% of net premiums for accident insurance under section 362(3) of the Duties Act 2001 (reprint current from 22 October 2026), read 19 November 2026, and the Queensland Revenue Office’s public ruling DA356.1 puts the GST in those net premiums.
  • South Australia: ReturnToWorkSA’s Your premium explained, read 19 November 2026: Premium = BP × (1 − D) + C − A + SUR, the discount factor of 5% for a base premium under $10,000 up to 30% at $1,000,000 or more, and claims costs capped at 3 × D × BP; its 3 examples, $8,550, $9,900 and $56,000 before GST and the WHS fee, are tested here. Its 2026-27 premium calculator workbook, read the same day, deducts the apprentice base premium, applies no discount or claims costs in the employer’s first full financial year, applies a $200 minimum and rounds the premium down to the whole dollar. The supplementary underwriting rate is taken as nil; GST is added; the work health and safety fee, which carries no GST, is not. ReturnToWorkSA’s apprentice incentive page, read 20 November 2026, deducts apprentice remuneration times the industry premium rate for an apprentice under an approved training contract in a trade declared under section 6 of the South Australian Skills Act 2008, or a group training organisation’s trainee in a declared vocation.
  • Western Australia, Tasmania, the Australian Capital Territory and the Northern Territory: privately underwritten. NT WorkSafe’s premiums bulletin (last updated 21 March 2025), read 19 November 2026: estimated wages times the rate plus GST, $15,400 on $400,000 at 3.50%, recalculated on actual wages of $500,000 to $19,250, an adjustment of $3,850 including GST, which this page opens on. WorkCover WA’s 2026/27 recommended premium rates (section 253 of the Workers Compensation and Injury Management Act 2023, April 2026), WorkSafe Tasmania’s suggested premium rates 2026-27 (an average of 2.18% of wages excluding GST) and the ACT’s suggested reasonable premium rates for 2026/27, each read 19 November 2026, are guides; the insurer sets the premium.
  • Rates: this page holds no rate table. The schemes publish theirs: icare’s WIC rates, WorkSafe Victoria’s industry rates, the industry premium rates in WorkCover Queensland’s gazette notice (per $100 of wages), ReturnToWorkSA’s industry premium rates and, as guides only, WorkCover WA’s recommended rates, WorkSafe Tasmania’s suggested rates and the ACT’s suggested reasonable premium rates. In Western Australia, Tasmania, the ACT and the Northern Territory each insurer sets its own rate.
  • Wages: each scheme’s document for the period decides what counts: icare’s Wages Definition Manual, WorkSafe Victoria’s How remuneration works (read 19 November 2026; it lists superannuation, fringe benefits, salary sacrifice and leave among remuneration, and sorts contractors, apprentices and termination payments into included or exempt), WorkCover Queensland’s wages definition, the WorkCover WA Remuneration Guidelines, ReturnToWorkSA’s remuneration guide, WorkSafe Tasmania’s guideline on the definition of wages, WorkSafe ACT’s wages and earnings guide and, in the Northern Territory, the insurer.
  • Supplied by you: the scheme, the wages, the rate, the apprentice wages, the actual wages and the facts each scheme asks for; only the chosen scheme’s answers are used. Each step is worked exactly and rounds half up to the cent once, except where a scheme rounds otherwise (Victoria’s remuneration to the dollar and WorkCover premium down to the cent; South Australia’s premium down to the dollar), so a step shown can differ by a cent from the figures above it. The adjustment uses the same rate and apprentice wages on the actual wages.

How the workers compensation premium calculator works

Every state and territory scheme bills a premium on the wages an employer estimates for the policy period and adjusts it to the wages actually paid. The calculator takes the wages, the rate your insurer gives your industry classification and the facts your scheme asks for, and applies the scheme’s published method as far as it goes for an employer whose premium does not yet turn on its own claims: icare’s small employer formula in New South Wales, the Premiums Order for a new or small employer in Victoria, WorkCover Queensland’s notice for a policy that starts in 2026-27, ReturnToWorkSA’s formula with its discount bands and claims cost cap in South Australia, and the insurer’s rate times wages in the other 4. It adds GST, and Queensland’s insurance duty, and, given the actual wages, works the premium again and shows the adjustment.

What the workers compensation premium calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employer and one policy, and no advice. It does not cover:

  • the industry classification or the rate, which are the insurer’s, and the wages, which each scheme’s definition decides, including whether a contractor or director counts as a worker;
  • experience-rated and performance-rated premiums: New South Wales above $30,000 of basic tariff premium and the Loss Prevention and Recovery products, Victorian employers that are neither new nor small, Queensland policies in force before 1 July 2026 or carried on from an earlier policy (reinstated within 6 months, or a business taken over: sections 11 and 12 of the notice) and Queensland default assessments, which have a $500 minimum (section 4.2), and a South Australian supplementary underwriting rate;
  • members of a group in New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland and South Australia, policies for less than 12 months in New South Wales, the Mine Safety Fund adjustment for mining WICs, the catastrophic claims contribution and the performance discount;
  • Comcare and self-insured employers, household workers, the South Australian work health and safety fee, discounts for paying early and instalment interest;
  • whether an employer is exempt from holding a policy, which the result flags where a scheme’s limit was read, and any policy year other than 2026-27.
