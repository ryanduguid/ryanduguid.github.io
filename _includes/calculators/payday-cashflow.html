Super guarantee cash timing for one quarter of 2026-27
Quarter
Pay cycle

A date in the quarter’s first 7 days for weekly pay, first 14 days for fortnightly pay or first month for monthly pay. Monthly paydays fall on the same date each month, or the month’s last day if it is shorter.

Include salary sacrificed to super and any other qualifying earnings under section 10A(1), such as an eligible contractor’s labour payments.

Due dates are from the Payday Super calendar, as the SGC and Payday Super calculator works them.

Sources applied

  • Payday Super: sections 6(1) (business day, usual period), 10A(1) (qualifying earnings) and 17A of the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 (Compilation No. 78, 1 July 2026), read 26 November 2026. On each payday (the QE day) the employer has a super guarantee amount of 12% of the qualifying earnings paid that day; the fund must receive it by the end of the usual period, the seventh business day after payday. A business day is any day other than a weekend or a public holiday for the whole of a state or territory.
  • Due dates: ATO: Payment deadlines for Payday Super (last updated 29 September 2026), read 26 November 2026, which also advises paying on payday so a clearing house has time to meet the 7 business days. Where the deadline turns on whether 24 or 31 December, part-day holidays, is a business day, the calculator shows the earliest possible date, as the SGC and Payday Super calculator explains.
  • The quarterly rule it replaced: section 23(6) of the Act as at 27 March 2026 (Compilation No. 77), read 26 November 2026: a contribution made within 28 days after the end of a quarter counts for that quarter. The due dates of 28 October, 28 January, 28 April and 28 July are from ATO: Module 5, paying quarterly super contributions (last updated 25 February 2026), read 26 November 2026, which lets a payment due on a weekend or public holiday be made on the next working day; none of the 4 dates in 2026-27 falls on a weekend or a holiday in the site’s calendar.
  • Transition: Schedule 1 item 182 of the Treasury Laws Amendment (Payday Superannuation) Act 2025, read 26 November 2026: the new law applies to a QE day from 1 July 2026, so the quarterly dates are a comparison only. The April to June 2026 quarter stays under the old law and was due on 28 July 2026, as ATO: Super guarantee due dates (last updated 21 April 2026), read 26 November 2026, says.
  • Rate: 12% for 2026-27 from ATO: Key superannuation rates and thresholds, super guarantee (last updated 17 April 2026), read 26 November 2026.
  • Supplied by you: the quarter, the pay cycle, the first payday and the earnings paid each payday. Each payday’s amount is worked exactly in cents and rounded half up to the cent once; the quarter’s total is their sum.

How the Payday Super cashflow calculator works

Until 30 June 2026 an employer could hold a quarter’s super guarantee until 28 days after the quarter ended. From 1 July 2026, under Payday Super, the employee’s fund must receive 12% of each payday’s qualifying earnings within 7 business days of that payday. The calculator lists the paydays in the chosen quarter of 2026-27, each payday’s super guarantee and the date the fund must receive it, and how many days earlier that is than the quarterly date would have been. The extra working capital is what has already gone to funds but would still be in the business under the quarterly rule. It builds with each payday and peaks at the whole quarter’s super guarantee from the last payday’s due date until the day before the quarterly date. The average days earlier weighs each payday’s amount by how much sooner it leaves. Both dates are when the fund must receive the money, so allow for a clearing house’s processing time before each. In the first quarter, July to September 2026, the April to June 2026 contributions, still due by 28 July 2026, also fall due alongside the first paydays under the new rule.

What the Payday Super cashflow calculator does not cover

The calculator is an estimate for planning and gives no advice. It does not cover:

  • the longer periods for a first contribution for a new employee or to a new fund, out-of-cycle pay, an ATO exceptional circumstances determination or bunched contributions: use the SGC and Payday Super calculator for those due dates;
  • earnings that change from one payday to the next, extra or irregular paydays, and the April to June 2026 quarter’s contributions;
  • each employee’s maximum contributions base of $270,830 for 2026-27: enter only earnings on which super guarantee is payable;
  • Norfolk Island salary or wages, which have a transitional rate of 11% for 2026-27;
  • the cost of the extra working capital, such as interest on an overdraft, and any super above the minimum;
  • paydays outside 2026-27.

Confirm the figures with the ATO or a registered tax agent before relying on them.
