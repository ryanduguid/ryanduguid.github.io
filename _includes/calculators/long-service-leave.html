Sources applied

  • New South Wales: section 4(2)(a) and (a3) of the Long Service Leave Act 1955 (version for 1 July 2025 to date), read 19 November 2026. 2 months (a month is 4 1/3 weeks) after 10 years and 1 month for each 5 years after; on termination from 10 to 15 years, for any reason, a proportion on 3 months for 15 years; after 15 years, each completed year since the last entitlement on 2 months for 10 years; from 5 to 10 years, a proportion on 2 months for 10 years only when the employer ends the employment for a reason other than serious and wilful misconduct, the worker leaves because of illness, incapacity, or domestic or other pressing necessity, or the worker dies.
  • Victoria: sections 6, 9 and 10 of the Long Service Leave Act 2018 (Authorised Version No. 006, as at 12 December 2025), read 19 November 2026. After 7 years, 1/60th of the period of continuous employment, paid in full when employment ends for any reason or on death; nothing before 7 years. Workforce Inspectorate Victoria’s long service leave page, read the same day, states it as one week for every 60 weeks, so this calculator divides the days by 420.
  • Queensland: section 95 of the Industrial Relations Act 2016 (reprint current from 1 January 2026), read 19 November 2026, for employees other than seasonal employees. 8.6667 weeks after 10 years, and after a further 5 years the same proportion for the whole further period; on termination after 10 years, for any reason, a proportionate payment for all of the service; from 7 to 10 years only on death, the employee leaving because of illness or a domestic or other pressing necessity, a dismissal because of illness or for a reason other than conduct, capacity or performance, an unfair dismissal, or a fixed term ending when the employee reasonably expected 10 years and was prepared to stay. Part years count as the Business Queensland tables, read the same day, count them, with the days over a 52-week year; their 15.8548 weeks for 18 years, 3 months, 2 weeks and 2 days is tested here.
  • Western Australia: section 8(2) and (3) of the Long Service Leave Act 1958 (official version 04-m0-00, as at 31 January 2025), read 19 November 2026. 8 2/3 weeks after 10 years and 4 1/3 weeks for each 5 years after; on termination after 10 years, each completed year since the last entitlement on 8 2/3 weeks for 10 years, except after dismissal for serious misconduct; from 7 to 10 years, a proportion for all of the service on any termination except dismissal for serious misconduct. The Lee and Anastasia examples on the department’s page (last updated 13 May 2026), read the same day, are tested here.
  • South Australia: section 5 of the Long Service Leave Act 1987 (version of 1 July 2017), read 19 November 2026. 13 weeks after 10 years and 1.3 weeks for each completed year after, payable on termination for any reason; from 7 to 10 years, 1.3 weeks for each completed year unless the worker was dismissed for serious and wilful misconduct or ended the contract unlawfully. Completed years only (subsection 5(7)). The Aisha, Ajay and Liam examples in SafeWork SA’s Guide to long service leave, read the same day, are tested here.
  • Tasmania: sections 8 and 9 of the Long Service Leave Act 1976 (version current from 10 December 2018), read 19 November 2026, for employees other than mining employees. 8 2/3 weeks after 10 years and 4 1/3 weeks for each 5 years after; on termination after 10 years, a proportion of 8 2/3 weeks for 10 years for the years since the last entitlement; from 7 to 10 years only on retirement at the age for retirement, illness, incapacity or domestic or other pressing necessity, or termination by the employer other than for serious and wilful misconduct; on death, 1/60th of the period since the last entitlement, or of all of it under 10 years. Part years count as WorkSafe Tasmania’s pro rata page (last updated 3 October 2024), read the same day, works them.
  • Australian Capital Territory: sections 3, 4, 11A and 11C of the Long Service Leave Act 1976 (republication R29, effective 19 November 2025), read 19 November 2026. 1/5 of a month for each completed year after 7 years, paid when employment ends for any reason; from 5 to 7 years, completed years and months on 2 months for 10 years, only for illness, incapacity or pressing necessity, retirement at or after the minimum retiring age, death, or termination by the employer other than for serious and wilful misconduct. A month is 52/12 weeks, as WorkSafe ACT’s calculation guidelines, read the same day, work it; their 6.0667 weeks after 7 years is tested here, and their $3,397.35 for it at $560 a week is 2 cents more than this calculator’s $3,397.33 because they multiply the rounded weeks.
  • Northern Territory: sections 8 and 10 of the Long Service Leave Act 1981 (reprint as in force at 14 October 2015), read 19 November 2026. 1.3 weeks for each completed year, taken after 10 years and each 5 years after, paid on termination for any reason, but only the completed 10 and 5-year periods after serious misconduct; from 7 to 10 years only on retirement at the age the employee may retire, termination by the employer other than for serious misconduct, or illness, incapacity or domestic or other pressing necessity.
  • Supplied by you: the Act that applies, the service dates, the days that do not count as service, the reason employment ends, the leave already taken or paid, and the weekly ordinary pay. Service runs to the end of the last day and is measured in completed years, completed months and days, a year or month that starts on a day a later month lacks, such as 29 February or the 31st, ending with that month’s last day; a part year is the months over 12 and the days over 365.25, as WorkSafe Tasmania’s example 2 works 13 years, 6 months and 14 days as 13.5383 years, except in Queensland, where the days are over 364. Weeks show rounded half up to 4 decimal places; the payment is the exact weeks times the weekly pay, rounded half up to the cent once, so 8 2/3 weeks at $1,500.00 is $13,000.00.

How the long service leave calculator works

Long service leave comes from each state and territory’s own Act, not the National Employment Standards. The calculator measures the service from the first day to the last, less the days you exclude, and applies the Act you choose. While employment continues, it gives the leave the employee can take now and when the next amount becomes available. When employment ends, it decides from the length of service and the reason whether the Act pays anything, works out the weeks it pays, deducts leave already taken or paid, and values the rest at the weekly ordinary pay. Each step names its provision. Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory give full leave after 7 years and the other states and territories after 10; New South Wales and the Australian Capital Territory can pay a pro rata amount from 5 years, the others from 7, and Victoria not before 7 years at all.

What the long service leave calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate under one Act for one employee, and no legal advice. It does not cover:

  • which Act applies to employment in more than one state or territory, and whether an award, enterprise agreement or contract gives a better entitlement, including the long service leave terms of a pre-2010 award or agreement that section 113 of the Fair Work Act 2009 preserves;
  • portable schemes, such as building and construction, contract cleaning, community services and security, and the black coal mining industry, Commonwealth and state public servants, Queensland seasonal employees in the sugar industry and meat works and other seasonal employees, and Tasmanian mining employees, who have their own rules;
  • whether a break keeps service continuous, which absences count, and whether a reason for leaving qualifies, such as a pressing necessity or serious misconduct: you decide those;
  • averaging pay for varied hours, casual or part-time work, commission or piece rates;
  • leave taken in advance of the entitlement, cashing out, and service before 23 June 1990, which some Acts count under earlier rules;
  • tax withheld from the payment.

The figures follow each Act as read on 19 November 2026; a future date assumes it is unchanged. Confirm the result with the state or territory’s long service leave regulator or a lawyer before relying on it.
