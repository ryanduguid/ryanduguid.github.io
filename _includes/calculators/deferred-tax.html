How the deferred tax calculator works

The calculator works one company’s tax effect worksheet under AASB 112 for an income year from 2020-21 to 2026-27. Each asset’s carrying amount less its tax base, or each liability’s tax base less its carrying amount, is a temporary difference: above nil it is taxable and below nil deductible. Taxable differences give a deferred tax liability. Deductible differences and unused tax losses, less those you say have no deferred tax asset because future taxable profit is not probable, give a deferred tax asset. Both are measured at the 25% or 30% you expect when they reverse, undiscounted.

The year’s deferred tax expense is the change in the net balance, split into the rate change on the opening balances, the change in recognition, and origination and reversal. Current tax is taxable income at the year’s company rate, 25% for a base rate entity (26% in 2020-21) or 30%, less non-refundable offsets. The reconciliation starts from accounting profit at that rate and shows each reconciling line and the difference left. Every amount is exact and rounded to the cent where the worksheet rounds.

What the deferred tax calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out tax bases or carrying amounts, or judge whether future taxable profit is probable. You enter them as established. It refuses business combinations, uncertain tax treatments, tax-consolidated group members and tax on items outside profit or loss. It refuses a rate change it cannot split without a reversal schedule. It leaves out the initial recognition exception, investments in subsidiaries, branches, associates and joint arrangements, unused tax credits, Pillar Two top-up taxes and prior year adjustments to current tax. It covers only an Australian resident company taxed at the section 23(2) rates, not life insurers, RSA providers, PDFs, non-profit companies or credit unions, and only Australian income tax, not foreign income tax. It does not decide how the balances are presented or offset. Results are planning estimates: check them against your records, the standard and the ATO’s guidance.
