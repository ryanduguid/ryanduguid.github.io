Sources applied

  • The credit: sections 41-5 and 43-5 of the Fuel Tax Act 2006 at the ATO’s legal database, read 19 November 2026: a credit for taxable fuel acquired for use in carrying on an enterprise, at the rate in force on the day it was acquired. No credit is allowed for fuel used in a vehicle of 4.5 tonnes gross vehicle mass or less travelling on a public road (section 41-20). For a heavy vehicle travelling on a public road the credit is reduced by the road user charge, unless that travel is incidental to the vehicle’s main use (subsections 43-10(3) and (4)), which the ATO’s heavy vehicle rate takes off.
  • Rates: the ATO’s fuel tax credit rates for business (last updated 29 July 2026) and its year pages for 2022-23 (27 June 2024), 2023-24 (27 June 2024), 2024-25 (29 January 2025), 2025-26 (28 April 2026) and 2026-27 (29 July 2026), all read 19 November 2026: 16 rate periods from 1 July 2022, each with the 6 rows of eligible fuel in 2 uses, 192 rates, all typed here and checked against those pages. The ATO gives the period from 3 August 2026 no end; rates are indexed in February and August, so this page holds it to 31 January 2027 and refuses later fuel.
  • Each line’s credit is the quantity times the rate in cents, divided by 100 and rounded half up to the cent, and the period’s amounts are added and claimed in whole dollars at label 7D, as the ATO’s Working out your fuel tax credits (last updated 6 July 2021), read 19 November 2026, works it.
  • Small claimants: PCG 2016/2, read 19 November 2026, for a business whose credits were $10,000 or less in the previous 12 months and are expected to be in the current 12 months (paragraph 4), for liquid fuels only. Method 1 (paragraph 9) takes the litres as the GST inclusive cost divided by the average GST inclusive price of the fuel type in the area of operation for the tax period, as the Australian Institute of Petroleum shows it; the credit uses the exact quotient and the litres show to 4 decimal places. Method 2 (paragraph 11) takes all the fuel as acquired on the tax period’s last day where the rate changes in the period because of indexation; it is refused across 29 September 2022, 1 April 2026 and 3 August 2026, when the rates changed for other reasons too. The ATO’s Simplified fuel tax credits (last updated 6 July 2021) summarises both.
  • Basic method for heavy vehicles: PCG 2021/2 paragraph 9 and the ATO’s Basic method for heavy vehicles (last updated 2 June 2021), both read 19 November 2026: public road litres are the public road kilometres, or the total kilometres times 98.4%, times the diesel consumption rate, and the rest of the diesel takes the other rate. Its Table 1 rigid truck rates are 0.18 litres a kilometre to 12 tonnes, 0.23 to 16.5, 0.28 to 24, 0.32 to 30 and 0.35 above. The ATO’s Jim (11,430 litres, 32,000 kilometres, a rigid truck over 30 tonnes) is tested here at the rates from 3 August 2026: $2,567.17.
  • Time limit: subsection 47-5(2) ends an entitlement not taken into account in a return given within 4 years after the day of acquisition. This page flags such a line and keeps it, because the limit does not reach a credit already taken into account in an assessment (Linfox Australia Pty Ltd v Commissioner of Taxation [2019] FCAFC 131) or the section 47-10 exception.
  • Supplied by you: the income year, each line’s fuel, day of acquisition, use and quantity or cost and average price, the heavy vehicle’s figures, the small claimant answer, the tax period and the day the return is given. Whether the business is entitled at all is not checked here.

How the fuel tax credit calculator works

The fuel tax credit calculator works out a business’s fuel tax credits for fuel acquired in one income year from 2022-23 to 2026-27. Each line takes the rate the ATO published for the day the fuel was acquired, for its fuel and its use: a heavy vehicle travelling on a public road takes the rate net of the road user charge, every other business use takes the full rate, and a light vehicle on a public road gets nothing. The page adds the lines and gives the whole dollar amount for label 7D on the business activity statement, with the rate, its period and its ATO page for each line.

A small claimant can also enter a line by its cost and the average pump price instead of its litres, split one heavy vehicle’s diesel between public roads and other use by the basic method, and take every line at the rate on the last day of a tax period that spans only an indexation change. Rates for every period since 1 July 2022 are held here, so an earlier period’s credits can be worked out for an amendment.

What the fuel tax credit calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether the business is entitled to a credit: you state the facts that settle it. Nor does it decide who acquired the fuel in a hire chain, whether a road is a public road or travel on it is incidental, whether a vehicle meets the environmental criteria of section 41-25, or how fuel is apportioned between uses: you state those. It does not hold the auxiliary equipment percentages of PCG 2016/11, the ATO’s list of heavy vehicles used mainly off public roads, the consumption rates for articulated combinations, or rates for aviation fuel, packaging and supply, domestic heating or electricity generation. It does not work Division 44 adjustments, the attribution of credits to tax periods or the conversion of LPG kilograms to litres. It refuses fuel acquired before 1 July 2022, after 31 January 2027, or outside the income year chosen.

Fuel acquired after 19 November 2026, when the rates were read, carries a warning to check the ATO’s rates page for a change since. Confirm the result with the ATO’s fuel tax credit tools or a registered tax or BAS agent before relying on it.
