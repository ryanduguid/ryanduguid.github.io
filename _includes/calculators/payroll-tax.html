Sources applied

  • Figures: this page’s payroll tax data file, which gives each threshold and rate with its provision, the date it took effect, its source and the date it was read.
  • New South Wales: Revenue NSW: payroll tax thresholds and rates (last updated 1 July 2026), read 17 November 2026: a $1,200,000 threshold apportioned by NSW wages over Australian wages, and 5.45%. The Payroll Tax Act 2007 (NSW) could not be read that day, so the page is the only source applied for New South Wales.
  • Victoria: Schedule 1 of the Payroll Tax Act 2007 (authorised version 041, as at 1 May 2026), read 17 November 2026, with the State Revenue Office’s current rates (updated 29 September 2026). The deduction is $1,000,000 apportioned by Victorian wages over Australian wages, reduced by 50% of Victorian wages above the apportioned $3,000,000 and never below nil (clause 5). The rate is 4.85%, or 1.2125% for a regional employer (section 3A). The mental health and wellbeing surcharge is 0.5% of Victorian wages above the apportioned $10,000,000 and 0.5% more above the apportioned $100,000,000; the COVID-19 debt temporary surcharge equals it until 30 June 2033 (clauses 1A, 1B and 5).
  • Queensland: sections 10, 29 and 43I of the Payroll Tax Act 1971 (reprint current as at 22 October 2026), read 17 November 2026. The deduction is $1,300,000 less $1 for every $7 of Australian wages above it, apportioned by Queensland wages; the rate is 4.75%, or 4.95% when Australian wages are more than $6,500,000. The mental health levy is 0.25% of Queensland wages above the apportioned $10,000,000 and a further 0.5% above the apportioned $100,000,000.
  • Western Australia: sections 8, 10 and 13 of the Pay-roll Tax Assessment Act 2002 and section 5(4) of the Pay-roll Tax Act 2002, read 17 November 2026: a $1,000,000 deduction that falls by $2 for every $13 of Australian wages above it and is nil from $7,500,000, apportioned by WA wages, and 5.5%.
  • South Australia: Schedule 1 of the Payroll Tax Act 2009 (version 31.3.2026), read 17 November 2026: no tax unless Australian wages are more than $1,500,000; a $600,000 deduction apportioned by SA wages; and 4.95%, phased in from $1,500,000 to $1,700,000 as (Australian wages − $1,500,000) ÷ $200,000 × 4.95% (clause 5(2)). RevenueSA’s guide and rate table cut the phased rate to 2 decimal places; this calculator applies the Act’s rate.
  • Tasmania: Schedule 1 of the Payroll Tax Act 2008 (version current from 1 April 2020), read 17 November 2026, with the State Revenue Office’s 2026-27 rates: 4% on the share of Australian wages from $1,250,000 to $2,000,000 and 6.1% on the share above $2,000,000 (clause 4).
  • Australian Capital Territory: Schedule 1 section 1.5 of the Payroll Tax Act 2011 (republication R20) and the payroll tax determination DI2026-151, in force from 1 July 2026, read 17 November 2026: a $1,750,000 threshold apportioned by ACT wages, and a rate on the whole taxable amount set by total Australian wages, from 6.75% up to $20 million to 8.75% above $150 million.
  • Northern Territory: section 7A and Schedule 1 of the Payroll Tax Act 2009 as in force at 1 July 2026, read 17 November 2026: a $2,500,000 deduction less $1 for every $2 of Australian wages above it, apportioned by NT wages, and 5.5%, or 6.5% when Australian wages are $100 million or more.
  • Supplied by you: the state or territory, its taxable wages and total Australian wages, in dollars and cents. Each amount is worked exactly and rounds half up to the cent once, and the payroll tax and the total add the amounts shown, so an amount shown can differ by a cent from the one above it times its rate. Revenue offices that round the deduction to the dollar first can differ by a few cents.

How the payroll tax calculator works

The payroll tax calculator estimates one employer’s payroll tax for the whole of 2026-27 in one state or territory. Each jurisdiction allows a deduction or tax-free threshold, which an employer that also pays wages elsewhere in Australia shares in proportion to its wages there. Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia and the Northern Territory reduce the deduction as Australian wages grow, until it reaches nil. The result shows the deduction, the amount taxed, the tax at each rate and, in Victoria and Queensland, each surcharge or levy on its own line, with the provision applied.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the tests hold them. Revenue NSW: $1,500,000 of NSW wages are taxed on $300,000, and $900,000 of $3,000,000 has a threshold of $360,000. The Victorian State Revenue Office: $80 million in Victoria and $70 million in New South Wales give surcharge thresholds of $5,333,333.33 and $53,333,333.33. The Queensland Revenue Office: $1,356,789 of $1,789,776 has a deduction of $932,460 to the dollar. RevenueWA: annual wages of $1,104,000 give $6,600; its other 2 examples round the deduction to the dollar and come out 1 and 3 cents lower than this calculator. RevenueSA: $800,000 of $1,600,000 has a deduction of $300,000 and $500,000 of $2,000,000 one of $150,000. The Tasmanian State Revenue Office: $2,400,000 gives $54,400, and $2,400,000 of $3,000,000 gives $72,800. The ACT Revenue Office and the Territory Revenue Office publish no worked figures that could be read, so the tests work examples from the Acts by hand: $84,375 on $3,000,000 in the Australian Capital Territory and $41,250 on $3,000,000 in the Northern Territory. This calculator opens on Revenue NSW’s first example.

What the payroll tax calculator does not compute

The payroll tax calculator gives one annual estimate and no advice. It does not decide which payments are taxable wages or where they are taxable, and does not cover:

  • an employer that is a member of a group, whose threshold and rate depend on the group’s wages and which members share the deduction;
  • an employer that paid wages for only part of 2026-27, whose threshold every jurisdiction reduces by the days it employed;
  • monthly or other periodic returns, and the annual reconciliation of amounts already paid;
  • exemptions, rebates and discounts, such as Queensland’s apprentice and trainee rebate and its 1% discount for regional employers, claimed in the return, and the ACT’s 6.85% cap for an eligible university;
  • any year other than 2026-27.

Confirm the figure with the revenue office or a registered tax agent before relying on it.
