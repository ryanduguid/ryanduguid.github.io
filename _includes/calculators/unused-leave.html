Sources applied

  • Assessable shares and the 30% cap: sections 83-10 and 83-15 (unused annual leave and leave loading) and 83-80, 83-85 and 83-90 (unused long service leave and its periods) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997, read on the ATO Legal Database 19 November 2026. All the leave is assessable except 95% of long service leave for the period before 16 August 1978, which is neither assessable nor exempt income. A tax offset keeps the rate at 30% on annual leave and loading paid on a genuine redundancy, early retirement scheme or invalidity, or for employment before 18 August 1993, and on long service leave from 16 August 1978 to 17 August 1993, and after it on those 3 reasons.
  • Withholding: ATO: Schedule 7, tax table for unused leave payments on termination of employment (published 17 June 2026, for payments from 1 July 2026), read 19 November 2026. It withholds 32% from the capped parts and works out the rest at marginal rates, with the 5% of pre-16 August 1978 long service leave added as its example for Norman does: the amount over the pays in a year, cents ignored, added to one pay’s normal gross earnings, and the extra the tax table then withholds, times the pays in a year, with no study and training support loan component. A post-17 August 1993 payment from normal termination under $300 has the lesser of that and 32% withheld. Each amount rounds to the nearest dollar, 50 cents up. Without a TFN it withholds 47% of the payment, or 45% for a foreign resident, cents ignored.
  • Tax table: ATO: Schedule 1, statement of formulas for calculating amounts to be withheld (published 17 June 2026), read 19 November 2026: scale 2 with the tax-free threshold claimed and scale 3 for a foreign resident, through the same engine as the take-home pay calculator.

How the unused leave calculator works

When employment ends, unused annual leave, leave loading and long service leave are taxed by when the leave accrued and why the employment ended. Leave for employment before 18 August 1993, and all of it from 16 August 1978 on a genuine redundancy, early retirement scheme or invalidity, is capped at 30% and withheld at 32%; it is lump sum A. Only 5% of long service leave for the period before 16 August 1978 is assessable; the whole amount is lump sum B. The rest goes through Schedule 7’s marginal rate calculation with that 5%, spread over a year of pays and withheld at the employee’s Schedule 1 rate, and is reported as unused leave on termination.

What the unused leave calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employee’s unused leave paid on termination from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, and no advice. It does not cover:

  • splitting a payment between the periods by days of service under sections 83-95 to 83-115: enter each part;
  • whether a termination is a genuine redundancy, an early retirement scheme or invalidity;
  • unused leave paid after an employee’s death;
  • a working holiday maker, whose payments Schedule 15 covers;
  • an employee who has not claimed the tax-free threshold, or claims a Medicare levy variation or the seniors and pensioners offset (scales 1, 5 and 6), or has a withholding variation;
  • a post-17 August 1993 payment under $300 with long service leave from before 16 August 1978, where Schedule 7 does not say how its limit applies;
  • employment termination payments, which the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator covers.

Confirm the figures with Schedule 7 or a registered tax agent before relying on them.
