Its endnotes show sections 291-20, 293-20 and 293-25 last amended by Act No. 81, 2016, for 2017-18, so the same rules apply to every year here. Section 293-30, which says which contributions count, lost paragraph (4)(d) from 1 July 2026 (Treasury Laws Amendment (Payday Superannuation) Act 2025, item 94); the calculator takes the contributions as your fund reports them.
  • The 15% rate: section 5 of the Superannuation (Sustaining the Superannuation Contribution Concession) Imposition Act 2013, read 19 November 2026. Neither Act prescribes rounding; the tax is rounded to the nearest cent.
  • Division 293 income: ‘income for surcharge purposes’ and ‘total net investment loss’ in section 995-1 of the 1997 Act, read in the same compilation: taxable income without an assessable FHSS released amount (nil where that is more) and with income on which family trust distribution tax was paid, plus the reportable fringe benefits total and total net investment loss, less the section 301-20(3) amount, a lump sum taxed at 0% for a person from preservation age to under 60, so nobody from 2024-25; section 293-20(1)(a) disregards reportable super contributions.

How the Division 293 tax calculator works

The Division 293 tax calculator works out the extra 15% tax on concessional super contributions for one income year when income and contributions together exceed $250,000. It adds up Division 293 income, the income measure used for the Medicare levy surcharge without reportable super contributions, then takes the contributions less any excess over the year’s cap, raised by unused cap carried forward when your total super balance allows it. Those are the low tax contributions. The taxable contributions are the lesser of the low tax contributions and the amount by which they and Division 293 income exceed $250,000, so only the part above the threshold is taxed, and never more than the contributions. Each step is shown, with the room left under the threshold when you are below it.

Excess concessional contributions are not taxed by Division 293: they are added to your assessable income and taxed at your marginal rate less a 15% offset instead. A one-off event, such as a capital gain, an employment termination payment or back pay, can take you over the threshold for a single year. The ATO assesses Division 293 tax on its own notice once it has your return and your fund’s contribution reports. It is payable 21 days after the notice, and you can elect within 60 days to have it released from super.

What the Division 293 tax calculator does not compute

The calculator covers 2021-22 to 2026-27, for contributions to accumulation interests. It does not compute:

  • defined benefit interests, whose defined benefit contributions the regulations work out and whose tax is deferred to a debt account (Subdivision 293-D), or the rules for State higher level office holders with constitutionally protected funds and for Commonwealth justices and judges (Subdivisions 293-E and 293-F);
  • contributions the Commissioner has disregarded or reallocated to another year, which stay in low tax contributions, or which contributions count: enter them as your fund reports them;
  • contributions that count differently for Division 293 than for the cap: a super guarantee amnesty amount the Commissioner paid to your fund, which is not a low tax contribution (section 293-30(4)(c)), and a contribution refunded under section 99G(6) of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993, which is not a concessional contribution (section 291-25(2)(e));
  • each part of Division 293 income, the unused cap or your total super balance, which you enter;
  • 2027-28 and later years, whose cap is not yet known, or years before 2021-22;
  • the refund for a former temporary resident who receives a departing Australia superannuation payment, interest, or the release from super itself.

Confirm the figures with your Division 293 notice from the ATO or a registered tax agent.
