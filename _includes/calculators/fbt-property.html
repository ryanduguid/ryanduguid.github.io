Sources applied

  • Valuation: sections 40, 42 and 43 of the Fringe Benefits Tax Assessment Act 1986 (Compilation No. 97, C2026C00332, 1 July 2026), read 19 November 2026. Section 42(1) values an in-house property fringe benefit by the first of its paragraphs that applies, each reduced by the recipient’s contribution; section 42(2) defines the arm’s length price of goods the provider bought. Section 43 values an external property fringe benefit at the provider’s cost price, the employer’s expenditure to a third-party provider or the notional value, less the contribution.
  • Otherwise deductible rule: section 44(1) takes the notional deduction, GD − RD, off the taxable value, TV − ND. GD is the once-only deduction the employee would have had for buying the property at its value before the contribution; RD is the deduction for the contribution. Section 17.5 of ATO: Fringe benefits tax - a guide for employers, chapter 17, read 19 November 2026, sets out the same steps and says the rule does not apply to decline in value deductions unless the cost is less than $301.
  • In-house reduction: section 62(1) reduces the total taxable value of an employee’s in-house fringe benefits for the year by up to $1,000; section 62(2) excludes a benefit provided under a salary packaging arrangement.
  • Examples: section 17.3 of the guide: the desk ($675 less $500, $175), the early payment discount ($950), the damaged racquet ($17.50) and the television bought for $500 ($100); section 17.5: $500 of goods used 80% for work ($50, or nil where the contribution allowed for the use).
  • Rounding: 75% of a price or value rounds half up to the cent; the Act prescribes no rounding. Supplied by you: the rule, the price or notional value, the contribution, the income use share, the deduction for the contribution and the in-house reduction left.

How the FBT property calculator works

The FBT property calculator values one property fringe benefit, goods or other property you give an employee in the FBT year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. An in-house benefit is goods identical or similar to those you, or an associate, sell in the ordinary course of business; any other property is an external benefit. Choose the rule that applies to the property. Under a salary packaging arrangement an in-house benefit takes the notional value. Goods you make take the lowest arm’s length price where you sell identical goods to the trade, 75% of the lowest public price where you sell them to the public, and otherwise 75% of the notional value. Goods you buy for your business take the lesser of the arm’s length purchase price and the notional value. The calculator takes the employee’s contribution off, never below nil, and shows any excess, which does not reduce another benefit.

Where the employee would have had a once-only income tax deduction had they bought the property themselves, the otherwise deductible rule reduces the value. The gross deduction is the value before the contribution × the share of use that would be deductible; the notional deduction is that less the deduction for the contribution. For an in-house benefit outside salary packaging, the calculator then takes off the part of the employee’s $1,000 in-house reduction for the year that their other in-house benefits have not used.

This page opens on the desk example in section 17.3 of the ATO’s guide for employers: a desk you make and sell to the public for at least $900, which an employee buys for $500, has a taxable value of 75% × $900 − $500 = $175. The tests also hold the guide’s other section 17.3 and 17.5 examples.

What the FBT property calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a property fringe benefit arises, whether property is in-house, or whether an exemption applies, such as property consumed on your premises on a working day (section 41), a minor benefit (section 58P) or an eligible work-related item such as a portable electronic device (section 58X). It does not value airline transport (paragraph 42(1)(ab)), a car property benefit, whose otherwise deductible amount follows the car substantiation rules, a benefit provided jointly to an employee and an associate, whose reduction is limited to the employee’s interest, or an expense payment, which has its own calculator. It does not check the declaration the otherwise deductible rule needs (paragraph 44(1)(c)) or work out the employee’s deduction. It values one benefit: the $1,000 in-house reduction is for all of an employee’s in-house fringe benefits in the year, so enter what the others have not used. It stops at the taxable value and does not gross it up or work out FBT. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s guide or a registered tax agent.
