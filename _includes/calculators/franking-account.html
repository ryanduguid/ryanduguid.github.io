Sources applied

  • The account: s 205-15 (credits, and the R&D deferral in subsection (4)), s 205-30 (debits), s 205-40, s 205-45 and s 205-50 (franking deficit tax, and refunds received in the 3 months after the year’s end) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997, read on the ATO Legal Database 19 November 2026. For deficit tax, a refund that would put the account into deficit or increase the deficit is treated as received just before year end.
  • Distributions and the benchmark rule: s 202-60 and s 202-65 (the maximum franking credit, the distribution over the gross-up rate, and the credit capped at it), s 203-20 to s 203-55 (the exemption, franking periods, the benchmark and the consequences of departing from it) and s 204-70 (significant variations), read on the ATO Legal Database 19 November 2026. A private company’s franking period is its income year; any other entity’s is each half of it. Over-franking tax and the under-franking debit are each the distribution times the percentage differential over the gross-up rate.
  • The offset: s 205-70, read on the ATO Legal Database 19 November 2026. Step 1 takes 30% off the part of the tax attributable to item 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 debits where it exceeds 10% of the year’s credits; there is no reduction without an item 1, 3, 5 or 6 debit, for a private company in its first year of income tax liability where that liability is at least 90% of the deficit, or under the Commissioner’s determination. The part is taken by the method in section B of the ATO’s instructions. The offset used is the least of it, with any brought forward, and the income tax left after the offsets applied first.
  • The taxes: the New Business Tax System (Franking Deficit Tax) Act 2002 and (Over-franking Tax) Act 2002, section 5 of each, read on the Federal Register of Legislation 19 November 2026. Due dates: s 214-150 and s 214-45. The ordinary deadline is 31 July. When a refund increases deficit tax, a separate return and payment are due 14 days after receipt if no return is outstanding. If an annual return is outstanding and the refund arrives in the 14 days ending on its due date, you can include the refund in that return or lodge a further return within 14 days of receipt.
  • The return: ATO: Franking account tax return 2026 instructions (published 30 May 2026), read 19 November 2026: labels A to F and K, codes S, D, F and P, and the examples the tests check: EKW Ltd (example 4), Salomon Pty Ltd (example 5), RI Pty Ltd (example 2) and XYZ, Dombey, Isanini and Acolmal (section C).
  • Supplied by you: the entity’s kind and corporate tax rate, each event and its type, the R&D part of a refund, each distribution and the credit allocated to it, and the earlier benchmark. Amounts are worked exactly and shown half up to the cent; the return’s whole dollars are not worked out.

It keeps the year’s account and the franking return’s figures; it does not lodge the return or decide what may be franked.

How the franking account calculator works

Each PAYG instalment, income tax payment and franking credit received credits the account on its day, and each franked distribution, refund and other debit takes it down. A deficit at 30 June gives rise to franking deficit tax. When you enter that deficit as next year’s opening balance, the calculator adds the matching credit on 1 July. The tax is also an offset against the company’s income tax, reduced by 30% of the part caused by its own distributions and refunds when that part is more than 10% of the year’s credits. The first distribution in each franking period sets the benchmark: one franked above it gives over-franking tax, one below it an extra debit, and a benchmark that moves too far from the last must be disclosed. A franking credit received is entered as the credit, not the dividend, and the offset is applied only when the income tax is given.

When a later refund changes the deficit tax, use the actual 30 June balance and R&D deferral for next year’s opening figures. In next year’s account, enter the refund on its receipt date and enter the additional franking deficit tax from that refund as a separate credit on that date.

What the franking account calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one entity’s income year from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026 or from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, and no advice. It does not cover:

  • consolidated groups, an entity that stops being a franking entity, New Zealand franking companies, pooled development funds, friendly societies, life insurance companies, exempting entities, a substituted accounting period or late balancer, and diverted profits tax;
  • whether a distribution is frankable, what the company may frank it to, or whether a statement given after a distribution was allowed (s 202-75);
  • a tax offset refund other than an income tax refund’s R&D part, the general interest charge on late franking tax, and the return’s amounts in whole dollars;
  • the company’s tax rate and maximum franking credit from turnover, which the company tax and franking calculator works out.

Confirm the figures with the ATO’s instructions or a registered tax agent before relying on them.
