How the foreign resident capital gains withholding calculator works

A purchaser of taxable Australian real property or a company title interest must pay the ATO an amount at settlement unless every vendor gives a clearance certificate (section 14-210 of Schedule 1 to the Taxation Administration Act 1953). The calculator ports the foreign-resident-withholding command of australian-accounting. The amount is the rate times the first element of the asset’s cost base, less any amount paid for an option you exercised to buy it (section 14-200(3)): 12.5% for a contract from 1 July 2017, when a market value under $750,000 is excluded, and 15% with no threshold for a contract from 1 January 2025.

With several vendors, each vendor’s share of the base follows their interest. A vendor with a clearance certificate takes nil, one with a notice of variation its varied rate (unless the sale price is above the notice’s maximum), and one with neither the full rate. With no document at all the single amount is rounded once and its cents shared out. An excluded transaction (section 14-215) or a class variation to nil gives nothing to pay.

What the foreign resident capital gains withholding calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a vendor is a foreign resident, whether a clearance certificate or variation is valid, or whether an exclusion applies: you enter those as given. It does not cover an indirect Australian real property interest other than a company title interest, an option or right to acquire one, look-through earnouts (section 14-205), contracts before 1 July 2017 or after 30 June 2027, or other withholding such as interest, dividends, royalties and managed investment trust payments. Where a notice’s maximum sale price falls between the base and the sale price, it refuses, as whether the notice applies turns on whether the maximum includes GST. The page takes 3 vendors; its WebMCP tool takes up to 50. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.
