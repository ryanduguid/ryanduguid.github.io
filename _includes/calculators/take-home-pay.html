Sources applied

  • Income tax, the low income tax offset, the low and middle income tax offset for 2021-22, the Medicare levy and the Medicare levy surcharge: as the income tax calculator works them out and cites them, on the salary in whole dollars, less any amount sacrificed and any standard deduction, as taxable income. Without cover all year, the surcharge is a single person’s for every day of the year.
  • Standard deduction for work-related expenses, from 2026-27: section 25-130 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (Compilation No. 268, 1 October 2026), read 17 November 2026: an Australian resident with assessable labour income deducts the lesser of $1,000 and that income, less the work-related deductions that section 25-130(2) lists; under section 25-130(3) premiums for income protection, sickness or accident insurance and trade, business or professional association fees do not reduce it. With the salary less any sacrifice as the only income and no deductions claimed, it is the lesser of $1,000 and that amount. ATO: Standard deduction for work-related expenses (last updated 2 November 2026), read 17 November 2026, says the ATO applies it automatically from the 2026-27 return. A foreign resident, and a working holiday maker taken as one, gets none.
  • Salary sacrificed to super: ATO: Salary sacrificing super (last updated 21 April 2026), read 18 November 2026: the amount sacrificed is not assessable income and is not subject to PAYG withholding, it counts in reportable super contributions, and the employer must still pay the full super guarantee as though there were no sacrifice (for 2026-27, qualifying earnings include it under section 10A(1)(h) of the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992). So the sacrifice, in whole dollars, joins repayment income for a study loan and income for surcharge purposes.
  • Concessional contributions cap: section 291-20 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (Compilation No. 268), read 18 November 2026, with the general cap from ATO: Contributions caps (Table 1.1, last updated 11 September 2026): $32,500 for 2026-27, $30,000 for 2024-25 and 2025-26 and $27,500 for 2021-22 to 2023-24. The super guarantee and the amount sacrificed are concessional contributions (section 291-25). Above the cap the calculator says so and assumes unused cap amounts carried forward under section 291-20(3) cover the excess; it does not work excess concessional contributions.
  • Division 293: section 293-20 of the same Act: when income for surcharge purposes, less reportable super contributions, plus the concessional contributions exceeds $250,000, the lesser of the contributions and the excess are taxable contributions. The calculator names that amount and does not work the tax, which is assessed separately; the Division 293 tax calculator works it.
  • PAYG withholding, 2026-27 only: Schedule 1, Coefficients to use in formulas for withholding from weekly payments, Using a formula and Working out the weekly earnings, and Schedule 8 for the study loan component (each published 17 June 2026, for payments from 1 July 2026), read 18 November 2026. Scale 2 (tax-free threshold claimed) for a resident and scale 3 for a foreign resident: y = ax − b on the weekly equivalent of each pay, in whole dollars plus 99 cents, rounded to the nearest dollar, doubled for a fortnight and times 13 ÷ 3 for a month. Each pay is the year’s salary less the sacrifice, divided by 52, 26 or 12 to the cent. The results match all 288 scale 2 and scale 3 amounts in the ATO’s withholding amounts sample data.
  • Study and training loan repayment: as the study and training loan repayment calculator works it out and cites it, with the same taxable income and the amount sacrificed as repayment income.
  • Super guarantee: for 2026-27, the charge percentage of 12 in section 17A(2) of the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 (Compilation No. 78, 1 July 2026), read 17 November 2026, on qualifying earnings up to the maximum contributions base of $270,830 for the year (section 10A(5)); for 2021-22 to 2025-26, 10%, 10.5%, 11%, 11.5% and 12% on ordinary time earnings up to a quarterly base of $58,920, $60,220, $62,270, $65,070 and $62,500, which is 4 times that for a year paid evenly. These are the general rates, not the Norfolk Island transitional rates. Rates and bases from ATO: Super guarantee (Tables 21, 23 and 24, last updated 17 April 2026), read 18 November 2026, and this site’s super guarantee and maximum contribution base tables. Table 24 starts at 2021-22, so 2020-21 is not offered here.
  • A package including super splits so that salary and super add back to it: super is the rate ÷ (100 + the rate) of the package, or the most the base allows. Each amount rounds half up to the cent.
  • Supplied by you: the year, your residency, the salary, its pay period and whether it includes super, the amount sacrificed, whether you have a loan and whether you had hospital cover.

How the take-home pay calculator works

The take-home pay calculator takes one salary for a year, month, fortnight or week and works out the year’s income tax, offsets, Medicare levy, any Medicare levy surcharge and any study or training loan repayment on it. Salary sacrificed to super comes off first. From 2026-27 it also takes off the standard deduction of up to $1,000 for work-related expenses. It then gives what is left for the year, a month, a fortnight and a week, and the super guarantee an employer must pay, which goes to a super fund and is not part of take-home pay.

Those take-home amounts are the year’s tax spread evenly: the year’s net amount divided by 12, 26 and 52. They are not the amounts an employer withholds each pay. For a weekly, fortnightly or monthly pay in 2026-27, the calculator also works the PAYG amount withheld from one pay under the ATO’s Schedule 1, and Schedule 8 with a study loan, and the year’s withholding less the year’s tax, which is the refund or amount owing when the tax return is assessed. For earlier years, and for working holiday makers, who are withheld from under another schedule, use the ATO’s tax withheld calculator.

The example on arrival is 2025-26, before the standard deduction: on $75,000 the Medicare levy is $1,500, as the ATO’s example of Matthew on What is the Medicare levy gives. In 2026-27 the same salary is $74,000 of taxable income.

When the salary includes super, the calculator splits it: at 12%, a package of $112,000 is $100,000 of salary and $12,000 of super. Super guarantee stops at the year’s maximum contribution base, so above it the super stays at the most the base allows.

What the take-home pay calculator does not compute

The calculator assumes one employer, a salary paid evenly over the full year, and no other income, deductions or offsets. It does not compute:

  • overtime, bonuses, allowances or leave loading, a 53rd weekly or 27th fortnightly pay, or withholding on scale 1, scale 5 or 6 or for a working holiday maker, a withholding declaration’s offsets or a Medicare levy variation;
  • work-related deductions you claim, which reduce the standard deduction dollar for dollar except those section 25-130(3) disregards, such as professional association fees;
  • salary sacrifice of anything but super, reportable fringe benefits, excess concessional contributions or Division 293 tax;
  • everything the income tax calculator leaves out, such as the Medicare levy reduction for families and the seniors and pensioners tax offset;
  • a family’s surcharge: this calculator works out the surcharge for a single person with no dependants, and the income tax calculator works out a family’s;
  • a study loan debt smaller than the repayment, which section 154-20(1)(c) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003 caps at what is still owed;
  • whether an employee is entitled to super guarantee, the lower transitional rate for employees on Norfolk Island (11% for 2026-27), or a second employer’s separate maximum contribution base;
  • super guarantee pay by pay: the calculator works it on the year’s earnings, rounded once, where an employer works it on each payday from 2026-27, so the year’s total can differ by cents, and to 2025-26 on each quarter’s earnings, so above the base a weekly or fortnightly salary with more pays in some quarters gets less: $260,000 paid fortnightly in 2025-26 can give $29,400, not $30,000.
