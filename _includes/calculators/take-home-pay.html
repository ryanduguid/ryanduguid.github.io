How the take-home pay calculator works

The take-home pay calculator takes one annual salary and works out the year’s income tax, low income tax offset, Medicare levy and any study or training loan repayment on it. From 2026-27 it first takes off the standard deduction of up to $1,000 for work-related expenses. It then gives what is left for the year, a month, a fortnight and a week. It also gives the super guarantee an employer must pay on that salary, which is paid to a super fund and is not part of take-home pay.

These are the year’s tax on that income spread evenly: the year’s net amount divided by 12, 26 and 52. They are not the amounts an employer withholds each pay under the ATO’s PAYG withholding schedules, which round each pay’s withholding and can differ from the year’s tax, with the difference settled when the tax return is assessed. Use the ATO’s tax withheld calculator for the amount withheld each pay. The example on arrival is 2025-26, before the standard deduction: on $75,000 the Medicare levy is $1,500, as the ATO’s example of Matthew on What is the Medicare levy gives. In 2026-27 the same salary is $74,000 of taxable income.

When the salary includes super, the calculator splits it: at 12%, a package of $112,000 is $100,000 of salary and $12,000 of super. Super guarantee stops at the year’s maximum contribution base, so above it the super stays at the most the base allows.

What the take-home pay calculator does not compute

The calculator assumes one employer, a salary paid evenly over the full year, and no other income, deductions, offsets or salary sacrifice. It does not compute:

  • the PAYG amount withheld each pay, overtime, bonuses, allowances or leave loading;
  • work-related deductions you claim, which reduce the standard deduction dollar for dollar;
  • salary sacrifice, reportable fringe benefits, or reportable super contributions, which would change repayment income;
  • everything the income tax calculator leaves out, such as the Medicare levy reduction for families, the Medicare levy surcharge and the seniors and pensioners tax offset;
  • whether an employee is entitled to super guarantee, or a second employer’s separate maximum contribution base.

Confirm the figures with the ATO’s Simple tax calculator or a registered tax agent.
