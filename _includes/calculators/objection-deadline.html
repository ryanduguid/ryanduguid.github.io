Income tax only: does item 1, 2, 3 or 3A of the table in subsection 170(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 apply to the assessment?

Item 1 covers most individuals, and items 2 and 3 companies and trusts that are small or medium business entities; each excludes, for example, a scheme to obtain a tax benefit. Item 3A covers their requests to amend. Which item applies is not decided here.

Sources applied

  • Periods: section 14ZW of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 (Compilation No. 227, 1 October 2026), read 15 November 2026. An income tax assessment allows 2 years after notice if item 1, 2, 3 or 3A of the table in subsection 170(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 applies, read 15 November 2026, and 4 years otherwise (paragraph 14ZW(1)(aa)); an FBT assessment allows 4 years (paragraph (1)(aaa)); any other decision allows 60 days after notice is served (paragraph (1)(c)). An amended assessment allows the later of 60 days after its notice and the original period after the first assessment’s notice (subsections (1B), (1BA) and (1C)).
  • Counting: under item 6 of subsection 36(1) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 (Compilation No. 39, 28 March 2026), read 15 November 2026, a period that begins after a day does not include that day. The Act does not define a year: this checker counts a period of years to the day before the same date in the later year, and a period that starts on 29 February to 28 February. Under subsection 36(2), a last day that is a Saturday, Sunday or holiday moves to the next day that is none of these; subsection 36(3) makes a holiday a public holiday in the place where the thing is done, or a day the office where it is done is closed for the whole day.
  • Late objections: subsections 14ZW(2) and (3) allow an objection after the period with a written request stating fully and in detail the circumstances and reasons for the delay; section 14ZX has the Commissioner agree or refuse, and a refusal can go to the Tribunal.
  • Supplied by you: the decision, which subsection 170(1) item applies and the notice dates. Whether the decision can be objected to, when notice was given and public holidays where you lodge are not checked here.

How the objection deadline checker works

The objection deadline checker gives the last day to lodge a taxation objection under section 14ZW of the Taxation Administration Act 1953. An income tax assessment allows 2 years after notice of the assessment is given when item 1, 2, 3 or 3A of the table in subsection 170(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 applies, and 4 years otherwise. An FBT assessment allows 4 years. Any other decision for which the section sets no other period allows 60 days after notice is served.

An amended assessment allows whichever ends later: 60 days after notice of the amended assessment, or the 2 or 4 years after notice of the first assessment for that year. The checker shows both dates and the later one.

Each period starts the day after notice, because subsection 36(1) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 leaves out the day a period begins after. A 2-year period from notice given on 28 February 2026 therefore starts on 1 March 2026 and ends on 29 February 2028. When the last day is a Saturday or Sunday, subsection 36(2) lets the objection be lodged on the next day that is not a weekend or holiday. The Act’s own example: a person who has until Saturday 31 March may apply on Monday 2 April.

What the objection deadline checker does not cover

The checker does not decide whether a decision can be objected to, which item of subsection 170(1) applies, or when notice was given, so keep the notice’s issue and receipt dates. It does not know public holidays: they differ by state and town, so check the last day and the day after it where you lodge. It does not cover private rulings, administrative penalties, retained refunds, deceased estates, specialised taxes or the other decisions for which section 14ZW sets its own period, or the time to seek review of an objection decision. Confirm the date with the notice of decision or a registered tax agent.
