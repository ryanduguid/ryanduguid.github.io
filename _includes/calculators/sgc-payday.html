Sources applied

  • Payday Super: sections 6(1) (business day, usual period, extended usual period, late period), 10A(5) and (6), 16B, 17A, 18C, 18D, 19A and 19B of the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 (Compilation No. 78, 1 July 2026), read 18 November 2026. The SG amount is 12% of qualifying earnings up to the year’s maximum contributions base; contributions the fund receives within the due period reduce it to the base shortfall; late contributions received before the assessment reduce that to the final shortfall. Notional earnings compound daily on the base shortfall at the GIC rate for each day, from the day after the due date to the day before the assessment, or to the day a late contribution clears the shortfall.
  • Due dates: section 18C(2) items 1 to 3 of the same Act, and the worked examples in ATO: Payment deadlines for Payday Super (last updated 29 September 2026), read 18 November 2026. The Node tests reproduce each of its due dates.
  • Administrative uplift: 60% (section 19B), less 20 percentage points if, in the 24 months ending on the payday, no ATO-initiated assessment is in force and no estimate has been made (regulation 13C, which starts that period on 1 July 2026 for paydays to 30 June 2028), and less 40, 35, 30 or 15 points for a voluntary disclosure statement lodged before the assessment within the 30, 60 or 120-day period starting on the payday, or later (regulation 13D), in the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Regulations 2018 (Compilation No. 8, 1 July 2026), read 18 November 2026.
  • Worked examples: ATO: What happens if you don’t pay super correctly (last updated 21 June 2026) and ATO: Making a voluntary disclosure for Payday Super (last updated 1 July 2026), both read 18 November 2026. Its Juliet example treats a statement lodged 37 days after the payday as within the 30-day period; this page follows regulation 13D, which counts from the payday.
  • Figures: the 12% charge percentage and the $270,830 maximum contributions base for 2026-27 (Table 23), and the quarterly maximum contribution bases of $60,220, $62,270, $65,070 and $62,500 for 2022-23 to 2025-26 (Table 24), from ATO: Key superannuation rates and thresholds, super guarantee (last updated 17 April 2026), read 18 November 2026. GIC rates to December 2026 from ATO: General interest charge rates (last updated 4 September 2026), read 18 November 2026; each day’s rate is the annual rate divided by the days in its calendar year (section 8AAD of the Taxation Administration Act 1953).
  • Transition: Schedule 1 items 182 and 186 of the Treasury Laws Amendment (Payday Superannuation) Act 2025, read 18 November 2026: the new law applies to paydays from 1 July 2026, and the old law, as in force on a quarter’s last day, to quarters ending before then.
  • Quarterly super: sections 6(1) (ordinary time earnings), 17, 19, 23, 31, 32 and 46 of the Act as at 27 March 2026 (Compilation No. 77), and regulation 13 of the Regulations as at 18 October 2022 (Compilation No. 7), both read 18 November 2026. The shortfall is the charge percentage of salary or wages up to the quarterly base, with the percentage reduced by contributions as a share of ordinary time earnings; nominal interest is 10% a year from the quarter’s first day to the day the charge is payable, the later of the lodgment day (the 28th of the second month after the quarter) and the day the statement is lodged; the administration component is $20. The method and the Imogen example follow ATO: Module 6, rectifying late payments (last updated 16 March 2026) and the ATO: Guide to completing the super guarantee charge statement (last updated 25 June 2026), both read 18 November 2026.
  • Supplied by you: the payday or quarter, the earnings, the contributions and when the fund received them, the assessment and disclosure dates and whether the ATO has assessed this employer before. Amounts are worked exactly in cents; the SG amount, notional earnings, uplift, shortfall and interest each round half up to the cent once.

How the SGC and Payday Super calculator works

From 1 July 2026, under Payday Super, an employer owes super guarantee for each payday, and the employee’s fund must receive it within 7 business days, or a longer period for a first contribution, out-of-cycle pay or an ATO determination. The calculator finds that due date from the site’s business days calendar, then works out the super guarantee charge for one employee if the fund received less than 12% of qualifying earnings by then: the final shortfall still unpaid at the assessment, notional earnings at the GIC rate on the amount that was late, and the administrative uplift, reduced for a first offence and a voluntary disclosure. For a quarter that ended by 30 June 2026 it applies the old law instead: the shortfall at that year’s charge percentage, 10% nominal interest and the $20 administration component.

What the SGC and Payday Super calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employee and one payday or quarter, and no advice. It does not cover:

  • the choice loading for not meeting choice of fund obligations;
  • the late payment offset under the old section 23A for quarterly contributions made late;
  • bunched contributions under section 18C(2) item 4;
  • a disclosed contribution with no receipt day, whose notional earnings section 36(3) works out as if the fund received it 7 business days after it was paid;
  • exemption certificates, and amounts paid that have not reached the fund;
  • GIC and penalties once the charge is payable;
  • paydays after 30 June 2027, whose maximum contributions base is not yet known, and quarters before July 2022.

Confirm the figure with the ATO or a registered tax agent before relying on it.
