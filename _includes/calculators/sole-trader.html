Sources applied

  • Income tax, the low income tax offset, the low and middle income tax offset for 2021-22, the Medicare levy and the Medicare levy surcharge: as the income tax calculator works them out and cites them, on the year’s taxable income in whole dollars, with the personal super contributions you claim, which are reportable super contributions, in income for surcharge purposes. Without cover all year, the surcharge is a single person’s for every day of the year.
  • Small business income tax offset: sections 328-355 to 328-375 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (Compilation No. 268, 1 October 2026), read 19 November 2026. Section 328-360(1) makes the offset 16% of total net small business income over taxable income, times the basic income tax liability, which is the tax on taxable income before any offset (section 4-10(3), step 2); section 328-360(2) caps it at $1,000. Total net small business income is the net small business income up to the taxable income. Net small business income is the business income less the deductions attributable to it (section 328-365), leaving out tax-related expenses, gifts and personal super contributions (section 328-370), so the fees, gifts and contributions you enter reduce taxable income but not the offset’s share. A business qualifies when its aggregated turnover is under $5 million (section 328-357) and its income is not personal services income outside a personal services business (section 328-365(1)(a)(ii)). Like the low income tax offsets it cannot take the tax below nil (section 63-10). ATO: Small business income tax offset (last updated 7 May 2025), read 19 November 2026, gives the rate of 16% for 2021-22 onwards.
  • Standard deduction for work-related expenses, from 2026-27: section 25-130 of the same Act: a resident deducts the lesser of $1,000 and assessable labour income, which section 25-130(4) limits to salary, wages and other payments that must be withheld from, so business income does not count. With no work-related deductions claimed, it is the lesser of $1,000 and the wages.
  • Study and training loan repayment: as the study and training loan repayment calculator works it out and cites it, with taxable income plus the personal super contributions you claim as repayment income (ATO: Income tests, last updated 8 June 2026, read 19 November 2026).
  • The business’s share: the year’s total tax, levies and repayment less the same worked on the wages, fees, gifts and contributions alone. That is the tax the business adds, on the assumption that the amount withheld from the wages covers the tax, levy and any study loan repayment on the wages alone, as it would for one job with the tax-free threshold claimed.
  • GST: sections 9-70 and 9-75 of the A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999 (Compilation No. 96, 1 January 2026), read 19 November 2026: GST is 10% of the value of a taxable supply, so the GST on the business income excluding GST is a tenth of it, and an invoice including GST holds 1/11 of its price as GST. The GST credits you enter come off it. ATO: Registering for GST (last updated 14 September 2026), read 19 November 2026: a business must register within 21 days once its GST turnover is $75,000 or more over this month and the previous 11, or is expected to be over this month and the next 11 (section 23-15 and the regulations); the calculator says so when a year’s business income is that much and the business is not registered.

Each amount rounds half up to the cent and each share to 2 decimal places.

How the sole trader tax set-aside calculator works

The calculator takes a year’s business income and expenses, any wages from a job and any tax agent fees, gifts or personal super contributions you claim. It works out the year’s income tax, the offsets, the Medicare levy, any Medicare levy surcharge and any study or training loan repayment. It then works the same on the wages alone. The difference is the tax the business adds, which nobody withholds, so it is the amount to set aside from business income.

The small business income tax offset is worked as section 328-360 sets it: 16% of the share of the tax on taxable income that the business profit makes up. On arrival the example shows it below its $1,000 cap: $20,000 of profit is 20/79 of $79,000 of taxable income, so the offset is 16% of 20/79 of $14,220, which is $576.00. The business also costs the low income tax offset the wages alone would have had, $115.00, which the difference picks up.

A business registered for GST also sets aside the GST on its income less its GST credits, paid with each activity statement. The calculator gives one share to keep from every invoice, covering both, and the year’s amount split by quarter, month and week. It also gives the tax as a share of the business income excluding GST, which as a PAYG instalment rate would cover this year’s tax on it.

What the sole trader tax set-aside calculator does not compute

The calculator is for an Australian resident aged 18 or over with one business as a sole trader, a full year of income and no dependants. It does not compute:

  • a business loss or losses carried forward; whether Division 35 defers a loss is worked out below;
  • personal services income rules, which can deny deductions: see the PSI rules checker;
  • GST-free or input-taxed sales, GST on a cash or accruals basis by period, or a GST refund when credits exceed the GST on income;
  • other income such as interest, dividends, rent or capital gains, or partnership and trust distributions, which change both the tax and the offset’s share, or reportable fringe benefits and salary sacrificed to super from the job, which count in repayment income and income for surcharge purposes;
  • work-related deductions against the wages, which reduce the standard deduction from 2026-27, or a job withheld from at the wrong rate;
  • super guarantee, which a sole trader does not pay for themselves, or personal super contributions beyond entering them as a deduction;
  • a family’s Medicare levy, levy reduction or surcharge, or the seniors and pensioners tax offset;
  • notional tax or the instalment rate the ATO sets, or when each instalment and activity statement is due;
  • a study loan debt smaller than the repayment, which section 154-20(1)(c) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003 caps at what is still owed;
  • 2027-28, whose working Australians tax offset (Subdivision 61-E, added by Act No. 49 of 2026) applies from that year.
