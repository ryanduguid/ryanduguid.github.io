How the blackhole expenditure calculator works

Section 40-880 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 lets a business deduct capital expenditure that no other provision deals with, often called blackhole expenditure: the costs of setting up, restructuring or winding up a business. The calculator ports the business-capital-expenditure command of australian-accounting. For each item it takes the expenditure to the extent of the business’s taxable purpose (subsections (3) and (4)), then deducts a fifth in the year incurred and in each of the next 4 income years (subsection (2)). A start-up cost of a proposed business, meaning advice or services on its structure or operation or a government agency’s fee, tax or charge, is deducted in full in the year incurred by a small business entity, an entity covered by subsection (2B), or an entity not in business and not connected with one (subsection (2A)).

An item for another entity’s former or proposed business, or for winding up a company, partnership or trust, is deductible only in connection with deriving your assessable income from that business (subsection (4)(b)). A proposed business must be reasonably expected to start within a reasonable time (subsection (7)). An exclusion in subsection (5), (8) or (9) leaves nothing to deduct, except that subsection (6) lifts paragraphs (5)(d) and (f) for expenditure preserving goodwill. The result gives each item’s deduction for the year you choose and what is left for later years.

What the blackhole expenditure calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether an outlay is capital, whether an exclusion applies, whether you are a small business entity or the business’s taxable purpose: you enter those as established. Expenditure that forms part of the cost of a depreciating asset (paragraph (5)(a)) belongs to Division 40, and expenditure that a capital gain or loss takes into account (paragraph (5)(f)) to the CGT provisions; the calculator refuses both. It does not cover expenditure incurred before 1 July 2020, when subsection (2A) had another form, or what happens to the fifths left when a business ceases or is sold within the 5 years. The page takes 2 items; its WebMCP tool takes any number. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.
