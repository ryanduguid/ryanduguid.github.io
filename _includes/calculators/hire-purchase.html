Sources applied

  • Schedule: the same formula and rounding as the loan repayment schedule. Each payment’s interest is the period rate times the balance since the last payment, rounded half up to the cent; the last payment takes the cents that rounding leaves, so the schedule closes on the balloon exactly.
  • Hire purchase and income tax: Division 240 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997, sections 240-10, 240-20, 240-25 and 240-60, read 18 November 2026 in the ATO’s legal database. The Division applies to a hire purchase agreement for any goods. The financier is taken to sell the goods to the hirer at the start, and the hirer to own them; the financier is taken to lend the hirer the notional loan principal, the price less what was paid at or before the start, and the payments repay it and pay its interest. Each period’s notional interest is the outstanding principal times the implicit interest rate worked out from the payments and any termination amount, and a period that falls partly in an income year counts in it as generally accepted accounting principles relate it.
  • Income years: the page shares a period that runs across 30 June by days, the days before 1 July rounded half up to the cent. That is this page’s choice for subsection 240-60(2), not a rule the Act states.
  • GST on hire purchase: ATO: GST, hire purchase and leasing (last updated 6 April 2017), read 18 November 2026. For an agreement entered into on or after 1 July 2012 every component is taxable, the credit component included. A buyer that accounts on a non-cash basis claims the full GST credit in the tax period of the first payment, or of the tax invoice if that comes first; a buyer on a cash basis can claim it up front in the same way. For an agreement entered into before 1 July 2012 the credit is for the principal component only.
  • GST on a chattel mortgage: ATO GST issues register, Financial services: questions and answers, issues 1.20 and 1.21 (updated 2 May 2013), read 18 November 2026. The buyer takes title at the purchase. On a non-cash basis the whole GST credit falls in the tax period the invoice is received or any payment is made, whichever is earlier; on a cash basis, in the period the borrowed funds pay for the goods in full.
  • Cars: subsection 69-10(1) of the GST Act, read 18 November 2026: where a car’s GST-inclusive market value exceeds the car limit for the financial year it is first used, the GST credit is the GST up to 1/11 of that limit. ATO: Purchasing a motor vehicle (last updated 3 June 2026), read 18 November 2026, gives $6,353 for 2026-27 (1/11 × $69,883) and lists the exceptions. Issue 1.17 of the register above applies the limit to a car bought on hire purchase.
  • Figures: supplied by you. The interest rate is yours or the agreement’s, not a market rate.

How the hire purchase and chattel mortgage schedule works

The calculator sets out a hire purchase agreement or a chattel mortgage payment by payment from the amount financed, weekly to yearly, in arrears or in advance, with any balloon or residual. Give any 3 of the regular payment, the amount financed, the number of payments and the annual nominal rate, and it works out the fourth. Under Division 240 the interest in each payment is the hirer’s notional interest at the implicit rate; working out the rate from the agreement’s payments finds that rate.

The calculator totals the interest, the finance charge, by income year from 1 July to 30 June, and sets out a journal for each payment: debit the liability with the principal, debit interest expense with the interest and credit the bank with the payment. The balloon has its own line. Download schedule CSV saves the schedule, the income years and the journal as plain figures.

The australian-accounting command hire-purchase-schedule works the same agreement monthly. It keeps every instalment as the agreement states it and puts the cents of rounding into each month’s interest, and it lists a month that runs across 30 June apart rather than sharing it. This page keeps the interest of each period rounded on its own and puts the cents into the last payment, so on the same agreement the two can differ by a few cents.

What the hire purchase and chattel mortgage schedule does not cover

The schedule does not work out GST, the GST credit or the car limit: it starts from the amount financed. Record the asset at its cost and the liability at the amount financed when the agreement starts; a deposit, trade-in, fees and stamp duty are yours to add. It does not state the income tax treatment of a chattel mortgage, which the sources applied do not cover, or of an early payout. It assumes a rate that does not change and payments on the same day of each period.

Leases are not covered: a lessee’s lease under AASB 16 is not worked here. Confirm the treatment with the agreement, the financier’s statements and a registered tax agent.
