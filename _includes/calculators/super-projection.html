Sources applied

  • Retirement age and today’s dollars: the definitions of ‘assumed retirement age’ and ‘present value’ in section 4 of ASIC Corporations (Superannuation Calculators and Retirement Estimates) Instrument 2022/603 (Compilation No. 1, 11 September 2024), read 19 November 2026: retirement at 67 unless you enter another age, and a future amount divided by (1 + 0.037) to the power of the years, the 3.7% reflecting long-term nominal wage growth in the 2023 Intergenerational Report. Section 7 sets the conditions for a calculator: you can change every assumption that is not fixed by law, and each is stated with why it is reasonable.
  • Investment returns, fees and contributions: ASIC’s Regulatory Guide 276 (September 2024), read 19 November 2026: default returns, fees and costs must be reasonable and may come from generic assumptions (RG 276.124 to 276.136), and a retirement estimate should assume contributions change with wage inflation (Table 3), as this calculator does.
  • The defaults for return, fees and premiums: the assumptions ASIC states for its own superannuation calculator (last updated 27 October 2026), read 19 November 2026: 6.1% a year after tax and investment fees for a balanced option, from the Willis Towers Watson Global Asset Model outlook at June 2026, and administration fees of $59 a year plus 0.11% of the balance and premiums of $599 a year, from APRA statistics at March 2026.
  • Contributions: the super guarantee of 12% and the 2026-27 maximum contribution base of $270,830, as the take-home pay calculator cites them; the 15% tax on concessional contributions, which are the fund’s assessable income under section 295-160 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 and taxed at 15% under section 26(1) of the Income Tax Rates Act 1986; the 2026-27 caps of $32,500 concessional and $130,000 non-concessional, from the ATO’s Contributions caps (Tables 1.1 and 4, last updated 11 September 2026), read 19 November 2026.
  • Supplied by you: your age, retirement age, balance, salary, contributions, return, fees, premiums, wage growth and the present value rate.

How the super projection calculator works

The super projection calculator projects one accumulation super account from 2026-27 to the age you retire, a year at a time, and gives the balance then in future dollars and at its present value, in the dollars of today’s wages. Each year your employer contributes its rate of your salary, up to the maximum contribution base, and your own contributions are added. The fund takes 15% contributions tax from the employer and salary sacrifice contributions, takes out the dollar fee, the percentage fee and the premiums, and claims them as a deduction, which saves 15% of them. The return and the percentage fee are worked on the balance at the start of the year and on half the year’s contributions less the dollar fee and premiums, as they come in through the year. After the first year, your salary, contributions, fees and premiums grow at the wage growth rate.

The present value divides the balance at retirement once by 1 plus the present value rate (3.7% unless you change it) to the power of the years projected, rounded to the cent, so that it reads against today’s wages. The calculator is general information about super, not advice: it shows the effect of the figures you enter and does not recommend a fund, an investment option or a contribution.

The assumptions and why they are reasonable

  • Retirement at 67: the default ASIC’s Instrument 2022/603 sets for anyone younger. Super is preserved until 60, so an earlier age is not accepted.
  • Today’s dollars at 3.7% a year: the rate the Instrument sets, the long-term growth in wages the Treasury’s 2023 Intergenerational Report expects, so the result compares with what wages buy now.
  • Wage growth of 3.7% a year for your salary, contributions, fees and premiums: the same long-term forecast, as ASIC’s guide expects contributions to rise with wages.
  • A return of 6.1% a year after investment fees and tax on earnings, steady every year: ASIC’s assumption for a balanced investment option. Real returns vary and can be negative in some years; your fund’s option may have a different mix.
  • Administration fees of $59 a year and 0.11% of the balance, and insurance premiums of $599 a year: ASIC’s assumptions from industry statistics. Your statement shows your own.
  • Employer contributions at the 12% super guarantee on salary up to the $270,830 maximum contribution base for 2026-27, which is assumed to grow with wages.
  • 15% contributions tax and the fund’s deduction for fees and premiums, as the law now stands; tax and super law are assumed not to change.
  • Contributions within the 2026-27 caps, with the caps assumed to rise with wages after that.

What the super projection calculator does not compute

The projection covers one accumulation account from 2026-27, with the rules of that year assumed to continue. It does not compute:

  • defined benefit interests, or an account already paying a pension or a transition to retirement income stream;
  • income in retirement, the age pension or how long the balance would last;
  • the low income super tax offset, the government co-contribution or spouse contributions, which would add to the balance of someone on a lower income;
  • Division 293 tax for income over $250,000, excess contributions, the nil non-concessional cap for a total super balance at the transfer balance cap, or tax on a withdrawal;
  • years of good and bad returns, career breaks, or contribution, activity, switching and adviser fees, any of which can lower the balance.

Use your fund’s own retirement estimate or a financial adviser for a forecast you would act on.
