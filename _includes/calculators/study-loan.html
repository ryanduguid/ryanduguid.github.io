Sources applied

  • Repayment income: section 154-5(1) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003 (Compilation No. 105, 17 October 2026), read 17 November 2026: taxable income without an assessable FHSS released amount, plus total net investment loss, reportable fringe benefits total, exempt foreign income and reportable super contributions.
  • 2025-26 and 2026-27: section 154-20, as the Universities Accord (Cutting Student Debt by 20 Per Cent) Act 2025 substituted it, read in the same compilation: the least of 15% of repayment income over the minimum repayment income up to the second threshold plus 17% above it, and 10% of all repayment income. The thresholds are indexed under section 154-25: $67,000 and $125,000 for 2025-26, and $69,528 and $129,717 for 2026-27, from ATO: Study and training loan repayment thresholds and rates (last updated 30 June 2026), read 17 November 2026.
  • 2024-25: section 154-20 before that Act, one rate from 1% to 10% of all repayment income above $54,434, from Table 3 of the same ATO page and this site’s study loan repayment table.
  • Rounding: whole-dollar income times 15%, 17% or 10% is whole cents; a 2024-25 half per cent can give half a cent, which rounds up. The Act prescribes no rounding.
  • Supplied by you: the year and each part of repayment income. The repayment cannot exceed what you still owe (section 154-20(1)(c)), which this calculator does not ask for.

How the study and training loan repayment calculator works

The study and training loan repayment calculator works out the compulsory repayment on a HELP, VET Student Loan, Student Start-up Loan or Australian Apprenticeship Support Loan debt for one income year. It first adds up repayment income: taxable income, reportable fringe benefits, total net investment loss, reportable super contributions and exempt foreign employment income.

From 2025-26 the repayment is marginal: nothing up to the minimum repayment income, then 15 cents for each dollar above it up to a second threshold and 17 cents above that, unless 10% of all repayment income is less. For 2026-27 the minimum repayment income is $69,528 and the second threshold $129,717, and 10% is the lesser amount from $186,051. For 2024-25 one percentage of the whole repayment income applied, from 1% above $54,434 to 10% from $159,664.

The ATO’s examples on its thresholds and rates page, read 17 November 2026, give Christina $2,527.80 and Priya $25,478 for 2026-27; this calculator gives the same. For Barry, with repayment income of $137,064, the ATO page shows $10,276.99 from a starting amount it prints as $9,028; section 154-20(2) gives 15% of $60,189, which is $9,028.35, so this calculator gives $10,277.34. For Branson in 2024-25 the page shows $5,485.52 as 5.5% of $99,736, which is $5,485.48, the figure this calculator gives.

What the study and training loan repayment calculator does not compute

The calculator gives the compulsory repayment for one income year. It does not compute:

  • the cap at the debt you still owe, or voluntary repayments;
  • the overseas debtors repayment levy on worldwide income for a foreign resident (section 154-16);
  • whether no repayment is due because your Medicare levy is not payable or is reduced under section 8 of the Medicare Levy Act 1986 (section 154-1(2));
  • the amounts an employer withholds each pay for a loan, or indexation of the debt;
  • years before 2024-25.

Confirm the figure with the ATO’s Study and training loan repayment calculator or a registered tax agent.
