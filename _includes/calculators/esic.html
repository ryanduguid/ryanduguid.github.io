How the ESIC tax offset calculator works

An investor in new shares of an early stage innovation company (ESIC) gets a non-refundable tax offset of 20% of the amount for the issue, under Subdivision 360-A of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997. The calculator ports the esic command of australian-accounting. It checks the company tests of section 360-40 for the year of issue: incorporated or registered recently enough, expenses of $1 million or less and assessable income of $200,000 or less in the previous year, not listed, not a foreign company, and 100 innovation points under section 360-45 or the principles-based test. It then checks the investor tests of sections 360-15 and 360-20, including the $50,000 limit where an offer needed a disclosure document. The offset is capped so the year’s offsets with affiliates and carried forward amounts do not exceed $200,000. Where the investor is entitled, the shares also take the modified CGT treatment of Subdivision 360-B.

What the ESIC tax offset calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether the principles-based test is met or which innovation items apply: you state them. It does not work out the offset for a trust’s or partnership’s members, whether the investor is a sophisticated investor, the offset carried forward to a later year, or the capital gain or loss when the shares are sold. Before 2018-19 it refuses a non-cash benefit and the 6-year incorporation route. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.
