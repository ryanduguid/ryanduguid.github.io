Sources applied

  • The carry-forward: subsections 291-20(1) to (7) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (Compilation No. 268, 1 October 2026, the latest), read 19 November 2026 on the ATO Legal Database. A year’s cap is increased only when its contributions would otherwise exceed it, the total superannuation balance just before the year is less than $500,000 and unapplied unused cap remains from one or more of the previous 5 years (subsection (3)); the increase is no more than the excess (subsection (4)), earliest year first (subsection (5)). A year’s unused cap is its shortfall below its cap (subsection (6)), and there is none before 2018-19 (subsection (7)).
  • The general caps: Table 1.1 of ATO: Contributions caps (last updated 11 September 2026), read 19 November 2026: $25,000 for 2018-19 to 2020-21, $27,500 for 2021-22 to 2023-24, $30,000 for 2024-25 and 2025-26 and $32,500 for 2026-27, the $25,000 of paragraph 291-20(2)(a) indexed. Its Table 2 is among the boundary cases.
  • Sam’s example: ATO: Concessional contributions cap (last updated 2 July 2026), read 19 November 2026.
  • A defined benefit interest is refused: Subdivision 291-C of the same Act counts its notional taxed contributions (section 291-170, read 19 November 2026) as concessional contributions.
  • The downsizer test: section 292-102 of the same Act on the ATO Legal Database, with its history notes, the source the australian-accounting contribution-caps command cites, read for it on 24 November 2026: age 55 or more for a contribution from 1 January 2023, a sale contract entered into on or after 1 July 2018, a contribution within 90 days after the change of ownership unless the Commissioner allows longer, and the lesser of $300,000 less your contributions already covered and the capital proceeds less contributions already covered from them. The test agrees with the command on 693 test cases, refusals included.
  • Supplied by you: every contribution and balance, as your fund reports them. A year over its cap without its balance, a gap in the years or a year missing from the previous 5 is refused.

How the concessional catch-up calculator works

The concessional catch-up calculator works out each earlier year’s unused concessional contributions cap, the shortfall of that year’s contributions below its general cap, from 2018-19. Where an earlier year’s contributions went over its cap and the balance just before it was under $500,000, it applies the unused cap of the 5 years before that one, earliest first, as subsections 291-20(4) and (5) required for that year. What is left of the previous 5 years’ unused cap is carried into the year you choose.

With a total super balance under $500,000 on 30 June before the year, the cap available is the general cap plus that carried amount. The calculator shows how much of it this year’s contributions use, any excess, and what is left of each year’s amount with the year after which it lapses. A proposed contribution is added to the contributions so far, so you can see how far it goes before it becomes excess.

The ATO’s concessional contributions cap page works Sam’s example: unused cap of $14,000, $13,000, $14,500, $13,500 and $12,500 from 2019-20 to 2023-24 gives $67,500, so his 2024-25 cap is $97,500, and contributions of $97,500 use it all with no excess. The calculator opens on his figures; the ATO says only that his balance was below $500,000, so the $450,000 is fictional.

A downsizer contribution, from selling a home you held for 10 years or more, counts towards neither cap. Answer yes to test one: you must be 55 or older when you make it, the sale contract must be from 1 July 2018 and the contribution within 90 days after the change of ownership unless the Commissioner allows longer. The part covered is limited to $300,000 less your contributions already covered and to the capital proceeds less contributions already covered from them, by you or your spouse.

What the concessional catch-up calculator does not cover

The calculator works the cap only. It does not decide what counts as a concessional contribution, in which year a contribution counts, or whether a deduction notice is valid, and it does not work out the tax on excess contributions or Division 293 tax. It does not cover a defined benefit interest, or non-concessional contributions and their bring-forward. It tests one downsizer contribution on the ages, days and amounts you give; it does not check the other conditions of section 292-102 you confirm, and whether a fund accepts the part the test does not cover is the fund’s decision. It covers 2024-25, 2025-26 and 2026-27.

Your carry-forward amounts are on ATO online services (Super, then Information, then Carry forward concessional contributions), which use the contributions your funds have reported. Confirm the figures there or with a registered tax agent before you contribute.
