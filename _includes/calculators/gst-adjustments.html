How the GST adjustments calculator works

The calculator works one of 6 GST adjustments or tests for a tax period in a financial year from 2020-21 to 2026-27, with each condition and its authority. For a change in creditable purpose, it finds whether the tax period ending 30 June is an adjustment period: one starting at least 12 months after the tax period the acquisition is attributable to, counted to 1, 2, 5 or 10 periods by the GST exclusive value and whether it relates to business finance. Disposal or loss ends the count early (sections 129-20 and 129-25). No adjustment arises at or below $1,000, or $10,000 for business finance. The full input tax credit times the difference between the intended or former application and the actual application is an increasing or decreasing adjustment (sections 129-70 and 129-75).

Annual apportionment tests eligibility under section 131-5 and compares the credit with the election, the GST on the supply, or that times the non-input-taxed and consideration extents, with the section 11-30 credit; the difference is the section 131-55 increasing adjustment. The financial acquisitions threshold is exceeded when financial acquisitions’ credits exceed $150,000 or 10% of all credits, for the past or the next 12 months (sections 189-5 and 189-10). A going concern is GST-free when all 5 conditions of section 38-325 are met. Second-hand goods give a credit of 1/11 of the consideration, capped above $300 at the GST on your resale (section 66-10). A bad debt gives 1/11 of the amount, reduced for a partly taxable supply or partly creditable acquisition (Division 21 and Subdivision 136-A). Amounts are worked exactly and rounded to the cent once.

What the GST adjustments calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide the facts. You enter each fact, amount and percentage as established, and it does not judge whether an apportionment method is fair or reasonable. It refuses cars and reduced credit acquisitions for annual apportionment, and farm land and part supplies for going concerns. For second-hand goods it refuses goods divided for resupply, valuable metal and goods leased before sale. For bad debts it refuses the margin scheme and other special rules, reduced credit acquisitions and annual apportionment, and a change of accounting basis. It does not work out the tax period an adjustment or credit is attributable to, substituted or concluding tax periods, importations, or a Commissioner’s determination of fewer adjustment periods. It takes as given, without checking, that the 2 applications leave out changes in applying a thing to financial supplies unless the financial acquisitions threshold is exceeded (section 129-5(2)), that there has been no Division 130 adjustment (section 129-15), that a reduced credit acquisition’s percentages are worked as GSTR 2000/24 paragraphs 63 to 65 direct (section 129-40(3)), and that every member of a GST group is eligible before its representative member elects (section 131-15). Results are planning estimates: check them against your records and the ATO’s guidance.
