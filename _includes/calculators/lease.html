Sources applied

  • Lease accounting: AASB 16 Leases, the compiled Standard on the AASB’s website (compilation 8 of 31 December 2023, for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2024), which the Federal Register of Legislation lists as the latest version, F2024C00049; both read 19 November 2026. Paragraph 26: the lease liability is the present value of the lease payments not paid at commencement, discounted at the rate implicit in the lease or, if that cannot be readily determined, the lessee’s incremental borrowing rate. Paragraph 27: those payments include amounts expected to be payable under residual value guarantees and a purchase option’s price if the lessee is reasonably certain to exercise it. Paragraph 24: the right-of-use asset’s cost adds the payments made at or before commencement less incentives received, initial direct costs and the estimated cost of dismantling, removal or restoration. Paragraphs 36 and 37: interest at a constant periodic rate on the remaining liability, and the payments reduce it. Paragraphs 31 and 32: depreciation under AASB 116, over the useful life where ownership transfers or a purchase option is reflected in the cost, otherwise to the earlier of the end of the useful life and the end of the lease term. Paragraphs 5 and 6 and Appendix A: a short-term lease, 12 months or less with no purchase option, may be expensed instead.
  • Worked example: Illustrative Examples accompanying IFRS 16, on the AASB’s website, read 19 November 2026. Example 13’s lease liability of CU355,391, right-of-use asset of CU420,391, interest of CU17,770 and CU16,158 in years 1 and 2 and depreciation of CU42,039 a year are what this page gives, to the nearest whole unit, and its tests check them.
  • Rounding: each period’s interest is the period rate on the liability, rounded half up to the cent; the period rate is the annual rate over the payments a year (52, 26, 12, 4 or 1). The payments stay as entered, so the last period’s interest takes the cents that rounding leaves. A payment period, or a period of depreciation, that runs across 30 June is shared between the 2 income years by days. These are this page’s conventions, not rules AASB 16 states.
  • GST in the comparison: ATO: GST, hire purchase and leasing (last updated 6 April 2017), read 19 November 2026. Each lease payment is treated as a separate purchase, with its GST credit in the tax period it is paid or due. A hire purchase agreement entered into on or after 1 July 2012 is taxable in every component, the credit charge included, and its whole GST credit is claimed in the period of the first payment, as in the ATO’s example of Albert’s second freezer: $3,654.54, one-eleventh of $40,200. This page rounds each credit half up to the cent, as its GST calculator does, so it gives $3,654.55 for that example. A purchase’s credit is taken on the commencement date.
  • Income tax in the comparison: ATO: Business and professional items 2026, Expenses P8 (last updated 30 May 2026), read 19 November 2026: lease expenses are what you incur on finance and operating leases; hire purchase expenses are not lease expenses; special rules apply to a leased car that costs more than the car limit; deductions exclude GST credits; and prepayments of $1,000 or more can be spread. Section 27-5 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997: no deduction for the part of an outgoing that is a GST credit. Sections 240-20, 240-25 and 240-60: under hire purchase the buyer is taken to own the asset from the start and to pay notional interest on a notional loan, shared into income years as accounting relates it. Sections 40-72 and 40-75: decline in value by the diminishing value or prime cost method, with the depreciation rates page’s rounding. All read 19 November 2026 in the ATO’s legal database.
  • Figures: supplied by you. The discount rate, the hire purchase rate, the effective life and the tax rate are yours, not market rates or a ruling.

How the AASB 16 lease calculator works

The calculator measures a lessee’s lease under AASB 16 at commencement and sets out each income year to the end of the lease. The lease liability is the payments not yet paid, with any residual value guarantee you expect to pay or purchase option you are reasonably certain to exercise, discounted at the rate you enter: the rate implicit in the lease or, if that cannot be readily determined, your incremental borrowing rate. A payment in advance falls on the commencement date, so it goes into the right-of-use asset’s cost, not the liability. The asset’s cost adds payments made before commencement, less incentives received, initial direct costs and the estimated cost to dismantle, remove or restore.

Each period’s interest is the period rate on the liability. The schedule sets out every payment’s interest and principal, which are the journal for that payment, and the income year table gives each year’s interest, payments, straight-line depreciation and the liability and asset at 30 June. Depreciation is an equal amount each payment period over the lease term, or each month over the useful life where ownership transfers. Download schedule CSV saves the tables as plain figures.

With the comparison on, the page puts the same asset under hire purchase at your rate and bought outright, beside the lease, for each income year to the year the lease term ends: cash paid with GST, GST credits, deductions, the tax they save at your rate and the net cost. The lease deducts its payments when they are paid. Hire purchase deducts its notional interest and, like buying, the asset’s decline in value. The tax value left at the end is what can still be deducted after it: the residual if a lease buys the asset, and the asset’s adjustable value otherwise.

What the AASB 16 lease calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a contract is or contains a lease, the lease term, the discount rate or whether a purchase option is reasonably certain. It flags a short-term lease but not a lease of a low-value asset, which paragraph 5(b) also lets the lessee expense. It does not remeasure a lease: a change in an index or rate, a lease modification, a reassessed term or option, an early payout, or variable payments that do not depend on an index or rate. It does not separate non-lease components, apply the revaluation or fair value models, impairment, a sale and leaseback, a lessor’s accounting, not-for-profit concessionary leases or deferred tax.

The comparison does not discount the cash flows, value the asset at the end of the term, apply the prepayment rules to a payment in advance, the luxury car lease rules (it refuses a car above the car limit), small business simplified depreciation or an instant asset write-off, or an asset first used after the commencement date. It takes GST credits on the payment dates rather than at the end of each tax period, and the tax saved at 30 June of each year. Confirm the treatment with the agreement, the financier’s statements and a registered tax agent or auditor.
