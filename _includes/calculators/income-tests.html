Sources applied

  • Income for surcharge purposes, reportable superannuation contributions and total net investment loss: section 995-1 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 (Compilation No. 268, 1 October 2026, the latest), read 19 November 2026 on the ATO Legal Database. Income for surcharge purposes is taxable income without the assessable FHSS released amount and with the family trust distribution amounts that subsection 271-105(1) of Schedule 2F to the 1936 Act kept out, plus the reportable fringe benefits total, reportable superannuation contributions and total net investment loss. The subsection 301-20(3) amount it takes off applies only below preservation age, which is 60 for everyone from 2024-25. Reportable superannuation contributions are reportable employer contributions and personal contributions deducted under Subdivision 290-C, reduced but not below zero by excess concessional contributions.
  • Division 293 income: paragraph 293-20(1)(a) of the same Act, income for surcharge purposes disregarding reportable superannuation contributions.
  • Rebate income: subsection 6(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936, read 19 November 2026 on the ATO Legal Database: taxable income without the FHSS amount, reportable superannuation contributions, total net investment loss and the adjusted fringe benefits total.
  • Adjusted fringe benefits total and adjusted taxable income: clauses 2, 4, 5 and 8 of Schedule 3 to the A New Tax System (Family Assistance) Act 1999 (Compilation No. 124, 1 July 2026, the latest), read 19 November 2026, which subsection 6(1) of the 1936 Act and section 995-1 adopt, disregarding clauses 3 and 3A. A section 57A employer’s amounts count before section 135Q of the Fringe Benefits Tax Assessment Act 1986 grosses them up at 1 / (1 − 47%), so times 0.53, rounded down to the dollar as the ATO’s Rebate income 2026 worksheet does (last updated 30 May 2026, read 19 November 2026). Adjusted taxable income adds target foreign income and tax-free pensions and takes off child support paid, with no floor.
  • Repayment income: subsection 154-5(1) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003, read 19 November 2026 on the ATO Legal Database: taxable income without the FHSS amount, total net investment loss, the reportable fringe benefits total, exempt foreign employment income and reportable superannuation contributions.
  • Co-contribution total income: section 8 of the Superannuation (Government Co-contribution for Low Income Earners) Act 2003, read 19 November 2026 on the ATO Legal Database: assessable income without the FHSS amount, plus the reportable fringe benefits total and reportable employer contributions reduced but not below zero by excess concessional contributions, less business deductions.
  • The same measures are on ATO: Income tests (last updated 8 June 2026), read 19 November 2026. Section 995-1 does not add exempt foreign employment income to income for surcharge purposes; ATO: Medicare levy surcharge income, thresholds and rates (last updated 22 June 2026) adds it where taxable income is $1 or more, and the result says when the two differ.
  • Supplied by you: residency and every amount, in whole dollars as the return takes them. A part larger than the amount that includes it is refused.

How the income tests calculator works

The income tests calculator adds up the 6 income measures that the income tests section of an individual tax return feeds, from the same amounts. Each measure starts from taxable income without any assessable first home super saver released amount, then adds the parts its own definition names.

  • Income for surcharge purposes, for the Medicare levy surcharge and the private health insurance rebate, adds family trust distribution amounts, fringe benefits, reportable super contributions and investment losses.
  • Division 293 income, for the extra 15% tax when it and low tax contributions together exceed $250,000, is the same less reportable super contributions.
  • Rebate income, for the seniors and pensioners tax offset, adds super contributions, investment losses and the adjusted fringe benefits total.
  • Repayment income, for study and training loans, adds investment losses, fringe benefits, exempt foreign employment income and super contributions.
  • Adjusted taxable income, for the dependant (invalid and invalid carer) tax offset, adds adjusted fringe benefits, target foreign income, investment losses, tax-free pensions and super contributions, less child support paid.
  • Co-contribution total income starts from assessable income instead, adds fringe benefits and employer contributions and takes off business deductions.

The ATO’s page on when to repay a study loan, last updated 13 October 2026, works Christina’s repayment income: taxable income of $50,420, a $1,250 net investment loss, $4,560 of reportable fringe benefits, $2,580 of exempt foreign employment income and $15,000 of reportable super contributions give $73,810. The calculator opens on her figures, with her loss taken as a financial investment loss, the benefits from an employer that pays FBT, the contributions from her employer, her target foreign income as the exempt income alone and assessable income equal to taxable income; those splits are fictional. Tom’s income for surcharge purposes of $117,000, from the ATO’s surcharge page, is among the boundary cases.

What the income tests calculator does not cover

The calculator works the measures only. It does not decide whether an offset, the surcharge, a study loan repayment, Division 293 tax or a co-contribution applies, or work out their amounts; the income tax calculator works the surcharge and a study loan repayment. It covers a person resident in Australia for the whole of 2024-25, 2025-26 or 2026-27: a foreign resident pays no Medicare levy or surcharge and repays a study loan on worldwide income, and a part-year resident’s measures were not checked.

It does not add a spouse’s income, work out family tax benefit or child care subsidy income, which also count a partner’s, or the Commonwealth Seniors Health Card income test, which Services Australia applies. It takes each amount as you enter it: it does not decide what is a reportable fringe benefit, target foreign income or a tax-free pension, or work out excess concessional contributions. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s income tests calculator or a registered tax agent.
