## What the inputs need

Each line is one employee or group: the leave hours accrued and unused at the reporting date and the hourly rate the leave would be paid at today, with any on-costs you treat as part of the benefit. Leave a line’s name and hours blank to drop it.

A line expected to be settled wholly within 12 months after the reporting date is short-term: measured undiscounted and current. Any other line is other long-term: give the whole years to settlement (1 to 50) and whether the entity has a right at the reporting date to defer settlement for at least 12 months. Without that right the line is current, whenever settlement is expected.

The probability of payment, salary growth and discount rate are yours, as percentages to 4 decimal places. AASB 119 para 83 sets the discount rate from market yields on high quality corporate bonds at the reporting date, or government bond yields where there is no deep market in such bonds.

## How the leave provisions calculator works

The leave provisions calculator measures an entity’s annual and long service leave at one reporting date, line by line, under AASB 119 Employee Benefits, and splits the total into current and non-current under AASB 101. A short-term line is the hours times the rate times the probability of payment, undiscounted. An other long-term line projects the value at today’s rate forward by salary growth for each whole year to settlement, weights it by the probability of payment and discounts it back at the discount rate over the same years. The arithmetic is carried to 40 significant digits and each line’s amounts are rounded to the cent. Lines the entity has no right to defer for 12 months are current.

Its results match the leave-provisions command of Ryan Duguid’s australian-accounting calculators on 347 test cases, refusals included.

## What the leave provisions calculator does not cover

The calculator does not set the discount rate, salary growth, probability of payment or settlement pattern: you state those, and it applies them without testing them. It refuses defined benefit plans, plan assets and reimbursement rights, and sick, parental, jury and military service leave, long-term disability and termination benefits. It does not work out entitlements under a State or Territory long service leave Act (the long service leave calculator does), part years to settlement, on-costs such as payroll tax and super unless you include them in the rate, the deferred tax on the provision or the journal entries. Results are planning estimates: check them against your records and your auditor’s view.

## How the wine equalisation tax calculator works

Wine equalisation tax (WET) is 29% of the taxable value of each assessable dealing in wine (section 5-5). A wholesale sale’s taxable value is its price excluding WET and GST, and GST is then 10% of the price including WET: the ATO’s $120 dozen bears WET of $34.80 and GST of $15.48, $170.28 in all. A retail sale or own use, such as wine used for tastings, is valued at a notional wholesale selling price; own use of wine bought under quote is valued at its purchase price excluding GST (section 5-5, items AD3c and AD13c). By the half retail price method that is 50% of the retail price including WET and GST (section 9-35), so the ATO’s $140 dozen at the cellar door bears WET of $20.30. Grape wine may instead use the average wholesale price method, the weighted average of the tax period’s wholesale prices for wine of the same vintage and variety (section 9-40), but only if at least 10% by value of those sales are wholesale sales (subsection 9-25(3)); wine that is not grape wine must use half retail (section 9-30). A sale under quote bears no WET (section 7-10). Each amount is rounded half up to the cent.

A producer that meets the conditions of section 19-5 earns a producer rebate of 29% of the wholesale price, or of the notional wholesale selling price, on each dealing it is liable for, and on a sale under quote unless the quote states the buyer will use the wine in manufacture, make a GST-free supply of it or sell it on under quote. The rebates for a financial year are capped, for the producer or for its group of associated producers together: $350,000 for dealings to 30 June 2026 and $400,000 from 1 July 2026 (subsections 19-15(2) and (3)). The calculator takes the rebates already claimed this year off the cap and gives what is left. The wine tax goes to label 1C and the rebate to label 1D of the business activity statement, each in whole dollars, never netted. It reproduces the ATO’s rebate table for 2026-27: WET of $11,890 and rebates of $16,240 on its retail, wholesale, quoted and tasting dealings.

## What the wine equalisation tax calculator does not cover

The calculator works dealings in the financial years 2023-24 to 2027-28 and refuses earlier years, which carry the transitional rebate rules for 2017 and earlier vintage wine. It refuses the average wholesale price method for wine that is not grape wine or without the 10% share of wholesale sales, and a rebate on own use of wine bought under quote. It takes each price as given, so it does not work out an arm’s length price (section 27-10) or the price of wine sold with other goods; it assumes GST applies to a sale and does not decide whether a supply is GST-free.

Not covered: imports and other customs dealings, whose WET the Department of Home Affairs collects; the special valuations of section 9-45; wine tax credits other than the producer rebate, such as bad debts and overpaid WET; the reduction of a rebate for earlier producers’ rebates on wine used in a blend; New Zealand participants’ rebates; whether producers are associated (the group’s claims are taken as given); and the tax period a dealing belongs to. A value or total of $70,368,744,177,664 or more is refused, as the page cannot show it to the cent. Rebates claimed above the cap are flagged, not worked out: section 19-25 makes the excess payable as wine tax at the end of the financial year. Have a registered tax agent confirm the result before you lodge.

## How the fuel tax credit calculator works

## How the luxury car tax calculator works

The luxury car tax calculator works out the tax on a tax period’s sales and importations of luxury cars in 2019-20 to 2026-27, with the price and change of use adjustments and labels 1E and 1F on the business activity statement. Each car takes the threshold for the financial year of its supply or importation, or the fuel-efficient car limit for a car rated at most 3.5 litres per 100 km from 1 July 2025 and at most 7 litres before. The tax is 33% of the GST-exclusive value over that threshold. Tax on an importation is shown apart, because it is paid to the Department of Home Affairs with any customs duty. A primary producer’s or tourism operator’s refund of the tax borne on a refund-eligible car, up to $10,000, is shown apart too, because it is claimed on the ATO’s form.

Its results match the luxury-car-tax command of Ryan Duguid’s australian-accounting calculators on the ATO’s examples and 306 test cases, refusals included.

## How the unused leave calculator works

When employment ends, unused annual leave, leave loading and long service leave are taxed by when the leave accrued and why the employment ended. Leave for employment before 18 August 1993, and all of it from 16 August 1978 on a genuine redundancy, early retirement scheme or invalidity, is capped at 30% and withheld at 32%; it is lump sum A. Only 5% of long service leave for the period before 16 August 1978 is assessable; the whole amount is lump sum B. The rest goes through Schedule 7’s marginal rate calculation with that 5%, spread over a year of pays and withheld at the employee’s Schedule 1 rate, and is reported as unused leave on termination.

## How the workers compensation premium calculator works

Every state and territory scheme bills a premium on the wages an employer estimates for the policy period and adjusts it to the wages actually paid. The calculator takes the wages, the rate your insurer gives your industry classification and the facts your scheme asks for, and applies the scheme’s published method as far as it goes for an employer whose premium does not yet turn on its own claims: icare’s small employer formula in New South Wales, the Premiums Order for a new or small employer in Victoria, WorkCover Queensland’s notice for a policy that starts in 2026-27, ReturnToWorkSA’s formula with its discount bands and claims cost cap in South Australia, and the insurer’s rate times wages in the other 4. It adds GST, and Queensland’s insurance duty, and, given the actual wages, works the premium again and shows the adjustment.

## What the workers compensation premium calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employer and one policy, and no advice. It does not cover:

- the industry classification or the rate, which are the insurer’s, and the wages, which each scheme’s definition decides, including whether a contractor or director counts as a worker;
- experience-rated and performance-rated premiums: New South Wales above $30,000 of basic tariff premium and the Loss Prevention and Recovery products, Victorian employers that are neither new nor small, Queensland policies in force before 1 July 2026 or carried on from an earlier policy (reinstated within 6 months, or a business taken over: sections 11 and 12 of the notice) and Queensland default assessments, which have a $500 minimum (section 4.2), and a South Australian supplementary underwriting rate;
- members of a group in New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland and South Australia, policies for less than 12 months in New South Wales, the Mine Safety Fund adjustment for mining WICs, the catastrophic claims contribution and the performance discount;
- Comcare and self-insured employers, household workers, the South Australian work health and safety fee, discounts for paying early and instalment interest;
- whether an employer is exempt from holding a policy, which the result flags where a scheme’s limit was read, and any policy year other than 2026-27.

Confirm the premium with your insurer before relying on it.
