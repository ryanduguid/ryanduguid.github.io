## How the FBT car parking calculator works

The FBT car parking calculator values the car parking fringe benefits an employer provides at one work car park in the FBT year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. It first applies the threshold test: a benefit arises only if a commercial parking station within 1 km charged a lowest all-day fee of more than $11.48 on Wednesday 1 April 2026, the first business day of the year. Enter the highest of the nearby stations’ lowest fees that day. At $11.48 or less, no car parking benefit arises and the taxable value is nil.

Where a benefit arises, the calculator values it by the method you choose. The commercial parking station method values each benefit at the lowest all-day fee charged nearby on its day; the market value method at a qualified valuer’s arm’s length amount; the average cost method at the average of the lowest fees on the first and last days a benefit was provided. Each takes off employee contributions, never below nil. The 12-week register method grosses a register’s 12 weeks up to a year: the register value × 52 ÷ 12 × the days in the availability period ÷ 366. The spaces method values each space at its daily rate amount × its days ÷ 366 × 228, scales the total down where fewer employees than spaces are covered, and takes off contributions.

This page opens on example 13 in section 16.2.4.4 of the ATO’s guide for employers: Rent-a-spot’s $272 monthly fee over 22 business days is $12.36 a day, above the $11.48 threshold. With Rent-a-spot the only station within 1 km, an employee who parks on each of those 22 days receives 22 benefits worth $271.92. The guide works the daily rate, not a total. The tests also hold the guide’s example 15, $18.50 a benefit by the average cost method, example 17, $5,416.67 by the register method, and example 18, $1,061.31 by the spaces method, each at its own year’s threshold.

The method is the employer’s choice, made by election for every method but the commercial parking station method; the calculator values the method chosen and does not recommend one.

## What the FBT car parking calculator does not cover

Apart from the threshold test, the calculator does not decide whether a car parking benefit arises. It does not check that a car was parked for more than 4 hours between 7 am and 7 pm at premises the employer controls near the employee’s primary place of employment, that the car was used to travel between home and work that day, and that a facility is a commercial parking station within 1 km by the shortest practicable route. It does not check that a fee is representative (sections 39AA and 39AB) or set at arm’s length (subsection 39E(2)), or that an election, valuer’s report or register is valid; it checks only a register’s age, not the 10% increase rule in subsection 39GF(3). Nor does it check that the numbers of employees and spaces behind the spaces method’s reduction are representative (subsection 39FB(6)). Under the station, market value and average cost methods it takes contributions off in total, which is right only where no benefit’s contribution is more than that benefit’s value: an excess on one benefit does not reduce another. It does not apply exemptions, such as the small business exemption in section 58GA, the exempt employers or the disabled persons’ parking exclusion. It values one work car park: under the commercial parking station method it takes one value for every benefit, and under the spaces method one group of spaces sharing a daily rate amount and availability period. It does not split the value between Type 1 and Type 2 benefits, gross it up or work out FBT. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s guide or a registered tax agent.

## How the FBT loan calculator works

The FBT loan calculator values one loan fringe benefit for the FBT year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. The notional interest is the interest the loan would have earned at the statutory interest rate, 8.27%, on its balance at the end of each day: the total of the daily balances × 8.27% ÷ 365. The gross taxable value is the amount by which that exceeds the interest that accrued on the loan for the year, never below nil.

Where the employee, not an associate, would have been able to deduct interest on the loan, the otherwise deductible rule reduces the value. The gross deduction is the notional interest × the share of the loan used to produce the employee’s assessable income. The notional deduction is the gross deduction less the deduction the employee could claim for the interest that accrued. The taxable value is the gross taxable value less the notional deduction, never below nil.

Section 8.8 of the ATO’s guide for employers works a $50,000 loan at 4% for the whole year, 60% used for interest-bearing investments, at the 5.65% rate of 2016-17: $825 less a $495 reduction, a taxable value of $330. This page opens on the same loan at 8.27%: notional interest of $4,135.00 less $2,000.00 accrued is $2,135.00; the gross deduction of $2,481.00 less the $1,200.00 deduction for the interest accrued leaves $1,281.00; the taxable value is $854.00. Run at 5.65%, the same steps give the guide’s $330, and its section 8.5 example’s $325; the tests hold both.

## What the FBT loan calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a loan fringe benefit arises or whether an exemption in section 17 applies, such as a loan at an arm’s length rate by a lender to the public or an advance for work expenses accounted for within 6 months. It does not treat deferred or unpaid interest, which can be a further loan or a debt waiver fringe benefit, a loan made before 1 July 1986 or a housing loan made before 3 April 1986, which can take a lower rate, a car loan, whose otherwise deductible amount follows the car substantiation rules in subsection 19(1), or a loan provided jointly to an employee and an associate. It does not work out the employee’s deduction, and it does not check the declaration the rule requires. A loan fringe benefit carries no GST credit, so it is grossed up at the Type 2 rate, 1.8868; this calculator stops at the taxable value and does not gross it up or work out FBT. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s guide or a registered tax agent.

## How the meal entertainment calculator works

The meal entertainment calculator values an employer’s meal entertainment fringe benefits for the FBT year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027, where the employer has elected to value them under Division 9A. Both methods start from the year’s total expenses for providing meal entertainment to everyone, employees, associates and clients alike, less contributions from employees and their associates that the employer did not reimburse.

Under the 50/50 split method the taxable value is half those expenses. Under the 12-week register method it is those expenses × the register percentage: the value of the meal entertainment fringe benefits the register records for employees and their associates, over the value of all the meal entertainment it records. A register kept for 12 weeks in the year ending 31 March 2027 is valid for that year and the 4 after it. The calculator refuses a register first valid before the year ending 31 March 2023, or one that ceased to be valid because a year’s expenses rose more than 20% above those of its first valid year.

The ATO’s guide for employers describes both methods but works no example in figures. This page opens on example 18 of the ATO’s FBT return 2026 instructions, $4,000 of meal entertainment under the 50/50 split: $2,000. For the register method, the tests work a register of $1,000 of fringe benefits out of $3,000 of meal entertainment from the Act: the register percentage shows as 33.33% but applies as a third exactly, so $900 of expenses gives $300.00.

When the register method is chosen, the result also shows the 50/50 split figure for the same expenses. Which method to elect, if either, is the employer’s choice; the calculator does not recommend one.

## What the meal entertainment calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide what is meal entertainment, whether the election is available or made, or whether a register meets the record and representative period rules in sections 37CC and 37CE. It does not value meal entertainment provided under a salary packaging arrangement, which cannot be elected, or entertainment valued by its actual cost as an expense payment, property or residual benefit. It does not apply exemptions; under the 50/50 split the property benefits and minor benefits exemptions do not apply. It does not take a register whose first valid year had nil expenses or check whether a later register replaced this one, split the value between Type 1 and Type 2 benefits, gross it up or work out FBT. ## How the vehicle classifier works

The vehicle classifier works out whether one vehicle bought in 2025-26 or 2026-27 is a car for income tax, GST and FBT, and what follows. The designed load is the gross vehicle mass on the compliance plate less the basic kerb weight, or the stated payload, which must agree with them. Under section 995-1 of ITAA 1997, a motor vehicle other than a motor cycle is a car if designed to carry a load under 1 tonne and fewer than 9 passengers, the driver counted.

For a car, the classifier asks whether it was designed principally to carry passengers. A passenger body was. A dual cab was if its seats, at 68 kg each (MT 2024), weigh more than the load left. For a single cab ute or panel van the answer turns on factors the page does not weigh (TD 94/19). The dual cab test is MT 2024’s, for FBT; no ATO ruling applies it to the car limit. So for a passenger body section 40-230 caps the cost to depreciate at the car limit, after any GST credit, and for a dual cab, a single cab ute or a panel van the car limit is not decided here; the page still gives a cost after the credit that is within the limit, as the limit would not reduce it. Section 69-10 of the GST Act caps the credit at 1/11 of the limit.

## Luxury car tax, the GST credit and FBT

Luxury car tax has its own car: a load under 2 tonnes and fewer than 9 passengers. The tax can apply above the threshold for its fuel consumption, unless it is a commercial vehicle not designed principally to carry passengers. For utes the classifier uses LCTD 2023/1’s simplified method, the ATO’s compliance approach: seats at 68 kg weighing less than half the load at supply, which is the designed load less any accessories then fitted. That approach says when the ATO will not review a sale, not whether the vehicle is a commercial vehicle in law, so it does not lift the GST credit cap: above the car limit the credit on a ute or van that is a car is not decided here. At exactly half, the 2 tests part: MT 2024 counts a dual cab as designed for passengers only when its seats weigh more than the load left, whilst LCTD 2023/1’s method needs them to weigh less than half the load. A 5-seat dual cab with a 680 kg load can therefore be exempt from FBT, whilst luxury car tax and the GST credit cap are not decided here.

For FBT, a single cab ute, a panel van, a dual cab not designed principally for passengers and any vehicle that is not a car can be exempt only if an employee’s private use was work-related travel or minor, infrequent and irregular use. You state that fact; the classifier never concludes it. A car designed principally for passengers gives a car fringe benefit, which the FBT car calculator values.

TD 94/19’s 4-wheel drive wagon, with 5 seats and an 865 kg load, carries passengers; at $75,000 in 2025-26 the ATO’s instant asset write-off page gives a $6,334 credit and a $68,666 cost, or the $69,674 car limit without a credit. MT 2024’s dual cab has 340 kg of passengers against 260 kg left. LCTD 2023/1’s example 5, a dual cab with 5 seats and 960 kg, is one the ATO will not review for luxury car tax. TD 2006/40’s $5,182 credit is $5,182.64 here, with its cents.

## What the vehicle classifier does not cover

The classifier does not decide whether private use was minor, infrequent and irregular, or whether a dual cab, single cab ute or panel van was designed mainly for carrying passengers for the car limit. Nor does it decide whether a ute or van is a commercial vehicle for the GST credit cap, whether a modification permanently changed a vehicle’s design (MT 2033 and LCTD 2023/1 paragraphs 16 to 30), or the principal purpose of a truck, bus or other body under 1 tonne, which turns on factors it does not weigh. Where LCTD 2023/1’s simplified method is not met it says the question is not decided here, and that method assumes a ute the Australian Design Rules class as a goods vehicle. It does not cover cars fitted out to carry people in wheelchairs, emergency vehicles, motor homes and campervans, taxis and limousines, leases and hire, a dealer quoting an ABN, a partly creditable purchase, or a year before 2025-26. It does not work out decline in value, luxury car tax payable or the taxable value of a fringe benefit. A classification is not advice: confirm it with the ATO’s guidance or a registered tax agent.

## How the PCG 2021/4 risk scorer works

Practical Compliance Guideline PCG 2021/4 sets out how the ATO rates the risk that an individual professional practitioner (IPP) does not return enough of their professional firm’s profit in their own hands. The scorer first asks the 2 gateways. If the arrangement is not commercially driven (Gateway 1) or has a high-risk feature (Gateway 2), it gives no score, because paragraph 12 makes the risk assessment framework unavailable.

It then scores up to 3 factors from 1 (lowest risk) to 6 (highest), using Table 1 at paragraph 76: the share of the whole of firm group profit entitlement the IPP returns personally, the total effective tax rate paid by the IPP and associated entities on that income, and, if you choose, the IPP’s remuneration as a percentage of a commercial benchmark for their services. It adds the scores and places the total in the green, amber or red zone of Table 2 at paragraph 78. If the IPP returns the whole entitlement, paragraph 81 makes the arrangement low risk and no other factor is scored.

The scorer reproduces the scores and zones of all 12 case studies in the guideline’s appendix. Case study 1 opens the page: Nicolas returns $330,000 of a $600,000 entitlement, 55% and a score of 4, and he and his company pay $189,367 of tax on it, 31.56% and a score of 3, so his aggregate of 7 is green. In case study 7, Jacinta’s $360,000 against a $350,000 benchmark adds a factor 3 score of 3, for an aggregate of 10 against all 3 factors, also green.

## What the PCG 2021/4 risk scorer does not cover

The scorer does not decide whether the guideline applies: whether the IPP provides professional services or manages the firm and is an equity holder, whether the firm’s income is personal services income and whether the structure is legally effective (paragraph 28). It does not test the gateways; it takes your answers. It does not work out the entitlement, the tax for each entity under paragraph 85, a part-time IPP’s full-time equivalent (paragraph 71) or a commercial benchmark. A green zone is not a ruling: PCG 2021/4 does not change the law, Part IVA can still apply, and the ATO may still review an arrangement whose profit allocation drifts (paragraph 107). Confirm the assessment with the guideline or a registered tax agent.

## How the GIC payment plan calculator works

The calculator compares paying a tax debt in full today with paying it by equal instalments. It works day by day from the first instalment to the last. Each day, the unpaid debt and any unpaid GIC grow by that day’s rate: the quarter’s annual GIC rate divided by 365, or 366 in a leap year, as sections 8AAC and 8AAD of the Taxation Administration Act 1953 set out. It finds the equal instalment that clears the debt and its GIC on the last instalment day, rounds it down to the cent, and lets the final instalment pay what is left. The GIC is the total paid on the plan less the debt.

In the example the page opens with, a $1,000 debt is paid in 3 monthly instalments from 1 October 2026, all at the October to December 2026 rate of 11.51%. Paying $336.56 on 1 October leaves $663.44, which grows by 31 days of GIC to $669.96 by 1 November. Paying $336.56 then leaves $333.40, which grows by 30 days to $336.56 on 1 December, the final instalment. The plan costs $1,009.68, so $9.68 of GIC over paying now.

## What the GIC payment plan calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out the debt, its due date or any GIC that accrued before the plan starts; enter the debt as it stands on the first instalment day. It does not model a missed or late instalment, a plan the ATO varies, a GIC remission, a refund offset against the debt or the deductibility of GIC. Rates after December 2026 are your assumption until the ATO publishes them. The ATO sets the instalments of an actual plan, and its figures may differ by cents from these. Confirm the amount payable with the ATO or a registered tax agent.

## How the 13-week cash forecast works

The 13-week cash forecast works out closing cash for each week and the funding gap against a buffer you set. Weekly closing cash = opening cash + receipts − payments. ## How the objection deadline checker works

The objection deadline checker gives the last day to lodge a taxation objection under section 14ZW of the Taxation Administration Act 1953. An FBT assessment allows 4 years. Any other decision for which the section sets no other period allows 60 days after notice is served.

An amended assessment allows whichever ends later: 60 days after notice of the amended assessment, or the 2 or 4 years after notice of the first assessment for that year. The checker shows both dates and the later one.

Each period starts the day after notice, because subsection 36(1) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 leaves out the day a period begins after. A 2-year period from notice given on 28 February 2026 therefore starts on 1 March 2026 and ends on 29 February 2028. When the last day is a Saturday or Sunday, subsection 36(2) lets the objection be lodged on the next day that is not a weekend or holiday. The Act’s own example: a person who has until Saturday 31 March may apply on Monday 2 April.

## What the objection deadline checker does not cover

The checker does not decide whether a decision can be objected to, which item of subsection 170(1) applies, or when notice was given, so keep the notice’s issue and receipt dates. It does not know public holidays: they differ by state and town, so check the last day and the day after it where you lodge. It does not cover private rulings, administrative penalties, retained refunds, deceased estates, specialised taxes or the other decisions for which section 14ZW sets its own period, or the time to seek review of an objection decision. Confirm the date with the notice of decision or a registered tax agent.

## How the PAYG instalments calculator works

The PAYG instalments calculator works out one quarterly pay as you go instalment by the method on the activity statement. Under option 1 the instalment is the amount the ATO notifies at T7. Varying it, the first instalment of the income year is 25% of the estimated tax for the year, the second 50%, the third 75% and the fourth 100%, each less the earlier instalments and plus any credits claimed for them. A negative result is a credit to claim at 5B.

Under option 2 the instalment is the quarter’s instalment income at T1 times the instalment rate at T2. A varied rate is the estimated tax for the year divided by the estimated instalment income, times 100. If the varied rate is lower than the earlier rate, the earlier instalments less any credits claimed, less the earlier instalment income times the varied rate, is a credit for 5B.

For 2026-27, the instalment amount the ATO notifies already includes its GDP adjustment of 5%, which raises the base year’s income to allow for growth; the adjustment does not change an instalment rate. The ATO’s own example: instalment income of $20,100 at a rate of 1.7% gives $341.70, entered as 341 at T11 and 5A.

## What the PAYG instalments calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out instalment income, the tax on it or whether you can choose between the 2 options. It does not cover monthly instalments, the 2 instalments a year some payers make, annual instalments or consolidated groups, and it does not estimate general interest charge on a variation that proves too low. It does not check whether you may claim a credit or when an instalment is due. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s PAYG instalments calculator or a registered tax agent.

## How the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator works

The redundancy and ETP withholding calculator works out how much a payer withholds from one life benefit employment termination payment (ETP) made in 2026-27. A genuine redundancy or early retirement scheme payment is tax-free up to $13,598 plus $6,801 for each completed year of service. The part above that limit is a code R ETP, as are payments for invalidity and compensation for personal injury, unfair dismissal, harassment or discrimination. The code R part is withheld at the rate within the cap up to the $270,000 ETP cap and at 47% above it.

The rest of the ETP is code O: a golden handshake, gratuity, non-genuine redundancy payment, severance pay, payment in lieu of notice, unused sick leave or unused rostered days off. Its cap is the smaller of the ETP cap left after the code R part and the whole-of-income cap, which is $180,000 less the other taxable payments you made to the employee in the year. The rate within the cap is 17% if the employee is their preservation age or older on 30 June 2027, otherwise 32%; 47% applies above the cap.

Schedule 11’s example 4 takes Alec, 30, made redundant after 5 years of service with a $48,000 genuine redundancy payment and a $17,678 gratuity, having been paid $140,000 of salary and wages in the year. It works out a $397 ETP over the $47,603 tax-free limit, withholds $127 from it and $5,657 from the gratuity within the $40,000 whole-of-income cap. This calculator opens on those figures, with a date of birth that makes Alec 30, and gives the same amounts.

## What the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a termination is a genuine redundancy, whether a payment is an ETP or which code applies. It takes the taxable component as you enter it and does not work out a tax-free component other than the redundancy limit, such as an invalidity segment. It covers one life benefit ETP for one termination: not death benefit ETPs, delayed termination payments, a later instalment for the same termination, an earlier ETP in the same year, an employee who has not given you their TFN, a foreign resident or a working holiday maker. It does not work out withholding on unused leave, which Schedule 7 covers. Confirm the figures with Schedule 11 or a registered tax agent.

## How the land tax calculator works

The calculator applies one state or territory’s general or trust scale to a total taxable land value and gives the tax for a full year. Below a jurisdiction’s threshold the tax is nil; the Australian Capital Territory has no threshold, and nor does a special trust in New South Wales. Above it, each scale charges a base amount plus a percentage of the value over a band’s threshold, except that Victoria charges a flat $500 from $50,000 and $975 from $100,000 up to $300,000. South Australia charges for every $100, or part of $100, so the excess is rounded up to the next $100, and RevenueSA issues no assessment where the calculated land tax is under $20; the calculator shows the amount and says so. The Australian Capital Territory adds its fixed charge of $1,778 to the valuation charge and assesses each quarter’s share by its days; the calculator gives the full-year amount. Each scale is the one in force for the land tax year current on 16 November 2026: the 2026 calendar year in New South Wales and Victoria, and the 2026-27 financial year in Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania and the Australian Capital Territory.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the calculator gives the same amount. ## Trust rates, surcharges and the metropolitan region improvement tax

Trust rates are a special trust’s rates in New South Wales, with no threshold; the trust surcharge rates in Victoria; the rates for companies and trustees, including super funds, in Queensland; and the trust rates in South Australia. Western Australia taxes a trustee’s land as if it were the trustee’s own, at the general rates.
