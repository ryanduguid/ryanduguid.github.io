### Which expenses can I claim for a rental property?

Loan interest, council rates and qualifying repairs and maintenance may be deductible for income-producing use. A depreciating asset costing $300 or less may qualify for an immediate deduction only if used predominantly to produce non-business assessable income and the set and identical or substantially identical asset cost tests are met. Check the second-hand residential asset restrictions before claiming. Other depreciating assets, eligible capital works and borrowing expenses have separate deduction rules. Apportion private use. Separate rental income, private use, borrowing costs, repairs and capital items before claiming expenses.

- Gather agent statements, invoices, loan interest, ownership details and rental availability records.
- Apportion costs where required and reconcile the deduction schedule to the supporting documents.

The treatment depends on the expense and property use; a rental label alone does not establish deductibility.

### Is work on my rental property a repair, an improvement or a depreciating asset?

Qualifying repairs and maintenance for income-producing use may be immediately deductible. Initial repairs for defects that existed when you acquired the property, improvements and replacements of an entire unit of property are capital. Assess any deduction under the depreciating asset or capital works rules separately. Eligible expenditure may form part of the CGT cost base. For property acquired after 7.30 pm (ACT time) on 13 May 1997, and for expenditure after 30 June 1999 on property acquired earlier, the cost base is reduced by capital works deductions claimed or claimable. Capital expenditure does not automatically produce a deduction over several years. Review what the work did to the property and when the relevant damage or condition arose.

- Keep invoices, scope of work, purchase condition and dates; split distinct components where supported.
- Assess repair, capital works and depreciating asset treatment separately and update the relevant schedules.

Cost alone does not distinguish a repair from an improvement or initial repair.

### How do I work out the capital gain when I sell an investment property?

The capital gain is the capital proceeds less the cost base. An Australian resident individual who owned the property for at least 12 months can usually reduce the gain by the 50% CGT discount. For a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027, the first enacted stage of reform treats gains that accrue from that date differently: cost base indexation replaces the discount for assets held at least 12 months, and a minimum 30% tax can apply to certain net capital gains of individuals. Gains accrued before then keep their existing treatment, such as the discount, but the pre-CGT exemption does not cover gains accruing from that date. An owner can choose to keep the current rules for an eligible new residential dwelling or affordable housing. Build the capital gains calculation from the relevant event, proceeds and adjusted cost base.

- Gather purchase and sale contracts, costs, ownership and occupancy history, and capital works records, and for a property acquired before 1 July 2027 and sold on or after that date, its market value immediately before that date, where required.
- Review any exemptions, adjustments, residency and discount conditions before calculating the net result.

A sale price minus purchase price calculation can omit material tax adjustments.

### How do capital losses and the CGT discount affect tax on shares I sell?

Apply capital losses first. An Australian resident individual who owned the shares for at least 12 months can then reduce the remaining gain by the 50% CGT discount. For a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027, the first enacted stage of reform treats gains that accrue from that date differently: cost base indexation replaces the discount for assets held at least 12 months, and a minimum 30% tax can apply to certain net capital gains of individuals. Gains accrued before then keep their existing treatment, such as the discount, but the pre-CGT exemption does not cover gains accruing from that date. Calculate each gain or loss, apply eligible capital losses, then consider any available discount.

- Retain trade records, cost base adjustments, holding periods and carried-forward loss balances, and for shares acquired before 1 July 2027 and sold on or after that date, each holding’s market value immediately before that date, where required.
- Reconcile the final calculation to the year’s CGT schedule and keep the remaining loss record.

The bounded worksheet assumes the gains, losses and discount eligibility have already been established.

### Do I owe tax when I sell, swap or spend cryptocurrency?

Often, yes. Selling crypto or swapping one crypto asset for another is a CGT event, so a gain can be taxable. Review each crypto disposal and income event, including swaps and spending, rather than only withdrawals to cash.

- Export exchange and wallet activity, fees and Australian dollar values with dates.
- Reconcile transfers between your own wallets separately from transactions that require tax review.

The tax treatment depends on the activity and event; this site does not import wallets or classify transactions.

### How do I report ETF distributions and their tax adjustments?

Declare ETF distributions, including amounts reinvested through a distribution reinvestment plan. Your ETF tax statement, such as an AMMA statement, shows the amounts to report and where, and non-assessable amounts mostly adjust the cost base of your units. Use the fund’s annual tax statement and any AMMA statement to reconcile the distribution components.

- Match cash, reinvestments, capital gains, foreign income and credits to the statement.
- Record cost base increases or decreases and carry them into the investment register.

Cash received alone can differ from the amount and components that belong in the tax return.

### What records does my accountant need for an SMSF annual return and audit?

Trustees must appoint an approved SMSF auditor each year, at least 45 days before the SMSF annual return is due. The auditor needs a statement of financial position and an operating statement before starting, and any requested information within 14 days; the audit must be finished before the return is lodged. Prepare the financial records and trustee evidence needed for both the return and the independent audit.

- Gather bank and investment records, valuations, contributions, pensions, minutes, member balances and supporting agreements.
- Reconcile opening and closing balances and resolve the auditor’s record requests before finalising the return.

This checklist does not perform an SMSF audit or certify compliance.

### When does an investment property become liable for land tax in NSW?

For owners entitled to the general threshold, NSW land tax for the 2026 land tax year applies when the combined taxable value of their non-exempt NSW land exceeds $1,075,000. Some trusts and related companies do not qualify for that threshold, and foreign-owner surcharge land tax has no tax-free threshold. Check ownership, land values, exemptions and any surcharge conditions for the relevant NSW land tax year.

- Reconcile all relevant NSW landholdings, ownership interests and valuation notices.
- Use Revenue NSW’s current assessment guidance and retain the calculation and exemption evidence.

Purchase price and one property’s value alone do not establish the full land tax position.

### Does a Hunter Valley coal mining employer pay the Coal LSL levy?

Yes, for eligible employees in the black coal mining industry. The employer reports and pays the levy monthly, currently 2.7% of each eligible employee’s eligible wages. The levy depends on whether each employee is an eligible employee in the black coal mining industry, then on that employee’s eligible wages for the month.

- List each employee’s role and check it against the Coal LSL eligible employee guidance.
- Work out each eligible employee’s eligible wages for the month using the section 3B formula that applies.

The calculator does not decide whether an employee is eligible; it calculates the levy on the wages you supply.

## How the GST calculator works

The GST calculator takes one amount for a wholly taxable supply and works out the GST, the GST-exclusive amount and the GST-inclusive amount. For a wholly taxable amount at 10% GST, exclusive amount / 10 gives GST; inclusive amount / 11 gives its GST component. Each result rounds once to cents.

The 10% rate and the one-eleventh fraction come from ATO: How GST works and the A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999.

## What the GST calculator does not decide

The GST calculator does not decide whether a supply is taxable, whether you must be registered for GST or what a BAS should report. Mixed, GST-free and input-taxed supplies need classification first.

## How the margin and markup calculator works

The margin and markup calculator works out gross profit, margin and markup from one sales figure and one direct cost figure. Gross profit = sales − direct cost. Margin = gross profit / sales × 100. Markup = gross profit / direct cost × 100. A zero denominator leaves that percentage undefined.

The margin and markup calculator does not set a price, test whether direct cost is complete or adjust for GST. Use a consistent GST basis and matching period for both figures.

## How the break-even calculator works

The break-even sales calculator works out how many whole units, and how much in sales, cover the fixed costs for a period. Contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit. Required units = fixed costs / contribution, rounded up. Required sales = whole units × price. Price must exceed variable cost per unit, or no break-even exists.

No statutory rate or threshold is applied. The definitions follow Business Queensland: break-even and profit.

## What the break-even calculator does not decide

The break-even sales calculator does not forecast demand or say that the units can be sold. It uses one price and one variable cost, so use one product or a stable representative mix and a consistent GST basis.

## How the fixed fee pricing calculator works

The fixed fee pricing calculator turns an estimate of hours into a fee you can quote before the work starts. Enter your hourly rate with a 0% margin, or your cost per hour with the margin you want the fee to carry: fee = hours × rate / (1 − margin). A margin is a share of the fee, so a 40% margin on a $95 cost is $158.33 an hour, not the $133 a 40% markup would give. The fee then rounds up to the cent or to the next $10, $50 or $100, so rounding never takes it below your rate or margin.

GST at 10% is added to the fee when you tick the box, and the fee including GST is split into monthly instalments. Each instalment is the total divided evenly and rounded down to the cent; the first instalment also carries the cents left over, at most one cent for each instalment after it. The instalments add up to the fee to the cent, every instalment after the first is the same amount for a recurring payment, and if the engagement ends early the client has paid at least an even share of the fee for each month paid. The effective hourly rate is the fee before GST over the hours, so you can see what rounding and the margin earn an hour.

## What the fixed fee pricing calculator leaves out

The fixed fee pricing calculator prices one piece of work. For several services or entities, price each and add the fees. It does not suggest a market rate, allow for work beyond the hours you estimated, or decide whether your supply is taxable: add GST only if you are registered for GST and the supply is taxable.

## How the loan repayment schedule calculator works

The calculator sets out a loan payment by payment, weekly, fortnightly, monthly, quarterly or yearly. Give any 3 of the regular payment, the amount financed, the number of payments and the annual nominal interest rate, with the balloon, and it works out the fourth. Each row shows the date, the balance before the payment, the payment, the interest since the last payment, the principal repaid and the balance after it.

The period rate is the annual nominal rate divided by the payments a year: 52 for weekly and 26 for fortnightly, so a year of weekly payments is taken as 52 weeks. Payments in arrears fall at the end of each period, the first one period after the start date. Payments in advance fall at the start, the first on the start date, so the first carries no interest. A balloon, or residual, is due at the end of the last period: with the last payment in arrears, or a period after it in advance. It can be an amount or a percentage of the amount financed, rounded half up to the cent.

Each period’s interest is rounded to the cent, so the last payment differs from the others by the cents that rounding leaves. In advance, the last payment leaves the balloon’s value a period before it falls due, rounded to the cent, and the balloon row’s interest is the rest of the balloon. The payment is the same as a spreadsheet’s PMT with the balloon as the future value and type 1 for advance.

The amount financed is the payments and balloon discounted at the rate, rounded half up to the cent. The number of payments is found by running the payment until one would take the balance to the balloon; that payment is the last and is smaller. The interest rate is found by bisection between 0% and 100% a year: halving 48 times leaves it within 0.0000000000004% a year of the rate at which the payments and balloon, discounted, equal the amount financed. The page refuses payments and a balloon that add up to less than the amount financed, which no rate of nil or more fits, payments that would need more than 100% a year, and a payment that never covers a period’s interest.

Download schedule CSV saves the inputs, totals and every row as plain figures.

## What the loan repayment schedule calculator leaves out

The calculator assumes a rate that does not change, payments on the same day of each period and no fees. It does not work out GST, stamp duty or the tax treatment of the interest or the asset. Lenders that accrue interest daily, or count a weekly year as 365 days, give slightly different figures. The result is not a lender’s quote.

## How the AASB 16 lease calculator works

The calculator measures a lessee’s lease under AASB 16 at commencement and sets out each income year to the end of the lease. ## How the borrowing expenses calculator works

The borrowing expenses calculator works out the deduction for costs of borrowing money, such as loan establishment fees, mortgage registration and lender’s mortgage insurance, under section 25-25 for up to 3 loans and every income year of each. Each year’s maximum is what is left of the expenditure over the days left in the period of the loan, times those days in the year, rounded to the cent. The deduction is that maximum times the year’s income use, which can change from year to year.

A loan repaid early ends its period that day. The remaining expenditure becomes the maximum for that year before apportionment. A loan that refinances an earlier one spreads its own expenses from its own first day. When everything incurred for borrowing in a year comes to $100 or less of income use, it is deducted that year instead.

The optional accounting column sets the same expenses against the loan under AASB 9: the effective interest rate that discounts the loan’s monthly payments to the amount borrowed less the expenses, the expenses recognised each year by that rate, the write-off when a loan is extinguished, the 10% test for a refinancing with the same lender, and the amortised cost at 30 June. Beside each year’s section 25-25 amount it shows the difference, and what tax and accounting each still carry at 30 June.

For the same-lender test, enter the part of the borrowing expenses paid to the lender or on its behalf. Enter zero if there were none. Leave fees received blank if there were none. Exclude fees paid to third parties for your own account from the 10% test.

## What the borrowing expenses calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide which costs are borrowing expenses rather than interest or a capital cost of the property, or how much of the money is used to produce income: you state those. It refuses a loan first drawn before 1 July 2017, an income year after 2027-28, a holiday home or other leisure facility unless subsection 26-50(3) applies all year (whether the anti-avoidance rule in subsection 26-50(7) stops it applying is yours to decide), and a refinancing whose dates do not meet. It works years after 2027-28 under section 25-25 as read and marks them projected. It does not apply the vacant land or residential loss limits, interest deductions, redraws, Division 7A loans or debt forgiveness.

### Vacant land and residential loss limits

The result shows the section 25-25 amount before any applicable limit. Section 26-102 can limit deductions for vacant land holding costs incurred from 1 July 2019, including ongoing costs of borrowing to acquire the land. From 2027-28, section 26-155 can restrict deductions for residential accommodation. Check whether either limit or an exception applies to your costs.

The accounting column takes every expense as integral to the effective interest rate, fixed monthly payments in arrears and the expected life you give, and shares a month’s amortisation across 30 June by days. It does not work a modification under 10%, a variable rate repricing, fair value through profit or loss, AASB 112 deferred tax or the AASB 123 capitalisation period. Confirm the result with the ATO’s Borrowing expenses page or a registered tax agent before relying on it.

## How the depreciation pools calculator works

The calculator works one of 3 Division 40, Subdivision 328-D or Division 43 worksheets for an income year from 2020-21 to 2026-27. A low-value pool declines by 18.75% of the low-cost assets and second element costs added in the year, and by 37.5% of the previous closing pool balance and the low-value assets added (section 40-440). The 2 parts are added before rounding. Termination values reduce the closing balance, and any excess over it is assessable income (section 40-445).

A general small business pool first adds the opening balance, the year’s additions and cost additions, less termination values (subsection 328-210(2)). An amount above zero and below the year’s low pool value threshold is deducted in full and the pool closes at zero: $20,000 from 2023-24, and no limit from 2020-21 to 2022-23. An amount below zero is assessable income (section 328-215). Otherwise the deduction is 30% of the opening balance and 15% of the additions and cost additions (section 328-190), each rounded to the cent. The ATO’s example of Loretta’s pool, with a $100,000 opening balance, $28,000 of additions and an $8,000 sale, gives a $34,200 deduction and an $85,800 closing balance.

A capital works deduction is your construction expenditure times the days used over 365, at 4% for a part used in the 4% manner or else 2.5%, then reduced to the taxable use and limited to the undeducted construction expenditure (section 43-210, or section 43-215 for works begun from 16 September 1987 to 26 February 1992). The ATO’s example 29 in Rental properties 2026, $115,800 for 346 days at 2.5%, gives $2,744.

## What the depreciation pools calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide which assets may go into a pool, whether you are a small business entity that chose simplified depreciation, or each asset’s taxable use: you enter the amounts after those choices. It does not work out temporary full expensing of individual assets from 2020-21 to 2022-23, the instant asset write-off or backing business investment, and refuses the one 2020-21 small business pool case where the 57.5% rate could apply. It leaves out the change of business use adjustment, roll-over relief, the car limit and balancing adjustments outside a pool. For capital works it refuses works begun before 16 September 1987 and the 4% rate for works begun before 27 February 1992, and does not decide whether the works qualify, work out the undeducted construction expenditure or split it amongst co-owners. Results are planning estimates: check them against your records and the ATO’s guidance.

## How the Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator works

The Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator works out the least a borrower must repay on one amalgamated loan in one income year after the year the loan was made. It applies the formula in subsection 109E(6) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936: the loan not repaid at the end of the previous income year, times the year’s benchmark interest rate, divided by 1 minus (1 ÷ (1 + the rate)) raised to the remaining term in years.

The interest for the year is the benchmark rate on the whole balance, and the principal is the rest of the repayment. That split assumes the whole repayment is made on the last day of the income year. The ATO works interest out from the daily balance, so a repayment made earlier in the year carries less interest and more of it reduces the principal.

The ATO’s worked example on Loans by private companies takes $55,000 at 5.95% with 7 years remaining and gives $9,835 to the nearest dollar; this calculator gives $9,834.92 for those figures.

## What the Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a payment or loan is a Division 7A loan, whether the loan agreement complies, whether a shortfall is a deemed dividend or how much of it, or how a distributable surplus limits one. It works one income year only, with no repayment schedule, and none for the year in which the loan was made. It takes the balance as you enter it: it does not work it out from the loans made, repayments made before the lodgment day or the previous year’s interest, and it does not combine several loans. It does not handle an income year that ends on a day other than 30 June. Subsection 109E(5) lets regulations set another amount, and this calculator applies the formula only. Confirm the figures with the ATO Division 7A calculator and decision tool or a registered tax agent.
