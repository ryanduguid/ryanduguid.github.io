## How the first home owner grant calculator works

The first home owner grant calculator gives the grant for a first home buyer who buys or builds a new home under a contract made from 1 July 2025 to 22 November 2026, beside the transfer duty the same buyer pays. It applies the contract-date grant and cap, paying nothing above the cap or for established homes except in the Northern Territory before 1 October 2025.

- New South Wales: $10,000; cap $600,000 for a home bought, $750,000 for land and building contract.
- Victoria: $10,000, or consideration if less, up to $750,000. For a home built the cap tests the building contract alone, so totals over $750,000 need that price entered.
- Queensland: $30,000, or the consideration if less, for a home worth under $750,000. The cap tests the greater of the price and the unencumbered value of the home and land, or for a home built the contract and the land’s value; enter that value where it differs.
- Western Australia: $10,000, or consideration if less. Cap is $800,000 south of the 26th parallel from 7 May 2026 ($750,000 before) and $1,000,000 north; enter any different cap value.
- South Australia: $15,000, or the consideration if less, with no value cap.
- Tasmania: $30,000 for contracts from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026, then $20,000 to 30 June 2027, or the consideration if less, with no value cap. The transaction must be completed within 24 months, or a period the Commissioner extends, and not replace a transaction for the same property, between or benefiting substantially the same parties, entered before 1 July 2025 for a 2025-26 contract or 1 July 2026 for a 2026-27 contract.
- Northern Territory: HomeGrown Territory Grant $50,000, or consideration if less, no cap. Established homes bought 1 July to 30 September 2025 get $10,000, or consideration if less. Must not replace a pre-1 October 2024 contract.
- Australian Capital Territory: $0 since 1 July 2019; the duty result applies the Home Buyer Concession Scheme.

## Eligibility facts and transfer duty

Each grant needs 4 facts, which you state and the calculator does not test. Every applicant is a person aged 18 or over. At least one applicant is an Australian citizen or permanent resident. No applicant or spouse is excluded by the prior ownership rules below. The applicants meet the residence requirement: 12 continuous months starting within 12 months in New South Wales; 12 months for at least one applicant in Victoria; 6 continuous months starting within 12 months in Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia and Tasmania; and 12 months after taking possession or completion in the Northern Territory. Without the building contract price entered, a home built also needs you to state that the price, excluding land, is at least the grant; with it entered, Queensland, Victoria, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania and the Northern Territory pay a lower contract price instead. A Northern Territory contract must not replace one signed before 1 October 2024. A Tasmanian transaction must be completed within 24 months, or a period the Commissioner extends, and not replace a transaction for the same property, between or benefiting substantially the same parties, entered before the 1 July starting its grant period. The calculator computes nothing until the facts a transaction needs are stated, and in Western Australia until you choose where the home is.

For a home bought, the result adds the transfer duty calculator’s duty for an eligible first home buyer and its general duty on the same value, for contracts its rates cover: from 1 July 2025 in Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and the Northern Territory, from 7 May 2026 in Western Australia, and from 1 July 2026 in New South Wales, Tasmania and the Australian Capital Territory. Earlier contracts there get the grant without the duty.

## Prior ownership rules

The sources read state these rules. New South Wales: no applicant or spouse owned a home or other residential property in Australia before 1 July 2000. Victoria: none owned one before 1 July 2000, or lived for a continuous 6 months or more in a home they owned on or after 1 July 2000. Western Australia: none owned residential property on or after 1 July 2000 and lived in it for a continuous 6 months or more beginning on or after 1 July 2004. Tasmania: none owned a residential property before 1 July 2000, or owned and lived in one for more than 6 months after 1 July 2000. For Queensland, South Australia and the Northern Territory the sources read do not set the rule out, so state the fact against the revenue office’s own eligibility rules.

## What the first home owner grant calculator does not compute

The calculator refuses a contract dated before 1 July 2025 or after 22 November 2026, and a value or building contract price below the grant amount in New South Wales, where the source read did not confirm the rule. Without the building contract price, it refuses a Victorian home built whose land and building total is over $750,000, since Victoria caps the building contract alone, and a home built unless you state that the building contract price is at least the grant. With the price entered, a contract below the grant gets the contract price, as each Act read pays the lesser of the consideration and the grant. It does not cover owner-builders, substantially renovated homes, the ADF and family violence exemptions, the Commissioner’s discretion over the residence requirement, or schemes that are not first home owner grants, such as regional building grants, shared equity schemes and Commonwealth deposit schemes. For a home built, the duty falls on the land, and the first home buyer concessions on vacant land are not computed. Confirm the grant with the revenue office or an approved agent before relying on it.

## How the payroll tax calculator works

The payroll tax calculator estimates one employer’s payroll tax for the whole of 2026-27 in one state or territory. Each jurisdiction allows a deduction or tax-free threshold, which an employer that also pays wages elsewhere in Australia shares in proportion to its wages there. Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia and the Northern Territory reduce the deduction as Australian wages grow, until it reaches nil. The result shows the deduction, the amount taxed, the tax at each rate and, in Victoria and Queensland, each surcharge or levy on its own line, with the provision applied.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the tests hold them. Revenue NSW: $1,500,000 of NSW wages are taxed on $300,000, and $900,000 of $3,000,000 has a threshold of $360,000. The Victorian State Revenue Office: $80 million in Victoria and $70 million in New South Wales give surcharge thresholds of $5,333,333.33 and $53,333,333.33. The Queensland Revenue Office: $1,356,789 of $1,789,776 has a deduction of $932,460 to the dollar. RevenueWA: annual wages of $1,104,000 give $6,600; its other 2 examples round the deduction to the dollar and come out 1 and 3 cents lower than this calculator. RevenueSA: $800,000 of $1,600,000 has a deduction of $300,000 and $500,000 of $2,000,000 one of $150,000. The Tasmanian State Revenue Office: $2,400,000 gives $54,400, and $2,400,000 of $3,000,000 gives $72,800. The ACT Revenue Office and the Territory Revenue Office publish no worked figures that could be read, so the tests work examples from the Acts by hand: $84,375 on $3,000,000 in the Australian Capital Territory and $41,250 on $3,000,000 in the Northern Territory. This calculator opens on Revenue NSW’s first example.

## What the payroll tax calculator does not compute

The payroll tax calculator gives one annual estimate and no advice. It does not decide which payments are taxable wages or where they are taxable, and does not cover:

- an employer that is a member of a group, whose threshold and rate depend on the group’s wages and which members share the deduction;
- an employer that paid wages for only part of 2026-27, whose threshold every jurisdiction reduces by the days it employed;
- monthly or other periodic returns, and the annual reconciliation of amounts already paid;
- exemptions, rebates and discounts, such as Queensland’s apprentice and trainee rebate and its 1% discount for regional employers, claimed in the return, and the ACT’s 6.85% cap for an eligible university;
- any year other than 2026-27.

Confirm the figure with the revenue office or a registered tax agent before relying on it.

## How the SGC and Payday Super calculator works

From 1 July 2026, under Payday Super, an employer owes super guarantee for each payday, and the employee’s fund must receive it within 7 business days, or a longer period for a first contribution, out-of-cycle pay or an ATO determination. The calculator finds that due date from the site’s business days calendar, then works out the super guarantee charge for one employee if the fund received less than 12% of qualifying earnings by then: the final shortfall still unpaid at the assessment, notional earnings at the GIC rate on the amount that was late, and the administrative uplift, which may be reduced based on the employer’s qualifying assessment and estimate history and any approved disclosure lodged before the assessment day. A contribution counts from the day the fund received it with what it needs to allocate it to the employee. If an unresolved part-day holiday changes the due date, the calculator shows the possible dates and withholds the charge. For a quarter that ended by 30 June 2026 it applies the old law instead: the shortfall at that year’s charge percentage, 10% nominal interest and the $20 administration component.

## What the SGC and Payday Super calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employee and one payday or quarter, and no advice. It does not cover:

- the choice loading for not meeting choice of fund obligations;
- the late payment offset under the old section 23A for quarterly contributions made late;
- bunched contributions under section 18C(2) item 4;
- a contribution made from 1 to 28 July 2026 that item 186 of the transition applies first to a shortfall for the quarter ending 30 June 2026: count only what is left of it;
- a disclosed contribution with no receipt day, whose notional earnings section 36(3) works out as if the fund received it 7 business days after it was paid;
- exemption certificates, and amounts paid that have not reached the fund;
- GIC and penalties once the charge is payable;
- Norfolk Island salary or wages, of which item 2(2) of Schedule 2 to the Tax and Superannuation Laws Amendment (Norfolk Island Reforms) Act 2015 (Compilation No. 1, 1 July 2026) for quarters to 30 June 2026, and item 189 of Schedule 1 to the Payday Superannuation Act for paydays in 2026-27, count only part, for a transitional rate of 7% to 10% for 2022-23 to 2025-26 and 11% for 2026-27 (Table 21 of the ATO’s key superannuation rates), both Acts read 19 November 2026: the calculator refuses them rather than apply the general rate;
- paydays after 30 June 2027, whose maximum contributions base is not yet known, and quarters before July 2022.

## How the Payday Super cashflow calculator works

Until 30 June 2026 an employer could hold a quarter’s super guarantee until 28 days after the quarter ended. From 1 July 2026, under Payday Super, the employee’s fund must receive 12% of each payday’s qualifying earnings within 7 business days of that payday. The calculator lists the paydays in the chosen quarter of 2026-27, each payday’s super guarantee and the date the fund must receive it, and how many days earlier that is than the quarterly date would have been. The extra working capital is what has already gone to funds but would still be in the business under the quarterly rule. It builds with each payday and peaks at the whole quarter’s super guarantee from the last payday’s due date until the day before the quarterly date. The average days earlier weighs each payday’s amount by how much sooner it leaves. Both dates are when the fund must receive the money, so allow for a clearing house’s processing time before each. In the first quarter, July to September 2026, the April to June 2026 contributions, still due by 28 July 2026, also fall due alongside the first paydays under the new rule.

## What the Payday Super cashflow calculator does not cover

The calculator is an estimate for planning and gives no advice. It does not cover:

- the longer periods for a first contribution for a new employee or to a new fund, out-of-cycle pay, an ATO exceptional circumstances determination or bunched contributions: use the SGC and Payday Super calculator for those due dates;
- earnings that change from one payday to the next, extra or irregular paydays, and the April to June 2026 quarter’s contributions;
- each employee’s maximum contributions base of $270,830 for 2026-27: enter only earnings on which super guarantee is payable;
- Norfolk Island salary or wages, which have a transitional rate of 11% for 2026-27;
- the cost of the extra working capital, such as interest on an overdraft, and any super above the minimum;
- paydays outside 2026-27.

Confirm the figures with the ATO or a registered tax agent before relying on them.

## How the long service leave calculator works

Long service leave comes from each state and territory’s own Act, not the National Employment Standards. The calculator measures the service from the first day to the last, less the days you exclude, and applies the Act you choose. While employment continues, it gives the leave the employee can take now and when the next amount becomes available. When employment ends, it decides from the length of service and the reason whether the Act pays anything, works out the weeks it pays, deducts leave already taken or paid, and values the rest at the weekly ordinary pay. Each step names its provision. Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory give full leave after 7 years and the other states and territories after 10; New South Wales and the Australian Capital Territory can pay a pro rata amount from 5 years, the others from 7, and Victoria not before 7 years at all.

## What the long service leave calculator does not cover

- which Act applies to employment in more than one state or territory, and whether an award, enterprise agreement or contract gives a better entitlement, including the long service leave terms of a pre-2010 award or agreement that section 113 of the Fair Work Act 2009 preserves;

- whether a break keeps service continuous, which absences count, and whether a reason for leaving qualifies, such as a pressing necessity or serious misconduct: you decide those;
- averaging pay for varied hours, casual or part-time work, commission or piece rates;
- leave taken in advance of the entitlement, cashing out, and service before 23 June 1990, which some Acts count under earlier rules;
- tax withheld from the payment.

## How the wine equalisation tax calculator works

Wine equalisation tax (WET) is 29% of the taxable value of each assessable dealing in wine (section 5-5). A wholesale sale’s taxable value is its price excluding WET and GST, and GST is then 10% of the price including WET: the ATO’s $120 dozen bears WET of $34.80 and GST of $15.48, $170.28 in all. A retail sale or own use, such as wine used for tastings, is valued at a notional wholesale selling price; own use of wine bought under quote is valued at its purchase price excluding GST (section 5-5, items AD3c and AD13c). By the half retail price method that is 50% of the retail price including WET and GST (section 9-35), so the ATO’s $140 dozen at the cellar door bears WET of $20.30. Grape wine may instead use the average wholesale price method, the weighted average of the tax period’s wholesale prices for wine of the same vintage and variety (section 9-40), but only if at least 10% by value of those sales are wholesale sales (subsection 9-25(3)); wine that is not grape wine must use half retail (section 9-30). A sale under quote bears no WET (section 7-10). Each amount is rounded half up to the cent.

A producer that meets the conditions of section 19-5 earns a producer rebate of 29% of the wholesale price, or of the notional wholesale selling price, on each dealing it is liable for, and on a sale under quote unless the quote states the buyer will use the wine in manufacture, make a GST-free supply of it or sell it on under quote. The rebates for a financial year are capped, for the producer or for its group of associated producers together: $350,000 for dealings to 30 June 2026 and $400,000 from 1 July 2026 (subsections 19-15(2) and (3)). The calculator takes the rebates already claimed this year off the cap and gives what is left. The wine tax goes to label 1C and the rebate to label 1D of the business activity statement, each in whole dollars, never netted. It reproduces the ATO’s rebate table for 2026-27: WET of $11,890 and rebates of $16,240 on its retail, wholesale, quoted and tasting dealings.

## What the wine equalisation tax calculator does not cover

The calculator works dealings in the financial years 2023-24 to 2027-28 and refuses earlier years, which carry the transitional rebate rules for 2017 and earlier vintage wine. It refuses the average wholesale price method for wine that is not grape wine or without the 10% share of wholesale sales, and a rebate on own use of wine bought under quote. It takes each price as given, so it does not work out an arm’s length price (section 27-10) or the price of wine sold with other goods; it assumes GST applies to a sale and does not decide whether a supply is GST-free.

Not covered: imports and other customs dealings, whose WET the Department of Home Affairs collects; the special valuations of section 9-45; wine tax credits other than the producer rebate, such as bad debts and overpaid WET; the reduction of a rebate for earlier producers’ rebates on wine used in a blend; New Zealand participants’ rebates; whether producers are associated (the group’s claims are taken as given); and the tax period a dealing belongs to. A value or total of $70,368,744,177,664 or more is refused, as the page cannot show it to the cent. Rebates claimed above the cap are flagged, not worked out: section 19-25 makes the excess payable as wine tax at the end of the financial year. Have a registered tax agent confirm the result before you lodge.
