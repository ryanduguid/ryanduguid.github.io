## What the inputs need

The most tax is what the lump sum can bear in the person’s return, before the Medicare levy. The amount withheld is what the fund takes out when it pays, under Schedule 12 of the withholding schedules; it is a credit against that tax. A departing Australia superannuation payment, to a former temporary resident who has left Australia, bears its own final tax.

For one lump sum from a complying fund paid on a condition of release: not an income stream commutation or a payment in breach of the payment rules. A roll-over and a payment to the trustee of a deceased estate are worked only for withholding. The fund states the interest’s value and tax free component just before the payment, and the untaxed element.

The lump sum’s tax free component is its share of the interest’s, rounded to the cent; the rest is taxable, taxed in the fund except the untaxed element. Leave that at 0 for a fund that pays tax on its income, as most do.

Needed only with an untaxed element. An untaxed element from service before 1 July 1983 is refused: part of it then becomes tax free under section 307-150.

You decide whether the person is a death benefits dependant: a spouse or former spouse, a child under 18, someone interdependent with or dependent on the member, or anyone if the member died in the line of duty (section 302-195).

Two registered medical practitioners, one a specialist, certify death is likely within 24 months. A disability superannuation benefit is refused: section 307-145 enlarges its tax free component.

The low rate cap applies only at preservation age and under 60, which from 1 July 2024 no one is. Enter the amounts counted for earlier lump sums. Other lump sums at that age in the same year share the cap, so they are refused while any is left.

### What the withholding and DASP inputs need

Withholding is worked for payments from 13 October 2020. With a TFN, a resident’s rate for each part is its ceiling rate plus the 2% Medicare levy, and the total rounds to the nearest dollar. Without one, the fund withholds 47% (45% for a foreign resident) of the taxable component, or of the untaxed element at 60 or over, ignoring cents. Without a TFN the rate turns on the payee’s age, which the calculator does not take for a death benefit, and none is stated for a member benefit under $200, so a non-dependant’s death benefit and such a member benefit are refused. The calculator assumes the payment is taxable in Australia and applies no tax treaty.

Take the components from the fund’s or the ATO’s statement. The working holiday maker rate applies when the payment includes amounts from contributions made whilst the person was a working holiday maker. An unclaimed money roll-over with an untaxed element is refused: section 5(2) taxes any excess untaxed roll-over amount in it at nil.

### What the income stream inputs need

An income stream payment is worked under Schedule 13, Part A, for payments in 2024-25 to 2026-27. Its tax free component is its share of the interest’s when the stream commenced. The fund withholds from the taxable component under 60, or only from the untaxed element at 60 or over, ignoring cents, by the PAYG tax table for the period: scale 2 with the tax-free threshold claimed, scale 1 without. It takes off a tax offset (15% of the taxed element for a disability super income stream or a dependant under 60, 10% of the untaxed element at 60 or over) and withholds the greater of the rest and the offset adjustment for the Medicare levy. Without a TFN it withholds 47% (45% for a foreign resident) with no offset. A capped defined benefit income stream to someone 60 or over is refused, as Parts B to E apply, as are a foreign resident with a TFN and a dependant under 60 whose deceased died at 60 or over.

## How the super lump sum tax calculator works

The super lump sum tax calculator splits one lump sum from a complying fund, paid from 1 July 2020 to 30 June 2027, into its components. It gives the most income tax each part can bear before the Medicare levy. A member’s treatment turns on age on the payment day and preservation age; a death benefit’s turns on whether the person receiving it is a death benefits dependant. Each rate is a ceiling: the offsets stop the tax on a part going above it, so a person on lower marginal rates pays less, and the Medicare levy is added on top.

It also works the amount the fund withholds from the same lump sum under Schedule 12, for a payment from 13 October 2020, including from a roll-over and a death benefit paid to a deceased estate; the amount withheld from one income stream payment under Schedule 13, Part A, from 1 July 2024; and the final tax on a departing Australia superannuation payment in 2025-26 to 2027-28.

Its results match the super-benefit-tax command of Ryan Duguid’s australian-accounting calculators on the ATO’s examples and 532 test cases, and its super-withholding and dasp-tax commands on 613 more and the super-withholding command’s income stream withholding on 359 more, refusals included.

## What the super lump sum tax calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work the tax at the person’s own marginal rates, the Medicare levy or the tax on an income stream in the person’s return. Nor does it work withholding from a capped defined benefit income stream to someone 60 or over (Schedule 13 Parts B to E), a commutation of an income stream, the tax on a roll-over or a deceased estate, a tax treaty, or payments from a non-complying or foreign fund. It does not decide dependency, a terminal medical condition, the components or the elements: you state those. It refuses a disability superannuation benefit, an untaxed element from service before 1 July 1983, and other lump sums in the same year that share the low rate cap.

Confirm the result with the fund, the ATO’s guidance on super lump sums or a registered tax agent before relying on it. The redundancy and ETP withholding calculator covers employment termination payments.

## How the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator works

The redundancy and ETP withholding calculator works out how much a payer withholds from one life benefit employment termination payment (ETP) made in 2026-27. A genuine redundancy or early retirement scheme payment is tax-free up to $13,598 plus $6,801 for each completed year of service. The part above that limit is a code R ETP, as are payments for invalidity and compensation for personal injury, unfair dismissal, harassment or discrimination. The code R part is withheld at the rate within the cap up to the $270,000 ETP cap and at 47% above it.

The rest of the ETP is code O: a golden handshake, gratuity, non-genuine redundancy payment, severance pay, payment in lieu of notice, unused sick leave or unused rostered days off. Its cap is the smaller of the ETP cap left after the code R part and the whole-of-income cap, which is $180,000 less the other taxable payments you made to the employee in the year. The rate within the cap is 17% if the employee is their preservation age or older on 30 June 2027, otherwise 32%; 47% applies above the cap.

Schedule 11’s example 4 takes Alec, 30, made redundant after 5 years of service with a $48,000 genuine redundancy payment and a $17,678 gratuity, having been paid $140,000 of salary and wages in the year. It works out a $397 ETP over the $47,603 tax-free limit, withholds $127 from it and $5,657 from the gratuity within the $40,000 whole-of-income cap. This calculator opens on those figures, with a date of birth that makes Alec 30, and gives the same amounts.

## What the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a termination is a genuine redundancy, whether a payment is an ETP or which code applies. It takes the taxable component as you enter it and does not work out a tax-free component other than the redundancy limit, such as an invalidity segment. It covers one life benefit ETP for one termination: not death benefit ETPs, delayed termination payments, a later instalment for the same termination, an earlier ETP in the same year, an employee who has not given you their TFN, a foreign resident or a working holiday maker. It does not work out withholding on unused leave, which Schedule 7 covers. Confirm the figures with Schedule 11 or a registered tax agent.

## How the land tax calculator works

The calculator applies one state or territory’s general or trust scale to a total taxable land value for a full year. Below the threshold tax is nil; the Australian Capital Territory and New South Wales special trusts have no threshold. Above it, each scale charges a base amount plus a percentage over the band threshold, except Victoria’s flat $500 from $50,000 and $975 from $100,000 up to $300,000. South Australia rounds each excess up to the next $100 and makes land tax under $20 nil (Land Tax Act 1936 (SA) s 6). Tasmania rounds to 10 cents, rounding up only if over 5 cents remain (Land Tax Rating Act 2000 (Tas) s 7). The Australian Capital Territory adds its $1,778 fixed charge to the valuation charge for the full-year amount. Scales are current on 16 November 2026: the 2026 calendar year in New South Wales and Victoria, and the 2026-27 financial year in Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania and the Australian Capital Territory.

Worked figures match published revenue-office examples. Revenue NSW gives $500 on $1,100,000, $9,600 for a special trust on $600,000, and $2,100 land tax plus $60,000 surcharge on $1,200,000. Victoria’s State Revenue Office gives $5,550 on $1,100,000, and $44,650 on $1,000,000 at general absentee rates. Queensland Revenue Office gives $1,300 on $680,000 and $87,000 on $6,400,000 for an individual, $106,500 on $6,400,000 for a company, $8,420 on $760,000 for a self-managed super fund trustee, and on $400,000 for an absentee or foreign company the statutory surcharge over $349,999 is $1,500.03 ($3,800.03 total). RevenueSA gives $2,670 on $1,470,000, and Tasmania’s surcharge guideline gives $8,000 on $400,000. Hand-worked checks cover the rest: $6,250 on $1,500,000 in Western Australia with $1,680 metropolitan region improvement tax, $2,500 at South Australia’s trust rates on $500,000, $3,237.50 on $600,000 in Tasmania and $7,418 on an average unimproved value of $600,000 in the Australian Capital Territory with $4,500 foreign ownership surcharge.

## Trust rates, surcharges and the metropolitan region improvement tax

Trust rates are a special trust’s rates in New South Wales, with no threshold; the trust surcharge rates in Victoria; the rates for companies and trustees, including super funds, in Queensland; and the trust rates in South Australia. Western Australia taxes a trustee’s land as if it were the trustee’s own, at the general rates.

A foreign or absentee owner pays: in New South Wales surcharge land tax of 5% of the land value of the residential land a foreign person owns, their share only and including land exempt from land tax, entered as the surcharge value; in Victoria the absentee owner surcharge of 4% of the total taxable value from $50,000 at the general rates (a Victorian absentee trust is refused because section 46IA of the Land Tax Act 2005 (Vic) apportions the surcharge by its absentee beneficiaries’ interests); in Queensland the absentee rates for an absentee individual, and for an absentee, a foreign company or the trustee of a foreign trust a surcharge of 3% of the value over $349,999 (Schedules 2 and 3 of the Land Tax Act 2010 (Qld)); in Tasmania the foreign investor land tax surcharge of 2% of the assessed land value of the foreign-owned share of residential General Land acquired on or after 1 July 2022, entered as the surcharge value (a surcharge value above the taxable land value, or a case where rounding land tax and surcharge to 10 cents separately differs from rounding their sum, is refused); and in the Australian Capital Territory the foreign ownership surcharge of 0.75% of the parcel’s average unimproved value.

Western Australia charges its metropolitan region improvement tax of 0.14% of the value over $300,000 with land tax where the land is in the Perth metropolitan region; the calculator adds it when all the land is. A surcharge and this tax are shown apart from the land tax, with the total.

## What the land tax calculator does not compute

The land tax calculator applies the scale for the rates and owner you choose. It does not decide which land is taxable, which rates apply, whether an owner is a foreign person or absentee, or what the taxable or surcharge value is; the foreign purchaser surcharge calculator applies each jurisdiction’s test of a foreign or absentee owner. These are not computed:

- New South Wales land tax exemptions and concessions, including the principal place of residence and primary production land exemptions, exemptions from surcharge land tax, and land of a company that is not its group’s concessional company, which pays 1.6% of its whole taxable value with no threshold, or 2% of it where the group’s land exceeds the premium threshold (Land Tax Act 1956, Schedule 13, Parts 3 and 4).
- Victoria land tax exemptions and concessions, exemptions from the absentee owner surcharge and vacant residential land tax.
- Queensland: exemptions and relief, exemptions from the foreign surcharge and the aggregation of land held for trusts with the same beneficiaries.
- Western Australia land tax exemptions, and the metropolitan region improvement tax on holdings only partly in the Perth metropolitan region.
- South Australia: exemptions, waiver or relief, and which land held on trust qualifies for the general rates.
- Tasmania: zero rate classifications, exemptions and rebates, including principal residence and primary production land, and which land the foreign investor land tax surcharge applies to.
- Australian Capital Territory land tax exemptions, when the foreign ownership surcharge applies, a unit’s share by its unit entitlement and the split into quarters. Land tax there applies to residential property that is not a principal place of residence.

Joint ownership, grouping of related companies and the apportionment of the tax between properties are not computed either. Confirm the figure with the revenue office’s own calculator or assessment notice before relying on it.

## How the transfer duty calculator works

The transfer duty calculator applies one state or territory’s general rates to a dutiable value. Most scales charge a base amount plus a rate for every $100, or part of $100, over a threshold, so the excess is rounded up to the next $100. Victoria takes a percentage of the excess without rounding it, and 5.5% of the whole value from $960,000 to $2 million. The Australian Capital Territory takes $4.54 per $100 of the whole value above $1,455,000. The Northern Territory uses a formula up to $525,000 and a single percentage of the whole value above it.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the calculator gives the same amount. Revenue NSW’s worked examples give $55,537 on $1,350,000, $14,437 on $450,000 and $203,237 on $4,000,000; its Transfer of Land or Business Calculator gave $14,441.50 on $450,050 when tried on 15 November 2026. The Victorian State Revenue Office gives $28,070 at the standard rate on $550,000 and $19,070 on $400,000. The Queensland Revenue Office gives $31,275 on $850,000. RevenueSA’s foreign ownership surcharge example gives stamp duty of $26,830 on $600,000. The Tasmanian duty calculator gave $18,247.50 on $500,000 on 15 November 2026. For Western Australia, the Australian Capital Territory and the Northern Territory the tests work examples by hand from the published rates: $32,315.50 on $800,000 in Western Australia, $17,880 on $650,000 in the Australian Capital Territory and $23,928.60 on $500,000 in the Northern Territory.

## Duty for a first home buyer

For an eligible first home buyer, the calculator applies the state or territory’s first home buyer concession on a home, new or established, bought to live in. Each scheme’s conditions, such as citizenship or residency, never having owned a home, age and moving in within a set time, are in the sources listed and are not tested.

- New South Wales: no duty up to $800,000. Below $1 million, the duty on $800,000, scaled down evenly to nil at $1 million, comes off the general duty, with the $20 minimum.
- Victoria: no duty up to $600,000. Up to $750,000, the general duty multiplied by the value over $600,000 and divided by $150,000.
- Queensland: the home concession rate less the first home concession amount for the value’s $10,000 band, from $17,350 below $710,000 down to nil from $800,000, so no duty up to $700,000. No duty on a new home, whatever its value.
- Western Australia: no duty up to $600,000. Up to $800,000, $16.15 for every $100, or part of $100, over $600,000.
- South Australia: no duty on a new home, whatever its value. An established home pays the general rate.
- Australian Capital Territory: no duty under the Home Buyer Concession Scheme, whatever the value. The scheme is open to buyers who have not owned property in the past 5 years, not only first home buyers.
- Tasmania and the Northern Territory: no first home buyer duty concession on a home, so the general rate applies. Tasmania’s exemption for established homes ended with settlements on 30 June 2026.

The NSW Government’s 2026-27 table gives $9,796.75 on $850,000, $19,593.50 on $900,000, $29,390.25 on $950,000 and $37,227.65 on $990,000. Revenue NSW’s First Home Buyers Assistance calculator gave $38,991.06 on $999,000 and $20 on $800,001 when tried on 16 November 2026. The Queensland Revenue Office gives $6,555 on $730,000 and $24,100 on $850,000 for an established first home. For Victoria and Western Australia the tests work examples by hand: $24,713.33 on $700,000 in Victoria and $16,150 on $700,000 in Western Australia.

First home owner grants are payments to the buyer, not duty concessions; the first home owner grant calculator works them out beside this duty. The sources: New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania, the Northern Territory’s HomeGrown Territory Grant, and the Australian Capital Territory’s First Home Owner Grant, which stopped on 1 July 2019.

## What the transfer duty calculator does not compute

The transfer duty calculator gives general transfer duty, and duty for an eligible first home buyer of a home. It applies no other concession, exemption or surcharge, does not test whether a buyer is eligible, and does not decide whether a transaction is dutiable or what its dutiable value is; the foreign purchaser surcharge calculator works out the foreign purchaser duty surcharge. These are not computed:

- New South Wales: duty on land other than residential land over $3,870,000, which the premium rate does not reach (the calculator applies it and says so in its result), the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme on vacant land, surcharge purchaser duty for foreign persons, including foreign trusts and companies, and landholder duty.
- Victoria: the principal place of residence concession, the first home buyer concession on vacant land, the off-the-plan concession, the City of Melbourne new residential property concession and exemption, the pensioner exemption and concession, and foreign purchaser additional duty.
- Queensland: the home concession for a buyer who is not a first home buyer, the first home vacant land concession, and additional foreign acquirer duty, which also reaches companies and trusts.
- Western Australia: the concessional rate for a principal place of residence or WA business asset up to $200,000, the first home owner rate on vacant land, the off-the-plan concession or rebate, and foreign transfer duty.
- South Australia: stamp duty relief for eligible first home buyers on vacant land, seniors downsizing stamp duty relief, home buyer ex gratia relief for victim-survivors of family, domestic or sexual violence, and the foreign ownership surcharge.
- Tasmania: concessions and exemptions, and the foreign investor duty surcharge.
- Australian Capital Territory: the lower rate for an eligible owner occupier transaction (Table 1 of the determination), the Home Buyer Concession Scheme on vacant land, and the rates for commercial property.
- Northern Territory: the house and land package exemption and the home incentive schemes in Part 5 of the Act.

Duty on a trust acquisition, a landholder or corporate trustee acquisition, a lease premium or a business sale, and the land tax that follows a purchase, are not computed either. Confirm the figure with the revenue office’s own calculator or a conveyancer before relying on it.

## How the first home owner grant calculator works

The first home owner grant calculator gives the grant for a first home buyer who buys or builds a new home under a contract made from 1 July 2025 to 22 November 2026, beside the transfer duty the same buyer pays. It applies the contract-date grant and cap, paying nothing above the cap or for established homes except in the Northern Territory before 1 October 2025.

- New South Wales: $10,000; cap $600,000 for a home bought, $750,000 for land and building contract.
- Victoria: $10,000, or consideration if less, up to $750,000. For a home built the cap tests the building contract alone, so totals over $750,000 need that price entered.
- Queensland: $30,000, or the consideration if less, for a home worth under $750,000. The cap tests the greater of the price and the unencumbered value of the home and land, or for a home built the contract and the land’s value; enter that value where it differs.
- Western Australia: $10,000, or consideration if less. Cap is $800,000 south of the 26th parallel from 7 May 2026 ($750,000 before) and $1,000,000 north; enter any different cap value.
- South Australia: $15,000, or the consideration if less, with no value cap.
- Tasmania: $30,000 for contracts from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026, then $20,000 to 30 June 2027, or the consideration if less, with no value cap. The transaction must be completed within 24 months, or a period the Commissioner extends, and not replace a transaction for the same property, between or benefiting substantially the same parties, entered before 1 July 2025 for a 2025-26 contract or 1 July 2026 for a 2026-27 contract.
- Northern Territory: HomeGrown Territory Grant $50,000, or consideration if less, no cap. Established homes bought 1 July to 30 September 2025 get $10,000, or consideration if less. Must not replace a pre-1 October 2024 contract.
- Australian Capital Territory: $0 since 1 July 2019; the duty result applies the Home Buyer Concession Scheme.

## Eligibility facts and transfer duty

Each grant needs 4 facts, which you state and the calculator does not test. Every applicant is a person aged 18 or over. At least one applicant is an Australian citizen or permanent resident. No applicant or spouse is excluded by the prior ownership rules below. The applicants meet the residence requirement: 12 continuous months starting within 12 months in New South Wales; 12 months for at least one applicant in Victoria; 6 continuous months starting within 12 months in Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia and Tasmania; and 12 months after taking possession or completion in the Northern Territory. Without the building contract price entered, a home built also needs you to state that the price, excluding land, is at least the grant; with it entered, Queensland, Victoria, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania and the Northern Territory pay a lower contract price instead. A Northern Territory contract must not replace one signed before 1 October 2024. A Tasmanian transaction must be completed within 24 months, or a period the Commissioner extends, and not replace a transaction for the same property, between or benefiting substantially the same parties, entered before the 1 July starting its grant period. The calculator computes nothing until the facts a transaction needs are stated, and in Western Australia until you choose where the home is.

For a home bought, the result adds the transfer duty calculator’s duty for an eligible first home buyer and its general duty on the same value, for contracts its rates cover: from 1 July 2025 in Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and the Northern Territory, from 7 May 2026 in Western Australia, and from 1 July 2026 in New South Wales, Tasmania and the Australian Capital Territory. ## Prior ownership rules

The sources read state these rules. New South Wales: no applicant or spouse owned a home or other residential property in Australia before 1 July 2000. Victoria: none owned one before 1 July 2000, or lived for a continuous 6 months or more in a home they owned on or after 1 July 2000. Western Australia: none owned residential property on or after 1 July 2000 and lived in it for a continuous 6 months or more beginning on or after 1 July 2004. Tasmania: none owned a residential property before 1 July 2000, or owned and lived in one for more than 6 months after 1 July 2000. For Queensland, South Australia and the Northern Territory the sources read do not set the rule out, so state the fact against the revenue office’s own eligibility rules.

## How the payroll tax calculator works

The payroll tax calculator estimates one employer’s payroll tax for the whole of 2026-27 in one state or territory. Each jurisdiction allows a deduction or tax-free threshold, which an employer that also pays wages elsewhere in Australia shares in proportion to its wages there. Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia and the Northern Territory reduce the deduction as Australian wages grow, until it reaches nil. The result shows the deduction, the amount taxed, the tax at each rate and, in Victoria and Queensland, each surcharge or levy on its own line, with the provision applied.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the tests hold them. Revenue NSW: $1,500,000 of NSW wages are taxed on $300,000, and $900,000 of $3,000,000 has a threshold of $360,000. The Victorian State Revenue Office: $80 million in Victoria and $70 million in New South Wales give surcharge thresholds of $5,333,333.33 and $53,333,333.33. The Queensland Revenue Office: $1,356,789 of $1,789,776 has a deduction of $932,460 to the dollar. RevenueWA: annual wages of $1,104,000 give $6,600; its other 2 examples round the deduction to the dollar and come out 1 and 3 cents lower than this calculator. RevenueSA: $800,000 of $1,600,000 has a deduction of $300,000 and $500,000 of $2,000,000 one of $150,000. The Tasmanian State Revenue Office: $2,400,000 gives $54,400, and $2,400,000 of $3,000,000 gives $72,800. The ACT Revenue Office and the Territory Revenue Office publish no worked figures that could be read, so the tests work examples from the Acts by hand: $84,375 on $3,000,000 in the Australian Capital Territory and $41,250 on $3,000,000 in the Northern Territory. This calculator opens on Revenue NSW’s first example.

## What the payroll tax calculator does not compute

The payroll tax calculator gives one annual estimate and no advice. It does not decide which payments are taxable wages or where they are taxable, and does not cover:

- an employer that is a member of a group, whose threshold and rate depend on the group’s wages and which members share the deduction;
- an employer that paid wages for only part of 2026-27, whose threshold every jurisdiction reduces by the days it employed;
- monthly or other periodic returns, and the annual reconciliation of amounts already paid;
- exemptions, rebates and discounts, such as Queensland’s apprentice and trainee rebate and its 1% discount for regional employers, claimed in the return, and the ACT’s 6.85% cap for an eligible university;
- any year other than 2026-27.

Confirm the figure with the revenue office or a registered tax agent before relying on it.
