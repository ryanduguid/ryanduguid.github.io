Enter the value the revenue office taxes, after exempt land is left out: in New South Wales the 3-year average of the land values, in Victoria and South Australia the total site value, in the Australian Capital Territory one parcel’s average unimproved value, and elsewhere the total land value of all the owner’s taxable land. The Northern Territory has no land tax.

Trust rates are a special trust’s in New South Wales, the trust surcharge rates in Victoria, those for companies and trustees in Queensland and those for land held on trust in South Australia. The calculator has none for Western Australia, Tasmania or the Australian Capital Territory.

The surcharge value is the land value the New South Wales or Tasmanian surcharge applies to, as set out below the calculator; elsewhere a surcharge applies to the taxable land value. The calculator has no foreign or absentee rates for Western Australia or South Australia.

## How the land tax calculator works

The calculator applies one state or territory’s general or trust scale to a total taxable land value and gives the tax for a full year. Below a jurisdiction’s threshold the tax is nil; the Australian Capital Territory has no threshold, and nor does a special trust in New South Wales. Above it, each scale charges a base amount plus a percentage of the value over a band’s threshold, except that Victoria charges a flat $500 from $50,000 and $975 from $100,000 up to $300,000. South Australia charges for every $100, or part of $100, so the excess is rounded up to the next $100, and RevenueSA issues no assessment where the calculated land tax is under $20; the calculator shows the amount and says so. The Australian Capital Territory adds its fixed charge of $1,778 to the valuation charge and assesses each quarter’s share by its days; the calculator gives the full-year amount. Each scale is the one in force for the land tax year current on 16 November 2026: the 2026 calendar year in New South Wales and Victoria, and the 2026-27 financial year in Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania and the Australian Capital Territory.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the calculator gives the same amount. Revenue NSW gives $500 on an average land value of $1,100,000, $9,600 for a special trust on $600,000, and $2,100 of land tax and $60,000 of surcharge land tax for a foreign owner of $1,200,000 of residential land. Victoria’s State Revenue Office gives $5,550 on $1,100,000, and its table with the absentee owner surcharge gives $44,650 on $1,000,000 at the general rates. The Queensland Revenue Office gives $1,300 on $680,000 and $87,000 on $6,400,000 for an individual, $106,500 on $6,400,000 for a company, $8,420 on $760,000 for the trustee of a self-managed super fund, and $3,800 on $400,000 for an absentee and for a foreign company, each with $1,500 of surcharge. RevenueSA’s 2026-27 guide gives $2,670 on $1,470,000. Tasmania’s foreign investor land tax surcharge guideline gives $8,000 of surcharge on $400,000. The tests work the rest by hand from the published rates: $6,250 on $1,500,000 in Western Australia with $1,680 of metropolitan region improvement tax, $2,500 at South Australia’s trust rates on $500,000, $3,237.50 on $600,000 in Tasmania and $7,418 on an average unimproved value of $600,000 in the Australian Capital Territory, with $4,500 of foreign ownership surcharge.

## Trust rates, surcharges and the metropolitan region improvement tax

Trust rates are a special trust’s rates in New South Wales, with no threshold; the trust surcharge rates in Victoria; the rates for companies and trustees, including super funds, in Queensland; and the trust rates in South Australia. Western Australia taxes a trustee’s land as if it were the trustee’s own, at the general rates.

A foreign or absentee owner pays: in New South Wales surcharge land tax of 5% of the land value of the residential land a foreign person owns, their share only and including land exempt from land tax, entered as the surcharge value; in Victoria the absentee owner surcharge of 4% of the total taxable value, from $50,000 at the general rates and from $25,000 at the trust rates; in Queensland the absentee rates for an absentee individual, and for an absentee, a foreign company or the trustee of a foreign trust a surcharge of 3% of the value over $350,000; in Tasmania the foreign investor land tax surcharge of 2% of the assessed land value of the foreign-owned share of residential General Land acquired on or after 1 July 2022, entered as the surcharge value; and in the Australian Capital Territory the foreign ownership surcharge of 0.75% of the parcel’s average unimproved value.

Western Australia charges its metropolitan region improvement tax of 0.14% of the value over $300,000 with land tax where the land is in the Perth metropolitan region; the calculator adds it when all the land is. A surcharge and this tax are shown apart from the land tax, with the total.

## What the land tax calculator does not compute

The land tax calculator applies the scale for the rates and owner you choose. It does not decide which land is taxable, which rates apply, whether an owner is a foreign person or absentee, or what the taxable or surcharge value is; the foreign purchaser surcharge calculator applies each jurisdiction’s test of a foreign or absentee owner. These are not computed:

- New South Wales: exemptions and concessions, including the principal place of residence and primary production land exemptions, exemptions from surcharge land tax, and land of a company that is not its group’s concessional company, which pays 1.6% of its whole taxable value with no threshold, or 2% of it where the group’s land exceeds the premium threshold (Land Tax Act 1956, Schedule 13, Parts 3 and 4).
- Victoria: exemptions and concessions, exemptions from the absentee owner surcharge and vacant residential land tax.
- Queensland: exemptions and relief, exemptions from the foreign surcharge and the aggregation of land held for trusts with the same beneficiaries.
- Western Australia: land tax exemptions, and the metropolitan region improvement tax on holdings only partly in the Perth metropolitan region.
- South Australia: exemptions, waiver or relief, and which land held on trust qualifies for the general rates.
- Tasmania: zero rate classifications, exemptions and rebates, including principal residence and primary production land, and which land the foreign investor land tax surcharge applies to.
- Australian Capital Territory: land tax exemptions, when the foreign ownership surcharge applies, a unit’s share by its unit entitlement and the split into quarters. Land tax there applies to residential property that is not a principal place of residence.

Joint ownership, grouping of related companies and the apportionment of the tax between properties are not computed either. Confirm the figure with the revenue office’s own calculator or assessment notice before relying on it.

## How the transfer duty calculator works

The transfer duty calculator applies one state or territory’s general rates to a dutiable value. Most scales charge a base amount plus a rate for every $100, or part of $100, over a threshold, so the excess is rounded up to the next $100. Victoria takes a percentage of the excess without rounding it, and 5.5% of the whole value from $960,000 to $2 million. The Australian Capital Territory takes $4.54 per $100 of the whole value above $1,455,000. The Northern Territory uses a formula up to $525,000 and a single percentage of the whole value above it.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the calculator gives the same amount. Revenue NSW’s worked examples give $55,537 on $1,350,000, $14,437 on $450,000 and $203,237 on $4,000,000; its Transfer of Land or Business Calculator gave $14,441.50 on $450,050 when tried on 15 November 2026. The Victorian State Revenue Office gives $28,070 at the standard rate on $550,000 and $19,070 on $400,000. The Queensland Revenue Office gives $31,275 on $850,000. RevenueSA’s foreign ownership surcharge example gives stamp duty of $26,830 on $600,000. The Tasmanian duty calculator gave $18,247.50 on $500,000 on 15 November 2026. For Western Australia, the Australian Capital Territory and the Northern Territory the tests work examples by hand from the published rates: $32,315.50 on $800,000 in Western Australia, $17,880 on $650,000 in the Australian Capital Territory and $23,928.60 on $500,000 in the Northern Territory.

## Duty for a first home buyer

For an eligible first home buyer, the calculator applies the state or territory’s first home buyer concession on a home, new or established, bought to live in. Each scheme’s conditions, such as citizenship or residency, never having owned a home, age and moving in within a set time, are in the sources listed and are not tested.

- New South Wales: no duty up to $800,000. Below $1 million, the duty on $800,000, scaled down evenly to nil at $1 million, comes off the general duty, with the $20 minimum.
- Victoria: no duty up to $600,000. Up to $750,000, the general duty multiplied by the value over $600,000 and divided by $150,000.
- Queensland: the home concession rate less the first home concession amount for the value’s $10,000 band, from $17,350 below $710,000 down to nil from $800,000, so no duty up to $700,000. No duty on a new home, whatever its value.
- Western Australia: no duty up to $600,000. Up to $800,000, $16.15 for every $100, or part of $100, over $600,000.
- South Australia: no duty on a new home, whatever its value. An established home pays the general rate.
- Australian Capital Territory: no duty under the Home Buyer Concession Scheme, whatever the value. The scheme is open to buyers who have not owned property in the past 5 years, not only first home buyers.
- Tasmania and the Northern Territory: no first home buyer duty concession on a home, so the general rate applies. Tasmania’s exemption for established homes ended with settlements on 30 June 2026.

The NSW Government’s 2026-27 table gives $9,796.75 on $850,000, $19,593.50 on $900,000, $29,390.25 on $950,000 and $37,227.65 on $990,000. Revenue NSW’s First Home Buyers Assistance calculator gave $38,991.06 on $999,000 and $20 on $800,001 when tried on 16 November 2026. The Queensland Revenue Office gives $6,555 on $730,000 and $24,100 on $850,000 for an established first home. For Victoria and Western Australia the tests work examples by hand: $24,713.33 on $700,000 in Victoria and $16,150 on $700,000 in Western Australia.

First home owner grants are payments to the buyer, not duty concessions, and are not computed: New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania, the Northern Territory’s HomeGrown Territory Grant, and the Australian Capital Territory’s First Home Owner Grant, which stopped on 1 July 2019.

## What the transfer duty calculator does not compute

The transfer duty calculator gives general transfer duty, and duty for an eligible first home buyer of a home. It applies no other concession, exemption or surcharge, does not test whether a buyer is eligible, and does not decide whether a transaction is dutiable or what its dutiable value is; the foreign purchaser surcharge calculator works out the foreign purchaser duty surcharge. These are not computed:

- New South Wales: duty on land other than residential land over $3,870,000, which the premium rate does not reach (the calculator applies it and says so in its result), the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme on vacant land, surcharge purchaser duty for foreign persons, including foreign trusts and companies, and landholder duty.
- Victoria: the principal place of residence concession, the first home buyer concession on vacant land, the off-the-plan concession, the City of Melbourne new residential property concession and exemption, the pensioner exemption and concession, and foreign purchaser additional duty.
- Queensland: the home concession for a buyer who is not a first home buyer, the first home vacant land concession, and additional foreign acquirer duty, which also reaches companies and trusts.
- Western Australia: the concessional rate for a principal place of residence or WA business asset up to $200,000, the first home owner rate on vacant land, the off-the-plan concession or rebate, and foreign transfer duty.
- South Australia: stamp duty relief for eligible first home buyers on vacant land, seniors downsizing stamp duty relief, home buyer ex gratia relief for victim-survivors of family, domestic or sexual violence, and the foreign ownership surcharge.
- Tasmania: concessions and exemptions, and the foreign investor duty surcharge.
- Australian Capital Territory: the lower rate for an eligible owner occupier transaction (Table 1 of the determination), the Home Buyer Concession Scheme on vacant land, and the rates for commercial property.
- Northern Territory: the house and land package exemption and the home incentive schemes in Part 5 of the Act.

Duty on a trust acquisition, a landholder or corporate trustee acquisition, a lease premium or a business sale, and the land tax that follows a purchase, are not computed either. Confirm the figure with the revenue office’s own calculator or a conveyancer before relying on it.

## How the concessional catch-up calculator works

The concessional catch-up calculator works out each earlier year’s unused concessional contributions cap, the shortfall of that year’s contributions below its general cap, from 2018-19. Where an earlier year’s contributions went over its cap and the balance just before it was under $500,000, it applies the unused cap of the 5 years before that one, earliest first, as subsections 291-20(4) and (5) required for that year. What is left of the previous 5 years’ unused cap is carried into the year you choose.

With a total super balance under $500,000 on 30 June before the year, the cap available is the general cap plus that carried amount. The calculator shows how much of it this year’s contributions use, any excess, and what is left of each year’s amount with the year after which it lapses. A proposed contribution is added to the contributions so far, so you can see how far it goes before it becomes excess.

The ATO’s concessional contributions cap page works Sam’s example: unused cap of $14,000, $13,000, $14,500, $13,500 and $12,500 from 2019-20 to 2023-24 gives $67,500, so his 2024-25 cap is $97,500, and contributions of $97,500 use it all with no excess. The calculator opens on his figures; the ATO says only that his balance was below $500,000, so the $450,000 is fictional.

## What the concessional catch-up calculator does not cover

The calculator works the cap only. It does not decide what counts as a concessional contribution, in which year a contribution counts, or whether a deduction notice is valid, and it does not work out the tax on excess contributions or Division 293 tax. It does not cover a defined benefit interest, or non-concessional contributions and their bring-forward. It covers 2024-25, 2025-26 and 2026-27.

Your carry-forward amounts are on ATO online services (Super, then Information, then Carry forward concessional contributions), which use the contributions your funds have reported. Confirm the figures there or with a registered tax agent before you contribute.

## How the income tax calculator works

The income tax calculator works out one individual’s tax for a full income year from 2020-21 to 2026-27: the tax on taxable income at the year’s rates, the offsets, the Medicare levy and the Medicare levy surcharge. The offsets are the low income tax offset and, for 2020-21 and 2021-22, the low and middle income tax offset. It then takes that total, and any study or training loan repayment, from the tax withheld you enter, to estimate a refund or an amount owing.

An Australian resident pays nothing on the first $18,200 and then each part of taxable income at its rate. The low income tax offset of up to $700 comes off a resident’s tax, but not below nil. The Medicare levy is 2% of a resident’s taxable income; up to the low-income threshold there is none, and from there to the phase-in limit it is 10% of the income over the threshold, if that is less. The ATO’s example on Medicare levy reduction for low-income earners, read 17 November 2026, gives Angie, single with $29,000 of taxable income in 2025-26, a levy of $98.90, as this calculator does.

The Medicare levy surcharge applies to a resident without private patient hospital cover whose income for surcharge purposes is above the year’s threshold. For each day without cover it charges 1%, 1.25% or 1.5% of taxable income and reportable fringe benefits, by the tier the income falls in.

For 2026-27 the Medicare levy thresholds are those the Act held on 17 November 2026, the same as 2025-26. If Parliament raises them for 2026-27, a person just above $28,011 will pay less than shown here.

## What the income tax calculator does not compute

The calculator gives the tax of one person who was a resident, a foreign resident or a working holiday maker for the whole year. It does not compute:

- the Medicare levy reduction for families, which depends on family taxable income and dependants, or a Medicare levy exemption;
- the seniors and pensioners tax offset and its higher Medicare levy thresholds, or any other offset, such as for private health insurance, dependants or super contributions;
- a change of family or cover status during the year beyond the days without cover, the private health insurance rebate, a spouse’s trust income that the surcharge test adds, the net amount on which family trust distribution tax has been paid, which counts in income for surcharge purposes and in the amount the surcharge is levied on, or exempt foreign employment income, which the ATO’s surcharge page adds to income for surcharge purposes when taxable income is $1 or more;
- a first home super saver released amount, which is taxed with a 30% offset but left out of repayment income and income for surcharge purposes, or, for 2023-24 and earlier, the taxed element of a super lump sum up to the low rate cap, which a person from preservation age to under 60 could leave out of income for surcharge purposes;
- a study loan debt smaller than the repayment: the repayment cannot exceed what is still owed (section 154-20(1)(c) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003), which the calculator does not ask for;
- a part-year resident’s reduced tax-free threshold, a working holiday maker who is an Australian resident, or one with other income besides working holiday taxable income;
- tax on superannuation or employment termination remainders, income averaging, or a minor’s unearned income;
- Division 293 tax, excess contributions, PAYG instalments or other credits besides the tax withheld you enter.

Confirm the figures with the ATO’s Simple tax calculator or a registered tax agent.

## How the take-home pay calculator works

The take-home pay calculator takes one salary for a year, month, fortnight or week and works out the year’s income tax, offsets, Medicare levy, any Medicare levy surcharge and any study or training loan repayment on it. Salary sacrificed to super comes off first. From 2026-27 it also takes off the standard deduction of up to $1,000 for work-related expenses. It then gives what is left for the year, a month, a fortnight and a week, and the super guarantee an employer must pay, which goes to a super fund and is not part of take-home pay.

Those take-home amounts are the year’s tax spread evenly: the year’s net amount divided by 12, 26 and 52. They are not the amounts an employer withholds each pay. For a weekly, fortnightly or monthly pay, the calculator also works the PAYG amount withheld from one pay under the year’s Schedule 1, and Schedule 8 with a study loan, and the year’s withholding less the year’s tax, which is the refund or amount owing when the tax return is assessed. Withholding does not allow for the low and middle income tax offset, so in 2021-22 the offset comes back as part of the refund: $3,000 a fortnight has $670 withheld each pay and $1,543 back. For working holiday makers, who are withheld from under another schedule, use the ATO’s tax withheld calculator.

The example on arrival is 2025-26, before the standard deduction: on $75,000 the Medicare levy is $1,500, as the ATO’s example of Matthew on What is the Medicare levy gives. In 2026-27 the same salary is $74,000 of taxable income.

When the salary includes super, the calculator splits it: at 12%, a package of $112,000 is $100,000 of salary and $12,000 of super. Super guarantee stops at the year’s maximum contribution base, so above it the super stays at the most the base allows.

## What the take-home pay calculator does not compute

The calculator assumes one employer, a salary paid evenly over the full year, and no other income, deductions or offsets. It does not compute:

- overtime, bonuses, allowances or leave loading, a 53rd weekly or 27th fortnightly pay, or withholding on scale 1, scale 5 or 6 or for a working holiday maker, a withholding declaration’s offsets or a Medicare levy variation;
- 2025-26 withholding with a study loan that mixes the 2 Schedule 8s: the year’s withholding and refund take the one from 24 September 2025 for every pay, and the component before that date is shown beside it;
- work-related deductions you claim, which reduce the standard deduction dollar for dollar except those section 25-130(3) disregards, such as professional association fees;
- salary sacrifice of anything but super, reportable fringe benefits, excess concessional contributions or Division 293 tax;
- everything the income tax calculator leaves out, such as the Medicare levy reduction for families and the seniors and pensioners tax offset;
- a family’s surcharge: this calculator works out the surcharge for a single person with no dependants, and the income tax calculator works out a family’s;

## How the lump sum in arrears calculator works

Salary, wages or a Centrelink or compensation payment that you receive as a lump sum, for earlier years, is taxed in the year you receive it, often at a higher rate than it would have been in the years it was for. The tax offset gives back the difference. The calculator first tests whether the arrears are at least 10% of the rest of your normal taxable income for the year. If they are, it works out the tax the arrears add this year and the tax they would have added in the years they accrued in, and the offset is the amount by which the first exceeds the second.

For the 2 latest accrual years it adds the arrears to that year’s taxable income at that year’s resident rates. Arrears from any earlier years are taxed at the average of those 2 years’ rates, so the oldest years’ arrears can share one row. Where a recent year was itself a year you got this offset for, or arrears of such a year accrued in it, its income is adjusted as the Act directs.

The ATO’s page on lump sum payments in arrears works Yan’s breakdown of arrears by year, received in 2025-26. The calculator opens on it; the ATO does not give Yan’s taxable incomes, so the $100,000, $30,000 and $17,000 are fictional. Anthony’s, Sudeep’s and Heather’s examples are among the boundary cases.

## What the lump sum in arrears calculator does not cover

The calculator works the offset for a person resident in Australia for the whole of the year of receipt and of each recent accrual year, entitled to the full tax-free threshold, for a year of receipt of 2024-25, 2025-26 or 2026-27. It does not decide what is a lump sum in arrears: it takes the arrears you enter as eligible income that accrued more than 12 months before you received it. It refuses an employment termination payment, unused leave paid on termination, a super lump sum or income stream, above-average special professional income or primary producer averaging in the year of receipt or a recent accrual year, and does not cover a minor’s income or a trustee.

It does not work out the Medicare levy or surcharge on the arrears, which have their own rules, or apply the offset against your tax. The ATO works the offset when you lodge with the amounts for each year; confirm the figures with the ATO or a registered tax agent.

## How the sole trader tax set-aside calculator works

The calculator takes a year’s business income and expenses, any wages from a job and any tax agent fees, gifts or personal super contributions you claim. It works out the year’s income tax, the offsets, the Medicare levy, any Medicare levy surcharge and any study or training loan repayment. It then works the same on the wages alone. The difference is the tax the business adds, which nobody withholds, so it is the amount to set aside from business income.

The small business income tax offset is worked as section 328-360 sets it: 16% of the share of the tax on taxable income that the business profit makes up. On arrival the example shows it below its $1,000 cap: $20,000 of profit is 20/79 of $79,000 of taxable income, so the offset is 16% of 20/79 of $14,220, which is $576.00. The business also costs the low income tax offset the wages alone would have had, $115.00, which the difference picks up.

A business registered for GST also sets aside the GST on its income less its GST credits, paid with each activity statement. The calculator gives one share to keep from every invoice, covering both, and the year’s amount split by quarter, month and week. It also gives the tax as a share of the business income excluding GST, which as a PAYG instalment rate would cover this year’s tax on it.

## What the sole trader tax set-aside calculator does not compute

- personal services income rules, which can deny deductions: see the PSI rules checker;
- GST-free or input-taxed sales, GST on a cash or accruals basis by period, or a GST refund when credits exceed the GST on income;
- other income such as interest, dividends, rent or capital gains, or partnership and trust distributions, which change both the tax and the offset’s share, or reportable fringe benefits and salary sacrificed to super from the job, which count in repayment income and income for surcharge purposes;
- work-related deductions against the wages, which reduce the standard deduction from 2026-27, or a job withheld from at the wrong rate;
- super guarantee, which a sole trader does not pay for themselves, or personal super contributions beyond entering them as a deduction;
