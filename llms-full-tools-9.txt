## How the PSI rules checker works

The PSI rules checker takes one individual’s personal services income for one income year and works through Part 2-42 in the order the Act sets. Each step on the result names the section it applies and what it found.

Income that is not mainly a reward for the individual’s personal efforts or skills is not PSI (subsection 84-5(1)), and income received as an employee or office holder falls outside Division 85 (subsection 85-35(1)). So does a payment to a religious practitioner by an entity carrying on an enterprise, for work in their religious vocation done as a member of a religious institution (subsection 85-35(2) and section 12-47 of Schedule 1 to the Taxation Administration Act 1953); check any other PSI they earn as a contractor. A personal services business determination in force settles the question (subsection 87-15(1)). Otherwise the results test comes first: if at least 75% of the PSI is paid for a result, with the plant and tools supplied and liability for defects, the PSI rules do not apply, however much comes from one payer (section 87-18).

If 80% or more of the PSI comes from one payer and its associates, the other 3 tests cannot be self-assessed and only a determination keeps the rules away (subsection 87-15(3)). The checker still works them out, because the Commissioner weighs them for a determination: 2 or more unrelated payers that engaged the individual or entity through offers to the public (section 87-20), principal work by others of at least 20% by market value or an apprentice for half the year (section 87-25), and qualifying business premises all year (section 87-30).

The checker reproduces the conclusion of each worked example in TR 2022/3 that reaches one on a test it applies, and of the matching examples on the ATO’s PSI pages.

## How to enter payers in the PSI rules checker

Enter each payer once, with its associates’ payments added in: the payer is whoever must pay under the contract, so a labour hire firm or agency is the payer, not its client (TR 2022/3 paragraphs 69 to 72). Payers that are associates of each other count as one payer too, as section 87-20 counts only payers that are not associates of each other: put them in one row, or choose the first of their rows under ‘Same payer as’ in each later one. Linked rows count as one payer in the unrelated clients test too, so 2 payers that are each an associate of a third, but not of each other, count once there; that can only make the test harder to pass. Count each Australian government agency as its own payer (section 87-35) and leave out income received as an employee. If the individual works through their own company, partnership or trust, choose that entity as who receives the income, even if it pays them a salary. Tick ‘Paid for a result’, ‘Supplies plant and tools’ and ‘Liable for defects’ only where the contract or industry custom makes it so (subsection 87-18(4)). The results test counts income, not payers, and a tick covers all of that row’s PSI: where only part of a payer’s PSI meets all 3, enter that part in a row of its own and choose the payer’s first row under ‘Same payer as’, so the 80% rule and section 87-20 still count one payer. For the market value of principal work by others, count work by any individual or entity engaged, an individual associate included, but not by an associate that is a company, partnership or trust, nor, for an entity, by an individual whose PSI it receives; a partner counts only if they earn no PSI through the partnership (subsections 87-25(1), (2) and (2A); TR 2022/3 paragraphs 110 to 116). With more than 5 payers, put the smallest together in one row only where every answer is the same; that can only make a test harder to pass.

## What the PSI rules checker leaves to you

The checker does not decide whether income is mainly a reward for personal efforts or skills, whether a contract pays for a result, whether an offer to the public won the work, who is an associate or what principal work is worth. These turn on facts and judgement, so it takes your answers and says so on each step; TR 2022/3 sets out the factors at paragraphs 34 to 49 and 77 to 148.

The checker does not allocate expenses across PSI, business income and other income. Division 85 limits deductions to the extent they relate to PSI (subsection 85-10(1)) but sets no method for the split, and TR 2022/3 leaves deductions to other rulings (paragraphs 6 and 31), so any split is practice rather than law. It does not work out the amount attributed under Division 86, PAYG withholding or a deduction for a net PSI loss.

The checker does not apply the agent rules when they could apply (section 87-40), decide a determination, which is the Commissioner’s (sections 87-60 and 87-65), or test Part IVA, which can still apply to a personal services business (TR 2022/3 paragraph 160). Several individuals working through one entity are tested one at a time (paragraph 76). Confirm the result with the ruling or a registered tax agent.

## How the 13-week cash forecast works

The 13-week cash forecast works out closing cash for each week and the funding gap against a buffer you set. Weekly closing cash = opening cash + receipts − payments. Each closing balance opens the next week. Funding gap = minimum buffer − lowest opening or weekly closing cash, with a floor of zero.

No statutory rate or threshold is applied. The layout follows business.gov.au: cash flow.

## What the 13-week cash forecast does not decide

The funding gap is a planning gap, not a lending decision. The 13-week cash forecast does not predict receipts or payments, and weekly totals can hide a shortage within the week. A receipt delayed beyond week 13 is reported separately and does not enter the closing balance.

The Lumbridge cash flow case works the same question with a 45-day receipt delay.

## How the objection deadline checker works

The objection deadline checker gives the last day to lodge a taxation objection under section 14ZW of the Taxation Administration Act 1953. An income tax assessment allows 2 years after notice of the assessment is given when item 1, 2, 3 or 3A of the table in subsection 170(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 applies, and 4 years otherwise. An FBT assessment allows 4 years. Any other decision for which the section sets no other period allows 60 days after notice is served.

An amended assessment allows whichever ends later: 60 days after notice of the amended assessment, or the 2 or 4 years after notice of the first assessment for that year. The checker shows both dates and the later one.

Each period starts the day after notice, because subsection 36(1) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 leaves out the day a period begins after. A 2-year period from notice given on 28 February 2026 therefore starts on 1 March 2026 and ends on 29 February 2028. When the last day is a Saturday or Sunday, subsection 36(2) lets the objection be lodged on the next day that is not a weekend or holiday. The Act’s own example: a person who has until Saturday 31 March may apply on Monday 2 April.

## What the objection deadline checker does not cover

The checker does not decide whether a decision can be objected to, which item of subsection 170(1) applies, or when notice was given, so keep the notice’s issue and receipt dates. It does not know public holidays: they differ by state and town, so check the last day and the day after it where you lodge. It does not cover private rulings, administrative penalties, retained refunds, deceased estates, specialised taxes or the other decisions for which section 14ZW sets its own period, or the time to seek review of an objection decision. Confirm the date with the notice of decision or a registered tax agent.

## How the PAYG instalments calculator works

The PAYG instalments calculator works out one quarterly pay as you go instalment by the method on the activity statement. Under option 1 the instalment is the amount the ATO notifies at T7. Varying it, the first instalment of the income year is 25% of the estimated tax for the year, the second 50%, the third 75% and the fourth 100%, each less the earlier instalments. The third and fourth add back any credits you were entitled to claim for earlier instalments, claimed or not; no credit can arise before the second, so the calculator refuses one entered for the first or second. A negative result is a credit to claim at 5B.

Under option 2 the instalment is the quarter’s instalment income at T1 times the instalment rate at T2. A varied rate is the estimated tax for the year divided by the estimated instalment income, times 100. If the varied rate is lower than the earlier rate, the earlier instalments less any credits claimed, less the earlier instalment income times the varied rate, is a credit for 5B.

The ATO’s own example: instalment income of $20,100 at a rate of 1.7% gives $341.70, entered as 341 at T11 and 5A.

## How the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator works

The redundancy and ETP withholding calculator works out how much a payer withholds from one life benefit employment termination payment (ETP) made in 2026-27. A genuine redundancy or early retirement scheme payment is tax-free up to $13,598 plus $6,801 for each completed year of service. The part above that limit is a code R ETP, as are payments for invalidity and compensation for personal injury, unfair dismissal, harassment or discrimination. The code R part is withheld at the rate within the cap up to the $270,000 ETP cap and at 47% above it.

The rest of the ETP is code O: a golden handshake, gratuity, non-genuine redundancy payment, severance pay, payment in lieu of notice, unused sick leave or unused rostered days off. Its cap is the smaller of the ETP cap left after the code R part and the whole-of-income cap, which is $180,000 less the other taxable payments you made to the employee in the year. The rate within the cap is 17% if the employee is their preservation age or older on 30 June 2027, otherwise 32%; 47% applies above the cap.

## How the land tax calculator works

The calculator applies one state or territory’s general or trust scale to a total taxable land value and gives the tax for a full year. Below a jurisdiction’s threshold the tax is nil; the Australian Capital Territory has no threshold, and nor does a special trust in New South Wales. Above it, each scale charges a base amount plus a percentage of the value over a band’s threshold, except that Victoria charges a flat $500 from $50,000 and $975 from $100,000 up to $300,000. South Australia charges for every $100, or part of $100, so the excess is rounded up to the next $100, and RevenueSA issues no assessment where the calculated land tax is under $20; the calculator shows the amount and says so. The Australian Capital Territory adds its fixed charge of $1,778 to the valuation charge and assesses each quarter’s share by its days; the calculator gives the full-year amount. Each scale is the one in force for the land tax year current on 16 November 2026: the 2026 calendar year in New South Wales and Victoria, and the 2026-27 financial year in Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania and the Australian Capital Territory.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the calculator gives the same amount. ## How the transfer duty calculator works

The transfer duty calculator applies one state or territory’s general rates to a dutiable value. Most scales charge a base amount plus a rate for every $100, or part of $100, over a threshold, so the excess is rounded up to the next $100. Victoria takes a percentage of the excess without rounding it, and 5.5% of the whole value from $960,000 to $2 million. The Australian Capital Territory takes $4.54 per $100 of the whole value above $1,455,000. The Northern Territory uses a formula up to $525,000 and a single percentage of the whole value above it.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the calculator gives the same amount. Revenue NSW’s worked examples give $55,537 on $1,350,000, $14,437 on $450,000 and $203,237 on $4,000,000; its Transfer of Land or Business Calculator gave $14,441.50 on $450,050 when tried on 15 November 2026. The Victorian State Revenue Office gives $28,070 at the standard rate on $550,000 and $19,070 on $400,000. The Queensland Revenue Office gives $31,275 on $850,000. RevenueSA’s foreign ownership surcharge example gives stamp duty of $26,830 on $600,000. The Tasmanian duty calculator gave $18,247.50 on $500,000 on 15 November 2026. For Western Australia, the Australian Capital Territory and the Northern Territory the tests work examples by hand from the published rates: $32,315.50 on $800,000 in Western Australia, $17,880 on $650,000 in the Australian Capital Territory and $23,928.60 on $500,000 in the Northern Territory.

## Duty for a first home buyer

For an eligible first home buyer, the calculator applies the state or territory’s first home buyer concession on a home, new or established, bought to live in. Each scheme’s conditions, such as citizenship or residency, never having owned a home, age and moving in within a set time, are in the sources listed and are not tested.

- New South Wales: no duty up to $800,000. Below $1 million, the duty on $800,000, scaled down evenly to nil at $1 million, comes off the general duty, with the $20 minimum.
- Victoria: no duty up to $600,000. Up to $750,000, the general duty multiplied by the value over $600,000 and divided by $150,000.
- Queensland: the home concession rate less the first home concession amount for the value’s $10,000 band, from $17,350 below $710,000 down to nil from $800,000, so no duty up to $700,000. No duty on a new home, whatever its value.
- Western Australia: no duty up to $600,000. Up to $800,000, $16.15 for every $100, or part of $100, over $600,000.
- South Australia: no duty on a new home, whatever its value. An established home pays the general rate.
- Australian Capital Territory: no duty under the Home Buyer Concession Scheme, whatever the value. The scheme is open to buyers who have not owned property in the past 5 years, not only first home buyers.
- Tasmania and the Northern Territory: no first home buyer duty concession on a home, so the general rate applies. Tasmania’s exemption for established homes ended with settlements on 30 June 2026.

## How the concessional catch-up calculator works

The concessional catch-up calculator works out each earlier year’s unused concessional contributions cap, the shortfall of that year’s contributions below its general cap, from 2018-19. Where an earlier year’s contributions went over its cap and the balance just before it was under $500,000, it applies the unused cap of the 5 years before that one, earliest first, as subsections 291-20(4) and (5) required for that year. What is left of the previous 5 years’ unused cap is carried into the year you choose.

With a total super balance under $500,000 on 30 June before the year, the cap available is the general cap plus that carried amount. The calculator shows how much of it this year’s contributions use, any excess, and what is left of each year’s amount with the year after which it lapses. A proposed contribution is added to the contributions so far, so you can see how far it goes before it becomes excess.

The ATO’s concessional contributions cap page works Sam’s example: unused cap of $14,000, $13,000, $14,500, $13,500 and $12,500 from 2019-20 to 2023-24 gives $67,500, so his 2024-25 cap is $97,500, and contributions of $97,500 use it all with no excess. The calculator opens on his figures; the ATO says only that his balance was below $500,000, so the $450,000 is fictional.

## What the concessional catch-up calculator does not cover

The calculator works the cap only. It does not decide what counts as a concessional contribution, in which year a contribution counts, or whether a deduction notice is valid, and it does not work out the tax on excess contributions or Division 293 tax. It does not cover a defined benefit interest, or non-concessional contributions and their bring-forward. It covers 2024-25, 2025-26 and 2026-27.

Your carry-forward amounts are on ATO online services (Super, then Information, then Carry forward concessional contributions), which use the contributions your funds have reported. Confirm the figures there or with a registered tax agent before you contribute.
