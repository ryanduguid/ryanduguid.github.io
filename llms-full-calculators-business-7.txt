## How the company tax and franking calculator works

The calculator applies the base rate entity test of section 23AA of the Income Tax Rates Act 1986 to this year’s figures: aggregated turnover under $50 million, and base rate entity passive income of no more than 80% of assessable income. A base rate entity pays 25% of its taxable income, and any other company 30% (subsection 23(2)).

The rate for franking a dividend comes from a second test. Section 995-1 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 runs the base rate entity test on the previous year’s turnover, passive income and assessable income, so a company can pay tax at 30% and frank at 25%, or the reverse; a company that did not exist in the previous year franks at 25%. The maximum franking credit is the dividend times 1 over the gross-up rate, which is 100% less that rate, over that rate (section 202-60): one third of the dividend at 25% and three sevenths at 30%. A credit above the maximum is taken to be the maximum (section 202-65).

The shareholder includes the dividend and its franking credit in assessable income and gets a tax offset equal to the credit (section 207-20). Tax at the marginal rate you enter, less the offset, is the extra tax the dividend brings; where the offset is larger, an individual gets the excess refunded (section 67-25).

The calculator reproduces the outcome of each ATO example: Happy Feet (3.8% passive income, 25%), Coffee and Cake (28.6%, 25%), Best Equity (100%, 30%), SoftDev (85%, 30%) and Hats 4 Cats ($170 million of aggregated turnover, 30%). For Dillmore Manufacture’s $100,000 dividend franked at 30% it gives $42,857.14, the exact credit; the ATO rounds the gross-up rate to 2.3333 and shows $42,857.75, which is more than the section 202-60 maximum.

## What the company tax and franking calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out aggregated turnover, assessable income or base rate entity passive income: enter figures you have established, with the turnover of connected entities and affiliates included, as the ATO’s Hats 4 Cats example shows. Taxable income gives the tax before offsets, PAYG instalments and other credits.

Not covered: the rates of RSA providers and pooled development funds (subsections 23(3) to (5)), life insurance companies (section 23A), not-for-profit companies and credit unions; corporate unit trusts and public trading trusts; and income years before 2021-22, when the base rate was 27.5% or 26%. A shareholder that is a company, trust, super fund or non-resident is outside its scope; a company shareholder cannot have excess franking credits refunded (section 67-25). Franking account balances, franking deficit tax, the benchmark rule and the holding period rule are not tested: the company tax and franking worksheets track the franking account and the benchmark rule. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.

## How the R&D tax offset calculator works

The calculator works one company’s research and development tax offset under section 355-100 for an income year from 2021-22 to 2026-27. The offset is worked on the notional deductions, or, where they total less than $20,000, on the part paid to research service providers that are not associates and as CRC contributions. A company with aggregated turnover under $20 million that exempt entities do not control gets its corporate tax rate plus 18.5 percentage points, and the offset is refundable. Any other company gets its corporate tax rate plus a premium of 8.5% on the part up to 2% of its total expenses and 16.5% on the part above, and the offset is non-refundable. The corporate tax rate is 25% for a base rate entity that is not an RSA provider, PDF or other company taxed at a special rate, and 30% otherwise. Notional deductions above $150 million get the corporate tax rate only.

A clawback amount is the recoupment amount you enter plus the lesser of the feedstock revenue and the feedstock expenditure. Section 355-450 works the offset again on the notional deductions less the clawback amount, and takes the clawback amount at the corporate tax rate as the deduction amount. The offset less the adjusted offset and the deduction amount, divided by the corporate tax rate for the present year, is included in assessable income in that year. Each amount is worked exactly and rounded to the cent once.

## What the R&D tax offset calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether activities are core or supporting R&D activities, whether they are registered with AusIndustry, whether the company is an R&D entity, or which expenditure it can deduct. It leaves out associate expenditure and its payment (section 355-480), overseas findings and activities overseas, R&D partnerships and offsets of earlier years. You enter the notional deductions, total expenses, turnover, control and clawback amounts as established. It does not work out the recoupment amount itself, including the grant cap and repayments, balancing adjustment clawbacks or catch up deductions. It assumes no earlier clawback or catch up amount for the offset year, and covers only companies and income years starting on or after 1 July 2021. It refuses a clawback amount when the notional deductions, or the notional deductions less the clawback, are under $20,000, because the Act and the ATO’s example do not settle how the threshold applies then. It does not carry a non-refundable offset forward. Results are planning estimates: check them against your records and the ATO’s guidance.

## How the deferred tax calculator works

The calculator works one company’s tax effect worksheet under AASB 112 for an income year from 2020-21 to 2026-27. Each asset’s carrying amount less its tax base, or each liability’s tax base less its carrying amount, is a temporary difference: above nil it is taxable and below nil deductible. Taxable differences give a deferred tax liability. Deductible differences and unused tax losses, less those you say have no deferred tax asset because future taxable profit is not probable, give a deferred tax asset. Both are measured at the 25% or 30% you expect when they reverse, undiscounted.

The year’s deferred tax expense is the change in the net balance, split into the rate change on the opening balances, the change in recognition, and origination and reversal. Current tax is taxable income at the year’s company rate, 25% for a base rate entity (26% in 2020-21) or 30%, less non-refundable offsets. The reconciliation starts from accounting profit at that rate and shows each reconciling line and the difference left. Every amount is exact and rounded to the cent where the worksheet rounds.

## What the deferred tax calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out tax bases or carrying amounts, or judge whether future taxable profit is probable. You enter them as established. It refuses business combinations, uncertain tax treatments, tax-consolidated group members and tax on items outside profit or loss. It refuses a rate change it cannot split without a reversal schedule. It leaves out the initial recognition exception, investments in subsidiaries, branches, associates and joint arrangements, unused tax credits, Pillar Two top-up taxes and prior year adjustments to current tax. It covers only an Australian resident company taxed at the section 23(2) rates, not life insurers, RSA providers, PDFs, non-profit companies or credit unions, and only Australian income tax, not foreign income tax. It does not decide how the balances are presented or offset. Results are planning estimates: check them against your records, the standard and the ATO’s guidance.

## How the salary against dividends calculator works

The company pays the owner the salary you enter and the super guarantee on it, both deductible, pays company tax on the profit left and pays the rest as a fully franked dividend. The owner adds the dividend and its franking credit to other income and the salary, less the $1,000 standard deduction on the salary from 2026-27, and pays tax and the Medicare levy on the total, less the credit. The extra tax this income brings, over the tax on the other income alone, comes off the salary and dividend to give the cash kept. The super fund pays 15% on the super guarantee, and Division 293 tax applies where taxable income and the contributions pass $250,000.

With every dollar paid out and franked at the company’s own rate, salary and dividends are taxed at the owner’s rates either way: the franking credit hands back the company tax. What differs is the super guarantee. Salary moves 10% to 12% of itself into super, taxed at 15% instead of the owner’s marginal rate, so more salary keeps more in all whenever that rate, with the Medicare levy, is above 15%, and less where the tax-free threshold, the offsets or the levy’s phase-in leave it lower. The calculator compares your split with all salary and all dividends, then checks every salary at which a tax rate, an offset, the levy, the super guarantee base or Division 293 changes, and gives the lowest salary that keeps the most, to within $1.

In the example, a $200,000 profit paid as $178,571.43 of salary and $21,428.57 of super guarantee keeps $146,463.02, of which $18,214.28 is in super; paid all as dividends it keeps $140,130.00, all in cash.

## What the salary against dividends calculator does not cover

The calculator assumes the whole profit is paid out in the year. Profit kept in the company is taxed at its rate and paid out later, which this calculator does not model; nor does it test the company’s franking account or the holding period rule, or work a year in which the franking rate, set from last year’s figures, differs from the company’s tax rate. A payment or loan to the owner that is neither salary nor a declared dividend can be an unfranked deemed dividend under Division 7A: see the Division 7A calculator. Where the company earns the owner’s personal services income, the PSI rules checker says whether that income is taxed to the owner whatever the company pays. The salary counts as wages for payroll tax and may need workers compensation cover; neither is counted.

It does not cover salary sacrifice, other concessional contributions, work-related deductions, reportable fringe benefits, net investment losses, family trust distribution tax income, a released first home super saver amount, a super lump sum taxed at 0%, the low income super tax offset, the Medicare levy surcharge, study loan repayments, a spouse or dependants, a non-resident owner, more than one owner, or income years before 2021-22. Super counts at its value after tax, though it stays in the fund until retirement. Have a registered tax agent review the arrangement before relying on a result.

## How the sole trader against company calculator works

As a sole trader the owner is taxed on the whole profit at the resident rates, less the low income tax offsets and the small business income tax offset, plus the Medicare levy. Drawings are not deductible, so the amount taken out changes nothing. Through a company the profit is split: the company pays the salary you enter and the super guarantee on it, both deductible, and pays company tax on the rest. The amount taken out less the salary is paid as a fully franked dividend from the profit after company tax, and whatever is left stays in the company. The owner pays tax and the Medicare levy on the salary (less the $1,000 standard deduction from 2026-27), the dividend and its franking credit, less the credit: the top-up tax is that tax less the tax on the salary alone, and is a refund where the credit is larger. The super fund pays 15% on the super guarantee, and Division 293 tax where taxable income and the contributions pass $250,000.

Each structure’s total adds those amounts for the one year. The 2 totals do not leave the money in the same place: the sole trader holds all the profit after tax, whilst the company structure leaves some in super and any profit not taken out in the company, whose later dividend may bring more personal tax or a refund. The calculator states each figure and the difference; it does not say which structure suits a business.

In the example, a $150,000 profit is taxed $38,570.00 as a sole trader, after the full $1,000 offset. Through a base rate entity paying a $60,000 salary and a $40,000 dividend, the total is $34,913.23, with $22,100.00 kept in the company and $6,120.00 in super.

## What the sole trader against company calculator does not cover

The calculator does not apply the personal services income rules: where the profit is mainly a reward for the owner’s personal efforts, Part 2-42 can tax it to the owner whatever the company pays (see the PSI rules checker). It does not apply Division 7A: a payment, loan or debt forgiveness to the owner that is not salary or a declared dividend can be an unfranked deemed dividend (see the Division 7A calculator). It does not cover the small business CGT concessions or the CGT discount, which a company cannot claim; state taxes such as payroll tax, land tax and duty; or the cost of running a company, such as ASIC fees, its own tax return and its accounts.

It also leaves out the tax on profit kept in the company once it is paid out, the company’s franking account and a year in which the franking rate, set from last year’s figures, differs from the tax rate; other income of the owner, a spouse or family members, trusts and partnerships; GST, PAYG instalments and losses; the Medicare levy surcharge, study loan repayments and the low income super tax offset; a non-resident owner; and income years before 2021-22. Super counts at its value after tax, though it stays in the fund until retirement. Have a registered tax agent review any change of structure before relying on a result.

## How the foreign resident capital gains withholding calculator works

A purchaser of taxable Australian real property or a company title interest must pay the ATO an amount at settlement unless every vendor gives a clearance certificate (section 14-210 of Schedule 1 to the Taxation Administration Act 1953). The calculator ports the foreign-resident-withholding command of australian-accounting. The amount is the rate times the first element of the asset’s cost base, less any amount paid for an option you exercised to buy it (section 14-200(3)): 12.5% for a contract from 1 July 2017, when a market value under $750,000 is excluded, and 15% with no threshold for a contract from 1 January 2025.

With several vendors, each vendor’s share of the base follows their interest. A vendor with a clearance certificate takes nil, one with a notice of variation its varied rate (unless the sale price is above the notice’s maximum), and one with neither the full rate. With no document at all the single amount is rounded once and its cents shared out. An excluded transaction (section 14-215) or a class variation to nil gives nothing to pay.

## How the partnership net income calculator works

## How the trust loss tests checker works

## How the foreign buyer surcharge calculator works

The calculator applies the chosen state or territory’s own test of who is foreign to your answers, once for its duty surcharge and once for its land tax surcharge, as each revenue office states them. The duty surcharge is its rate times the dutiable value of the buyer’s share: 9% in New South Wales, 8% in Victoria, Queensland and Tasmania (1.5% on Tasmanian primary production land), and 7% in Western Australia and South Australia. The ACT and the Northern Territory charge none.

The land tax surcharge is for a full land tax year on the land value you enter: 5% in New South Wales, 4% in Victoria from $50,000 of taxable land ($25,000 for a trust), 3% of the value over $349,999 in Queensland, 2% in Tasmania and 0.75% in the ACT. Western Australia and South Australia charge none, and the Northern Territory has no land tax. Victoria charges a fixed trust’s surcharge on the land held for absentee beneficiaries only. The general transfer duty is worked on the whole property, for all buyers together, at the general rates.

The calculator reproduces the revenue offices’ examples: Revenue NSW’s $90,000 of surcharge purchaser duty and $39,187 of transfer duty on $1 million, and $60,000 of surcharge land tax on $1.2 million; Victoria’s $80,000 for Andrew, $16,000 for Emily’s $200,000 share and $20,000 for Clara’s half of a fixed trust’s $1 million; RevenueSA’s $42,000 and $21,000 for Peter; Western Australia’s $14,000 for Simon; Queensland’s $1,500.03 on $400,000; and Tasmania’s $8,000 on $400,000.

## Who is foreign in each state and territory

An individual who is an Australian citizen, holds a permanent visa or is a New Zealand citizen on a special category visa is not foreign for Queensland, Western Australian, South Australian and Tasmanian duty. New South Wales also requires a permanent resident or New Zealand citizen to have been in Australia 200 days of the previous 12 months (Revenue Ruling G009); Victoria gives a New Zealand citizen no exception unless they ordinarily reside in Australia for 6 consecutive months around settlement. Victorian and Queensland land tax charge absentees: non-citizens without a permanent visa who do not ordinarily reside in Australia and were away on the day or for over 6 months of the year.

A company is foreign when it is incorporated outside Australia, or foreign persons with associates hold 50% or more (more than 50% in Victoria) of its shares or votes, or in New South Wales, Queensland, Tasmania and Western Australia of its potential voting power. New South Wales instead asks whether one foreign person with associates holds 20% or more, or foreign persons together 40% or more, wherever the company is incorporated. Trusts and super funds follow the same shares of capital or members’ balances. Victorian land tax counts absentee interests instead, which you enter apart: a company pays absentee owner surcharge when absentees hold more than 50%, a fixed trust on the land held for absentee beneficiaries, and a complying super fund none. A foreign person who ordinarily lives in Australia is no absentee.

A discretionary trust is foreign in New South Wales if a foreign person could benefit, now or by amending the deed (section 104JA); in Victoria and Tasmania if foreign persons could be given more than 50%, or 50% or more, of the capital; in Queensland if foreign takers in default hold 50% or more; in Western Australia if a foreign person controls it, as a foreign trustee or appointor does, or foreign takers in default hold 50% or more; in South Australia if a trustee, appointor, named object or taker in default is foreign; and in the ACT only if a named foreign beneficiary could receive 50% or more. Victorian land tax looks only at named (specified) beneficiaries who are absentees.

The FIRB fee is the 2026-27 fee for one application to acquire residential land, which FIRB indexes each 1 July. The calculator does not decide whether the buyer is a foreign person under the Foreign Acquisitions and Takeovers Act 1975, whether an exception to the ban on established dwellings applies, or the lower fee rules, exemption certificates, fee waivers and the fees for agricultural and commercial land. The comparison leaves out land tax, since the land value entered is the buyer’s land in the chosen state or territory only.

The calculator does not decide exemptions, refunds or ex gratia relief, which each Commissioner grants on application. Among them are Australian-based developers and build to rent in New South Wales and Victoria, exempt permanent residents who live in the home in New South Wales, significant contributors and large developers in Queensland, and refunds when a buyer stops being foreign within 12 months in South Australia.
