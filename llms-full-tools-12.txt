## How the franking account calculator works

Each PAYG instalment, income tax payment and franking credit received credits the account on its day, and each franked distribution, refund and other debit takes it down. A deficit at 30 June gives rise to franking deficit tax. When you enter that deficit as next year’s opening balance, the calculator adds the matching credit on 1 July. The tax is also an offset against the company’s income tax, reduced by 30% of the part caused by its own distributions and refunds when that part is more than 10% of the year’s credits. The first distribution in each franking period sets the benchmark: one franked above it gives over-franking tax, one below it an extra debit, and a benchmark that moves too far from the last must be disclosed. A franking credit received is entered as the credit, not the dividend, and the offset is applied only when the income tax is given.

When a later refund changes the deficit tax, use the actual 30 June balance and R&D deferral for next year’s opening figures. In next year’s account, enter the refund on its receipt date and enter the additional franking deficit tax from that refund as a separate credit on that date.

## What the franking account calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one entity’s income year from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026 or from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, and no advice. It does not cover:

- consolidated groups, an entity that stops being a franking entity, New Zealand franking companies, pooled development funds, friendly societies, life insurance companies, exempting entities, a substituted accounting period or late balancer, and diverted profits tax;
- whether a distribution is frankable, what the company may frank it to, or whether a statement given after a distribution was allowed (s 202-75);
- a tax offset refund other than an income tax refund’s R&D part, the general interest charge on late franking tax, and the return’s amounts in whole dollars;
- the company’s tax rate and maximum franking credit from turnover, which the company tax and franking calculator works out.

Confirm the figures with the ATO’s instructions or a registered tax agent before relying on them.

## How the company tax and franking calculator works

The calculator applies the base rate entity test of section 23AA of the Income Tax Rates Act 1986 to this year’s figures: aggregated turnover under $50 million, and base rate entity passive income of no more than 80% of assessable income. A base rate entity pays 25% of its taxable income, and any other company 30% (subsection 23(2)).

The rate for franking a dividend comes from a second test. Section 995-1 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 runs the base rate entity test on the previous year’s turnover, passive income and assessable income, so a company can pay tax at 30% and frank at 25%, or the reverse; a company that did not exist in the previous year franks at 25%. The maximum franking credit is the dividend times 1 over the gross-up rate, which is 100% less that rate, over that rate (section 202-60): one third of the dividend at 25% and three sevenths at 30%. A credit above the maximum is taken to be the maximum (section 202-65).

The shareholder includes the dividend and its franking credit in assessable income and gets a tax offset equal to the credit (section 207-20). Tax at the marginal rate you enter, less the offset, is the extra tax the dividend brings; where the offset is larger, an individual gets the excess refunded (section 67-25).

The calculator reproduces the outcome of each ATO example: Happy Feet (3.8% passive income, 25%), Coffee and Cake (28.6%, 25%), Best Equity (100%, 30%), SoftDev (85%, 30%) and Hats 4 Cats ($170 million of aggregated turnover, 30%). For Dillmore Manufacture’s $100,000 dividend franked at 30% it gives $42,857.14, the exact credit; the ATO rounds the gross-up rate to 2.3333 and shows $42,857.75, which is more than the section 202-60 maximum.

## What the company tax and franking calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out aggregated turnover, assessable income or base rate entity passive income: enter figures you have established, with the turnover of connected entities and affiliates included, as the ATO’s Hats 4 Cats example shows. Taxable income gives the tax before offsets, PAYG instalments and other credits.

Not covered: the rates of RSA providers and pooled development funds (subsections 23(3) to (5)), life insurance companies (section 23A), not-for-profit companies and credit unions; corporate unit trusts and public trading trusts; and income years before 2021-22, when the base rate was 27.5% or 26%. A shareholder that is a company, trust, super fund or non-resident is outside its scope; a company shareholder cannot have excess franking credits refunded (section 67-25). Franking account balances, franking deficit tax, the benchmark rule and the holding period rule are not tested: the company tax and franking worksheets track the franking account and the benchmark rule. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.

## How the salary against dividends calculator works

The company pays the owner the salary you enter and the super guarantee on it, both deductible, pays company tax on the profit left and pays the rest as a fully franked dividend. The owner adds the dividend and its franking credit to other income and the salary, less the $1,000 standard deduction on the salary from 2026-27, and pays tax and the Medicare levy on the total, less the credit. The extra tax this income brings, over the tax on the other income alone, comes off the salary and dividend to give the cash kept. The super fund pays 15% on the super guarantee, and Division 293 tax applies where taxable income and the contributions pass $250,000.

With every dollar paid out and franked at the company’s own rate, salary and dividends are taxed at the owner’s rates either way: the franking credit hands back the company tax. What differs is the super guarantee. Salary moves 10% to 12% of itself into super, taxed at 15% instead of the owner’s marginal rate, so more salary keeps more in all whenever that rate, with the Medicare levy, is above 15%, and less where the tax-free threshold, the offsets or the levy’s phase-in leave it lower. The calculator compares your split with all salary and all dividends, then checks every salary at which a tax rate, an offset, the levy, the super guarantee base or Division 293 changes, and gives the lowest salary that keeps the most, to within $1.

In the example, a $200,000 profit paid as $178,571.43 of salary and $21,428.57 of super guarantee keeps $146,463.02, of which $18,214.28 is in super; paid all as dividends it keeps $140,130.00, all in cash.

## What the salary against dividends calculator does not cover

The calculator assumes the whole profit is paid out in the year. Profit kept in the company is taxed at its rate and paid out later, which this calculator does not model; nor does it test the company’s franking account or the holding period rule, or work a year in which the franking rate, set from last year’s figures, differs from the company’s tax rate. A payment or loan to the owner that is neither salary nor a declared dividend can be an unfranked deemed dividend under Division 7A: see the Division 7A calculator. Where the company earns the owner’s personal services income, the PSI rules checker says whether that income is taxed to the owner whatever the company pays. The salary counts as wages for payroll tax and may need workers compensation cover; neither is counted.

It does not cover salary sacrifice, other concessional contributions, work-related deductions, reportable fringe benefits, net investment losses, family trust distribution tax income, a released first home super saver amount, a super lump sum taxed at 0%, the low income super tax offset, the Medicare levy surcharge, study loan repayments, a spouse or dependants, a non-resident owner, more than one owner, or income years before 2021-22. Super counts at its value after tax, though it stays in the fund until retirement. Have a registered tax agent review the arrangement before relying on a result.

## How the foreign buyer surcharge calculator works

The calculator applies the chosen state or territory’s own test of who is foreign to your answers, once for its duty surcharge and once for its land tax surcharge, as each revenue office states them. The duty surcharge is its rate times the dutiable value of the buyer’s share: 9% in New South Wales, 8% in Victoria, Queensland and Tasmania (1.5% on Tasmanian primary production land), and 7% in Western Australia and South Australia. The ACT and the Northern Territory charge none.

The land tax surcharge is for a full land tax year on the land value you enter: 5% in New South Wales, 4% in Victoria from $50,000 of taxable land ($25,000 for a trust), 3% of the value over $350,000 in Queensland, 2% in Tasmania and 0.75% in the ACT. Western Australia and South Australia charge none, and the Northern Territory has no land tax. Victoria charges a fixed trust’s surcharge on the land held for absentee beneficiaries only. The general transfer duty is worked on the whole property, for all buyers together, at the general rates.

The calculator reproduces the revenue offices’ examples: Revenue NSW’s $90,000 of surcharge purchaser duty and $39,187 of transfer duty on $1 million, and $60,000 of surcharge land tax on $1.2 million; Victoria’s $80,000 for Andrew, $16,000 for Emily’s $200,000 share and $20,000 for Clara’s half of a fixed trust’s $1 million; RevenueSA’s $42,000 and $21,000 for Peter; Western Australia’s $14,000 for Simon; Queensland’s $1,500 on $400,000; and Tasmania’s $8,000 on $400,000.

## Who is foreign in each state and territory

An individual who is an Australian citizen, holds a permanent visa or is a New Zealand citizen on a special category visa is not foreign for Queensland, Western Australian, South Australian and Tasmanian duty. New South Wales also requires a permanent resident or New Zealand citizen to have been in Australia 200 days of the previous 12 months (Revenue Ruling G009); Victoria gives a New Zealand citizen no exception unless they ordinarily reside in Australia for 6 consecutive months around settlement. Victorian and Queensland land tax charge absentees: non-citizens without a permanent visa who do not ordinarily reside in Australia and were away on the day or for over 6 months of the year.

A company is foreign when it is incorporated outside Australia, or foreign persons with associates hold 50% or more (more than 50% in Victoria) of its shares or votes, or in New South Wales, Queensland, Tasmania and Western Australia of its potential voting power. New South Wales instead asks whether one foreign person with associates holds 20% or more, or foreign persons together 40% or more, wherever the company is incorporated. Trusts and super funds follow the same shares of capital or members’ balances. Victorian land tax counts absentee interests instead, which you enter apart: a company pays absentee owner surcharge when absentees hold more than 50%, a fixed trust on the land held for absentee beneficiaries, and a complying super fund none. A foreign person who ordinarily lives in Australia is no absentee.

A discretionary trust is foreign in New South Wales if a foreign person could benefit, now or by amending the deed (section 104JA); in Victoria and Tasmania if foreign persons could be given more than 50%, or 50% or more, of the capital; in Queensland if foreign takers in default hold 50% or more; in Western Australia if a foreign person controls it, as a foreign trustee or appointor does, or foreign takers in default hold 50% or more; in South Australia if a trustee, appointor, named object or taker in default is foreign; and in the ACT only if a named foreign beneficiary could receive 50% or more. Victorian land tax looks only at named (specified) beneficiaries who are absentees.

## What the foreign buyer surcharge calculator leaves to you

The FIRB fee is the 2026-27 fee for one application to acquire residential land, which FIRB indexes each 1 July. The calculator does not decide whether the buyer is a foreign person under the Foreign Acquisitions and Takeovers Act 1975, whether an exception to the ban on established dwellings applies, or the lower fee rules, exemption certificates, fee waivers and the fees for agricultural and commercial land. The comparison leaves out land tax, since the land value entered is the buyer’s land in the chosen state or territory only.

The calculator does not decide exemptions, refunds or ex gratia relief, which each Commissioner grants on application. Among them are Australian-based developers and build to rent in New South Wales and Victoria, exempt permanent residents who live in the home in New South Wales, significant contributors and large developers in Queensland, and refunds when a buyer stops being foreign within 12 months in South Australia. When one is sought, it refuses. It also refuses a partner (provisional) or retirement visa holder, whom the revenue offices treat differently, and does not trace interests through other companies and trusts or decide who is an associate.

Not covered: landholder and corporate trustee acquisitions; concessions on general duty; Queensland’s absentee land tax rates, which replace the general rates; Victorian trust surcharge rates; control of a Western Australian discretionary trust by a foreign person other than its trustee or appointor, such as one whose consent a distribution needs; control of a Victorian company through its board or through influence the Commissioner finds, rather than through shares or votes; a Victorian beneficiary nomination for trust land bought before 2006, under which the surcharge starts at $50,000 rather than $25,000; joint land tax assessments; property converted to residential use later; and a surcharge charged again when a buyer becomes foreign within 3 years. The transfer duty calculator and the land tax calculator work the general duty with first home concessions and the general land tax. ## How the ASIC fees calculator works

For a standard fee, the calculator returns ASIC’s 2026-27 amount. For a late fee, it compares the day ASIC received the payment or document with the due date you enter, as INFO 30 sets the tiers by the day ASIC receives them. A payment or document received on or before the due date is not late. A review fee paid after its due date but within one month after it costs $102, and later $428 (Schedule 1 Part 3 of the review fees regulations); a document lodged late attracts the same 2 amounts (Schedule 2 item 14 of the fees regulations). A late fee is on top of the fee itself, and ASIC can charge more than one late fee when several obligations are missed.

The one month begins the day after the due date, which is not counted (item 6 of subsection 36(1) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901). Under subsection 2G(1), a month runs to just before the same day of the next calendar month, or to the end of that month when it has no such day. A fee due on Tuesday 15 September 2026 is within one month if received by Thursday 15 October; one due on Monday 30 November 2026 is within one month if received by Thursday 31 December, because that month starts on 1 December.

## What the ASIC fees calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out a due date. An annual review fee is due 2 months after the review date (subsection 1351(3) of the Corporations Act 2001), a review date can change, and a document’s lodgement period depends on the document, so you enter the due date shown by ASIC or the form’s rules. When a deadline falls on a weekend or public holiday, subsection 105(3) of the Corporations Act or subsection 36(2) of the Acts Interpretation Act may allow the next business day. The calculator does not apply that. When the due date or the last day of the first month is a Saturday or Sunday, it declines to give a tier for a received date that such a move could change, up to the following Wednesday and, after a weekend due date, the end of the month that would follow the moved date. It does not check public holidays.

Not covered: due dates and received dates before 1 July 2026 or after 30 June 2027, including a fee due in one financial year and received in the next, because the sources read do not settle which year’s indexed amount applies; upfront review fees; deregistration and the review fee exceptions in subsection 1351(4); registered schemes, notified foreign passport funds, small transferring financial institutions and CCIVs; registered Australian bodies and foreign companies; credit licensee late fees; fee waivers and reviews of a late fee; and the industry funding levy. Check the amount against ASIC’s invoice before you pay.

## How the business days calculator works

The calculator skips Saturdays, Sundays and the public holidays of the state or territory you choose, using each government’s published dates for 2026 and 2027. Between 2 dates, it counts the business days after the start date up to and including the end date, so a Friday to the next Monday is 1. Forward or back, it finds the date that is that many business days after or before the start, and lists each weekday holiday it skipped.

‘Any state or territory’ skips a day that is a public holiday throughout any one of them, as the site’s Payday Super business days table counts them, and leaves out Melbourne Cup Day and the WA King’s Birthday because neither applies throughout its state.

## What the business days calculator does not cover

Part-day holidays, such as the evenings of Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve in South Australia and the Northern Territory, count as business days. So do regional holidays, among them the Royal Queensland Show in Brisbane, Tasmania’s Royal Hobart Regatta and Recreation Day, and show days, and days off for one group only, such as Tasmania’s Easter Tuesday for the public service. A council or region’s local substitute for Melbourne Cup Day or the WA King’s Birthday is not applied: the state date is still skipped.

The calculator does not decide whether a law counts business days, clear days or calendar days, or which state’s holidays apply. Dates after 31 December 2027 are refused until the governments confirm the following year, and Victorian dates after 31 August 2027 until the AFL schedules its Grand Final. The calculator does not work out a superannuation guarantee or Payday Super due date.
