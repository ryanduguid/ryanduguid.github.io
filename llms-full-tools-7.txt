## How the PSI rules checker works

The PSI rules checker takes one individual’s personal services income for one income year and works through Part 2-42 in the order the Act sets. Each step on the result names the section it applies and what it found.

Income that is not mainly a reward for the individual’s personal efforts or skills is not PSI (subsection 84-5(1)), and income received as an employee falls outside Division 85 (subsection 85-35(1)). A personal services business determination in force settles the question (subsection 87-15(1)). Otherwise the results test comes first: if at least 75% of the PSI is paid for a result, with the plant and tools supplied and liability for defects, the PSI rules do not apply, however much comes from one payer (section 87-18).

If 80% or more of the PSI comes from one payer and its associates, the other 3 tests cannot be self-assessed and only a determination keeps the rules away (subsection 87-15(3)). The checker still works them out, because the Commissioner weighs them for a determination: 2 or more unrelated payers that engaged the individual or entity through offers to the public (section 87-20), principal work by others of at least 20% by market value or an apprentice for half the year (section 87-25), and qualifying business premises all year (section 87-30).

The checker reproduces the conclusion of each worked example in TR 2022/3 that reaches one on a test it applies, and of the matching examples on the ATO’s PSI pages.

## How to enter payers in the PSI rules checker

Enter each payer once, with its associates’ payments added in: the payer is whoever must pay under the contract, so a labour hire firm or agency is the payer, not its client (TR 2022/3 paragraphs 69 to 72). Payers that are associates of each other share one row too, as section 87-20 counts only payers that are not associates of each other. Count each Australian government agency as its own payer (section 87-35) and leave out income received as an employee. If the individual works through their own company, partnership or trust, choose that entity as who receives the income, even if it pays them a salary. Tick ‘Paid for a result’, ‘Supplies plant and tools’ and ‘Liable for defects’ only where the contract or industry custom makes it so (subsection 87-18(4)). The results test counts income, not payers, and a tick covers all of that payer’s PSI: where only part of it meets all 3, leave the boxes unticked, which can only make the results test harder to pass, and work out that part yourself. For the market value of principal work by others, count work by any individual or entity engaged, an individual associate included, but not by an associate that is a company, partnership or trust, nor, for an entity, by an individual whose PSI it receives; a partner counts only if they earn no PSI through the partnership (subsections 87-25(1), (2) and (2A); TR 2022/3 paragraphs 110 to 116). With more than 5 payers, put the smallest together in one row only where every answer is the same; that can only make a test harder to pass.

## What the PSI rules checker leaves to you

The checker does not decide whether income is mainly a reward for personal efforts or skills, whether a contract pays for a result, whether an offer to the public won the work, who is an associate or what principal work is worth. These turn on facts and judgement, so it takes your answers and says so on each step; TR 2022/3 sets out the factors at paragraphs 34 to 49 and 77 to 148.

The checker does not allocate expenses across PSI, business income and other income. Division 85 limits deductions to the extent they relate to PSI (subsection 85-10(1)) but sets no method for the split, and TR 2022/3 leaves deductions to other rulings (paragraphs 6 and 31), so any split is practice rather than law. It does not work out the amount attributed under Division 86, PAYG withholding or a deduction for a net PSI loss.

The checker does not apply the agent rules when they could apply (section 87-40), decide a determination, which is the Commissioner’s (sections 87-60 and 87-65), or test Part IVA, which can still apply to a personal services business (TR 2022/3 paragraph 160). Several individuals working through one entity are tested one at a time (paragraph 76). Confirm the result with the ruling or a registered tax agent.

## How the staff cost calculator works

The staff cost calculator adds up the annual cost of one staff member from figures you supply. Annual cost = wages + supplied super amount + other annual costs. Monthly average = annual cost / 12. Include expected insurance, payroll tax, recruitment, relief staff and equipment in other costs. Paid leave already within salary must not be added again.

No statutory rate or threshold is applied.

## What the staff cost calculator does not decide

## How the 13-week cash forecast works

The 13-week cash forecast works out closing cash for each week and the funding gap against a buffer you set. Weekly closing cash = opening cash + receipts − payments. Each closing balance opens the next week. Funding gap = minimum buffer − lowest opening or weekly closing cash, with a floor of zero.

No statutory rate or threshold is applied. The layout follows business.gov.au: cash flow.

## What the 13-week cash forecast does not decide

The funding gap is a planning gap, not a lending decision. The 13-week cash forecast does not predict receipts or payments, and weekly totals can hide a shortage within the week. A receipt delayed beyond week 13 is reported separately and does not enter the closing balance.

The Lumbridge cash flow case works the same question with a 45-day receipt delay.

## How the objection deadline checker works

The objection deadline checker gives the last day to lodge a taxation objection under section 14ZW of the Taxation Administration Act 1953. An income tax assessment allows 2 years after notice of the assessment is given when item 1, 2, 3 or 3A of the table in subsection 170(1) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 applies, and 4 years otherwise. An FBT assessment allows 4 years. Any other decision for which the section sets no other period allows 60 days after notice is served.

An amended assessment allows whichever ends later: 60 days after notice of the amended assessment, or the 2 or 4 years after notice of the first assessment for that year. The checker shows both dates and the later one.

Each period starts the day after notice, because subsection 36(1) of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 leaves out the day a period begins after. A 2-year period from notice given on 28 February 2026 therefore starts on 1 March 2026 and ends on 29 February 2028. When the last day is a Saturday or Sunday, subsection 36(2) lets the objection be lodged on the next day that is not a weekend or holiday. The Act’s own example: a person who has until Saturday 31 March may apply on Monday 2 April.

## What the objection deadline checker does not cover

The checker does not decide whether a decision can be objected to, which item of subsection 170(1) applies, or when notice was given, so keep the notice’s issue and receipt dates. It does not know public holidays: they differ by state and town, so check the last day and the day after it where you lodge. It does not cover private rulings, administrative penalties, retained refunds, deceased estates, specialised taxes or the other decisions for which section 14ZW sets its own period, or the time to seek review of an objection decision. Confirm the date with the notice of decision or a registered tax agent.

## How the PAYG instalments calculator works

The PAYG instalments calculator works out one quarterly pay as you go instalment by the method on the activity statement. Under option 1 the instalment is the amount the ATO notifies at T7. Varying it, the first instalment of the income year is 25% of the estimated tax for the year, the second 50%, the third 75% and the fourth 100%, each less the earlier instalments and plus any credits claimed for them. A negative result is a credit to claim at 5B.

Under option 2 the instalment is the quarter’s instalment income at T1 times the instalment rate at T2. A varied rate is the estimated tax for the year divided by the estimated instalment income, times 100. If the varied rate is lower than the earlier rate, the earlier instalments less any credits claimed, less the earlier instalment income times the varied rate, is a credit for 5B.

For 2026-27, the instalment amount the ATO notifies already includes its GDP adjustment of 5%, which raises the base year’s income to allow for growth; the adjustment does not change an instalment rate. The ATO’s own example: instalment income of $20,100 at a rate of 1.7% gives $341.70, entered as 341 at T11 and 5A.

## What the PAYG instalments calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out instalment income, the tax on it or whether you can choose between the 2 options. It does not cover monthly instalments, the 2 instalments a year some payers make, annual instalments or consolidated groups, and it does not estimate general interest charge on a variation that proves too low. It does not check whether you may claim a credit or when an instalment is due. Confirm the figures with the ATO’s PAYG instalments calculator or a registered tax agent.

## How the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator works

The redundancy and ETP withholding calculator works out how much a payer withholds from one life benefit employment termination payment (ETP) made in 2026-27. A genuine redundancy or early retirement scheme payment is tax-free up to $13,598 plus $6,801 for each completed year of service. The part above that limit is a code R ETP, as are payments for invalidity and compensation for personal injury, unfair dismissal, harassment or discrimination. The code R part is withheld at the rate within the cap up to the $270,000 ETP cap and at 47% above it.

The rest of the ETP is code O: a golden handshake, gratuity, non-genuine redundancy payment, severance pay, payment in lieu of notice, unused sick leave or unused rostered days off. Its cap is the smaller of the ETP cap left after the code R part and the whole-of-income cap, which is $180,000 less the other taxable payments you made to the employee in the year. The rate within the cap is 17% if the employee is their preservation age or older on 30 June 2027, otherwise 32%; 47% applies above the cap.

Schedule 11’s example 4 takes Alec, 30, made redundant after 5 years of service with a $48,000 genuine redundancy payment and a $17,678 gratuity, having been paid $140,000 of salary and wages in the year. It works out a $397 ETP over the $47,603 tax-free limit, withholds $127 from it and $5,657 from the gratuity within the $40,000 whole-of-income cap. This calculator opens on those figures, with a date of birth that makes Alec 30, and gives the same amounts.

## What the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a termination is a genuine redundancy, whether a payment is an ETP or which code applies. It takes the taxable component as you enter it and does not work out a tax-free component other than the redundancy limit, such as an invalidity segment. It covers one life benefit ETP for one termination: not death benefit ETPs, delayed termination payments, a later instalment for the same termination, an earlier ETP in the same year, an employee who has not given you their TFN, a foreign resident or a working holiday maker. It does not work out withholding on unused leave, which Schedule 7 covers. Confirm the figures with Schedule 11 or a registered tax agent.

## How the land tax calculator works

The calculator applies one state or territory’s general or trust scale to a total taxable land value and gives the tax for a full year. Below a jurisdiction’s threshold the tax is nil; the Australian Capital Territory has no threshold, and nor does a special trust in New South Wales. Above it, each scale charges a base amount plus a percentage of the value over a band’s threshold, except that Victoria charges a flat $500 from $50,000 and $975 from $100,000 up to $300,000. South Australia charges for every $100, or part of $100, so the excess is rounded up to the next $100, and RevenueSA issues no assessment where the calculated land tax is under $20; the calculator shows the amount and says so. The Australian Capital Territory adds its fixed charge of $1,778 to the valuation charge and assesses each quarter’s share by its days; the calculator gives the full-year amount. Each scale is the one in force for the land tax year current on 16 November 2026: the 2026 calendar year in New South Wales and Victoria, and the 2026-27 financial year in Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania and the Australian Capital Territory.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the calculator gives the same amount. Revenue NSW gives $500 on an average land value of $1,100,000, $9,600 for a special trust on $600,000, and $2,100 of land tax and $60,000 of surcharge land tax for a foreign owner of $1,200,000 of residential land. Victoria’s State Revenue Office gives $5,550 on $1,100,000, and its table with the absentee owner surcharge gives $44,650 on $1,000,000 at the general rates. The Queensland Revenue Office gives $1,300 on $680,000 and $87,000 on $6,400,000 for an individual, $106,500 on $6,400,000 for a company, $8,420 on $760,000 for the trustee of a self-managed super fund, and $3,800 on $400,000 for an absentee and for a foreign company, each with $1,500 of surcharge. ## Trust rates, surcharges and the metropolitan region improvement tax

Trust rates are a special trust’s rates in New South Wales, with no threshold; the trust surcharge rates in Victoria; the rates for companies and trustees, including super funds, in Queensland; and the trust rates in South Australia. Western Australia taxes a trustee’s land as if it were the trustee’s own, at the general rates.

A foreign or absentee owner pays: in New South Wales surcharge land tax of 5% of the land value of the residential land a foreign person owns, their share only and including land exempt from land tax, entered as the surcharge value; in Victoria the absentee owner surcharge of 4% of the total taxable value, from $50,000 at the general rates and from $25,000 at the trust rates; in Queensland the absentee rates for an absentee individual, and for an absentee, a foreign company or the trustee of a foreign trust a surcharge of 3% of the value over $350,000; in Tasmania the foreign investor land tax surcharge of 2% of the assessed land value of the foreign-owned share of residential General Land acquired on or after 1 July 2022, entered as the surcharge value; and in the Australian Capital Territory the foreign ownership surcharge of 0.75% of the parcel’s average unimproved value.

Western Australia charges its metropolitan region improvement tax of 0.14% of the value over $300,000 with land tax where the land is in the Perth metropolitan region; the calculator adds it when all the land is. A surcharge and this tax are shown apart from the land tax, with the total.

## What the land tax calculator does not compute

The land tax calculator applies the scale for the rates and owner you choose. It does not decide which land is taxable, which rates apply, whether an owner is a foreign person or absentee, or what the taxable or surcharge value is; the foreign purchaser surcharge calculator applies each jurisdiction’s test of a foreign or absentee owner. These are not computed:

- New South Wales: exemptions and concessions, including the principal place of residence and primary production land exemptions, exemptions from surcharge land tax, and land of a company that is not its group’s concessional company, which pays 1.6% of its whole taxable value with no threshold, or 2% of it where the group’s land exceeds the premium threshold (Land Tax Act 1956, Schedule 13, Parts 3 and 4).
- Victoria: exemptions and concessions, exemptions from the absentee owner surcharge and vacant residential land tax.
- Queensland: exemptions and relief, exemptions from the foreign surcharge and the aggregation of land held for trusts with the same beneficiaries.
- Western Australia: land tax exemptions, and the metropolitan region improvement tax on holdings only partly in the Perth metropolitan region.
- South Australia: exemptions, waiver or relief, and which land held on trust qualifies for the general rates.
- Tasmania: zero rate classifications, exemptions and rebates, including principal residence and primary production land, and which land the foreign investor land tax surcharge applies to.
- Australian Capital Territory: land tax exemptions, when the foreign ownership surcharge applies, a unit’s share by its unit entitlement and the split into quarters. Land tax there applies to residential property that is not a principal place of residence.

Joint ownership, grouping of related companies and the apportionment of the tax between properties are not computed either. Confirm the figure with the revenue office’s own calculator or assessment notice before relying on it.

The transfer duty calculator gives general transfer duty, and duty for an eligible first home buyer of a home. These are not computed:

- New South Wales: duty on land other than residential land over $3,870,000, which the premium rate does not reach (the calculator applies it and says so in its result), the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme on vacant land, surcharge purchaser duty for foreign persons, including foreign trusts and companies, and landholder duty.

## How the income tax calculator works

The income tax calculator works out one individual’s tax for a full income year from 2020-21 to 2026-27: the tax on taxable income at the year’s rates, the offsets, the Medicare levy and the Medicare levy surcharge. The offsets are the low income tax offset and, for 2020-21 and 2021-22, the low and middle income tax offset. It then takes that total, and any study or training loan repayment, from the tax withheld you enter, to estimate a refund or an amount owing.

An Australian resident pays nothing on the first $18,200 and then each part of taxable income at its rate. The low income tax offset of up to $700 comes off a resident’s tax, but not below nil. The Medicare levy is 2% of a resident’s taxable income; up to the low-income threshold there is none, and from there to the phase-in limit it is 10% of the income over the threshold, if that is less. The ATO’s example on Medicare levy reduction for low-income earners, read 17 November 2026, gives Angie, single with $29,000 of taxable income in 2025-26, a levy of $98.90, as this calculator does.

The Medicare levy surcharge applies to a resident without private patient hospital cover whose income for surcharge purposes is above the year’s threshold. For each day without cover it charges 1%, 1.25% or 1.5% of taxable income and reportable fringe benefits, by the tier the income falls in.

For 2026-27 the Medicare levy thresholds are those the Act held on 17 November 2026, the same as 2025-26. If Parliament raises them for 2026-27, a person just above $28,011 will pay less than shown here.

## What the income tax calculator does not compute

The calculator gives the tax of one person who was a resident, a foreign resident or a working holiday maker for the whole year. It does not compute:

- the Medicare levy reduction for families, which depends on family taxable income and dependants, or a Medicare levy exemption;
- the seniors and pensioners tax offset and its higher Medicare levy thresholds, or any other offset, such as for private health insurance, dependants or super contributions;
- a change of family or cover status during the year beyond the days without cover, the private health insurance rebate, a spouse’s trust income that the surcharge test adds, the net amount on which family trust distribution tax has been paid, which counts in income for surcharge purposes and in the amount the surcharge is levied on, or exempt foreign employment income, which the ATO’s surcharge page adds to income for surcharge purposes when taxable income is $1 or more;
- a first home super saver released amount, which is taxed with a 30% offset but left out of repayment income and income for surcharge purposes, or, for 2023-24 and earlier, the taxed element of a super lump sum up to the low rate cap, which a person from preservation age to under 60 could leave out of income for surcharge purposes;
- a study loan debt smaller than the repayment: the repayment cannot exceed what is still owed (section 154-20(1)(c) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003), which the calculator does not ask for;
- a part-year resident’s reduced tax-free threshold, a working holiday maker who is an Australian resident, or one with other income besides working holiday taxable income;
- tax on superannuation or employment termination remainders, income averaging, or a minor’s unearned income;
- Division 293 tax, excess contributions, PAYG instalments or other credits besides the tax withheld you enter.

## How the take-home pay calculator works

The take-home pay calculator takes one salary for a year, month, fortnight or week and works out the year’s income tax, offsets, Medicare levy, any Medicare levy surcharge and any study or training loan repayment on it. Salary sacrificed to super comes off first. From 2026-27 it also takes off the standard deduction of up to $1,000 for work-related expenses. It then gives what is left for the year, a month, a fortnight and a week, and the super guarantee an employer must pay, which goes to a super fund and is not part of take-home pay.

Those take-home amounts are the year’s tax spread evenly: the year’s net amount divided by 12, 26 and 52. They are not the amounts an employer withholds each pay. For a weekly, fortnightly or monthly pay, the calculator also works the PAYG amount withheld from one pay under the year’s Schedule 1, and Schedule 8 with a study loan, and the year’s withholding less the year’s tax, which is the refund or amount owing when the tax return is assessed. Withholding does not allow for the low and middle income tax offset, so in 2021-22 the offset comes back as part of the refund: $3,000 a fortnight has $670 withheld each pay and $1,543 back. For working holiday makers, who are withheld from under another schedule, use the ATO’s tax withheld calculator.

The example on arrival is 2025-26, before the standard deduction: on $75,000 the Medicare levy is $1,500, as the ATO’s example of Matthew on What is the Medicare levy gives. In 2026-27 the same salary is $74,000 of taxable income.

When the salary includes super, the calculator splits it: at 12%, a package of $112,000 is $100,000 of salary and $12,000 of super. Super guarantee stops at the year’s maximum contribution base, so above it the super stays at the most the base allows.

## What the take-home pay calculator does not compute

The calculator assumes one employer, a salary paid evenly over the full year, and no other income, deductions or offsets. It does not compute:

- overtime, bonuses, allowances or leave loading, a 53rd weekly or 27th fortnightly pay, or withholding on scale 1, scale 5 or 6 or for a working holiday maker, a withholding declaration’s offsets or a Medicare levy variation;
- 2025-26 withholding with a study loan that mixes the 2 Schedule 8s: the year’s withholding and refund take the one from 24 September 2025 for every pay, and the component before that date is shown beside it;
- work-related deductions you claim, which reduce the standard deduction dollar for dollar except those section 25-130(3) disregards, such as professional association fees;
- salary sacrifice of anything but super, reportable fringe benefits, excess concessional contributions or Division 293 tax;
- everything the income tax calculator leaves out, such as the Medicare levy reduction for families and the seniors and pensioners tax offset;
- a family’s surcharge: this calculator works out the surcharge for a single person with no dependants, and the income tax calculator works out a family’s;
- a study loan debt smaller than the repayment, which section 154-20(1)(c) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003 caps at what is still owed;
- whether an employee is entitled to super guarantee, the lower transitional rate for employees on Norfolk Island (11% for 2026-27), or a second employer’s separate maximum contribution base;
- super guarantee pay by pay: the calculator works it on the year’s earnings, rounded once, where an employer works it on each payday from 2026-27, so the year’s total can differ by cents, and to 2025-26 on each quarter’s earnings, so above the base a weekly or fortnightly salary with more pays in some quarters gets less: $260,000 paid fortnightly in 2025-26 can give $29,400, not $30,000.
