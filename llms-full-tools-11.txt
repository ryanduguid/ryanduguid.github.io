## How the long service leave calculator works

Long service leave comes from each state and territory’s own Act, not the National Employment Standards. The calculator measures the service from the first day to the last, less the days you exclude, and applies the Act you choose. While employment continues, it gives the leave the employee can take now and when the next amount becomes available. When employment ends, it decides from the length of service and the reason whether the Act pays anything, works out the weeks it pays, deducts leave already taken or paid, and values the rest at the weekly ordinary pay. Each step names its provision. Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory give full leave after 7 years and the other states and territories after 10; New South Wales and the Australian Capital Territory can pay a pro rata amount from 5 years, the others from 7, and Victoria not before 7 years at all.

## What the long service leave calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate under one Act for one employee, and no legal advice. It does not cover:

- which Act applies to employment in more than one state or territory, and whether an award, enterprise agreement or contract gives a better entitlement, including the long service leave terms of a pre-2010 award or agreement that section 113 of the Fair Work Act 2009 preserves;
- portable schemes, such as building and construction, contract cleaning, community services and security, and the black coal mining industry, Commonwealth and state public servants, Queensland seasonal employees in the sugar industry and meat works and other seasonal employees, and Tasmanian mining employees, who have their own rules;
- whether a break keeps service continuous, which absences count, and whether a reason for leaving qualifies, such as a pressing necessity or serious misconduct: you decide those;
- averaging pay for varied hours, casual or part-time work, commission or piece rates;
- leave taken in advance of the entitlement, cashing out, and service before 23 June 1990, which some Acts count under earlier rules;
- tax withheld from the payment.

The figures follow each Act as read on 19 November 2026; a future date assumes it is unchanged. Confirm the result with the state or territory’s long service leave regulator or a lawyer before relying on it.

## How the fuel tax credit calculator works

The fuel tax credit calculator works out a business’s fuel tax credits for fuel acquired in one income year from 2022-23 to 2026-27. Each line takes the rate the ATO published for the day the fuel was acquired, for its fuel and its use: a heavy vehicle travelling on a public road (fuel for a bus’s or coach’s passenger air-conditioning included) takes the rate net of the road user charge, every other business use takes the full rate, and a light vehicle on a public road gets nothing. The page adds the lines and gives the whole dollar amount for label 7D on the business activity statement, with the rate, its period and its ATO page for each line.

A small claimant can also enter a line by its cost and the average pump price instead of its litres, split one heavy vehicle’s diesel between public roads and other use by the basic method, and take every line at the rate on the last day of a tax period that spans only an indexation change. Rates for every period since 1 July 2022 are held here, so an earlier period’s credits can be worked out for an amendment.

## What the fuel tax credit calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether the business is entitled to a credit: you state the facts that settle it. Nor does it decide who acquired the fuel in a hire chain, whether a road is a public road or travel on it is incidental, whether a vehicle meets the environmental criteria of section 41-25, or how fuel is apportioned between uses: you state those. It does not hold the auxiliary equipment percentages of PCG 2016/11, the ATO’s list of heavy vehicles used mainly off public roads, the consumption rates for articulated combinations, or rates for aviation fuel, packaging and supply, domestic heating or electricity generation. It does not work Division 44 adjustments, the attribution of credits to tax periods or the conversion of LPG kilograms to litres. It refuses fuel acquired before 1 July 2022, after 31 January 2027, or outside the income year chosen.

Fuel acquired after 19 November 2026, when the rates were read, carries a warning to check the ATO’s rates page for a change since. Confirm the result with the ATO’s fuel tax credit tools or a registered tax or BAS agent before relying on it.

## How the unused leave calculator works

When employment ends, unused annual leave, leave loading and long service leave are taxed by when the leave accrued and why the employment ended. Leave for employment before 18 August 1993, and all of it from 16 August 1978 on a genuine redundancy, early retirement scheme or invalidity, is capped at 30% and withheld at 32%; it is lump sum A. Only 5% of long service leave for the period before 16 August 1978 is assessable; the whole amount is lump sum B. The rest goes through Schedule 7’s marginal rate calculation with that 5%, spread over a year of pays and withheld at the employee’s Schedule 1 rate, and is reported as unused leave on termination.

## What the unused leave calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employee’s unused leave paid on termination from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, and no advice. It does not cover:

- splitting a payment between the periods by days of service under sections 83-95 to 83-115: enter each part;
- whether a termination is a genuine redundancy, an early retirement scheme or invalidity;
- unused leave paid after an employee’s death;
- a working holiday maker, whose payments Schedule 15 covers;
- an employee who has not claimed the tax-free threshold, or claims a Medicare levy variation or the seniors and pensioners offset (scales 1, 5 and 6), or has a withholding variation;
- a post-17 August 1993 payment under $300 with long service leave from before 16 August 1978, where Schedule 7 does not say how its limit applies;
- employment termination payments, which the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator covers.

## How the company tax and franking calculator works

The calculator applies the base rate entity test of section 23AA of the Income Tax Rates Act 1986 to this year’s figures: aggregated turnover under $50 million, and base rate entity passive income of no more than 80% of assessable income. A base rate entity pays 25% of its taxable income, and any other company 30% (subsection 23(2)).

The rate for franking a dividend comes from a second test. Section 995-1 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 runs the base rate entity test on the previous year’s turnover, passive income and assessable income, so a company can pay tax at 30% and frank at 25%, or the reverse; a company that did not exist in the previous year franks at 25%. The maximum franking credit is the dividend times 1 over the gross-up rate, which is 100% less that rate, over that rate (section 202-60): one third of the dividend at 25% and three sevenths at 30%. A credit above the maximum is taken to be the maximum (section 202-65).

The shareholder includes the dividend and its franking credit in assessable income and gets a tax offset equal to the credit (section 207-20). Tax at the marginal rate you enter, less the offset, is the extra tax the dividend brings; where the offset is larger, an individual gets the excess refunded (section 67-25).

The calculator reproduces the outcome of each ATO example: Happy Feet (3.8% passive income, 25%), Coffee and Cake (28.6%, 25%), Best Equity (100%, 30%), SoftDev (85%, 30%) and Hats 4 Cats ($170 million of aggregated turnover, 30%). For Dillmore Manufacture’s $100,000 dividend franked at 30% it gives $42,857.14, the exact credit; the ATO rounds the gross-up rate to 2.3333 and shows $42,857.75, which is more than the section 202-60 maximum.

## What the company tax and franking calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out aggregated turnover, assessable income or base rate entity passive income: enter figures you have established, with the turnover of connected entities and affiliates included, as the ATO’s Hats 4 Cats example shows. Taxable income gives the tax before offsets, PAYG instalments and other credits.

Not covered: the rates of RSA providers and pooled development funds (subsections 23(3) to (5)), life insurance companies (section 23A), not-for-profit companies and credit unions; corporate unit trusts and public trading trusts; and income years before 2021-22, when the base rate was 27.5% or 26%. A shareholder that is a company, trust, super fund or non-resident is outside its scope; a company shareholder cannot have excess franking credits refunded (section 67-25). Franking account balances, franking deficit tax, the benchmark rule and the holding period rule are not tested: the company tax and franking worksheets track the franking account and the benchmark rule. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.

## How the salary against dividends calculator works

The company pays the owner the salary you enter and the super guarantee on it, both deductible, pays company tax on the profit left and pays the rest as a fully franked dividend. The owner adds the dividend and its franking credit to other income and the salary, less the $1,000 standard deduction on the salary from 2026-27, and pays tax and the Medicare levy on the total, less the credit. The extra tax this income brings, over the tax on the other income alone, comes off the salary and dividend to give the cash kept. The super fund pays 15% on the super guarantee, and Division 293 tax applies where taxable income and the contributions pass $250,000.

With every dollar paid out and franked at the company’s own rate, salary and dividends are taxed at the owner’s rates either way: the franking credit hands back the company tax. What differs is the super guarantee. Salary moves 10% to 12% of itself into super, taxed at 15% instead of the owner’s marginal rate, so more salary keeps more in all whenever that rate, with the Medicare levy, is above 15%, and less where the tax-free threshold, the offsets or the levy’s phase-in leave it lower. The calculator compares your split with all salary and all dividends, then checks every salary at which a tax rate, an offset, the levy, the super guarantee base or Division 293 changes, and gives the highest salary within $1 of the most kept.

In the example, a $200,000 profit paid as $178,571.43 of salary and $21,428.57 of super guarantee keeps $146,463.02, of which $18,214.28 is in super; paid all as dividends it keeps $140,130.00, all in cash.

## What the salary against dividends calculator does not cover

The calculator assumes the whole profit is paid out in the year. Profit kept in the company is taxed at its rate and paid out later, which this calculator does not model; nor does it test the company’s franking account or the holding period rule. A payment or loan to the owner that is neither salary nor a declared dividend can be an unfranked deemed dividend under Division 7A: see the Division 7A calculator. Where the company earns the owner’s personal services income, the PSI rules checker says whether that income is taxed to the owner whatever the company pays. The salary counts as wages for payroll tax and may need workers compensation cover; neither is counted.

It does not cover salary sacrifice, other concessional contributions, the low income super tax offset, the Medicare levy surcharge, study loan repayments, a spouse or dependants, a non-resident owner, more than one owner, or income years before 2021-22. Super counts at its value after tax, though it stays in the fund until retirement. Have a registered tax agent review the arrangement before relying on a result.
