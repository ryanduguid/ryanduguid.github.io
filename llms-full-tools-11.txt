## How the long service leave calculator works

Long service leave comes from each state and territory’s own Act, not the National Employment Standards. The calculator measures the service from the first day to the last, less the days you exclude, and applies the Act you choose. While employment continues, it gives the leave the employee can take now and when the next amount becomes available. When employment ends, it decides from the length of service and the reason whether the Act pays anything, works out the weeks it pays, deducts leave already taken or paid, and values the rest at the weekly ordinary pay. Each step names its provision. Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory give full leave after 7 years and the other states and territories after 10; New South Wales and the Australian Capital Territory can pay a pro rata amount from 5 years, the others from 7, and Victoria not before 7 years at all.

## What the long service leave calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate under one Act for one employee, and no legal advice. It does not cover:

- which Act applies to employment in more than one state or territory, and whether an award, enterprise agreement or contract gives a better entitlement, including the long service leave terms of a pre-2010 award or agreement that section 113 of the Fair Work Act 2009 preserves;
- portable schemes, such as building and construction, contract cleaning, community services and security, and the black coal mining industry, Commonwealth and state public servants, Queensland seasonal employees in the sugar industry and meat works and other seasonal employees, and Tasmanian mining employees, who have their own rules;
- whether a break keeps service continuous, which absences count, and whether a reason for leaving qualifies, such as a pressing necessity or serious misconduct: you decide those;
- averaging pay for varied hours, casual or part-time work, commission or piece rates;
- leave taken in advance of the entitlement, cashing out, and service before 23 June 1990, which some Acts count under earlier rules;
- tax withheld from the payment.

The figures follow each Act as read on 19 November 2026; a future date assumes it is unchanged. Confirm the result with the state or territory’s long service leave regulator or a lawyer before relying on it.

## How the wine equalisation tax calculator works

Wine equalisation tax (WET) is 29% of the taxable value of each assessable dealing in wine (section 5-5). A wholesale sale’s taxable value is its price excluding WET and GST, and GST is then 10% of the price including WET: the ATO’s $120 dozen bears WET of $34.80 and GST of $15.48, $170.28 in all. A retail sale or own use, such as wine used for tastings, is valued at a notional wholesale selling price. By the half retail price method that is 50% of the retail price including WET and GST (section 9-35), so the ATO’s $140 dozen at the cellar door bears WET of $20.30. Grape wine may instead use the average wholesale price method, the weighted average of the tax period’s wholesale prices for wine of the same vintage and variety (section 9-40), but only if at least 10% by value of those sales are wholesale sales (subsection 9-25(3)); wine that is not grape wine must use half retail (section 9-30). A sale under quote bears no WET (section 7-10). Each amount is rounded half up to the cent.

A producer that meets the conditions of section 19-5 earns a producer rebate of 29% of the wholesale price, or of the notional wholesale selling price, on each dealing it is liable for, and on a sale under quote unless the quote states the buyer will use the wine in manufacture, make a GST-free supply of it or sell it on under quote. The rebates for a financial year are capped, for the producer or for its group of associated producers together: $350,000 for dealings to 30 June 2026 and $400,000 from 1 July 2026 (subsections 19-15(2) and (3)). The calculator takes the rebates already claimed this year off the cap and gives what is left. The wine tax goes to label 1C and the rebate to label 1D of the business activity statement, each in whole dollars, never netted. It reproduces the ATO’s rebate table for 2026-27: WET of $11,890 and rebates of $16,240 on its retail, wholesale, quoted and tasting dealings.

## What the wine equalisation tax calculator does not cover

The calculator works dealings in the financial years 2023-24 to 2027-28 and refuses earlier years, which carry the transitional rebate rules for 2017 and earlier vintage wine. It refuses the average wholesale price method for wine that is not grape wine or without the 10% share of wholesale sales, and a rebate on own use of wine bought under quote. It takes each price as given, so it does not work out an arm’s length price (section 27-10) or the price of wine sold with other goods; it assumes GST applies to a sale and does not decide whether a supply is GST-free.

Not covered: imports and other customs dealings, whose WET the Department of Home Affairs collects; the special valuations of section 9-45; wine tax credits other than the producer rebate, such as bad debts and overpaid WET; the reduction of a rebate for earlier producers’ rebates on wine used in a blend; New Zealand participants’ rebates; whether producers are associated (the group’s claims are taken as given); and the tax period a dealing belongs to. Rebates claimed above the cap are flagged, not worked out: section 19-25 makes the excess payable as wine tax at the end of the financial year. Have a registered tax agent confirm the result before you lodge.

## How the fuel tax credit calculator works

The fuel tax credit calculator works out a business’s fuel tax credits for fuel acquired in one income year from 2022-23 to 2026-27. Each line takes the rate the ATO published for the day the fuel was acquired, for its fuel and its use: a heavy vehicle travelling on a public road (fuel for a bus’s or coach’s passenger air-conditioning included) takes the rate net of the road user charge, every other business use takes the full rate, and a light vehicle on a public road gets nothing. The page adds the lines and gives the whole dollar amount for label 7D on the business activity statement, with the rate, its period and its ATO page for each line.

A small claimant can also enter a line by its cost and the average pump price instead of its litres, split one heavy vehicle’s diesel between public roads and other use by the basic method, and take every line at the tax period’s final rate when an applicable rate changes solely because of indexation. Rates for every period since 1 July 2022 are held here, so an earlier period’s credits can be worked out for an amendment.

## What the fuel tax credit calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether the business is entitled to a credit: you state the facts that settle it. Nor does it decide who acquired the fuel in a hire chain, whether a road is a public road or travel on it is incidental, whether a vehicle meets the environmental criteria of section 41-25, or how fuel is apportioned between uses: you state those. It does not hold the auxiliary equipment percentages of PCG 2016/11, the ATO’s list of heavy vehicles used mainly off public roads, the consumption rates for articulated combinations, or rates for aviation fuel, packaging and supply, domestic heating or electricity generation. It does not work Division 44 adjustments, the attribution of credits to tax periods or the conversion of LPG kilograms to litres. It refuses fuel acquired before 1 July 2022, after 31 January 2027, or outside the income year chosen.

Fuel acquired after 19 November 2026, when the rates were read, carries a warning to check the ATO’s rates page for a change since. Confirm the result with the ATO’s fuel tax credit tools or a registered tax or BAS agent before relying on it.

## How the unused leave calculator works

When employment ends, unused annual leave, leave loading and long service leave are taxed by when the leave accrued and why the employment ended. Leave for employment before 18 August 1993, and all of it from 16 August 1978 on a genuine redundancy, early retirement scheme or invalidity, is capped at 30% and withheld at 32%; it is lump sum A. Only 5% of long service leave for the period before 16 August 1978 is assessable; the whole amount is lump sum B. The rest goes through Schedule 7’s marginal rate calculation with that 5%, spread over a year of pays and withheld at the employee’s Schedule 1 rate, and is reported as unused leave on termination.

## What the unused leave calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employee’s unused leave paid on termination from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, and no advice. It does not cover:

- splitting a payment between the periods by days of service under sections 83-95 to 83-115: enter each part;
- whether a termination is a genuine redundancy, an early retirement scheme or invalidity;
- unused leave paid after an employee’s death;
- a working holiday maker, whose payments Schedule 15 covers;
- an employee who has not claimed the tax-free threshold, or claims a Medicare levy variation or the seniors and pensioners offset (scales 1, 5 and 6), or has a withholding variation;
- a post-17 August 1993 payment under $300 with long service leave from before 16 August 1978, where Schedule 7 does not say how its limit applies;
- employment termination payments, which the redundancy and ETP withholding calculator covers.

Confirm the figures with Schedule 7 or a registered tax agent before relying on them.

## How the franking account calculator works

Each PAYG instalment, income tax payment and franking credit received credits the account on its day, and each franked distribution, refund and other debit takes it down. A deficit at 30 June gives rise to franking deficit tax. When you enter that deficit as next year’s opening balance, the calculator adds the matching credit on 1 July. The tax is also an offset against the company’s income tax, reduced by 30% of the part caused by its own distributions and refunds when that part is more than 10% of the year’s credits. The first distribution in each franking period sets the benchmark: one franked above it gives over-franking tax, one below it an extra debit, and a benchmark that moves too far from the last must be disclosed. A franking credit received is entered as the credit, not the dividend, and the offset is applied only when the income tax is given.

When a later refund changes the deficit tax, use the actual 30 June balance and R&D deferral for next year’s opening figures. In next year’s account, enter the refund on its receipt date and enter the additional franking deficit tax from that refund as a separate credit on that date.

## What the franking account calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one entity’s income year from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026 or from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, and no advice. It does not cover:

- consolidated groups, an entity that stops being a franking entity, New Zealand franking companies, pooled development funds, friendly societies, life insurance companies, exempting entities, a substituted accounting period or late balancer, and diverted profits tax;
- whether a distribution is frankable, what the company may frank it to, or whether a statement given after a distribution was allowed (s 202-75);
- a tax offset refund other than an income tax refund’s R&D part, the general interest charge on late franking tax, and the return’s amounts in whole dollars;
- the company’s tax rate and maximum franking credit from turnover, which the company tax and franking calculator works out.

Confirm the figures with the ATO’s instructions or a registered tax agent before relying on them.
