## How the GST margin scheme calculator works

The GST margin scheme calculator first checks the conditions of section 75-5 of the GST Act that it can decide: what is sold, a written agreement with the buyer, and how you acquired the property. The sale must be a taxable sale (you are registered for GST, you sell in the course of your enterprise, and the sale is not GST-free or input taxed) of a freehold interest in land, a stratum unit or a lease of at least 50 years, and you must not have acquired it with GST on the whole price. The written agreement is made on or before the sale, or within a further period the Commissioner allows.

Under the GST margin scheme the GST is 1/11 of the margin. Subtract the acquisition consideration from the GST-inclusive price (subsection 75-10(2)). A nil or negative margin gives no GST. Leave building and development costs out (section 75-14). You may claim input tax credits on creditable purchases (section 11-5). For a subdivided lot, use only its share of the consideration (section 75-15), measured fairly by area, number of lots or anticipated selling price (GSTR 2006/8, paragraphs 58 to 61).

From a GST-exclusive price the calculator adds GST of one tenth of the price less the consideration, which is 1/11 of the margin on the resulting price (GSTR 2006/8, paragraph 145). Beside the margin scheme it gives the GST without it, 1/11 of the GST-inclusive price, and the difference. Each GST rounds to the nearest cent, half a cent up (section 9-90). The buyer cannot claim an input tax credit on a margin scheme sale (section 75-20), so the scheme suits a buyer who could not claim one anyway.

The calculator reproduces GSTR 2006/8’s worked examples: $20,000 on Bob’s house and land (Example 3), $4,000 on Josine’s 800 square metre lot (Example 4), $13,636.36 on Adam’s Lot 1 (Example 6) and a $100,000 GST-inclusive price from Example 14’s $96,364, here $100,000.40 to the cent.

## Approved valuation dates for GST margin scheme sales

The GST margin scheme calculator checks the date an approved valuation applies to for property held since before 1 July 2000. For a covered sale, use a valuation as at 1 July 2000 if you were registered or required to be registered for GST then. For later registration and unimproved property sold outside government, use the earlier of the effective date and the application date, or the effective date if the Commissioner registered you without an application (subsection 75-10(3) and GSTR 2006/7, paragraphs 60 and 61).

Get separate advice on the GST margin scheme valuation date if you registered or first became required to register after 1 July 2000 and the property already had improvements on that day. Items 2 and 3 of subsection 75-10(3) specify different valuation dates. The calculator also refers government supplies of property unimproved on that day for separate review under item 4. The valuer may prepare the report later than the date the valuation applies to.

## What the GST margin scheme calculator does not cover

The GST margin scheme calculator refuses a sale to your associate, because section 75-13 takes the GST-inclusive market value as the price. It also refuses property inherited, acquired within a GST group or joint venture, as a GST-free going concern, farm land or government land, or from an associate: paragraphs 75-5(3)(b) to (g), item 2A of subsection 75-10(3) and section 75-11 set their own tests and margins. It refuses property acquired partly in a sale with GST on the whole price, which has an increasing adjustment (section 75-22).

Not covered: adjustments for bad debts and for consideration paid later (sections 75-25 and 75-27), the valuation day for government land under item 4 of subsection 75-10(3), and whether the sale is taxable in the first place, such as whether residential premises are new. The calculator takes your valuation as given and does not check that it meets the Commissioner’s requirements for an approved valuation (section 75-35). Have a registered tax agent confirm eligibility and the result before you settle.

## How the GST adjustments calculator works

The calculator works one of 6 GST adjustments or tests for a tax period in a financial year from 2020-21 to 2026-27, with each condition and its authority. For a change in creditable purpose, it finds whether the tax period ending 30 June is an adjustment period: one starting at least 12 months after the tax period the acquisition is attributable to, counted to 1, 2, 5 or 10 periods by the GST exclusive value and whether it relates to business finance. Disposal or loss ends the count early (sections 129-20 and 129-25). No adjustment arises at or below $1,000, or $10,000 for business finance. The full input tax credit times the difference between the intended or former application and the actual application is an increasing or decreasing adjustment (sections 129-70 and 129-75).

Annual apportionment tests eligibility under section 131-5 and compares the credit with the election, the GST on the supply, or that times the non-input-taxed and consideration extents, with the section 11-30 credit; the difference is the section 131-55 increasing adjustment. The financial acquisitions threshold is exceeded when financial acquisitions’ credits exceed $150,000 or 10% of all credits, for the past or the next 12 months (sections 189-5 and 189-10). A going concern is GST-free when all 5 conditions of section 38-325 are met. Second-hand goods give a credit of 1/11 of the consideration, capped above $300 at the GST on your resale (section 66-10). A bad debt gives 1/11 of the amount, reduced for a partly taxable supply or partly creditable acquisition (Division 21 and Subdivision 136-A). Amounts are worked exactly and rounded to the cent once.

## What the GST adjustments calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide the facts. You enter each fact, amount and percentage as established, and it does not judge whether an apportionment method is fair or reasonable. It refuses cars and reduced credit acquisitions for annual apportionment, and farm land and part supplies for going concerns. For second-hand goods it refuses goods divided for resupply, valuable metal and goods leased before sale. For bad debts it refuses the margin scheme and other special rules, reduced credit acquisitions and annual apportionment, and a change of accounting basis. It does not work out the tax period an adjustment or credit is attributable to, substituted or concluding tax periods, importations, or a Commissioner’s determination of fewer adjustment periods. It takes as given, without checking, that the 2 applications leave out changes in applying a thing to financial supplies unless the financial acquisitions threshold is exceeded (section 129-5(2)), that there has been no Division 130 adjustment (section 129-15), that a reduced credit acquisition’s percentages are worked as GSTR 2000/24 paragraphs 63 to 65 direct (section 129-40(3)), and that every member of a GST group is eligible before its representative member elects (section 131-15). Results are planning estimates: check them against your records and the ATO’s guidance.

## How the CGT calculator works

The CGT calculator works out the capital gain or capital loss from one sale (CGT event A1) for an Australian resident individual, trust, complying super fund or company, for a sale contract from 1 July 2020 to 30 June 2027. The sale contract’s date decides the income year and the 12 months held; the cost base adds the purchase price, buying costs, capital improvements, title costs, selling costs and, for an asset bought after 20 August 1991, costs of owning that were not deducted, less capital works deductions.

It then works the gain by each method the owner can use. The discount method halves the gain of an individual or trust, and takes a third off a complying super fund’s, once the asset was held at least 12 months, not counting the day bought or the day sold. The indexation method, for an asset bought before 21 September 1999 (sections 114-1 and 114-10 end it at 11.45 am that day, so a purchase on the day is refused), indexes each cost (except costs of owning) to the September 1999 quarter; a company must use it and gets no discount. Capital losses this year and then net capital losses from earlier years come off the gain before the discount, which is why indexation sometimes wins.

For a home, it counts the days of the ownership period, from settlement to settlement, when the home was not the main residence: before moving in, unless that was as soon as practicable, and each period away that is not treated as the main residence. A period away can be treated as the main residence indefinitely while empty, and for 6 years while rented, the 6 years starting again each time you move back in. The taxable share is those days over the ownership period, plus the floor area used to produce income for the days it was. If the home was first rented or used to produce income after 20 August 1996, was fully exempt until then and is now only partly exempt, section 118-192 treats it as bought then for its market value, and the method follows that date.

The result gives the amounts for question 18 of the supplementary tax return, in whole dollars as the return takes them, and for an individual an estimate of the extra tax and Medicare levy.

## How to enter a sale in the CGT calculator

Enter the dates of the contracts, not of settlement: the sale contract’s date is when the CGT event happens (subsection 104-10(3)). Put stamp duty and legal fees on buying in buying costs, with the date they were incurred; a deposit paid at the contract is part of the purchase price. Put capital improvements, and costs of defending your title, in the 2 cost rows with their dates; add 2 costs incurred in the same quarter together. Selling costs are agent’s commission, advertising and legal fees on the sale. Costs of owning are interest, rates, land tax, insurance and repairs you could not deduct, such as for a home or vacant land. Leave out any cost to the extent you deducted or can deduct it (subsections 110-45(1B) and (2)), except capital works: enter those costs in full and the deductions in their own field. Leave out depreciating assets such as carpets and appliances, which have their own balancing adjustment.

For a home, enter the settlement dates and the day you moved in, then each period you lived elsewhere before the sale. Tick ‘Rented’ for a period it was rented or available for rent, and ‘Choose’ for the periods you treat as your main residence; you cannot treat another home as your main residence for those periods (subsection 118-145(4)). A rented room or a surgery goes in the floor area fields, with the days that use ran; use that continued while you were away stays taxable (subsection 118-190(3)). Enter the market value at first use to produce income in case section 118-192 applies; the result says whether it did.

## What the CGT calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one sale, and no advice. It does not cover:

- sale contracts from 1 July 2027, under Schedule 1 to the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026, and before 1 July 2020;
- foreign and temporary residents, life insurance companies, collectables, personal use assets, and CGT events other than a sale;
- the test of section 115-45 for shares or trust interests held at least 10% with associates, a rented absence that ends on the day the ATO’s examples 72 and 80 count the 6 years apart, use of part of a home to produce income that began while you were away (subsection 118-190(3)), and costs of owning a home when section 118-192 applies;
- other capital gains in the same year, and splitting a parcel of shares between the methods, as example 12 of the ATO’s guide does;
- the small business concessions in Division 152, roll-overs, and the extra discount for affordable housing (section 115-125);
- the 6 months for 2 homes when changing main residences (section 118-140), land over 2 hectares, spouses with different main residences, inherited homes and building on land you owned;
- indexing capital works deductions, which come off at their face amount, capital works deductions on an asset bought before 13 May 1997 and improved after 30 June 1999 (subsection 110-45(1A)), and a substituted accounting period.

Confirm the figure with the ATO or a registered tax agent before relying on it.
