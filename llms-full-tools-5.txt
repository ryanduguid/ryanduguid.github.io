## How the GST calculator works

The GST calculator takes one amount for a wholly taxable supply and works out the GST, the GST-exclusive amount and the GST-inclusive amount. For a wholly taxable amount at 10% GST, exclusive amount / 10 gives GST; inclusive amount / 11 gives its GST component. Each result rounds once to cents.

The 10% rate and the one-eleventh fraction come from ATO: How GST works and the A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999.

## What the GST calculator does not decide

The GST calculator does not decide whether a supply is taxable, whether you must be registered for GST or what a BAS should report. Mixed, GST-free and input-taxed supplies need classification first.

## How the margin and markup calculator works

The margin and markup calculator works out gross profit, margin and markup from one sales figure and one direct cost figure. Gross profit = sales − direct cost. Margin = gross profit / sales × 100. Markup = gross profit / direct cost × 100. A zero denominator leaves that percentage undefined.

## How the break-even calculator works

The break-even sales calculator works out how many whole units, and how much in sales, cover the fixed costs for a period. Contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit. Required units = fixed costs / contribution, rounded up. Required sales = whole units × price. Price must exceed variable cost per unit, or no break-even exists.

No statutory rate or threshold is applied. The definitions follow Business Queensland: break-even and profit.

## What the break-even calculator does not decide

The break-even sales calculator does not forecast demand or say that the units can be sold. It uses one price and one variable cost, so use one product or a stable representative mix and a consistent GST basis.

## How the monthly loan payment calculator works

The monthly loan payment calculator works out a fixed monthly payment, the interest and principal in the first payment, and estimated total interest. For fixed monthly interest r and n payments: payment = principal × r / (1 − (1 + r) raised to −n). At zero interest, payment = principal / n.

No statutory rate or external source is applied. The interest rate is yours, not a market or lender rate.

## What the monthly loan payment calculator leaves out

The monthly loan payment calculator assumes payments in arrears, with no fees or balloon, and a rate that does not change. Total interest uses the unrounded payment and rounds once, so it is an estimate; lender rounding and daily accrual can differ. For $100,000 at 6% over 360 months it gives $115,838.19, where a cent-by-cent schedule of $599.55 payments, with interest rounded each month and a final payment of $600.00, gives $115,838.45. The result is not a lender’s quote.

## How the Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator works

The Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator works out the least a borrower must repay on one amalgamated loan in one income year after the year the loan was made. It applies the formula in subsection 109E(6) of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936: the loan not repaid at the end of the previous income year, times the year’s benchmark interest rate, divided by 1 minus (1 ÷ (1 + the rate)) raised to the remaining term in years.

The interest for the year is the benchmark rate on the whole balance, and the principal is the rest of the repayment. That split assumes the whole repayment is made on the last day of the income year. The ATO works interest out from the daily balance, so a repayment made earlier in the year carries less interest and more of it reduces the principal.

The ATO’s worked example on Loans by private companies takes $55,000 at 5.95% with 7 years remaining and gives $9,835 to the nearest dollar; this calculator gives $9,834.92 for those figures.

## What the Division 7A minimum yearly repayment calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a payment or loan is a Division 7A loan, whether the loan agreement complies, whether a shortfall is a deemed dividend or how much of it, or how a distributable surplus limits one. It works one income year only, with no repayment schedule, and none for the year in which the loan was made. It takes the balance as you enter it: it does not work it out from the loans made, repayments made before the lodgment day or the previous year’s interest, and it does not combine several loans. It does not handle an income year that ends on a day other than 30 June. Subsection 109E(5) lets regulations set another amount, and this calculator applies the formula only. Confirm the figures with the ATO Division 7A calculator and decision tool or a registered tax agent.

## How the 13-week cash forecast works

The 13-week cash forecast works out closing cash for each week and the funding gap against a buffer you set. Weekly closing cash = opening cash + receipts − payments. Each closing balance opens the next week. Funding gap = minimum buffer − lowest opening or weekly closing cash, with a floor of zero.

No statutory rate or threshold is applied. The layout follows business.gov.au: cash flow.

## What the 13-week cash forecast does not decide

The funding gap is a planning gap, not a lending decision. The 13-week cash forecast does not predict receipts or payments, and weekly totals can hide a shortage within the week. A receipt delayed beyond week 13 is reported separately and does not enter the closing balance.

The Lumbridge cash flow case works the same question with a 45-day receipt delay.

## What Ozzit is

An Excel workbook, not a program. Open it, copy a function into your own model, and the formula travels with the file. Every definition is a native LAMBDA stored as a defined name, so the arithmetic stays inside the workbook where a reviewer can read it.

The v3.5.0 workbook carries 148 named formulas: 143 LAMBDA functions and 5 help tables that document them inside the workbook. Each module also ships an editable demonstration worksheet, and the formula source is published as plain text in the repository’s src/ folder and indexed in functions.csv.

The Australian helpers are deliberately small: GST addition and extraction with a 10% default rate, a 1 July financial year label, 5 helpers for the ATO’s small business benchmark ratios, 2 modelling depreciation schedules and 4 AASB 16 lessee schedules. The rest of the library is general financial modelling.

## Who it is for and what it needs

Ozzit suits an accountant or analyst who builds models in Excel and wants the working visible rather than hidden in an add-in or a macro. It is not a bookkeeping tool, and it never touches a ledger.

### Extract GST from an inclusive amount

oz.GSTExtractλ: Returns the GST contained in one or more GST-inclusive amounts.

Signature: GSTExtractλ(Amounts, [Rate]). Square brackets mark optional arguments.

```
=oz.GSTExtractλ(1100)
```

Expected display: 100.00, displayed to two decimal places.

Limits: Assumes the whole amount is taxable at 10%. The function returns an unrounded binary floating-point result. Use ROUND(...,2) where cent rounding is required; the reviewer decides the treatment.

## One worked example

Take $1,100 that is assumed to be wholly taxable and GST-inclusive at 10%. Enter this formula in a cell of the Ozzit workbook:

```
=oz.GSTExtractλ(1100)
```

The result is 100.00. Check: 1100 / 11 = 100.00.

That is the whole of the function’s contribution: 10% arithmetic on an amount you supply.

Whether the supply is wholly taxable, whether a tax invoice supports it and whether the credit is claimable in that period are decisions for the reviewer. The function cannot see the evidence and does not classify the supply.

The 19 October 2026 native Excel record evaluates this formula against the exact v3.5.0 release workbook. It returned 99.999999999999986 before display rounding. The file hash was unchanged after the check. The repository also retains its historical v3.2.0 screenshot record.

## Limits

The functions calculate; a person decides the accounting treatment and signs off.

- The GST helpers apply 10% unless you supply another rate. They do not classify a supply, test registration or prepare a BAS.
- The ATO benchmark ratio helpers restate the ATO’s arithmetic for amounts you supply. They ship no benchmark ranges and do not read a tax return.
- oz.PrimeCostλ and oz.DiminishingValueλ are modelling schedules. They are not ATO Division 40 methods and they do not implement immediate write-off or low-value pools.
- The AASB 16 functions are lessee arithmetic. They do not decide the lease term, what counts as a lease payment or whether an arrangement contains a lease, and they do not cover lessor accounting or the short-term and low-value exemptions.
- The library contains no individual income tax, Medicare levy, Division 7A interest or tax rate cards.
- The 13-week cash flow, practice calculator and retention reconciliation workbooks in the repository’s templates folder are separate templates, not functions in this library.
- Known defects are listed in the repository’s changelog and issues. Check a schedule against an independent calculation before relying on it.

## Common questions

### What is Ozzit?

An Excel workbook containing 143 native LAMBDA functions and 5 help tables for financial modelling under Australian conventions, published free under the Apache License 2.0. It has no add-in, macro or external dependency.

### Which Excel versions can run it?

Microsoft 365 or Excel 2024 or later, which support LAMBDA and dynamic arrays. LibreOffice and earlier Excel versions cannot evaluate the functions.

### Does Ozzit calculate Australian tax?

No. It performs 10% GST arithmetic and labels Australian financial years, and its depreciation and lease functions are modelling schedules rather than ATO or standard-setting methods. It contains no income tax, Medicare levy or Division 7A calculations.

### oz.CountDOWλ

oz.CountDOWλ(Starts, Ends, DayOfWeek)

Count instances of a specific day of the week between two dates.

Parameters:

Starts (required)

One of more start dates (text or numeric) in a row or column

Ends (required)

End dates (text or numeric) for each start date

DayOfWeek (required)

1=Sunday, 7=Saturday.

=oz.CountDOWλ("23/7/2026", "11/8/2026",  1)

### oz.RateAtDateλ

oz.RateAtDateλ(EffectiveDates, Rates, Dates)

Returns the rate in effect on each date from a table of effective dates and rates, refusing gaps, unsorted or repeated dates

Returns the rate in effect on each date from your table of effective dates and rates.

Note: Each rate applies from its effective date until the next one. Dates may be one date or a row or column of them, and the result takes their shape. A date before the first effective date has no rate in effect and returns #N/A. The table must be complete and in order, so instead of a result it returns a message when EffectiveDates and Rates differ in length or hold a blank or text entry, when the dates are not in ascending order, or when a date repeats. A text or blank date in Dates returns #VALUE!. The library ships no rate table: supply your own, with its source and the date you checked it. ScheduleRatesλ() lays rates across a timeline without these checks.

Parameters:

EffectiveDates (required)

A row or column of dates from which each rate applies, in ascending order.

Rates (required)

The rate that applies from each effective date, the same length as EffectiveDates.

Dates (required)

The date, or a row or column of dates, to look up.

```
=oz.RateAtDateλ(DATE(2026, {1;7;10}, 1), {0.04;0.045;0.05}, DATE(2026, 8, 15))
```

### oz.DateDifλ

oz.DateDifλ(StartDate, EndDate, [Unit])

Whole years, months or days between two dates, and the remainders DATEDIF gets wrong

Whole years, months or days between two dates, and the remainders.

Note: Excel documents DATEDIF as a legacy compatibility function and warns its MD unit can return a negative or wrong day count. This counts a month as complete when EDATE() of the start date has arrived, so a month after 31 January is the last day of February, and takes every remainder from there. A reversed range or an unknown unit returns #NUM!.

Parameters:

StartDate (required)

The earlier date, numeric or text.

EndDate (required)

The later date, numeric or text.

Unit (optional, Default = D)

Y whole years, M whole months, D days, YM months left after the whole years, YD days left after the whole years, MD days left after the whole months.

```
=oz.DateDifλ(DATE(2026,1,31), DATE(2026,3,1), "MD")
```
