Announced tax measures not yet law

Fifteen tax and super measures from the 2026-27 Budget and the 2025-26 MYEFO have no bill named on the ATO's list, so no calculator or workpaper should apply them. Three more are enacted but start on 1 July 2027. The $1,000 instant deduction, the instant asset write-off and the tax loss carry-back are law and in force.

Read 20 September 2026 from the ATO's latest news on tax and superannuation law and policy (QC43473, which the ATO last updated 18 September 2026). The status column repeats what the ATO's Developments column says; a measure the ATO marks TBD has no bill, exposure draft or regulation to point to.

Announced, no bill yet

An announcement is not law. Until a bill passes both houses and receives Royal Assent, the existing law applies, and the ATO's guidance on announced measures sets out what happens if a taxpayer anticipates a change that then passes differently or not at all. Fourteen of these rows sat at TBD on the ATO's list when it was read. The MYEFO philanthropy package shows Various instead: several parts at different stages, none with a bill named.

Common questions

Is the $1,000 instant tax deduction law?

Yes. The Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 26 June 2026 and the deduction applies from 1 July 2026, as the ATO's list records.

Is the negative gearing and CGT discount reform law?

Yes, with a later start. The same Tax Reform No. 1 Acts enact it and it starts on 1 July 2027. Nothing changes for the 2026-27 income year.

What does TBD mean on the ATO's list?

The ATO's Developments column names the bill, Act or instrument once one exists. TBD means there is none yet: no exposure draft, no bill and no regulation. Treat the measure as an announcement only.

How often is this page checked?

At the events in the rates register's review cadence: the 1 July rollover, the May Budget, the December MYEFO and each sitting of Parliament, plus any reader report.
