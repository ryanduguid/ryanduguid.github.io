# Individual income tax rates for residents

15% is the first rate above the $18,200 tax-free threshold for 2026-27, down from 16% for 2024-25 and 2025-26.

These are the ATO’s rates for someone who was an Australian resident for tax purposes for the full year and is entitled to the full tax-free threshold. They do not include the 2% Medicare levy. When checked on 14 November 2026 the ATO had published Medicare levy low-income thresholds to 2025-26 only.

Published 14 November 2026. Last reviewed 14 November 2026. Source checked 14 November 2026.

Read on 14 November 2026 from the ATO’s Tax rates: Australian resident page (last updated 13 August 2026), its Medicare levy reduction for low-income earners page (last updated 30 June 2026) and its myTax Medicare levy reduction or exemption instructions for 2020-21 to 2024-25.

## Rates for 2026-27

15% on taxable income from $18,201 to $45,000. Each rate applies only to the income inside its band, so the base amount is the tax on all the income below the band.

Taxable income | Tax on this income
$0 to $18,200 | Nil
$18,201 to $45,000 | 15% of the excess over $18,200
$45,001 to $135,000 | $4,020 plus 30% of the excess over $45,000
$135,001 to $190,000 | $31,020 plus 37% of the excess over $135,000
$190,001 and over | $51,370 plus 45% of the excess over $190,000

## Medicare levy low-income thresholds

The Medicare levy is 2% of taxable income. A single person not entitled to the seniors and pensioners tax offset pays no levy on taxable income at or below the lower threshold and a reduced levy up to the upper threshold. Thresholds for families and for people entitled to that offset are on the ATO pages and are not listed here.

Income year | Lower threshold | Upper threshold
2025-26 | $28,011 | $35,013
2024-25 | $27,222 | $34,027
2023-24 | $26,000 | $32,500
2022-23 | $24,276 | $30,345
2021-22 | $23,365 | $29,206
2020-21 | $23,226 | $29,032

The table lists only thresholds the ATO has published. On 14 November 2026 its Medicare levy reduction page gave thresholds for 2025-26 and no later year, so 2026-27 is not listed. Under the law in force that day the same figures apply to 2026-27: section 3(1) of the Medicare Levy Act 1986, in compilation 53 of 1 July 2026 (the latest on 14 November 2026), sets $28,011 and $35,013, and Schedule 5 to the Treasury Laws Amendment (Delivering an Efficient and Trusted Tax System) Act 2026 (No. 58, 2026) applies them to 2025-26 and later years. That Act received assent on 30 June 2026, the last day of 2025-26, and raised that year’s figures, so a later Act could still change them for 2026-27. The ATO’s myTax instructions for 2019-20 give no threshold table, so that year is not listed.

## Common questions

### What are the resident income tax rates for 2026-27?

Nil up to $18,200, 15% from $18,201 to $45,000, 30% from $45,001 to $135,000, 37% from $135,001 to $190,000 and 45% from $190,001, each on the income in that band. The rates do not include the 2% Medicare levy.

### What changed for 2026-27?

The rate on taxable income from $18,201 to $45,000 fell from 16% to 15%, so the tax on $45,000 fell from $4,288 to $4,020. The band limits did not change.

### What are the 2026-27 Medicare levy low-income thresholds?

Under the law in force on 14 November 2026, $28,011 and $35,013 for a single person not entitled to the seniors and pensioners tax offset, the same as 2025-26. Section 3(1) of the Medicare Levy Act 1986, as compiled on 1 July 2026, sets those figures, and Act No. 58 of 2026 applies them to 2025-26 and later years. The ATO had not published 2026-27 thresholds that day, and a later Act can still change them.

### Do these rates apply to everyone?

No. The ATO gives them for someone who was an Australian resident for tax purposes for the full year and is entitled to the full tax-free threshold. Check the ATO’s guidance for any other case.
