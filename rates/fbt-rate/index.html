FBT rate and gross-up rates

The fringe benefits tax rate is 47% for the FBT year ending 31 March 2027, as it has been for every year since the one ending 31 March 2018. The Type 1 gross-up rate is 2.0802 and the Type 2 rate is 1.8868. The FBT year runs from 1 April to 31 March.

Verified 20 September 2026 against the ATO's FBT rates and thresholds page (last updated 20 May 2026) for the years ending 31 March 2023 to 2027, and its historical rates and thresholds page (last updated 1 April 2026) for the years ending 31 March 2018 to 2022. The rate is set by the Fringe Benefits Tax Assessment Act 1986.

Type 1 applies where the provider is entitled to a GST credit for providing the benefit; Type 2 applies where it is not. Reportable fringe benefits on an employee's income statement are always grossed up at the Type 2 rate. How the rate and the gross-up fit together

FBT is charged on the grossed-up taxable value, not the taxable value itself. The gross-up converts the benefit to the pre-tax salary that would have bought it, so the 47% rate then produces the same tax as the top marginal rate plus Medicare levy. A $10,000 Type 2 benefit grosses up to $18,868 and attracts $8,867.96 of FBT.

Common questions

What is the FBT rate for the year ending 31 March 2027?

47%. The ATO lists the same rate for the FBT years ending 31 March 2023 through 2027.

What are the FBT gross-up rates?

Type 1 is 2.0802 and Type 2 is 1.8868, unchanged for every FBT year ending 31 March 2018 to 2027.

When does the FBT year start and end?

It runs from 1 April to 31 March, so the year ending 31 March 2027 began on 1 April 2026.

Which gross-up rate applies to reportable fringe benefits?

The lower Type 2 rate, whether or not the benefit itself was Type 1. The reporting threshold is a taxable value above $2,000, which grosses up to $3,773.
