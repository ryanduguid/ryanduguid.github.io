## How the GST calculator works

The GST calculator takes one amount for a wholly taxable supply and works out the GST, the GST-exclusive amount and the GST-inclusive amount. For a wholly taxable amount at 10% GST, exclusive amount / 10 gives GST; inclusive amount / 11 gives its GST component. Each result rounds once to cents.

The 10% rate and the one-eleventh fraction come from ATO: How GST works and the A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999.

## What the GST calculator does not decide

The GST calculator does not decide whether a supply is taxable, whether you must be registered for GST or what a BAS should report. Mixed, GST-free and input-taxed supplies need classification first.

# Margin and markup calculator

Sales (AUD) Direct cost (AUD)

Calculate Fictional example: the first boundary case below. Change the inputs and calculate your own. Inputs: sales $100.00, direct cost $80.00. Gross profit $20.00 Margin 20.00% Markup 25.00%

## How the margin and markup calculator works

The margin and markup calculator works out gross profit, margin and markup from one sales figure and one direct cost figure. Gross profit = sales − direct cost. Margin = gross profit / sales × 100. Markup = gross profit / direct cost × 100. A zero denominator leaves that percentage undefined.

No statutory rate or threshold is applied. Gross profit and margin follow Business Queensland: break-even and profit; markup follows Business Queensland: pricing products and services.

## What the margin and markup calculator does not decide

The margin and markup calculator does not set a price, test whether direct cost is complete or adjust for GST. Use a consistent GST basis and matching period for both figures.

## How the break-even calculator works

The break-even sales calculator works out how many whole units, and how much in sales, cover the fixed costs for a period. Contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit. Required units = fixed costs / contribution, rounded up. Required sales = whole units × price. Price must exceed variable cost per unit, or no break-even exists.

No statutory rate or threshold is applied. The definitions follow Business Queensland: break-even and profit.

## What the break-even calculator does not decide

The break-even sales calculator does not forecast demand or say that the units can be sold. It uses one price and one variable cost, so use one product or a stable representative mix and a consistent GST basis.

## How the fixed fee pricing calculator works

The fixed fee pricing calculator turns an estimate of hours into a fee you can quote before the work starts. Enter your hourly rate with a 0% margin, or your cost per hour with the margin you want the fee to carry: fee = hours × rate / (1 − margin). A margin is a share of the fee, so a 40% margin on a $95 cost is $158.33 an hour, not the $133 a 40% markup would give. The fee then rounds up to the cent or to the next $10, $50 or $100, so rounding never takes it below your rate or margin.

GST at 10% is added to the fee when you tick the box, and the fee including GST is split into monthly instalments. Each instalment is the total divided evenly and rounded down to the cent; the first instalment also carries the cents left over, at most one cent for each instalment after it. The instalments add up to the fee to the cent, every instalment after the first is the same amount for a recurring payment, and if the engagement ends early the client has paid at least an even share of the fee for each month paid. The effective hourly rate is the fee before GST over the hours, so you can see what rounding and the margin earn an hour.

## What the fixed fee pricing calculator leaves out

The fixed fee pricing calculator prices one piece of work. For several services or entities, price each and add the fees. It does not suggest a market rate, allow for work beyond the hours you estimated, or decide whether your supply is taxable: add GST only if you are registered for GST and the supply is taxable.

## How the loan repayment schedule calculator works

The calculator sets out a loan payment by payment, weekly, fortnightly, monthly, quarterly or yearly. Give any 3 of the regular payment, the amount financed, the number of payments and the annual nominal interest rate, with the balloon, and it works out the fourth. Each row shows the date, the balance before the payment, the payment, the interest since the last payment, the principal repaid and the balance after it.

The period rate is the annual nominal rate divided by the payments a year: 52 for weekly and 26 for fortnightly, so a year of weekly payments is taken as 52 weeks. Payments in arrears fall at the end of each period, the first one period after the start date. Payments in advance fall at the start, the first on the start date, so the first carries no interest. A balloon, or residual, is due at the end of the last period: with the last payment in arrears, or a period after it in advance. It can be an amount or a percentage of the amount financed, rounded half up to the cent.

Each period’s interest is rounded to the cent, so the last payment differs from the others by the cents that rounding leaves. In advance, the last payment leaves the balloon’s value a period before it falls due, rounded to the cent, and the balloon row’s interest is the rest of the balloon. The payment is the same as a spreadsheet’s PMT with the balloon as the future value and type 1 for advance.

The amount financed is the payments and balloon discounted at the rate, rounded half up to the cent. The number of payments is found by running the payment until one would take the balance to the balloon; that payment is the last and is smaller. The interest rate is found by bisection between 0% and 100% a year: halving 48 times leaves it within 0.0000000000004% a year of the rate at which the payments and balloon, discounted, equal the amount financed. The page refuses payments and a balloon that add up to less than the amount financed, which no rate of nil or more fits, payments that would need more than 100% a year, and a payment that never covers a period’s interest.

Download schedule CSV saves the inputs, totals and every row as plain figures.

## What the loan repayment schedule calculator leaves out

The calculator assumes a rate that does not change, payments on the same day of each period and no fees. It does not work out GST, stamp duty or the tax treatment of the interest or the asset. Lenders that accrue interest daily, or count a weekly year as 365 days, give slightly different figures. The result is not a lender’s quote.

## How the hire purchase and chattel mortgage schedule works

The calculator sets out a hire purchase agreement or a chattel mortgage payment by payment from the amount financed, weekly to yearly, in arrears or in advance, with any balloon or residual. Give any 3 of the regular payment, the amount financed, the number of payments and the annual nominal rate, and it works out the fourth. Under Division 240 the interest in each payment is the hirer’s notional interest at the implicit rate; working out the rate from the agreement’s payments finds that rate.

The calculator totals the interest, the finance charge, by income year from 1 July to 30 June, and sets out a journal for each payment: debit the liability with the principal, debit interest expense with the interest and credit the bank with the payment. The balloon has its own line. Download schedule CSV saves the schedule, the income years and the journal as plain figures.

The australian-accounting command hire-purchase-schedule works the same agreement monthly. It keeps every instalment as the agreement states it and puts the cents of rounding into each month’s interest, and it lists a month that runs across 30 June apart rather than sharing it. This page keeps the interest of each period rounded on its own and puts the cents into the last payment, so on the same agreement the two can differ by a few cents.

## What the hire purchase and chattel mortgage schedule does not cover

The schedule does not work out GST, the GST credit or the car limit: it starts from the amount financed. Record the asset at its cost and the liability at the amount financed when the agreement starts; a deposit, trade-in, fees and stamp duty are yours to add. It does not state the income tax treatment of a chattel mortgage, which the sources applied do not cover, or of an early payout. It assumes a rate that does not change and payments on the same day of each period.

Leases are not covered: a lessee’s lease under AASB 16 is not worked here. Confirm the treatment with the agreement, the financier’s statements and a registered tax agent.

## How the AASB 16 lease calculator works

The calculator measures a lessee’s lease under AASB 16 at commencement and sets out each income year to the end of the lease. The lease liability is the payments not yet paid, with any residual value guarantee you expect to pay or purchase option you are reasonably certain to exercise, discounted at the rate you enter: the rate implicit in the lease or, if that cannot be readily determined, your incremental borrowing rate. A payment in advance falls on the commencement date, so it goes into the right-of-use asset’s cost, not the liability. The asset’s cost adds payments made before commencement, less incentives received, initial direct costs and the estimated cost to dismantle, remove or restore.

Each period’s interest is the period rate on the liability. The schedule sets out every payment’s interest and principal, which are the journal for that payment, and the income year table gives each year’s interest, payments, straight-line depreciation and the liability and asset at 30 June. Depreciation is an equal amount each payment period over the lease term, or each month over the useful life where ownership transfers. Download schedule CSV saves the tables as plain figures.

With the comparison on, the page puts the same asset under hire purchase at your rate and bought outright, beside the lease, for each income year to the year the lease term ends: cash paid with GST, GST credits, deductions, the tax they save at your rate and the net cost. The lease deducts its payments when they are paid. Hire purchase deducts its notional interest and, like buying, the asset’s decline in value. The tax value left at the end is what can still be deducted after it: the residual if a lease buys the asset, and the asset’s adjustable value otherwise.

## What the AASB 16 lease calculator does not cover

The calculator does not decide whether a contract is or contains a lease, the lease term, the discount rate or whether a purchase option is reasonably certain. It flags a short-term lease but not a lease of a low-value asset, which paragraph 5(b) also lets the lessee expense. It does not remeasure a lease: a change in an index or rate, a lease modification, a reassessed term or option, an early payout, or variable payments that do not depend on an index or rate. It does not separate non-lease components, apply the revaluation or fair value models, impairment, a sale and leaseback, a lessor’s accounting, not-for-profit concessionary leases or deferred tax.

The comparison does not discount the cash flows, value the asset at the end of the term, apply the prepayment rules to a payment in advance, the luxury car lease rules (it refuses a car above the car limit), small business simplified depreciation or an instant asset write-off, or an asset first used after the commencement date. It takes GST credits on the payment dates rather than at the end of each tax period, and the tax saved at 30 June of each year. Confirm the treatment with the agreement, the financier’s statements and a registered tax agent or auditor.

## How the rental property calculator works

The rental property calculator works out one owner’s rental income and deductions for one residential property and one income year, on the lines of the ATO’s rental property worksheet (Rental properties guide 2026, Example 33). Rent and other rental income are assessable (section 6-5). Co-owners who are not in a business of letting share income and expenses by legal interest, whatever they agree between them (TR 93/32), so the calculator takes the whole property’s amounts and multiplies them by your interest. Your loans are entered as your own and are not divided.

An expense of the property for the whole time you held it in the year, such as rates, insurance or repairs, is apportioned by the days it was let or available for rent on commercial terms over the days you held it. An expense solely for renting, such as agent commission or advertising for tenants, is not (the guide’s time-based method and Example 34). Travel is shown but not deducted: section 26-31 denies it to an individual not in a business of letting from 1 July 2017. Interest on a loan used partly for the rental is the interest times the principal used for the rental over the total principal, then the time-based share (TR 2000/2 paragraphs 15 and 16).

Capital works are 2.5% of the construction expenditure a year, or 4% for a building begun from 18 July 1985 to 15 September 1987 or taken to have begun before 16 September 1987 under section 43-220, times the days used over 365. The deduction is never more than the construction expenditure not yet deducted (sections 43-25, 43-210 and 43-240). Each asset’s decline in value is taken as you enter it, at your interest and the time-based share, and is nil where section 40-27 denies it for a second-hand asset or one used privately. The borrowing expenses line is the year’s figure from the borrowing expenses calculator. Each line is rounded to the cent, where the guide’s examples round to the dollar. The calculator reproduces Example 33: deductions of $20,193.33 and a net rent of −$10,893.33, the guide’s $20,629 less the $436 of travel its Note 2 says needs an exception.

Given your other taxable income for 2024-25 to 2026-27, the calculator works out the tax at the resident scale with and without the net rent, its cents dropped as a return shows them, and the difference.

## Choices the rental property calculator makes

The calculator makes 2 counting choices the ATO gives no worked example for, as the rental-property command of the australian-accounting calculators does. The use period of capital works counts both its first and last day (section 43-230(2)). The time-based share applies to a decline in value you enter, as it does to the other lines. The 4% rate for a residential building begun from 18 July 1985 rests on the guide’s Table 1, not on a provision read. A building begun from 22 August 1979 to 17 July 1985 qualifies only if it was intended on completion for short-term traveller accommodation or non-residential use, so the calculator refuses it rather than give nil.

## What the rental property calculator does not cover

The calculator works the income years 2020-21 to 2026-27 and refuses 2027-28, from which section 26-155 can limit residential rental deductions. It refuses a holiday home whose costs section 26-50 denies (TR 2026/1), a loan redrawn or drawn further in the year (TR 2000/2 paragraphs 23 to 25), an asset acquired on 9 May 2017, the day section 40-27 starts at 7.30 pm, and the effect on tax before 2024-25. It also refuses works or an asset first used after you first held the property in a year it was not available every day, as the days of rental use are then not known.

Not covered: a business of letting; a company, trust or other entity; letting part of a dwelling or at less than market rent; GST on short stays; the 4% rate for build to rent or short-term traveller accommodation; vacant land under section 26-102; a tax loss under Division 36; and the Medicare levy, the surcharge, offsets and study loan repayments. The calculator never derives construction expenditure from a price or a valuation. A value or total of $70,368,744,177,664 or more is refused, as the page cannot show it to the cent. Have a registered tax agent confirm the result before you lodge.
