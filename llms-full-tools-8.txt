## How the take-home pay calculator works

The take-home pay calculator takes one salary for a year, month, fortnight or week and works out the year’s income tax, offsets, Medicare levy, any Medicare levy surcharge and any study or training loan repayment on it. Salary sacrificed to super comes off first. From 2026-27 it also takes off the standard deduction of up to $1,000 for work-related expenses. It then gives what is left for the year, a month, a fortnight and a week, and the super guarantee an employer must pay, which goes to a super fund and is not part of take-home pay.

Those take-home amounts are the year’s tax spread evenly: the year’s net amount divided by 12, 26 and 52. They are not the amounts an employer withholds each pay. For a weekly, fortnightly or monthly pay, the calculator also works the PAYG amount withheld from one pay under the year’s Schedule 1, and Schedule 8 with a study loan, and the year’s withholding less the year’s tax, which is the refund or amount owing when the tax return is assessed. Withholding does not allow for the low and middle income tax offset, so in 2021-22 the offset comes back as part of the refund: $3,000 a fortnight has $670 withheld each pay and $1,543 back. For working holiday makers, who are withheld from under another schedule, use the ATO’s tax withheld calculator.

The example on arrival is 2025-26, before the standard deduction: on $75,000 the Medicare levy is $1,500, as the ATO’s example of Matthew on What is the Medicare levy gives. In 2026-27 the same salary is $74,000 of taxable income.

When the salary includes super, the calculator splits it: at 12%, a package of $112,000 is $100,000 of salary and $12,000 of super. Super guarantee stops at the year’s maximum contribution base, so above it the super stays at the most the base allows.

## What the take-home pay calculator does not compute

The calculator assumes one employer, a salary paid evenly over the full year, and no other income, deductions or offsets. It does not compute:

- overtime, bonuses, allowances or leave loading, a 53rd weekly or 27th fortnightly pay, or withholding on scale 1, scale 5 or 6 or for a working holiday maker, a withholding declaration’s offsets or a Medicare levy variation;
- 2025-26 withholding with a study loan that mixes the 2 Schedule 8s: the year’s withholding and refund take the one from 24 September 2025 for every pay, and the component before that date is shown beside it;
- work-related deductions you claim, which reduce the standard deduction dollar for dollar except those section 25-130(3) disregards, such as professional association fees;
- salary sacrifice of anything but super, reportable fringe benefits, excess concessional contributions or Division 293 tax;
- everything the income tax calculator leaves out, such as the Medicare levy reduction for families and the seniors and pensioners tax offset;
- a family’s surcharge: this calculator works out the surcharge for a single person with no dependants, and the income tax calculator works out a family’s;
- a study loan debt smaller than the repayment, which section 154-20(1)(c) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003 caps at what is still owed;
- whether an employee is entitled to super guarantee, the lower transitional rate for employees on Norfolk Island (11% for 2026-27), or a second employer’s separate maximum contribution base;
- super guarantee pay by pay: the calculator works it on the year’s earnings, rounded once, where an employer works it on each payday from 2026-27, so the year’s total can differ by cents, and to 2025-26 on each quarter’s earnings, so above the base a weekly or fortnightly salary with more pays in some quarters gets less: $260,000 paid fortnightly in 2025-26 can give $29,400, not $30,000.

Confirm the figures with the ATO’s Simple tax calculator or a registered tax agent.

## How the lump sum in arrears calculator works

Salary, wages or a Centrelink or compensation payment that you receive as a lump sum, for earlier years, is taxed in the year you receive it, often at a higher rate than it would have been in the years it was for. The tax offset gives back the difference. The calculator first tests whether the arrears are at least 10% of the rest of your normal taxable income for the year. If they are, it works out the tax the arrears add this year and the tax they would have added in the years they accrued in, and the offset is the amount by which the first exceeds the second.

For the 2 latest accrual years it adds the arrears to that year’s taxable income at that year’s resident rates. Arrears from any earlier years are taxed at the average of those 2 years’ rates, so the oldest years’ arrears can share one row. Where a recent year was itself a year you got this offset for, or arrears of such a year accrued in it, its income is adjusted as the Act directs.

The ATO’s page on lump sum payments in arrears works Yan’s breakdown of arrears by year, received in 2025-26. The calculator opens on it; the ATO does not give Yan’s taxable incomes, so the $100,000, $30,000 and $17,000 are fictional. Anthony’s, Sudeep’s and Heather’s examples are among the boundary cases.

## What the lump sum in arrears calculator does not cover

The calculator works the offset for a person resident in Australia for the whole of the year of receipt and of each recent accrual year, entitled to the full tax-free threshold, for a year of receipt of 2024-25, 2025-26 or 2026-27. It does not decide what is a lump sum in arrears: it takes the arrears you enter as eligible income that accrued more than 12 months before you received it. It refuses an employment termination payment, unused leave paid on termination, a super lump sum or income stream, above-average special professional income or primary producer averaging in the year of receipt or a recent accrual year, and does not cover a minor’s income or a trustee.

It does not work out the Medicare levy or surcharge on the arrears, which have their own rules, or apply the offset against your tax. The ATO works the offset when you lodge with the amounts for each year; confirm the figures with the ATO or a registered tax agent.

## How the study and training loan repayment calculator works

The study and training loan repayment calculator works out the compulsory repayment on a HELP, VET Student Loan, Student Start-up Loan or Australian Apprenticeship Support Loan debt for one income year. It first adds up repayment income: taxable income, reportable fringe benefits, total net investment loss, reportable super contributions and exempt foreign employment income.

From 2025-26 the repayment is marginal: nothing up to the minimum repayment income, then 15 cents for each dollar above it up to a second threshold and 17 cents above that, unless 10% of all repayment income is less. For 2026-27 the minimum repayment income is $69,528 and the second threshold $129,717, and 10% is the lesser amount from $186,051. For 2024-25 one percentage of the whole repayment income applied, from 1% above $54,434 to 10% from $159,664.

The boundary cases below take the inputs of the 4 worked examples on the ATO’s thresholds and rates page, read 17 November 2026, and work each one from section 154-20. For Barry’s 2026-27 repayment income of $137,064, section 154-20(2) gives 15% of the $60,189 from $69,528 to $129,717, which is $9,028.35, plus 17% of the $7,347 above $129,717, which is $1,248.99: $10,277.34 in all. For Branson’s 2024-25 repayment income of $99,736, the 5.5% rate gives $5,485.48.

## What the study and training loan repayment calculator does not compute

The calculator gives the compulsory repayment for one income year. It does not compute:

- the cap at the debt you still owe, or voluntary repayments;
- the overseas debtors repayment levy on worldwide income for a foreign resident (section 154-16);
- whether no repayment is due because your Medicare levy is not payable or is reduced under section 8 of the Medicare Levy Act 1986 (section 154-1(2));
- the amounts an employer withholds each pay for a loan, which the take-home pay calculator works for 2026-27, or indexation of the debt;
- years before 2020-21.

Confirm the figure with the ATO’s Study and training loan repayment calculator or a registered tax agent.

## How the sole trader tax set-aside calculator works

The calculator takes a year’s business income and expenses, any wages from a job and any tax agent fees, gifts or personal super contributions you claim. It works out the year’s income tax, the offsets, the Medicare levy, any Medicare levy surcharge and any study or training loan repayment. It then works the same on the wages alone. The difference is the tax the business adds, which nobody withholds, so it is the amount to set aside from business income.

The small business income tax offset is worked as section 328-360 sets it: 16% of the share of the tax on taxable income that the business profit makes up. On arrival the example shows it below its $1,000 cap: $20,000 of profit is 20/79 of $79,000 of taxable income, so the offset is 16% of 20/79 of $14,220, which is $576.00. The business also costs the low income tax offset the wages alone would have had, $115.00, which the difference picks up.

A business registered for GST also sets aside the GST on its income less its GST credits, paid with each activity statement. The calculator gives one share to keep from every invoice, covering both, and the year’s amount split by quarter, month and week. It also gives the tax as a share of the business income excluding GST, which as a PAYG instalment rate would cover this year’s tax on it.

## What the sole trader tax set-aside calculator does not compute

The calculator is for an Australian resident aged 18 or over with one business as a sole trader, a full year of income and no dependants. It does not compute:

- a business loss, which the non-commercial loss rules in Division 35 may defer, or losses carried forward;
- personal services income rules, which can deny deductions: see the PSI rules checker;
- GST-free or input-taxed sales, GST on a cash or accruals basis by period, or a GST refund when credits exceed the GST on income;
- other income such as interest, dividends, rent or capital gains, or partnership and trust distributions, which change both the tax and the offset’s share, or reportable fringe benefits and salary sacrificed to super from the job, which count in repayment income and income for surcharge purposes;
- work-related deductions against the wages, which reduce the standard deduction from 2026-27, or a job withheld from at the wrong rate;
- super guarantee, which a sole trader does not pay for themselves, or personal super contributions beyond entering them as a deduction;
- a family’s Medicare levy, levy reduction or surcharge, or the seniors and pensioners tax offset;
- notional tax or the instalment rate the ATO sets, or when each instalment and activity statement is due;
- a study loan debt smaller than the repayment, which section 154-20(1)(c) of the Higher Education Support Act 2003 caps at what is still owed;
- 2027-28, whose working Australians tax offset (Subdivision 61-E, added by Act No. 49 of 2026) applies from that year.

## How the income tests calculator works

The income tests calculator adds up the 6 income measures that the income tests section of an individual tax return feeds, from the same amounts. Each measure starts from taxable income without any assessable first home super saver released amount, then adds the parts its own definition names.

- Income for surcharge purposes, for the Medicare levy surcharge and the private health insurance rebate, adds family trust distribution amounts, fringe benefits, reportable super contributions and investment losses.
- Division 293 income, for the extra 15% tax when it and low tax contributions together exceed $250,000, is the same less reportable super contributions.
- Rebate income, for the seniors and pensioners tax offset, adds super contributions, investment losses and the adjusted fringe benefits total.
- Repayment income, for study and training loans, adds investment losses, fringe benefits, exempt foreign employment income and super contributions.
- Adjusted taxable income, for the dependant (invalid and invalid carer) tax offset, adds adjusted fringe benefits, target foreign income, investment losses, tax-free pensions and super contributions, less child support paid.
- Co-contribution total income starts from assessable income instead, adds fringe benefits and employer contributions and takes off business deductions.

## What the income tests calculator does not cover

The calculator works the measures only. ## How the super projection calculator works

The super projection calculator projects one accumulation super account from 2026-27 to the age you retire, a year at a time, and gives the balance then in future dollars and at its present value, in the dollars of today’s wages. Each year your employer contributes its rate of your salary, up to the maximum contribution base, and your own contributions are added. The fund takes 15% contributions tax from the employer and salary sacrifice contributions, takes out the dollar fee, the percentage fee and the premiums, and claims them as a deduction, which saves 15% of them. The return is earned on the balance at the start of the year and on half the year’s contributions less fees, as they come in through the year. After the first year, your salary, contributions, fees and premiums grow at the wage growth rate.

The present value is the balance at retirement divided by 1.037 for each year projected, so that it reads against today’s wages. The calculator is general information about super, not advice: it shows the effect of the figures you enter and does not recommend a fund, an investment option or a contribution.

## The assumptions and why they are reasonable

- Retirement at 67: the default ASIC’s Instrument 2022/603 sets for anyone younger. Super is preserved until 60, so an earlier age is not accepted.
- Today’s dollars at 3.7% a year: the rate the Instrument sets, the long-term growth in wages the Treasury’s 2023 Intergenerational Report expects, so the result compares with what wages buy now.
- Wage growth of 3.7% a year for your salary, contributions, fees and premiums: the same long-term forecast, as ASIC’s guide expects contributions to rise with wages.
- A return of 6.1% a year after investment fees and tax on earnings, steady every year: ASIC’s assumption for a balanced investment option. Real returns vary and can be negative in some years; your fund’s option may have a different mix.
- Administration fees of $59 a year and 0.11% of the balance, and insurance premiums of $599 a year: ASIC’s assumptions from industry statistics. Your statement shows your own.
- Employer contributions at the 12% super guarantee on salary up to the $270,830 maximum contribution base for 2026-27, which is assumed to grow with wages.
- 15% contributions tax and the fund’s deduction for fees and premiums, as the law now stands; tax and super law are assumed not to change.
- Contributions within the 2026-27 caps, with the caps assumed to rise with wages after that.

## What the super projection calculator does not compute

The projection covers one accumulation account from 2026-27, with the rules of that year assumed to continue. It does not compute:

- defined benefit interests, or an account already paying a pension or a transition to retirement income stream;
- income in retirement, the age pension or how long the balance would last;
- the low income super tax offset, the government co-contribution or spouse contributions, which would add to the balance of someone on a lower income;
- Division 293 tax for income over $250,000, excess contributions, the nil non-concessional cap for a total super balance at the transfer balance cap, or tax on a withdrawal;
- years of good and bad returns, career breaks, or contribution, activity, switching and adviser fees, any of which can lower the balance.

Use your fund’s own retirement estimate or a financial adviser for a forecast you would act on.

## How the payroll tax calculator works

The payroll tax calculator estimates one employer’s payroll tax for the whole of 2026-27 in one state or territory. Each jurisdiction allows a deduction or tax-free threshold, which an employer that also pays wages elsewhere in Australia shares in proportion to its wages there. Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia and the Northern Territory reduce the deduction as Australian wages grow, until it reaches nil. The result shows the deduction, the amount taxed, the tax at each rate and, in Victoria and Queensland, each surcharge or levy on its own line, with the provision applied.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the tests hold them. Revenue NSW: $1,500,000 of NSW wages are taxed on $300,000, and $900,000 of $3,000,000 has a threshold of $360,000. The Victorian State Revenue Office: $80 million in Victoria and $70 million in New South Wales give surcharge thresholds of $5,333,333.33 and $53,333,333.33. The Queensland Revenue Office: $1,356,789 of $1,789,776 has a deduction of $932,460 to the dollar. RevenueWA: annual wages of $1,104,000 give $6,600; its other 2 examples round the deduction to the dollar and come out 1 and 3 cents lower than this calculator. RevenueSA: $800,000 of $1,600,000 has a deduction of $300,000 and $500,000 of $2,000,000 one of $150,000. The Tasmanian State Revenue Office: $2,400,000 gives $54,400, and $2,400,000 of $3,000,000 gives $72,800. The ACT Revenue Office and the Territory Revenue Office publish no worked figures that could be read, so the tests work examples from the Acts by hand: $84,375 on $3,000,000 in the Australian Capital Territory and $41,250 on $3,000,000 in the Northern Territory. This calculator opens on Revenue NSW’s first example.

## What the payroll tax calculator does not compute

The payroll tax calculator gives one annual estimate and no advice. It does not decide which payments are taxable wages or where they are taxable, and does not cover:

- an employer that is a member of a group, whose threshold and rate depend on the group’s wages and which members share the deduction;
- an employer that paid wages for only part of 2026-27, whose threshold every jurisdiction reduces by the days it employed;
- monthly or other periodic returns, and the annual reconciliation of amounts already paid;
- exemptions, rebates and discounts, such as Queensland’s apprentice and trainee rebate and its 1% discount for regional employers, claimed in the return, and the ACT’s 6.85% cap for an eligible university;
- any year other than 2026-27.

Confirm the figure with the revenue office or a registered tax agent before relying on it.

## How the SGC and Payday Super calculator works

From 1 July 2026, under Payday Super, an employer owes super guarantee for each payday, and the employee’s fund must receive it within 7 business days, or a longer period for a first contribution, out-of-cycle pay or an ATO determination. The calculator finds that due date from the site’s business days calendar, then works out the super guarantee charge for one employee if the fund received less than 12% of qualifying earnings by then: the final shortfall still unpaid at the assessment, notional earnings at the GIC rate on the amount that was late, and the administrative uplift, reduced if the ATO had not itself assessed or estimated the employer’s SG charge from 1 July 2026 to the payday, and for a voluntary disclosure. A contribution counts from the day the fund received it with what it needs to allocate it to the employee. For a quarter that ended by 30 June 2026 it applies the old law instead: the shortfall at that year’s charge percentage, 10% nominal interest and the $20 administration component.

## What the SGC and Payday Super calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employee and one payday or quarter, and no advice. It does not cover:

- the choice loading for not meeting choice of fund obligations;
- the late payment offset under the old section 23A for quarterly contributions made late;
- bunched contributions under section 18C(2) item 4;
- a contribution made from 1 to 28 July 2026 that item 186 of the transition applies first to a shortfall for the quarter ending 30 June 2026: count only what is left of it;
- a disclosed contribution with no receipt day, whose notional earnings section 36(3) works out as if the fund received it 7 business days after it was paid;
- exemption certificates, and amounts paid that have not reached the fund;
- GIC and penalties once the charge is payable;
- paydays after 30 June 2027, whose maximum contributions base is not yet known, and quarters before July 2022.

Confirm the figure with the ATO or a registered tax agent before relying on it.
