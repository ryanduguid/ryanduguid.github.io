## How the foreign buyer surcharge calculator works

The calculator applies the chosen state or territory’s own test of who is foreign to your answers, once for its duty surcharge and once for its land tax surcharge, as each revenue office states them. The duty surcharge is its rate times the dutiable value of the buyer’s share: 9% in New South Wales, 8% in Victoria, Queensland and Tasmania (1.5% on Tasmanian primary production land), and 7% in Western Australia and South Australia. The ACT and the Northern Territory charge none.

The land tax surcharge is for a full land tax year on the land value you enter: 5% in New South Wales, 4% in Victoria from $50,000 of taxable land ($25,000 for a trust), 3% of the value over $350,000 in Queensland, 2% in Tasmania and 0.75% in the ACT. Western Australia and South Australia charge none, and the Northern Territory has no land tax. Victoria charges a fixed trust’s surcharge on the land held for absentee beneficiaries only. The general transfer duty is worked on the whole property, for all buyers together, at the general rates.

The calculator reproduces the revenue offices’ examples: Revenue NSW’s $90,000 of surcharge purchaser duty and $39,187 of transfer duty on $1 million, and $60,000 of surcharge land tax on $1.2 million; Victoria’s $80,000 for Andrew, $16,000 for Emily’s $200,000 share and $20,000 for Clara’s half of a fixed trust’s $1 million; RevenueSA’s $42,000 and $21,000 for Peter; Western Australia’s $14,000 for Simon; Queensland’s $1,500 on $400,000; and Tasmania’s $8,000 on $400,000.

## Who is foreign in each state and territory

An individual who is an Australian citizen, holds a permanent visa or is a New Zealand citizen on a special category visa is not foreign for Queensland, Western Australian, South Australian and Tasmanian duty. New South Wales also requires a permanent resident or New Zealand citizen to have been in Australia 200 days of the previous 12 months (Revenue Ruling G009); Victoria gives a New Zealand citizen no exception unless they ordinarily reside in Australia for 6 consecutive months around settlement. Victorian and Queensland land tax charge absentees: non-citizens without a permanent visa who do not ordinarily reside in Australia and were away on the day or for over 6 months of the year.

A company is foreign when it is incorporated outside Australia, or foreign persons with associates hold 50% or more (more than 50% in Victoria). New South Wales instead asks whether one foreign person with associates holds 20% or more, or foreign persons together 40% or more, wherever the company is incorporated. Trusts and super funds follow the same shares of capital or members’ balances, except that Victoria charges absentee owner surcharge on a fixed trust with any absentee beneficiary and none on a complying super fund.

A discretionary trust is foreign in New South Wales if a foreign person could benefit, now or by amending the deed (section 104JA); in Victoria and Tasmania if foreign persons could be given more than 50%, or 50% or more, of the capital; in Queensland if foreign takers in default hold 50% or more; in Western Australia if a foreign person controls it or foreign takers in default hold 50% or more; in South Australia if a trustee, appointor, named object or taker in default is foreign; and in the ACT only if a named foreign beneficiary could receive 50% or more. Victorian land tax looks only at named (specified) beneficiaries who are absentees.

## What the foreign buyer surcharge calculator leaves to you

The calculator does not decide exemptions, refunds or ex gratia relief, which each Commissioner grants on application. Among them are Australian-based developers and build to rent in New South Wales and Victoria, exempt permanent residents who live in the home in New South Wales, significant contributors and large developers in Queensland, and refunds when a buyer stops being foreign within 12 months in South Australia. When one is sought, it refuses. It also refuses a partner (provisional) or retirement visa holder, whom the revenue offices treat differently, and does not trace interests through other companies and trusts or decide who is an associate.

Not covered: the Commonwealth’s foreign investment application fees and its restrictions on foreign purchases of established homes from 1 April 2025, which RevenueSA notes; landholder and corporate trustee acquisitions; concessions on general duty; Queensland’s absentee land tax rates, which replace the general rates; Victorian trust surcharge rates; joint land tax assessments; property converted to residential use later; and a surcharge charged again when a buyer becomes foreign within 3 years. The transfer duty calculator and the land tax calculator work the general duty with first home concessions and the general land tax. Confirm the result with the revenue office or a registered adviser before relying on it.

## How the business days calculator works

The calculator skips Saturdays, Sundays and the public holidays of the state or territory you choose, using each government’s published dates for 2026 and 2027. Between 2 dates, it counts the business days after the start date up to and including the end date, so a Friday to the next Monday is 1. Forward or back, it finds the date that is that many business days after or before the start, and lists each weekday holiday it skipped.

‘Any state or territory’ skips a day that is a public holiday throughout any one of them, as the site’s Payday Super business days table counts them, and leaves out Melbourne Cup Day and the WA King’s Birthday because neither applies throughout its state.

## What the business days calculator does not cover

Part-day holidays, such as the evenings of Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve in South Australia and the Northern Territory, count as business days. So do regional holidays, among them the Royal Queensland Show in Brisbane, Tasmania’s Royal Hobart Regatta and Recreation Day, and show days, and days off for one group only, such as Tasmania’s Easter Tuesday for the public service. A council or region’s local substitute for Melbourne Cup Day or the WA King’s Birthday is not applied: the state date is still skipped.

The calculator does not decide whether a law counts business days, clear days or calendar days, or which state’s holidays apply. Dates after 31 December 2027 are refused until the governments confirm the following year, and Victorian dates after 31 August 2027 until the AFL schedules its Grand Final. The calculator does not work out a superannuation guarantee or Payday Super due date.

## What Ozzit is

An Excel workbook, not a program. Open it, copy a function into your own model, and the formula travels with the file. Every definition is a native LAMBDA stored as a defined name, so the arithmetic stays inside the workbook where a reviewer can read it.

The v3.6.0 workbook carries 155 named formulas: 150 LAMBDA functions and 5 help tables that document them inside the workbook. Each module also ships an editable demonstration worksheet, and the formula source is published as plain text in the repository’s src/ folder and indexed in functions.csv.

The Australian helpers are deliberately small: GST addition and extraction with a 10% default rate, a 1 July financial year label, 5 helpers for the ATO’s small business benchmark ratios, 2 modelling depreciation schedules and 4 AASB 16 lessee schedules. ## One worked example

Take $1,100 that is assumed to be wholly taxable and GST-inclusive at 10%. Enter this formula in a cell of the Ozzit workbook:

```
=oz.GSTExtractλ(1100)
```

The result is 100.00. Check: 1100 / 11 = 100.00.

That is the whole of the function’s contribution: 10% arithmetic on an amount you supply.

Whether the supply is wholly taxable, whether a tax invoice supports it and whether the credit is claimable in that period are decisions for the reviewer. The function cannot see the evidence and does not classify the supply.

The 15 November 2026 native Excel record evaluates this formula against the exact v3.6.0 release workbook. It returned 99.999999999999986 before display rounding. The file hash was unchanged after the check. The repository also retains its historical v3.2.0 screenshot record.

## Limits

The functions calculate; a person decides the accounting treatment and signs off.

- The GST helpers apply 10% unless you supply another rate. They do not classify a supply, test registration or prepare a BAS.
- The ATO benchmark ratio helpers restate the ATO’s arithmetic for amounts you supply. They ship no benchmark ranges and do not read a tax return.
- oz.PrimeCostλ and oz.DiminishingValueλ are modelling schedules. They are not ATO Division 40 methods and they do not implement immediate write-off or low-value pools.
- The AASB 16 functions are lessee arithmetic. They do not decide the lease term, what counts as a lease payment or whether an arrangement contains a lease, and they do not cover lessor accounting or the short-term and low-value exemptions.
- The library contains no individual income tax, Medicare levy, Division 7A interest or tax rate cards.
- The 13-week cash flow, practice calculator and retention reconciliation workbooks in the repository’s templates folder are separate templates, not functions in this library.
- Known defects are listed in the repository’s changelog and issues. Check a schedule against an independent calculation before relying on it.

## Common questions

### What is Ozzit?

An Excel workbook containing 150 native LAMBDA functions and 5 help tables for financial modelling under Australian conventions, published free under the Ryan Duguid Public Attribution Licence 1.0. It has no add-in, macro or external dependency.

### Which Excel versions can run it?

Microsoft 365 or Excel 2024 or later, which support LAMBDA and dynamic arrays. LibreOffice and earlier Excel versions cannot evaluate the functions.

### Does Ozzit calculate Australian tax?

No. It performs 10% GST arithmetic and labels Australian financial years, and its depreciation and lease functions are modelling schedules rather than ATO or standard-setting methods. It contains no income tax, Medicare levy or Division 7A calculations.

### Where do I get the current release?

Release v3.6.0, published on 14 November 2026, is the current release and the one this page documents. It adds 7 functions, bringing the total to 150, and is the first release under the Ryan Duguid Public Attribution Licence 1.0; v3.5.0 stays under the Apache License 2.0. Each release ships ozzit.xlsx with a SHA256SUMS file and a provenance record on its GitHub release page. Compare the downloaded file’s SHA-256 with the published value before use. Read the v3.6.0 release page.

# Ozzit Dates functions

The 17 Dates formulas cover periods, timelines, schedules, overlaps and Australian financial year labels. Each entry gives what the workbook’s own help supplies: the signature, parameters, notes and worked examples.

Release v3.6.0. Read from the released Dates formula source, which the workbook compiles.

Published 20 October 2026. Last reviewed 20 October 2026.

## Using these functions

Each named formula is a native Excel LAMBDA in the free Ozzit workbook, or one of its 5 help tables. It needs Microsoft 365 or Excel 2024 or later. Enter a call in a cell of the workbook, or copy the definition from its name manager into your own model. Leave room for any spilled result.

The text below is the help each function carries in the v3.6.0 workbook. The workbook remains the authority, and a calculation does not decide an accounting treatment. Download the workbook and check its hash.

### oz.IsOccurrenceDateλ

oz.IsOccurrenceDateλ(Dates, FirstOccurrence, [LastOccurrence], [Repeats])

Returns TRUE if Date passed is when something happens. This was developed for budgeting where we have expenses that start, and sometimes repeat at regular intervals.

Returns TRUE if a Date is when something happens. Developed for budgeting, where expenses start and sometimes repeat at regular intervals.

Note: A monthly, quarterly, semi-annual or annual item that starts on the 29th, 30th or 31st falls on the last day of any shorter month.

Parameters:

Dates (required)

Date to test. Normally this is horizontal timeline by day

FirstOccurrence (required)

When something that may repeat, happens for the first time.

LastOccurrence (optional)

When something that may repeat, happens for the last time. If omitted, it is assumed repeating items repeat forever.

Repeats (optional)

A code that conveys how often this thing repeats. Repeat codes are O=One time/never repeats (default) A=Annually S=Semi-Annually Q=Quarterly M=Monthly B=BiWeekly W=Weekly X=Every Workday (Monday to Friday) D=Daily

### oz.RateAtDateλ

oz.RateAtDateλ(EffectiveDates, Rates, Dates)

Returns the rate in effect on each date from a table of effective dates and rates, refusing gaps, unsorted or repeated dates

Returns the rate in effect on each date from your table of effective dates and rates.

Note: Each rate applies from its effective date until the next one. Dates may be one date or a row or column of them, and the result takes their shape. A date before the first effective date has no rate in effect and returns #N/A. The table must be complete and in order, so instead of a result it returns a message when EffectiveDates and Rates differ in length or hold a blank or text entry, when the dates are not in ascending order, or when a date repeats. A text or blank date in Dates returns #VALUE!. The library ships no rate table: supply your own, with its source and the date you checked it. ScheduleRatesλ() lays rates across a timeline without these checks.

Parameters:

EffectiveDates (required)

A row or column of dates from which each rate applies, in ascending order.

Rates (required)

The rate that applies from each effective date, the same length as EffectiveDates.

Dates (required)

The date, or a row or column of dates, to look up.

```

### oz.DateDifλ

oz.DateDifλ(StartDate, EndDate, [Unit])

Whole years, months or days between two dates, and the remainders DATEDIF gets wrong

Whole years, months or days between two dates, and the remainders.

Note: Excel documents DATEDIF as a legacy compatibility function and warns its MD unit can return a negative or wrong day count. This counts a month as complete when EDATE() of the start date has arrived, so a month after 31 January is the last day of February, and takes every remainder from there. A reversed range or an unknown unit returns #NUM!.

Parameters:

StartDate (required)

EndDate (required)

The later date, numeric or text.

Unit (optional, Default = D)

```
