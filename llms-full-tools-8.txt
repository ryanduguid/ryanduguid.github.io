## How the study and training loan repayment calculator works

The study and training loan repayment calculator works out the compulsory repayment on a HELP, VET Student Loan, Student Start-up Loan or Australian Apprenticeship Support Loan debt for one income year. It first adds up repayment income: taxable income, reportable fringe benefits, total net investment loss, reportable super contributions and exempt foreign employment income.

From 2025-26 the repayment is marginal: nothing up to the minimum repayment income, then 15 cents for each dollar above it up to a second threshold and 17 cents above that, unless 10% of all repayment income is less. For 2026-27 the minimum repayment income is $69,528 and the second threshold $129,717, and 10% is the lesser amount from $186,051. For 2024-25 one percentage of the whole repayment income applied, from 1% above $54,434 to 10% from $159,664.

The boundary cases below take the inputs of the 4 worked examples on the ATO’s thresholds and rates page, read 17 November 2026, and work each one from section 154-20. For Barry’s 2026-27 repayment income of $137,064, section 154-20(2) gives 15% of the $60,189 from $69,528 to $129,717, which is $9,028.35, plus 17% of the $7,347 above $129,717, which is $1,248.99: $10,277.34 in all. For Branson’s 2024-25 repayment income of $99,736, the 5.5% rate gives $5,485.48.

## What the study and training loan repayment calculator does not compute

The calculator gives the compulsory repayment for one income year. It does not compute:

- the cap at the debt you still owe, or voluntary repayments;
- the overseas debtors repayment levy on worldwide income for a foreign resident (section 154-16);
- whether no repayment is due because your Medicare levy is not payable or is reduced under section 8 of the Medicare Levy Act 1986 (section 154-1(2));
- the amounts an employer withholds each pay for a loan, which the take-home pay calculator works for 2026-27, or indexation of the debt;
- years before 2020-21.

Confirm the figure with the ATO’s Study and training loan repayment calculator or a registered tax agent.

## How the payroll tax calculator works

The payroll tax calculator estimates one employer’s payroll tax for the whole of 2026-27 in one state or territory. Each jurisdiction allows a deduction or tax-free threshold, which an employer that also pays wages elsewhere in Australia shares in proportion to its wages there. Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia and the Northern Territory reduce the deduction as Australian wages grow, until it reaches nil. The result shows the deduction, the amount taxed, the tax at each rate and, in Victoria and Queensland, each surcharge or levy on its own line, with the provision applied.

Where a revenue office publishes worked figures, the tests hold them. Revenue NSW: $1,500,000 of NSW wages are taxed on $300,000, and $900,000 of $3,000,000 has a threshold of $360,000. The Victorian State Revenue Office: $80 million in Victoria and $70 million in New South Wales give surcharge thresholds of $5,333,333.33 and $53,333,333.33. The Queensland Revenue Office: $1,356,789 of $1,789,776 has a deduction of $932,460 to the dollar. RevenueWA: annual wages of $1,104,000 give $6,600; its other 2 examples round the deduction to the dollar and come out 1 and 3 cents lower than this calculator. RevenueSA: $800,000 of $1,600,000 has a deduction of $300,000 and $500,000 of $2,000,000 one of $150,000. The Tasmanian State Revenue Office: $2,400,000 gives $54,400, and $2,400,000 of $3,000,000 gives $72,800. The ACT Revenue Office and the Territory Revenue Office publish no worked figures that could be read, so the tests work examples from the Acts by hand: $84,375 on $3,000,000 in the Australian Capital Territory and $41,250 on $3,000,000 in the Northern Territory. This calculator opens on Revenue NSW’s first example.

## What the payroll tax calculator does not compute

The payroll tax calculator gives one annual estimate and no advice. It does not decide which payments are taxable wages or where they are taxable, and does not cover:

- an employer that is a member of a group, whose threshold and rate depend on the group’s wages and which members share the deduction;
- an employer that paid wages for only part of 2026-27, whose threshold every jurisdiction reduces by the days it employed;
- monthly or other periodic returns, and the annual reconciliation of amounts already paid;
- exemptions, rebates and discounts, such as Queensland’s apprentice and trainee rebate and its 1% discount for regional employers, claimed in the return, and the ACT’s 6.85% cap for an eligible university;
- any year other than 2026-27.

Confirm the figure with the revenue office or a registered tax agent before relying on it.

## How the SGC and Payday Super calculator works

From 1 July 2026, under Payday Super, an employer owes super guarantee for each payday, and the employee’s fund must receive it within 7 business days, or a longer period for a first contribution, out-of-cycle pay or an ATO determination. The calculator finds that due date from the site’s business days calendar, then works out the super guarantee charge for one employee if the fund received less than 12% of qualifying earnings by then: the final shortfall still unpaid at the assessment, notional earnings at the GIC rate on the amount that was late, and the administrative uplift, reduced if the ATO had not itself assessed or estimated the employer’s SG charge from 1 July 2026 to the payday, and for a voluntary disclosure. A contribution counts from the day the fund received it with what it needs to allocate it to the employee. For a quarter that ended by 30 June 2026 it applies the old law instead: the shortfall at that year’s charge percentage, 10% nominal interest and the $20 administration component.

## What the SGC and Payday Super calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employee and one payday or quarter, and no advice. It does not cover:

- the choice loading for not meeting choice of fund obligations;
- the late payment offset under the old section 23A for quarterly contributions made late;
- bunched contributions under section 18C(2) item 4;
- a contribution made from 1 to 28 July 2026 that item 186 of the transition applies first to a shortfall for the quarter ending 30 June 2026: count only what is left of it;
- a disclosed contribution with no receipt day, whose notional earnings section 36(3) works out as if the fund received it 7 business days after it was paid;
- exemption certificates, and amounts paid that have not reached the fund;
- GIC and penalties once the charge is payable;
- paydays after 30 June 2027, whose maximum contributions base is not yet known, and quarters before July 2022.

Confirm the figure with the ATO or a registered tax agent before relying on it.

## How the long service leave calculator works

Long service leave comes from each state and territory’s own Act, not the National Employment Standards. The calculator measures the service from the first day to the last, less the days you exclude, and applies the Act you choose. While employment continues, it gives the leave the employee can take now and when the next amount becomes available. When employment ends, it decides from the length of service and the reason whether the Act pays anything, works out the weeks it pays, deducts leave already taken or paid, and values the rest at the weekly ordinary pay. Each step names its provision. Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory give full leave after 7 years and the other states and territories after 10; New South Wales and the Australian Capital Territory can pay a pro rata amount from 5 years, the others from 7, and Victoria not before 7 years at all.

## What the long service leave calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate under one Act for one employee, and no legal advice. It does not cover:

- which Act applies to employment in more than one state or territory, and whether an award, enterprise agreement or contract gives a better entitlement, including the long service leave terms of a pre-2010 award or agreement that section 113 of the Fair Work Act 2009 preserves;
- portable schemes, such as building and construction, contract cleaning, community services and security, and the black coal mining industry, Commonwealth and state public servants, and Tasmanian mining employees, who have their own rules;
- whether a break keeps service continuous, which absences count, and whether a reason for leaving qualifies, such as a pressing necessity or serious misconduct: you decide those;
- averaging pay for varied hours, casual or part-time work, commission or piece rates;
- leave taken in advance of the entitlement, cashing out, and service before 23 June 1990, which some Acts count under earlier rules;
- tax withheld from the payment.

The figures follow each Act as read on 19 November 2026; a future date assumes it is unchanged. Confirm the result with the state or territory’s long service leave regulator or a lawyer before relying on it.

## How the company tax and franking calculator works

The calculator applies the base rate entity test of section 23AA of the Income Tax Rates Act 1986 to this year’s figures: aggregated turnover under $50 million, and base rate entity passive income of no more than 80% of assessable income. A base rate entity pays 25% of its taxable income, and any other company 30% (subsection 23(2)).

The rate for franking a dividend comes from a second test. Section 995-1 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 runs the base rate entity test on the previous year’s turnover, passive income and assessable income, so a company can pay tax at 30% and frank at 25%, or the reverse; a company that did not exist in the previous year franks at 25%. The maximum franking credit is the dividend times 1 over the gross-up rate, which is 100% less that rate, over that rate (section 202-60): one third of the dividend at 25% and three sevenths at 30%. A credit above the maximum is taken to be the maximum (section 202-65).

The shareholder includes the dividend and its franking credit in assessable income and gets a tax offset equal to the credit (section 207-20). Tax at the marginal rate you enter, less the offset, is the extra tax the dividend brings; where the offset is larger, an individual gets the excess refunded (section 67-25).

The calculator reproduces the outcome of each ATO example: Happy Feet (3.8% passive income, 25%), Coffee and Cake (28.6%, 25%), Best Equity (100%, 30%), SoftDev (85%, 30%) and Hats 4 Cats ($170 million of aggregated turnover, 30%). For Dillmore Manufacture’s $100,000 dividend franked at 30% it gives $42,857.14, the exact credit; the ATO rounds the gross-up rate to 2.3333 and shows $42,857.75, which is more than the section 202-60 maximum.

## What the company tax and franking calculator does not cover

The calculator does not work out aggregated turnover, assessable income or base rate entity passive income: enter figures you have established, with the turnover of connected entities and affiliates included, as the ATO’s Hats 4 Cats example shows. Taxable income gives the tax before offsets, PAYG instalments and other credits.

Not covered: the rates of RSA providers and pooled development funds (subsections 23(3) to (5)), life insurance companies (section 23A), not-for-profit companies and credit unions; corporate unit trusts and public trading trusts; and income years before 2021-22, when the base rate was 27.5% or 26%. A shareholder that is a company, trust, super fund or non-resident is outside its scope; a company shareholder cannot have excess franking credits refunded (section 67-25). Franking account balances, franking deficit tax, the benchmark rule and the holding period rule are not tested: the company tax and franking worksheets track the franking account and the benchmark rule. Have a registered tax agent confirm the figures and the result.

## How the foreign buyer surcharge calculator works

The calculator applies the chosen state or territory’s own test of who is foreign to your answers, once for its duty surcharge and once for its land tax surcharge, as each revenue office states them. The duty surcharge is its rate times the dutiable value of the buyer’s share: 9% in New South Wales, 8% in Victoria, Queensland and Tasmania (1.5% on Tasmanian primary production land), and 7% in Western Australia and South Australia. The ACT and the Northern Territory charge none.

The land tax surcharge is for a full land tax year on the land value you enter: 5% in New South Wales, 4% in Victoria from $50,000 of taxable land ($25,000 for a trust), 3% of the value over $350,000 in Queensland, 2% in Tasmania and 0.75% in the ACT. Western Australia and South Australia charge none, and the Northern Territory has no land tax. Victoria charges a fixed trust’s surcharge on the land held for absentee beneficiaries only. The general transfer duty is worked on the whole property, for all buyers together, at the general rates.

The calculator reproduces the revenue offices’ examples: Revenue NSW’s $90,000 of surcharge purchaser duty and $39,187 of transfer duty on $1 million, and $60,000 of surcharge land tax on $1.2 million; Victoria’s $80,000 for Andrew, $16,000 for Emily’s $200,000 share and $20,000 for Clara’s half of a fixed trust’s $1 million; RevenueSA’s $42,000 and $21,000 for Peter; Western Australia’s $14,000 for Simon; Queensland’s $1,500 on $400,000; and Tasmania’s $8,000 on $400,000.

## Who is foreign in each state and territory

An individual who is an Australian citizen, holds a permanent visa or is a New Zealand citizen on a special category visa is not foreign for Queensland, Western Australian, South Australian and Tasmanian duty. New South Wales also requires a permanent resident or New Zealand citizen to have been in Australia 200 days of the previous 12 months (Revenue Ruling G009); Victoria gives a New Zealand citizen no exception unless they ordinarily reside in Australia for 6 consecutive months around settlement. Victorian and Queensland land tax charge absentees: non-citizens without a permanent visa who do not ordinarily reside in Australia and were away on the day or for over 6 months of the year.

A company is foreign when it is incorporated outside Australia, or foreign persons with associates hold 50% or more (more than 50% in Victoria) of its shares or votes, or in New South Wales, Queensland, Tasmania and Western Australia of its potential voting power. New South Wales instead asks whether one foreign person with associates holds 20% or more, or foreign persons together 40% or more, wherever the company is incorporated. Trusts and super funds follow the same shares of capital or members’ balances. Victorian land tax counts absentee interests instead, which you enter apart: a company pays absentee owner surcharge when absentees hold more than 50%, a fixed trust on the land held for absentee beneficiaries, and a complying super fund none. A foreign person who ordinarily lives in Australia is no absentee.

A discretionary trust is foreign in New South Wales if a foreign person could benefit, now or by amending the deed (section 104JA); in Victoria and Tasmania if foreign persons could be given more than 50%, or 50% or more, of the capital; in Queensland if foreign takers in default hold 50% or more; in Western Australia if a foreign person controls it, as a foreign trustee or appointor does, or foreign takers in default hold 50% or more; in South Australia if a trustee, appointor, named object or taker in default is foreign; and in the ACT only if a named foreign beneficiary could receive 50% or more. Victorian land tax looks only at named (specified) beneficiaries who are absentees.

## What the foreign buyer surcharge calculator leaves to you

The calculator does not decide exemptions, refunds or ex gratia relief, which each Commissioner grants on application. Among them are Australian-based developers and build to rent in New South Wales and Victoria, exempt permanent residents who live in the home in New South Wales, significant contributors and large developers in Queensland, and refunds when a buyer stops being foreign within 12 months in South Australia. When one is sought, it refuses. It also refuses a partner (provisional) or retirement visa holder, whom the revenue offices treat differently, and does not trace interests through other companies and trusts or decide who is an associate.

Not covered: the Commonwealth’s foreign investment application fees and its restrictions on foreign purchases of established homes from 1 April 2025, which RevenueSA notes; landholder and corporate trustee acquisitions; concessions on general duty; Queensland’s absentee land tax rates, which replace the general rates; Victorian trust surcharge rates; control of a Western Australian discretionary trust by a foreign person other than its trustee or appointor, such as one whose consent a distribution needs; control of a Victorian company through its board or through influence the Commissioner finds, rather than through shares or votes; a Victorian beneficiary nomination for trust land bought before 2006, under which the surcharge starts at $50,000 rather than $25,000; joint land tax assessments; property converted to residential use later; and a surcharge charged again when a buyer becomes foreign within 3 years. The transfer duty calculator and the land tax calculator work the general duty with first home concessions and the general land tax. Confirm the result with the revenue office or a registered adviser before relying on it.

## How the business days calculator works

The calculator skips Saturdays, Sundays and the public holidays of the state or territory you choose, using each government’s published dates for 2026 and 2027. Between 2 dates, it counts the business days after the start date up to and including the end date, so a Friday to the next Monday is 1. Forward or back, it finds the date that is that many business days after or before the start, and lists each weekday holiday it skipped.

‘Any state or territory’ skips a day that is a public holiday throughout any one of them, as the site’s Payday Super business days table counts them, and leaves out Melbourne Cup Day and the WA King’s Birthday because neither applies throughout its state.

## What the business days calculator does not cover

Part-day holidays, such as the evenings of Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve in South Australia and the Northern Territory, count as business days. So do regional holidays, among them the Royal Queensland Show in Brisbane, Tasmania’s Royal Hobart Regatta and Recreation Day, and show days, and days off for one group only, such as Tasmania’s Easter Tuesday for the public service. A council or region’s local substitute for Melbourne Cup Day or the WA King’s Birthday is not applied: the state date is still skipped.

The calculator does not decide whether a law counts business days, clear days or calendar days, or which state’s holidays apply. Dates after 31 December 2027 are refused until the governments confirm the following year, and Victorian dates after 31 August 2027 until the AFL schedules its Grand Final. The calculator does not work out a superannuation guarantee or Payday Super due date.
