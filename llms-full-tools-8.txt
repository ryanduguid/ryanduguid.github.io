## How the SGC and Payday Super calculator works

From 1 July 2026, under Payday Super, an employer owes super guarantee for each payday, and the employee’s fund must receive it within 7 business days, or a longer period for a first contribution, out-of-cycle pay or an ATO determination. The calculator finds that due date from the site’s business days calendar, then works out the super guarantee charge for one employee if the fund received less than 12% of qualifying earnings by then: the final shortfall still unpaid at the assessment, notional earnings at the GIC rate on the amount that was late, and the administrative uplift, reduced for a first offence and a voluntary disclosure. For a quarter that ended by 30 June 2026 it applies the old law instead: the shortfall at that year’s charge percentage, 10% nominal interest and the $20 administration component.

## What the SGC and Payday Super calculator does not cover

The calculator gives an estimate for one employee and one payday or quarter, and no advice. It does not cover:

- the choice loading for not meeting choice of fund obligations;
- the late payment offset under the old section 23A for quarterly contributions made late;
- bunched contributions under section 18C(2) item 4;
- a contribution made from 1 to 28 July 2026 that item 186 of the transition applies first to a shortfall for the quarter ending 30 June 2026: count only what is left of it;
- a disclosed contribution with no receipt day, whose notional earnings section 36(3) works out as if the fund received it 7 business days after it was paid;
- exemption certificates, and amounts paid that have not reached the fund;
- GIC and penalties once the charge is payable;
- paydays after 30 June 2027, whose maximum contributions base is not yet known, and quarters before July 2022.

Confirm the figure with the ATO or a registered tax agent before relying on it.

## What Ozzit is

An Excel workbook, not a program. Open it, copy a function into your own model, and the formula travels with the file. Every definition is a native LAMBDA stored as a defined name, so the arithmetic stays inside the workbook where a reviewer can read it.

The v3.6.0 workbook carries 155 named formulas: 150 LAMBDA functions and 5 help tables that document them inside the workbook. Each module also ships an editable demonstration worksheet, and the formula source is published as plain text in the repository’s src/ folder and indexed in functions.csv.

The Australian helpers are deliberately small: GST addition and extraction with a 10% default rate, a 1 July financial year label, 5 helpers for the ATO’s small business benchmark ratios, 2 modelling depreciation schedules and 4 AASB 16 lessee schedules. The rest of the library is general financial modelling.

Release v3.6.0 adds 7 functions: oz.Varianceλ and oz.VariancePctλ for budget variance, oz.FinancialMonthλ, oz.FinancialQuarterλ and oz.YearToDateλ for financial-year periods, oz.LevelPaymentλ for a level loan payment when each period has its own rate, and oz.CorkscrewRateλ for a corkscrew whose interest runs on the average of each period’s opening and closing balances. Each has its entry in the index below.

## Who it is for and what it needs

Ozzit suits an accountant or analyst who builds models in Excel and wants the working visible rather than hidden in an add-in or a macro. It is not a bookkeeping tool, and it never touches a ledger.

## One worked example

Take $1,100 that is assumed to be wholly taxable and GST-inclusive at 10%. Enter this formula in a cell of the Ozzit workbook:

```
=oz.GSTExtractλ(1100)
```

The result is 100.00. Check: 1100 / 11 = 100.00.

That is the whole of the function’s contribution: 10% arithmetic on an amount you supply.

Whether the supply is wholly taxable, whether a tax invoice supports it and whether the credit is claimable in that period are decisions for the reviewer. The function cannot see the evidence and does not classify the supply.

The 15 November 2026 native Excel record evaluates this formula against the exact v3.6.0 release workbook. It returned 99.999999999999986 before display rounding. The file hash was unchanged after the check. The repository also retains its historical v3.2.0 screenshot record.

## Limits

The functions calculate; a person decides the accounting treatment and signs off.

- The GST helpers apply 10% unless you supply another rate. They do not classify a supply, test registration or prepare a BAS.
- The ATO benchmark ratio helpers restate the ATO’s arithmetic for amounts you supply. They ship no benchmark ranges and do not read a tax return.
- oz.PrimeCostλ and oz.DiminishingValueλ are modelling schedules. They are not ATO Division 40 methods and they do not implement immediate write-off or low-value pools.
- The AASB 16 functions are lessee arithmetic. They do not decide the lease term, what counts as a lease payment or whether an arrangement contains a lease, and they do not cover lessor accounting or the short-term and low-value exemptions.
- The library contains no individual income tax, Medicare levy, Division 7A interest or tax rate cards.
- The 13-week cash flow, practice calculator and retention reconciliation workbooks in the repository’s templates folder are separate templates, not functions in this library.
- Known defects are listed in the repository’s changelog and issues. Check a schedule against an independent calculation before relying on it.

## Common questions

### What is Ozzit?

An Excel workbook containing 150 native LAMBDA functions and 5 help tables for financial modelling under Australian conventions, published free under the Ryan Duguid Public Attribution Licence 1.0. It has no add-in, macro or external dependency.

### Which Excel versions can run it?

Microsoft 365 or Excel 2024 or later, which support LAMBDA and dynamic arrays. LibreOffice and earlier Excel versions cannot evaluate the functions.

### Does Ozzit calculate Australian tax?

No. It performs 10% GST arithmetic and labels Australian financial years, and its depreciation and lease functions are modelling schedules rather than ATO or standard-setting methods. It contains no income tax, Medicare levy or Division 7A calculations.

### Where do I get the current release?

Release v3.6.0, published on 14 November 2026, is the current release and the one this page documents. It adds 7 functions, bringing the total to 150, and is the first release under the Ryan Duguid Public Attribution Licence 1.0; v3.5.0 stays under the Apache License 2.0. Each release ships ozzit.xlsx with a SHA256SUMS file and a provenance record on its GitHub release page. Compare the downloaded file’s SHA-256 with the published value before use. Read the v3.6.0 release page.

> Parameters and worked examples for 17 Ozzit Dates Excel LAMBDA functions: periods, timelines, schedules, overlaps and Australian financial year labels.

# Ozzit Dates functions

The 17 Dates formulas cover periods, timelines, schedules, overlaps and Australian financial year labels. Each entry gives what the workbook’s own help supplies: the signature, parameters, notes and worked examples.

Release v3.6.0. Read from the released Dates formula source, which the workbook compiles.

Published 20 October 2026. Last reviewed 20 October 2026.

## Using these functions

Each named formula is a native Excel LAMBDA in the free Ozzit workbook, or one of its 5 help tables. It needs Microsoft 365 or Excel 2024 or later. Enter a call in a cell of the workbook, or copy the definition from its name manager into your own model. Leave room for any spilled result.

The text below is the help each function carries in the v3.6.0 workbook. The workbook remains the authority, and a calculation does not decide an accounting treatment. Download the workbook and check its hash.

### oz.IsOccurrenceDateλ

oz.IsOccurrenceDateλ(Dates, FirstOccurrence, [LastOccurrence], [Repeats])

Returns TRUE if Date passed is when something happens. This was developed for budgeting where we have expenses that start, and sometimes repeat at regular intervals.

Returns TRUE if a Date is when something happens. Developed for budgeting, where expenses start and sometimes repeat at regular intervals.

Note: A monthly, quarterly, semi-annual or annual item that starts on the 29th, 30th or 31st falls on the last day of any shorter month.

Parameters:

Dates (required)

Date to test. Normally this is horizontal timeline by day

FirstOccurrence (required)

When something that may repeat, happens for the first time.

LastOccurrence (optional)

Repeats (optional)

### oz.RateAtDateλ

oz.RateAtDateλ(EffectiveDates, Rates, Dates)

Returns the rate in effect on each date from a table of effective dates and rates, refusing gaps, unsorted or repeated dates

Returns the rate in effect on each date from your table of effective dates and rates.

Note: Each rate applies from its effective date until the next one. Dates may be one date or a row or column of them, and the result takes their shape. A date before the first effective date has no rate in effect and returns #N/A. The table must be complete and in order, so instead of a result it returns a message when EffectiveDates and Rates differ in length or hold a blank or text entry, when the dates are not in ascending order, or when a date repeats. Dates (required)

### oz.DateDifλ

oz.DateDifλ(StartDate, EndDate, [Unit])

Whole years, months or days between two dates, and the remainders DATEDIF gets wrong

Whole years, months or days between two dates, and the remainders.

Note: Excel documents DATEDIF as a legacy compatibility function and warns its MD unit can return a negative or wrong day count. This counts a month as complete when EDATE() of the start date has arrived, so a month after 31 January is the last day of February, and takes every remainder from there. A reversed range or an unknown unit returns #NUM!.

Parameters:

StartDate (required)

The earlier date, numeric or text.

EndDate (required)

The later date, numeric or text.

Unit (optional, Default = D)

Y whole years, M whole months, D days, YM months left after the whole years, YD days left after the whole years, MD days left after the whole months.

```
=oz.DateDifλ(DATE(2026,1,31), DATE(2026,3,1), "MD")
```

The workbook help shows 1.

# Ozzit Essentials functions

The 17 Essentials formulas cover row and column totals, averages, counts and range conversion for dynamic arrays. Each entry gives what the workbook’s own help supplies: the signature, parameters, notes and worked examples.

Release v3.6.0. Read from the released Essentials formula source, which the workbook compiles.

Published 20 October 2026. Last reviewed 20 October 2026.

## Using these functions

Each named formula is a native Excel LAMBDA in the free Ozzit workbook, or one of its 5 help tables. It needs Microsoft 365 or Excel 2024 or later. Enter a call in a cell of the workbook, or copy the definition from its name manager into your own model. Leave room for any spilled result.

The text below is the help each function carries in the v3.6.0 workbook. The workbook remains the authority, and a calculation does not decide an accounting treatment. Download the workbook and check its hash.

### oz.IsBetweenEλ

oz.IsBetweenEλ( Value, Low, Hi, [Inclusive])

Determine if a value is between a lower and upper limit

Determine if a value is between a lower and upper limit.

Parameters:

Value (required)

A value or array of values to compare.

Low (required)

The lower limit that the value must be greater than

Hi (required)

The higher limit that the value must be less than

Inclusive (optional)

If set to TRUE (default) the value can be equal to Low and/or Hi

```
=IsBetweenEλ(1, 2, 4)
```

```
=IsBetweenEλ(2, 2, 4)
```

The workbook help shows TRUE.

```
=IsBetweenEλ(2, 2, 4, FALSE)
```

The workbook help shows FALSE.
